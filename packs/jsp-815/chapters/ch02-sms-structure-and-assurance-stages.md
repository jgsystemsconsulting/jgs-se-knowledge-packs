## Core Idea

The Defence SMS is a four-stage Plan-Do-Check-Act system that divides Defence Area activity into 12 Elements, 72 Expectations, and supporting Performance Statements. Dir-DSS owns the Safety Function, the Safety Operating Model, and second-line assurance against the SMS. Assurance levels are sequential (unsatisfactory, limited, moderate, substantial) and build on each prior stage. The Framework states alignment to ISO 45001 with additional and different Defence requirements; this pack never reproduces ISO wording.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Plan-Do-Check-Act (Figure 2, named only).** A management system here means the practices, processes, documents, and information systems that organise, direct, and control safety management (often called safety management arrangements). The Defence SMS is based on the four-stage Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle that delivers and continually improves performance against safety management (paragraphs 14 to 16).
- **Three properties of the Defence SMS (paragraph 17).** It is non-prescriptive and uses devolved accountability so each Defence Area manages safety through its own governance and operational context while staying consistent with Defence requirements. It sets responsibilities plus Expectations and Performance Statements that all Defence Areas must conform to when building governance, strategies, processes, and performance indicators, including systems to find and fix performance failures. It is aligned to ISO 45001 (occupational health and safety management systems) but carries additional and different requirements for Defence needs.
- **Goal-based ambition (paragraph 18).** JSP 815 sets the safety ambition of Defence and leaves each Defence Area free to develop tailored pathways to that ambition.
- **Defence Safety Function (paragraphs 19 to 20).** The Function is the structure and processes that let Defence deliver operational capability while minimising risk to personnel and equipment, supporting a proactive safety culture. On behalf of Director General People and Standards and the Permanent Secretary, Dir-DSS holds Function leadership and corporate governance of Defence safety, owns the Function Strategy, the Safety Operating Model, and the overarching Defence SMS, and conducts second-line Function assurance against the SMS and Dir-DSS-owned policies.
- **Element / Expectation / Performance Statement hierarchy (paragraphs 21 to 23, Figures 3 and 4).** Twelve Elements cover Defence Area activities holistically. Each Element has Expectations describing activities expected in a Defence Area's arrangements (typical processes, governance, and behaviours). Each Expectation is articulated by Performance Statements that show how compliance and continual improvement are demonstrated, with example performance across assurance levels from unsatisfactory through limited and moderate to substantial. Some Elements may not apply fully to every activity (example given: Element 8 often not required for Adventurous Training).
- **Assurance stages (paragraphs 31 to 32, Figure 5).** Levels are sequential and cumulative: substantial only after moderate expectations and requirements are met, and so on. Figure 5 gives typical characteristics of processes and controls at each level, aligned with Government Internal Audit Agency (GIAA) assessment criteria.

## Key Concepts

### Must and should

Where the Defence SMS says must, the action is compulsory. Where it says should, the action is not compulsory but is considered good practice (Terms and definitions).

### Using the SMS (paragraphs 25 to 27)

Each Defence Area must develop and implement safety management arrangements that meet the 12 Elements and accompanying Expectations. Defence Areas should take an evidence-based approach; several data sources will usually feed current performance measurement. Documentation listed in each Element is a starting point for assessing arrangements, supporting evidence against each Expectation, and judging overall Element performance.

### Relationship to the Defence EMS (paragraphs 28 to 30)

JSP 816 sets the Defence Environmental Management System. It also has 12 Elements with Expectations and performance statements aligned in structure to the Defence SMS, with content amended for environmental management. Defence Areas may combine safety and environmental arrangements where that fits. JSP 816 should be read with other Defence policy as appropriate (examples named: JSP 418, JSP 426, JSP 392, JSP 850). This pack does not carry JSP 816 content.

### Assurance levels (Figure 5, GIAA-aligned criteria summarised)

For each level the Framework asks, in substance, three questions of key controls: are prescribed policies, processes, and controls embedded; do they help manage key risks; do they operate as intended?

- **Substantial:** strong evidence that prescribed policies, processes, and key controls are fully embedded; that they help manage key risks; and that they operate as intended with no weaknesses identified.
- **Moderate:** evidence that controls are fully embedded but could be improved; that they help manage key risks but could be improved; and that they operate as intended with some minor non-compliance.
- **Limited:** some but not enough evidence that controls are operating and embedded; lack of confidence that controls are designed to manage key risks; evidence that controls are not operating as intended or fail in numerous instances.
- **Unsatisfactory:** evidence that prescribed controls are lacking, poorly defined, or not embedded; that designed controls do not help manage key risks; and that controls are not measured for compliance or are not adhered to.

### ISO 45001 alignment claim (as F states it)

Preface paragraph 2: the policy has been developed to align with ISO 45001 requirements. Paragraph 17c: the Defence SMS is aligned to ISO 45001 but has some additional and different requirements to meet the specific needs of Defence. Clause-number mapping lives in Annex F and is treated in ch10; no ISO standard text appears in this pack.

### Three Lines of Defence pointer

## Mental Models

- **PDCA is the spine; Elements are the ribs.** Plan, Do, Check, and Act organise the work; the 12 Elements are the topic cuts across that cycle.
- **Expectation number is the cite key.** Quote E4.7, not a Performance Statement cell. Performance Statements show what evidence looks like across assurance levels; they are not a second rulebook.
- **Substantial sits on moderate.** You do not jump the ladder; each stage builds on the one below.
- **Aligned is not identical.** ISO 45001 alignment is a Framework claim plus Defence deltas, not a licence to paste ISO clauses into Defence arrangements from this pack.
- **Function owns the system; Areas own the arrangements.** Dir-DSS owns SMS and 2LOD Function assurance; each Defence Area builds and runs its own evidence-based arrangements.

## Anti-patterns

- Copying another Defence Area's SMS wholesale without tailoring to local activity and governance.
- Treating Performance Statement examples as mandatory checklists or quoting them as rules.
- Claiming substantial assurance while moderate gaps remain open.
- Equating "aligned to ISO 45001" with "ISO 45001 is sufficient for Defence."
- Skipping Elements that look civilian-only (or military-only) without checking applicability notes.
- Running 1LOD self-checks and calling them Function assurance (2LOD is a Dir-DSS Function duty).

## Key Takeaways

1. Defence SMS = PDCA + 12 Elements + 72 Expectations + Performance Statement themes under Dir-DSS ownership.
2. Devolved accountability: common Expectations, local arrangements, evidence-based assessment.
3. Assurance levels are unsatisfactory, limited, moderate, substantial; sequential and GIAA-aligned on embed, risk relevance, and operation of controls.
4. ISO 45001 alignment is stated with Defence-specific extras; clause numbers only appear via Annex F in ch10.
5. JSP 816 mirrors the structure for environment; combining arrangements is allowed, content stays separate.
6. Read ch01 for the publication map; open Element chapters for direction against each Expectation group.
