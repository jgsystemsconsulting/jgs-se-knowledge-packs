## Core Idea

JSP 815 is the overarching Defence Safety Management System (SMS). It turns the Secretary of State's Health and Safety Policy Statement into a goal-based framework that every Defence Area must align to when building its own safety management arrangements. This chapter is a map: which named publication answers which kind of need, who owns the SMS, and how far duty of care reaches. Detail on Elements, Expectations, and assurance stages lives in later chapters.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **SofS Policy Statement as authority.** The Framework takes its authority from the Secretary of State for Defence's Policy Statement for Health and Safety in Defence. Senior leaders of Defence Areas must comply with Defence Regulations, the Department's SMS, supporting policy, governance, and risk management, and must set health and safety management arrangements consistent with those requirements in their areas of responsibility (Introduction paragraphs 1 to 2; Preface paragraph 3).
- **JSP 815 as the Defence SMS.** JSP 815 is the minimum necessary management arrangements for complying with Defence safety policy. It is made of 12 Elements and associated Annexes that provide direction that must be followed and guidance that should be followed so Defence Areas meet their legal duty of care (Preface paragraphs 1 to 2; Introduction paragraph 2).
- **Devolved, goal-based model.** The SMS avoids prescribing one approach for every user. It sets goals and describes what good looks like so each Defence Area can tailor arrangements to its activities while relating them back to JSP 815 (Purpose paragraphs 12 to 13; see also ch02).
- **Policy and regulation stack (Figure 1, named only).** The Framework places JSP 815 with the Defence Safety Function Operating Model and supporting publications JSP 375, JSP 376, and JSP 426. Figure 1 is the Defence safety policy and regulation framework diagram; this pack does not reproduce it (Purpose paragraph 12).

### Duty of care scope (Framework Scope and Purpose)

The policy in JSP 815:

- applies to all those employed by Defence (military and civilian), including those under 18 (for example recruits and apprentices);
- applies to those working on behalf of, or under the supervision of, Defence (for example contractors or visitors);
- applies to all Defence activities in any location (UK or overseas);
- is not written for young persons in the cadet forces, Defence-run schools, nurseries, and similar. Those organisations must keep their own safety policies and governance and provide statutory-compliant infrastructure and safe systems of work. They may use JSP 815 as a reference point, adapted for young persons and any applicable Department for Education rules. Cadet forces guidance is set out in JSP 814 (Scope a to d and footnote 1).

Duty of care under JSP 815 is the direction Defence should follow toward personnel. It also helps demonstrate compliance with safety legislation such as the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (HSWA74), applicable parts of other legislation such as the Equality Act 2010, and relevant Government and Defence policy and regulations (Purpose paragraph 5). Leaders must extend duty of care to all personnel under their area of responsibility; where risk assessments identify exposure, risks must be reduced to as low as is reasonably practicable (ALARP), controls implemented, and reasonable adjustments agreed where needed (Purpose paragraphs 7 to 8).

"Safety" in JSP 815 encompasses health by providing safe systems of work and protecting people from harm and ill-health. Some passages still say health and safety where statute or Defence policy does. Other health topics sit with the Healthcare and Medical Operational Capability Function (JSP 950) and the People Function (JSP 661) (Introduction paragraph 4).

### Defence Area

In Defence Safety Policy (JSP 815, JSP 375, and JSP 376), a Defence Area is one of the four top-level areas: Department of State (DOS); Armed Forces (MSHQ and Military Commands); National Armaments Director (NAD) Group; and the Defence Nuclear Enterprise (DNE). For the Armed Forces, Defence Area responsibility also applies to the component Military Commands (Army, Air, Navy, and CSOC) (Terms and definitions).

### Jurisdiction note

References to legislation in the SMS generally mean UK law. For overseas activity or presence, the SofS Policy Statement requires Defence Areas to consider the latest Defence policy, guidance on applying UK standards, and the host nation's relevant safety expectations where these are not aligned (Preface paragraph 4).

### Owner and contacts

The Director of Defence Safety and Support (Dir-DSS) owns JSP 815 and leads the Defence Safety Function. The document is subject to scheduled review. Feedback routes to the Directorate of Defence Safety and Support (DDSS) (Preface paragraphs 5 to 6; Foreword). Nothing in JSP 815 removes the duty to comply with applicable statute, the SofS Policy Statement, other JSPs, or Defence Regulations (Disclaimer).

### What this pack does not carry from the map

DSA regulators, detailed workplace procedures, acquisition safety methods, EMS content, and cadet-force rules are outside this orientation slice. ## Mental Models

- **Policy Statement first, JSP 815 second, Defence Area arrangements third.** Authority flows SofS Policy Statement to Defence SMS to local safety management arrangements.
- **Title map, not content map.** Naming JSP 375 or JSP 816 tells you which shelf to pull; it does not import that shelf into this pack.
- **Duty of care is people-and-place wide; document scope is not.** Cadets, schools, and nurseries still need safe arrangements, but JSP 815 is not written as their primary rulebook (JSP 814 and own policies).
- **SMS and EMS are twins, not one book.** JSP 815 and JSP 816 share structure so organisations may combine arrangements; they remain separate JSPs (paragraphs 28 to 29).

## Anti-patterns

- Treating JSP 815 as optional guidance above a local SMS that ignores the 12 Elements.
- Reading "supported by JSP 375 / 376 / 426" as permission to quote those JSPs from this pack.
- Applying JSP 815 unchanged as the cadet-force or school rulebook instead of JSP 814 and organisation-specific policy.
- Assuming overseas activity drops UK standards without reading the SofS overseas formulation (host law, then UK standards so far as reasonably practicable where host law falls short).
- Looking here for DSA regulator procedures; use ch04 and the Defence regulations themselves.

## Key Takeaways

1. JSP 815 is the overarching Defence SMS under the SofS Health and Safety Policy Statement; Dir-DSS owns it via the Defence Safety Function.
2. Every Defence Area must maintain safety management arrangements that align with the Defence SMS and show compliance with it.
3. Use the title map: JSP 815 for SMS structure; Safety Function Operating Model for function roles; JSP 375 / 376 / 426 as named supports; JSP 816 for EMS; JSP 814 for cadet forces.
4. Duty of care covers Defence employees (including under-18s in scope), people working on behalf of or under Defence supervision, and all Defence activity UK and overseas; cadets, schools, and nurseries are out of this JSP's drafting scope.
5. This chapter is orientation only. Structure, Elements, and assurance stages are ch02; Element detail starts at ch03.
