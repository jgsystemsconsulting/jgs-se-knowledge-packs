## Core Idea

Element 5 is control of activity: Safe Systems of Work, legal Duty of Care, delegated authority, competence of those holding authority, elevation of significant risk, letters of delegation, the duty to pause or cease when risk is no longer ALARP and tolerable, and contractor control on the Defence estate. Duty Holding is an enhanced construct for selected high Risk-to-Life military activities; it does not replace Duty of Care. Annex C extends duty of care onto deployments. Annex D defines Head of Establishment (HoE) appointment and stop-work authority on land-based sites.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Safe System of Work (paragraph 3).** Every Defence activity runs inside an SSW: control measures from risk assessment grouped as safe person, safe practice, safe place, and safe equipment. Applies to military and civilian employees and to people working on behalf of Defence, in any location. JSP 375 Chapter 8 sets SSW requirements (named only).
- **4Cs for visiting workers and contractors (Purpose paragraph 2).** Coordination, co-operation, communication, and control. Method detail in JSP 375 Chapter 34 (named only).
- **Duty of Care (paragraphs 4 to 8).** Legal obligation on employers to take reasonable steps for health, safety, and welfare of employees and others affected (HSWA sections 2 and 3). As a legal concept, individuals also owe and are owed duties to prevent foreseeable harm. For Defence it runs from SofS down the chain of command. Those controlling activity must mitigate to ALARP and tolerable and may pause or cease activity when that standard fails. Common-law duty of care follows the relationship, not the map: it applies on operations and exercises in the UK and overseas even where statute is geographically limited. SofS Policy Statement overseas formulation matches ch01/ch04 (host law; UK standards SFAIRP where host law falls short).
- **Duty Holding construct (paragraphs 9 to 24).** Introduced after the Nimrod Review in aviation; now available across Defence domains for certain activities. Enhances, does not replace, legal Duty of Care. Aimed at military activities that are high risk with credible, reasonably foreseeable Risk to Life (RtL). Four levels: Executive Duty Holder (EDH), Senior Duty Holder (SDH), Operating Duty Holder (ODH), Delivery Duty Holder (DDH). Not to be conflated with "duty holder" language in HSWA74.
- **Duty Holder Facing organisations (paragraphs 25 to 27).** Organisations whose activities and decisions affect a Duty Holder's risk picture must assure the Duty Holder, report failures to meet commitments, and support safe delivery.
- **Contracting controls (paragraphs 28 to 31).** Contractors supporting Duty Holding activities, working on the Defence estate, or designing/manufacturing/importing/supplying for Defence must meet specified safety cooperation and standards; overseas manufacturers and suppliers need the same requirements in contract.
- **Annex C deployments.** Duty of care on operations and exercises has no geographical boundary even when HSWA itself is mostly UK-limited.
- **Annex D HoE.** Land-based site accountable person with formal appointment, day-to-day access control, and authority to stop activity when SSW is compromised. Sea or air platform CO equivalents follow their Defence Area organisation and arrangements.

### When Duty Holding must apply (paragraph 11)

The Defence Area's most senior leader applies Duty Holding to military activities that:

- are justified and present a credible and reasonably foreseeable Risk to Life; and
- Duty of Care, other statutory arrangements, and/or risk control are considered inadequate and need enhanced safety management arrangements; or
- are mandated through regulation.

Defence Areas must keep a record of activities under Duty Holding (other than those mandated by regulation) and make it available for assurance or on request by senior Defence leaders (SofS, PUS, Dir-DSS, DG DSA) (paragraph 12).

**RtL (footnote paraphrase):** outcome of an activity with high probability of fatality; people should face harm risk only where benefit is clearly defined and risks are adequately controlled.

**Military activities (footnote paraphrase):** directly or indirectly linked to military outputs, at the discretion of the Service Chief or Defence Area senior leader.

### Duty Holding management principles (paragraphs 13 to 23, summarised)

- Four levels (EDH, SDH, ODH, DDH) of trained, competent, empowered people who hold safety risk where the framework applies; each is responsible for safe conduct in their AoR with RtL ALARP and tolerable.
- Senior leaders of the four Defence Areas (CDS, PUS, NAD, CDN) provide leadership for safety in their Areas; EDH formally appoints SDH(s) by letter of appointment.
- SDH normally decides which activities in the AoR need Duty Holding (except regulation-mandated cases), and is personally responsible for ensuring those activities are properly managed.
- If SDH cannot mitigate so that risk is ALARP and tolerable, elevate; if still not ALARP and tolerable or it is a serious RtL concern, stop unless an overwhelming and urgent operational imperative is accepted at the proper level (Element 5 paragraphs 18 to 20 detail the stop/continue logic).
- ODH and DDH must demonstrate competence and control appropriate to their tier.
- All Duty Holders should complete DSA-approved Generic Duty Holders training; ten principles appear in Element 5 Table 1 (not reproduced cell-by-cell here; use source table when applying the construct).

### Annex C: duty of care on deployments

- HSWA employer duties are largely UK-scoped; common-law and SofS Policy Statement still require duty of care on UK and overseas deployments (paragraphs 1 to 2).
- Suitable and sufficient risk assessment and ALARP management still apply; JSP 375 Chapter 8 is the named policy for Defence safety risk assessments.
- Collaborative or secondment control under a foreign state or other government department needs host-law compliance plus UK standards SFAIRP where host law is weaker, and information/instruction/training for standard differences (paragraph 4).
- **Operating risks:** known or apparent before deployment, inside a defined operating envelope (example: asbestos, fire).
- **Operational risks:** incurred on combat operations or when the Operational Commander requires force elements outside the defined envelope (paragraph 5).
- Dynamic assessment may be needed; elevation path through the chain of command must be clear. Duty Holding on deployment follows Element 5 when the senior leader or regulation says it applies; deployed force elements must understand and practise arrangements including elevation (paragraphs 6 to 7).
- Planning liaison as a minimum among Tasking Authority, Deploying Organisation, Force Generating Command, Operational Commander (Duty Holder where applicable) or person in charge, and specialist advisers; environmental health on reconnaissance for force health protection (FHP) and environmental and industrial hazards (EIH) before main force arrival (paragraphs 8 to 9).

### Annex D: Head of Establishment

- **HoE:** generally the person responsible for resilient, efficient, safe, legal, secure, and compliant operation of a land-based site or establishment. Must be formally appointed with terms of reference that include suitable and sufficient systems to control safety risks.
- **Accountable Person:** term used elsewhere in JSP 815 and JSP 375 for HoE, OC, Station Commander, and similar; Annex D standardises on HoE.
- Senior leaders appoint HoE via line management or chain of command. HoE should accept and understand the role. Day-to-day authority includes control of access/egress for the defined estate and authority to stop any or all activities when any part of the SSW is compromised (paragraph 1).
- HoE must understand safety responsibilities for temporary or permanent premises; where premises are controlled but not Crown-owned, responsibilities are defined with owners. Establishments must meet UK H&S legislation or elevate for mitigation decisions (paragraph 2).
- HoE training via the Defence Area; Safety Centres (or equivalent) advise (paragraph 3).
- Risk control depends on delegated authority inside secure boundaries; HoE may also be appointed as a Duty Holder (paragraph 4). Commercial novelty must not compromise duty of care (paragraph 5).
- HoE must be able to demonstrate the defined role and the control systems required by Annex D paragraph 6 (read full list in source when appointing).

## Mental Models

- **SSW is the default operating mode; Duty Holding is the enhanced overlay.** Most activity needs SSW and Duty of Care. Duty Holding adds named holders for selected RtL military activity.
- **Letter then competence then control.** Expectations 5.1, 5.2, and 5.4 form the appointment chain; 5.5 and 5.6 are the duties that come with it.
- **Stop-work is a duty, not a mutiny.** Expectation 5.6 only works if culture from Element 1 protects good-faith cessation.
- **Deployment does not dissolve duty of care.** Statute may thin overseas; relationship-based duty and SofS policy do not.
- **HoE owns the site SSW boundary.** Access control and stop authority are the practical teeth of land estate safety.

## Anti-patterns

- Appointing Duty Holders on paper without DSA-approved training, letters, or recorded activity lists.
- Using Duty Holding language for ordinary workplace H&S and ignoring HSWA duty-holder meanings (Element 5 warns against conflation).
- Contractors on estate without 4Cs-style control, or Duty Holder Facing organisations that do not report assurance gaps to the Duty Holder.
- Continuing activity after residual risk leaves ALARP and tolerable without proper elevation or stop.
- Deploying without EIH/FHP input or without a practised elevation path.
- HoE without formal ToRs, without stop authority in practice, or blocked by commercial arrangements from exercising duty of care.

## Key Takeaways

1. Expectations 5.1 to 5.7: delegate authority, assure competence, elevate significant risk, letter of delegation, ALARP mitigation duty, cease authority, implement SSW.
2. Duty Holding (EDH/SDH/ODH/DDH) enhances Duty of Care for justified military activity with credible foreseeable RtL when ordinary controls are inadequate or regulation mandates it; keep a record for assurance.
3. Annex C: duty of care on deployments; operating vs operational risk; planning roles; EIH/FHP early.
4. Annex D: HoE formally appointed for land sites, trained, able to control access and stop work when SSW breaks.
5. Contractors and Duty Holder Facing organisations are inside the control system, not outside it.
