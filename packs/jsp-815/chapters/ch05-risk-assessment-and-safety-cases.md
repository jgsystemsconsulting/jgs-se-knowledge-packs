## Core Idea

Element 4 is how a Defence Area knows its hazards, keeps risk ALARP and tolerable, elevates what local control cannot hold, tracks change, and, when a single risk assessment is not enough, builds and maintains a safety case. The smoke question "when does a Defence Area need a safety case?" answers from paragraphs 30 to 32 and Expectation 4.7: structured argument plus evidence that a system is safe in a defined operating environment, required when complexity, novelty, scale, or risk profile defeats a single activity or workplace assessment; not required for simple routine work already held ALARP and tolerable by suitable assessments and SOPs.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Legal duty spine (paragraphs 3 to 4).** HSWA sections 2 and 3 and MHSWR 1999 regulation 3 require suitable and sufficient risk assessment. Defence requires commanders, managers, and accountable persons to assess and mitigate risks to people under their responsibility and anyone else affected. Defence safety risk assessments must follow JSP 375 Chapter 8 (named only; procedures not reproduced here).
- **Safety risk management framework (paragraphs 5 to 6).** Owned by the Defence Area senior leader. Covers identification, assessment, mitigation, reporting, monitoring, and governance. Must ensure suitable and sufficient proportionate assessments; safe systems of work or training procedures; defined competence for roles; and elevation when risk exceeds the commander or manager's authority to accept, including reasonably foreseeable risk to life (RtL).
- **Risk profile (paragraphs 7 to 8).** Examines nature and level of hazards, likelihood of harm, severity, and control effectiveness. Each Defence Area must establish and regularly review its risk profile, aligned with activity planning (tools such as SWOT are suggested).
- **ALARP and tolerability (paragraphs 12 to 18).** SFAIRP is the HSWA phrasing; ALARP is Defence's usual process term. A risk is ALARP when reduced to a level where further reduction is grossly disproportionate to the benefit gained. Tolerability is the separate judgement, especially on higher-risk activity, whether the remaining risk is acceptable given the benefit. Both are required: ALARP alone is not the whole test.
- **Elevation and golden thread (paragraphs 19 to 24).** Risks beyond authority, competence, or resources, or where control adequacy is uncertain, must rise through the chain of command so senior leaders can oversee, resource, direct, or accept. Records preserve decision rationale.
- **Change and OSA link (paragraphs 25 to 29).** Significant changes to equipment, infrastructure, ways of working, or associated specifications need evaluation, risk assessment, approval, and documentation before implementation (JSP 375 Chapter 35 named). Organisational changes may need an Organisational Safety Assessment (OSA); Element 2 covers change and OSA further.
- **Safety case construct (paragraphs 30 to 34, Figure 2 described).** Structured argument supported by evidence; composition may combine equipment safety cases, activity or workplace assessments, SOPs, and other records (specialist assessments, OSAs, inspections, exemption certificates, permits, training). Scope proportional to risk and complexity.
- **Methods (paragraphs 35 to 41).** Dir-DSS recommends the bow-tie method for strategic safety risks. Defence maintains safety risk matrices; a Defence 5x5 matrix is the recommended common language, consistent in approach with JSP 892.

### When a safety case is required (E4 paragraphs 30 to 32; Expectation 4.7)

**Definition (paragraph 30):** a safety case is a structured argument, supported by a body of evidence, that provides a compelling, comprehensible, and valid justification that a system is safe for a given application within a defined operating environment.

**Required when (paragraph 31):** Defence work-related activities are conducted on, or form part of, a system where a single safety risk assessment for the activity or the workplace is not sufficient to demonstrate adequate risk control because of complexity, novelty, scale, or the risk profile. A system may comprise equipment, infrastructure, people, processes, organisational arrangements, or any combination, including integration and interaction.

**Not required when (paragraph 32):** the activity is simple (non-complex, routine). If risks can be managed ALARP and tolerable through suitable and sufficient risk assessments and SOPs or work instructions, those arrangements remain the primary means of control. Reserve safety cases for systems where complexity, integration, novelty, or uncertainty needs a structured demonstration.

**What it demonstrates (paragraph 33):** system safety risks identified, assessed, and managed ALARP and tolerable; appropriate controls supporting a safe system of work; a holistic view of cumulative and interrelated risks in one coherent evidence body. Distinct from routine workplace assessments described in JSP 375 Volume 1 Chapter 8.

**Proportion and naming (paragraph 34):** scope and complexity match risk and system complexity, from a single equipment safety case to a broader case wrapping multiple equipment cases plus activity or workplace assessments. Defence Areas may use other labels (for example safety management plan) for the same principle. Equipment safety cases in more detail: Element 7 and JSP 376 (named only).

**Footnote system definition:** Element 4 footnotes a Def Stan 00-056 definition of system. That standard is outside this pack's cleared sources; treat the footnote as a pointer only, not as pack content.

### Elevation triggers (paragraphs 20 to 22, summarised)

Elevate when existing controls are no longer effective or proportional and injury potential or strategic safety implications rise; when likelihood or severity increases from new information, operational change, or occurrence data; or when acceptance authority sits above the person conducting or supervising the activity.

### Strategic risk reporting (paragraphs 42 to 52, summarised)

The Defence Board oversees MOD risk and agrees Principal Risks. A Principal Safety Risk accumulates strategic safety risks. Defence Areas provide regular risk returns; DDSS collates function and principal risk updates. Top strategic safety risks are reviewed at least quarterly in the Area. Three Lines of Defence apply as set out in the Safety Function material and Element 12.

## Mental Models

- **Profile then assess then control then elevate.** Expectation order 4.1 to 4.3 is a pipeline, not a menu.
- **ALARP and tolerable are a pair.** Meeting ALARP with an intolerable residual still fails the Element 4 test.
- **Safety case is for system inadequacy of a single RA.** The trigger is insufficiency of one activity or workplace assessment, not prestige of the programme.
- **Change is a risk event.** Expectation 4.6 exists because static registers go stale the day the organisation, kit, or process moves.
- **Bow-tie and 5x5 are tools under the framework, not replacements for it.**

## Anti-patterns

- Writing a safety case for routine low-complexity work already covered by RA and SOPs (wastes effort; confuses 4.7).
- Skipping a safety case where integration, novelty, or scale make a single RA inadequate.
- Local acceptance of RtL or other risk beyond delegated authority without elevation (fails 4.3).
- Communicating risk only inside a specialist cell while operators never see controls (fails 4.4).
- Implementing significant change then assessing risk afterwards (fails 4.6 and JSP 375 Chapter 35 direction as Element 4 cites it).
- Quoting Performance Statement cells as the safety case standard; the case must satisfy Expectation 4.7 and paragraphs 30 to 34.

## Key Takeaways

1. Expectations 4.1 to 4.7 cover profile and hazards, risk management, elevation, communication, continual improvement, change tracking, and safety cases.
2. **Safety case trigger:** required for system-level Defence work where one activity or workplace RA cannot show adequate control (complexity, novelty, scale, risk profile); not required for simple routine activity held ALARP and tolerable by RA and SOPs.
3. Safety case = structured argument + evidence that a system is safe for a given application in a defined operating environment; proportionate; maintained and reviewed.
4. Elevation keeps a golden thread to authority that can resource or accept; leadership must stay active on elevated risks.
5. Methods: bow-tie recommended for strategic risks; Defence 5x5 matrix for common rating language; detail of workplace RA method sits in JSP 375 Chapter 8.
