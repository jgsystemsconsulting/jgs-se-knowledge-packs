## Core Idea

Element 9 is how the Area knows whether safety arrangements work: leading, lagging, and cultural indicators; trend review; management information; and data-aware leadership decisions on cost, schedule, and military capability that include safety impact. Element 10 is what happens when control fails or nearly fails: open reporting culture, end-to-end report-and-record systems, investigation resource, corrective action and learning, and tested emergency and business continuity plans that include safety. Element 11 is who the Area talks to and how: identify stakeholders, engage and consult (workforce, Trade Unions, suppliers, contractors, others affected), build working relations for continual improvement, give people easy access to up-to-date safety information, and provide anonymous routes for safety concerns. Together the three Elements close the Check and much of the Act side of PDCA for day-to-day safety management.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Three indicator families (E9.1).** Safety performance monitoring uses documented leading indicators (predictive / process health), lagging indicators (outcomes already realised), and cultural indicators (behaviours and reporting climate). One family alone is not a complete picture.
- **Review, MI, and decision link (E9.2 to 9.4).** Regular performance review and trend analysis drive corrective plans. Management information from indicators and trends is produced, reported, reviewed, and acted on in time. Leadership decisions on cost, schedule, and capability are expected to be data driven and to assess potential safety impact, not to trade safety silently against other performance axes.
- **Open reporting to close-out chain (E10.1 to 10.4).** Culture of reporting accidents, incidents, and near misses; a system consistent with Defence safety policy from first submission to close-out; resource to investigate; systems to implement corrective actions and learning for continual improvement. Near misses are in scope, not optional colour.
- **Emergency and business continuity with safety content (E10.5).** Plans exist, are tested regularly, and consider safety matters (evacuation, response roles, continuity of critical safety controls).
- **Stakeholder cycle (E11.1 to 11.5).** Identify internal and external stakeholders and their safety roles; manage engagement and consultation (including workforce, Trade Unions, suppliers, contractors, and others affected); build working relations that improve safety; give people, contractors, and the supply chain easy access to current role-relevant safety information; enable anonymous raising of safety-related concerns.

### How the three Elements mesh

- Element 9 without Element 10 under-reads failure signals; Element 10 without Element 9 cannot show whether corrective actions moved the indicators.
- Element 11 without Element 9/10 consultation becomes poster campaigns; reporting culture (10.1) and anonymous routes (11.5) need the Just Culture story from Element 1 (ch03).
- Leadership decision quality (9.4) is the bridge from MI packs to the governance equal-partner claim in Element 1.

## Mental Models

- **Leading tells you the brakes work; lagging tells you the crash rate.** Cultural indicators tell you whether people will still report the near miss.
- **Close-out is part of reporting.** A report that never reaches investigation or action is theatre (10.2 to 10.4).
- **Consultation is two-way by design.** Expectation 11.2 and 11.3 fail if Trade Unions and contractors only receive broadcasts.
- **Anonymous is a safety control, not a grievance gadget.** 11.5 exists so fear of identification does not kill hazard reports.
- **Capability decisions carry safety load.** 9.4 rejects the idea that schedule or cost choices are safety-neutral by default.

## Anti-patterns

- Indicator sets that are only injury counts (no leading or cultural measures).
- MI packs that arrive after the decision forum has already locked cost and schedule.
- Investigation without resource, or actions closed on paper without effectiveness check.
- Emergency plans never tested, or tested without safety roles and communications.
- Safety information buried on a SharePoint only permanent staff can find; contractors left out (fails 11.4).
- No anonymous channel, or a channel that never feeds the incident or assurance system (fails 11.5 and weakens 10.1).

## Key Takeaways

1. Element 9: leading, lagging, and cultural indicators; trend review; MI; safety-aware leadership decisions on cost, schedule, and capability.
2. Element 10: open reporting culture; policy-consistent report-to-close-out system; investigation resource; corrective learning; tested emergency and continuity plans with safety content.
3. Element 11: stakeholder identification; engagement and consultation (including Trade Unions and supply chain); working relations for improvement; accessible current safety information; anonymous concern routes.
4. Near misses are first-class inputs to both performance and incident systems.
5. These three Elements supply much of the evidence Element 12 assurance and Annex H audits sample.
