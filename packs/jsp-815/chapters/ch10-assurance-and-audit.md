## Core Idea

Element 12 turns assurance into an operating loop: risk-based first-line assurance by those who deliver the activity, mechanisms that enable second-line function assurance and third-line independent audit plus external assurance, an annual self-assessment against the 12 Elements that feeds leadership and the annual assurance report path, formal leadership review of whether safety management arrangements meet objectives, and corrective action on regulator enforcement. Annex F is the map from JSP 815 Expectations to ISO 45001 clause numbers (numbers only in this pack). Annex H V1.2 is the audit manual: principles, a staged audit process for 1LOD use (and usable more widely), rating against GIAA-aligned assurance levels, report and follow-up discipline. Areas may use other recognised audit methods if they still evidence pan-Defence policy requirements.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Three Lines of Defence (Element 12; Annex H paragraphs 4 to 5).** 1LOD: self-assurance by those directly responsible for delivering activities. 2LOD: oversight of management of those activities (function assurance, including DDSS roles as the Framework describes). 3LOD: independent of the Defence Area (including DSA and other independent review). Independent reviews assess arrangements against the SofS Policy Statement and pan-Defence safety policy and may benchmark against statute or Defence regulation; they support Area improvement and departmental reporting including the DSA Annual Assurance Report path.
- **Element 12 Expectations spine.** Risk-based 1LOD scaled to Area size and complexity (12.1); conduct 1LOD and enable 2LOD, 3LOD, and external assurance (12.2); annual self-assessment against SMS Elements to leadership, informing annual assurance report generation (12.3); leadership formal review of arrangement effectiveness against objectives using assurance evidence (12.4); mechanisms to correct against Defence and statutory regulator enforcement actions (12.5).
- **Annex F mapping rule (this pack).** Table rows show JSP 815 Expectation identifier, JSP-side theme (Expectation wording paraphrased or shortened from the Element text), and ISO 45001 clause number(s) only. No ISO 45001 requirement text is quoted or paraphrased here. Where Annex F's layout does not pin a discrete clause number against an Expectation in the extract, the clause cell says so.
- **Annex H audit basis (V1.2).** Audit is a systematic, documented process for obtaining and evaluating objective evidence against agreed standards, policies, procedures, requirements, regulation, and legislation (Annex H Purpose). The Defence process is based on ISO 19011 guidelines for auditing management systems; Areas may use other methodologies suited to their business if they still evidence pan-Defence requirements such as self-assurance and incident management. Audits may be combined with environmental (JSP 816) and fire (JSP 426) scopes. Evidence supports compliance, continual improvement, and summarised departmental performance reporting. Link to Element 9 performance reporting is explicit in Annex H.
- **Audit principles (Annex H paragraph 9).** Integrity; fair presentation; due professional care; confidentiality; independence (free from bias and conflict of interest); evidence-based approach with verifiable sampling; competence of audit leads (training, knowledge, skills, experience, behaviours, and method understanding).
- **GIAA-aligned assurance levels in findings (Annex H paragraph 31).** Unsatisfactory, Limited, Moderate, Substantial (same ladder as Framework assurance stages in ch02). Findings also mark non-compliance, opportunities for improvement, and good practice.
- **Figure 1 (Annex H), described only.** Audit Process Map: programme and plan, pre-audit engagement, research and document review, fieldwork and evidence, opening and closing meetings, report, issue/debrief, follow-up, and programme improvement. Not reproduced as graphics.

### Annex H audit method (V1.2), paraphrased steps

Use as a 1LOD audit method guide; 2LOD/3LOD programmes and other methods may apply the same discipline.

1. **Own the purpose.** Derive safety management responsibility from the SofS Policy Statement and Defence safety policy; JSP 815 is the minimum management arrangements. Assure arrangements through monitoring, review, governance, audit, and inspection to measure, correct, improve, and evidence performance (AH paragraphs 1 to 3).
2. **Set or join an audit programme.** Plan over a defined period from risk appetite, auditor/auditee availability, geography, and related factors (paragraph 13). DDSS and DSA submit 2LOD/3LOD programmes to the Defence Safety Committee for ratification by end of the preceding December; add audits mid-cycle after Service Inquiries, incidents, or statutory regulator intervention (paragraph 16). DSA may also use inspection, document review, and permissioning (paragraph 17).
3. **Plan the audit.** Audit Team Leader (ATL) agrees times, dates, objectives, scope, and method with the Area point of contact; issues formal notification to the organisation head (Appendix 1 example letter); requests planning documents and areas of interest; keeps the plan flexible. Plan content: objectives, scope, criteria, location, duration, team roles, follow-up from previous audit, post-audit follow-up (paragraphs 14 to 15). Reach clear agreement on authority, scope, method, resources, timescales, outputs, brief/debrief, and sites (paragraph 18).
4. **Pre-audit meeting.** Normally no later than three months before start (exceptions for familiarity or non-viable travel: use correspondence and online meetings). Meet safety team and related leads including Trade Union representatives as appropriate; agree scope and outcomes; explain method and documents; agree outline programme and Head-of-Area opening brief date; arrange escorts; discuss specific risks for deeper look (paragraphs 19 to 20).
5. **Research and document review.** Understand both the standard under test and the auditee's methods. Review documents before or during fieldwork. Annex G toolkit is named as a guide to expected documents per Element (not in this pack). Follow audit threads and corporate knowledge including prior audits (paragraphs 22 to 24). Typical pre-audit sources: O&A Statement; safety management arrangements; prior assurance reports and close-out; SQEP impact assessments and mitigations; board/committee safety papers; enforcement history; incident and lessons data; assurance and improvement plans; annual safety reports; risk registers; agreements with other organisations on safety.
6. **Gather verifiable evidence.** Sample information relevant to objectives, scope, and criteria, including interfaces. Accept only verifiable information as evidence; record evidence that leads to findings; respond to new or changed risks found in fieldwork (paragraph 25).
7. **Opening meeting (in-brief).** ATL and team brief the Head of Defence Area or empowered representative: scope/method/purpose; programme of visits; invite senior concerns, risks, and good practices; describe debrief and report format; offer optional hot debrief at end of fieldwork (paragraph 26).
8. **Evaluate arrangements.** Assess against policy, legislation, and applicable Defence regulation and against performance. Incomplete or draft systems may shift the ATL toward gap analysis and consultancy support; note that incomplete systems are still findings needing urgent action for policy compliance. Use system requirements such as Appendix 2, backed by JSP 815 detail. Combine interviews, document review, and site/process surveys; select interviewees for the scope (paragraphs 27 to 30).
9. **Form findings and assurance level.** Evaluate evidence against Element 12 Expectations and GIAA categories: Unsatisfactory, Limited, Moderate, or Substantial; mark non-compliance, improvement opportunities, and good practice. Multi-auditor teams agree findings before close. Keep working papers with evidence at least until the next audit of that Area (paragraphs 31 to 33).
10. **Site verification.** After rating how arrangements are designed, verify they are in use and effective via site visits. Record observations with working papers. Treat local requests for immediate full findings carefully when the site is only a sample inside a wider scope; consult the auditee sponsor before wide share (paragraphs 34 to 35).
11. **Closing meeting.** Present findings for fact checking to Area leader or empowered representative, activity owners, and the safety lead. Cover sampled evidence, understood findings, corrective actions, complaints, and appeals (paragraphs 36 to 37).
12. **Audit report.** ATL owns the report inside the agreed timeframe. Contents: introduction (scope, team, collaboration); narratives on each of the 12 Element headings with observations and recommended corrective actions as the basis for the auditee Action Plan; conclusions and when the auditee should acknowledge findings and present a post-audit action plan; annexes (ToRs, findings, places visited, documents reviewed, prior recommendation progress, completed Rating Evaluation); Executive Summary. Annex G template is named as a scoring option Areas may adapt; alternatives allowed (paragraphs 38 to 40).
13. **Issue, debrief, follow-up.** Auditing authority approves issue. Formal debrief to Head of Organisation or empowered representative whenever practicable. Auditee produces Action Plan; ATL reviews adequacy and negotiates if insufficient. Follow-up visit usually six to nine months later unless the standard used says otherwise; focus on report issues; post-visit letter records progress and next review timing. Feed policy implementation problems to Area safety leads and Dir-DSS as needed (paragraphs 41 to 47).
14. **Improve the programme and feed leadership.** Programme owner reviews whether objectives were met; lessons improve the next programme (paragraph 48). Audit outcomes enter leadership review of arrangements and senior Action Plan reporting (paragraph 49; ties to Expectation 12.4 and Element 9). Share good practice with anonymity retained where possible (paragraph 50).
15. **Link ratings to statute and JSP 815.** Areas show how arrangements meet the SofS Policy Statement and applicable statute/regulation. Appendix 2 rating evaluation or Annex G self-assessment toolkit (named only) can score against the 12 Elements; no mechanical one-to-one Element crosswalk is assumed; auditors apply judgement and record the assessment method for equivalence over time (paragraphs 51 to 54). Appendices 1 to 3 hold example notification letter, rating evaluation sheets, and rating categories (use in source when running an audit).

## Mental Models

- **1LOD is not optional homework before the real audit.** Expectation 12.1 is continuous risk-based assurance; 2LOD/3LOD sample and challenge it.
- **Self-assessment feeds the annual story.** 12.3 is the Area's structured look in the mirror that leadership and departmental reporting reuse.
- **Enforcement close-out is assurance, not correspondence.** 12.5 tracks Defence and statutory regulator actions to effective correction.
- **Annex F is a bridge, not an identity.** Clause numbers show alignment points; JSP 815 still carries Defence-specific Expectations ISO does not mirror one-for-one (especially equipment and infrastructure lifecycle Elements).
- **Annex H is a method, not the only method.** Areas may use other recognised approaches if evidence of pan-Defence requirements still appears.
- **Substantial sits on Moderate.** Findings use the same cumulative ladder described in ch02.

## Anti-patterns

- Waiting for 3LOD to discover gaps that 1LOD never sampled (fails 12.1 to 12.2).
- Annual self-assessment copied forward without fresh evidence (fails 12.3).
- Leadership "noting" assurance reports without decisions on effectiveness gaps (fails 12.4).
- Regulator enforcement letters filed without funded corrective action (fails 12.5).
- Audits without independence, competence, or verifiable evidence (breaches Annex H principles).
- Reports that assert assurance levels without working papers retained to the next cycle.
- Pasting ISO 45001 clause text into Area procedures from this pack; use clause numbers and read ISO from a licensed source if needed.
- Treating Annex G toolkit or Appendix 2 forms as optional mystery; they are named tools in AH even though Annex G itself is out of this pack.

## Key Takeaways

1. Expectations 12.1 to 12.5: risk-based 1LOD; enable 2LOD/3LOD/external; annual Element self-assessment; leadership effectiveness review; enforcement corrective action.
2. Three Lines of Defence structure assurance from deliverers through function oversight to independent review supporting Area and departmental reporting.
3. Annex F maps each Expectation to ISO 45001 clause numbers only; this pack carries no ISO requirement wording.
4. Annex H V1.2 audit method: programme, plan, pre-audit, research, evidence, open, evaluate, find and rate, verify on site, close, report, debrief, follow up, improve programme, feed leadership, share good practice.
5. Assurance levels in audit findings match the Framework ladder: Unsatisfactory, Limited, Moderate, Substantial.
