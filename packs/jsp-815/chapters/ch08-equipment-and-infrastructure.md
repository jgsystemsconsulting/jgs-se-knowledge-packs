## Core Idea

Element 7 covers equipment across the CADMID/T lifecycle (Concept, Assessment, Demonstration, Manufacture, In-service, Disposal/Termination). Element 8 is the infrastructure counterpart: Concept through Assessment, Design, Manufacture and Construction, Use, Maintenance, and Disposal. Both Elements demand lifecycle risk identification, control and elevation, statutory and Defence-regulation compliance (or controlled exemption/waiver/concession), operation inside design intent, change control, supply-chain risk management, and lesson sharing. Element 7 adds equipment safety cases and platform integration risk. Early lifecycle phases hold the greatest chance to design hazards out; in-service drift of use, limits, maintenance, and training is where residual risk grows if unmanaged.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **CADMID/T for equipment (E7 paragraphs 3 to 13).** After a capability requirement is expressed, Defence runs six CADMID/T phases aligned with HMT Green Book thinking. Approval points correspond to ownership and decision gates across the lifecycle. Concept and design stages, and the safety case built there, are the main chance to embed inherent safety and to define intended operating envelopes. Manufacture decisions on design amendments and process change need safety evaluation. Compliant use belongs in training and operating documentation. Shortfalls in maintenance, operator training, or spares force fresh risk assessment. In-service factors (role change, environment, life extension, modification, software) can change the risk picture. Operating limits need regular review so equipment stays inside design and approved envelopes. Disposal and termination still carry safety duties.
- **HSWA section 6 theme for articles for use at work (E7 paragraphs 14 onward, summarised).** Persons who design, manufacture, import, or supply articles for use at work must, so far as is reasonably practicable, design and construct so that articles are safe and without risks to health when being set, used, cleaned, or maintained by a person at work; provide adequate information about use; and take similar care on subsequent serious risk knowledge. Element 7 folds this statutory duty into Defence equipment arrangements across the lifecycle and requires exemption, waiver, or concession routes when compliance cannot be achieved (ties to Element 3 / Annex B process).
- **Infrastructure lifecycle (E8 Purpose and Expectations).** Same control logic as equipment, applied to the built estate and related systems: identify and assess risks through the full infrastructure lifecycle; control and mitigate, elevating to Duty Holder, Senior Responsible Owner (SRO), Head of Establishment, or competent person as needed; keep statutory and Defence-regulation compliance (or controlled relief); maintain and operate within intended use; control physical and major software changes; manage infrastructure supply-chain risk; share lessons from design, acquisition, build, operation, modification, and maintenance.
- **Common control pattern across E7 and E8.** Lifecycle risk identification; safety case or equivalent demonstration where required (equipment safety cases explicit in 7.2); compliance or documented relief; stay inside design/operating intent and communicate limits to operators and maintainers; evaluate, risk-assess, approve, and document physical and major software changes; know supply-chain safety dependencies; share lessons. Element 7 Expectation 7.8 adds integration of equipment and systems onto platforms.

### Equipment safety case pointer (E7.2)

Element 7 expects equipment safety cases to be developed, maintained, and used to identify, evaluate, control, and mitigate risk through CADMID/T, with elevation to the appropriate Duty Holder (if Duty Holding applies) or Senior Responsible Owner where necessary. The general safety-case trigger and definition live in Element 4 (ch05); Element 7 is the equipment-lifecycle application.

### Infrastructure elevation path (E8.2)

When infrastructure residual risk cannot be held inside Area controls, Element 8 names Duty Holder, SRO, Head of Establishment, and competent person as elevation and advice points. That links estate control (ch06 Annex D HoE) to acquisition/project SRO accountability and, where applicable, Duty Holding.

## Mental Models

- **Design-out beats inspect-in.** Concept and design stages are the cheap safety window; in-service inspection cannot fully recover a bad inherent design.
- **Limits are controls only if operators know them.** Expectation 7.4 / 8.4 fail when limits live in a file the maintainer never sees.
- **Change is a new design decision.** Major software and physical change (7.5 / 8.5) re-enter the risk and, where required, safety-case loop.
- **Supply chain carries your residual risk.** Bought-in equipment or works inherit into the Area's duty of care (7.6 / 8.6).
- **Platform integration is its own hazard set.** Meeting component safety cases does not automatically prove the integrated platform is safe (7.8).

## Anti-patterns

- Treating CADMID/T safety as an acquisition-phase paperwork spike that stops at ISD (in-service date).
- Operating beyond approved envelopes, or life-extending without re-assessment (fails 7.4 / 8.4 and 7.2).
- Silent modifications and undocumented software drops (fails 7.5 / 8.5).
- Compliance gaps without exemption, waiver, or concession control (fails 7.3 / 8.3; see ch04).
- Ignoring contractor design or build safety standards on the estate (fails 8.6 and links to Element 5 contractor control).
- Lessons locked in a single project office so the next programme repeats the same failure (fails 7.7 / 8.7).

## Key Takeaways

1. Element 7 Expectations 7.1 to 7.8: CADMID/T risk, equipment safety cases, compliance or relief, operating limits, change control, supply chain, lessons, platform integration.
2. Element 8 Expectations 8.1 to 8.7: infrastructure lifecycle risk, control and elevation (Duty Holder / SRO / HoE / competent person), compliance or relief, intended use, change control, supply chain, lessons.
3. Early lifecycle decisions dominate inherent safety; in-service change and limit drift dominate residual risk growth.
4. HSWA section 6 duties on designers, manufacturers, importers, and suppliers sit inside Element 7's compliance story for articles for use at work.
5. Equipment safety cases (7.2) specialise the Element 4 safety-case construct; integration risk (7.8) is explicit and separate.
