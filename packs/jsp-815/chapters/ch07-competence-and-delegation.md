## Core Idea

Element 6 is the people side of the SMS: enough resource against the risk profile, clear safety roles and delegations, workforce plans that keep safety skills available, training that includes safety skills, and a competence process that assesses qualifications, behaviours, and skills. Annex E lists the safety topics that letters of delegation and terms of reference for leaders, Accountable Persons, and Duty Holders must cover. Competence without recorded delegation, or letters without competence and resource behind them, both fail the Element.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Safety roles first, then resource (Purpose paragraphs 2 to 3; paragraphs 4 to 8).** Defence Areas identify every safety role in their operating model, including roles outside the Area that still affect safety outcomes. Dir-DSS, as Safety Function leader, articulates need on the Safety and Environment profession, defines where safety skills are required, reviews current skills and knowledge, runs gap analysis, sets an action plan, and identifies development opportunities. The Area must make sufficient resources available for those roles and should map safety roles on HR systems with SQEP (Suitably Qualified and Experienced Personnel) criteria. Strategic workforce planning covers responsible roles, safety critical posts, recruitment, deployment, career development, retention, and succession.
- **Role classes (paragraphs 9 to 22).** Safety management roles sit in arrangements and job descriptions. Guidance covers Heads of Establishment (and Accountable Person synonyms), commanders and managers, and all personnel. HoE keeps facilities and activities under suitable and sufficient safety control within the estate boundary (links to Element 5 Annex D). Commanders and managers set tone, receive task-specific and safety training, manage significant hazards in their AoR, run workplace inspections at least every six months (JSP 375 Chapter 4 named), report accidents and near misses, and retain safety records (JSP 375 Chapter 39 named). All personnel take reasonable care, follow Defence and local safety arrangements, operate only authorised equipment they are trained for, use PPE as directed, and use local reporting routes.
- **Learning and development (paragraphs 23 to 26).** UK health and safety law requires employers to provide employees with adequate health and safety training. Areas should record their safety training approach, review it periodically (JSP 822 named), and deliver training needed for role and activity.
- **Competence (paragraphs 27 to 36).** DDSS issues a Safety and Environment skills and competencies site as a reference for role profiles. Element 6 cites the HSE definition of competence as the combination of training, skills, experience, and knowledge that a person has and their ability to apply them to perform a task safely (source attribution only; full HSE text not reproduced here). Anyone controlling or requiring a Defence activity must specify the competence needed. Heightened competence expectations apply where people hold delegated legal or Defence safety responsibility, influence strategic safety decisions, assure organisational compliance, or are named accountable persons. Typical roles include commanders and managers, HoE, Duty Holders, Safety Centre Directors, CESO or equivalent, SHEF advisors, fire safety managers, and Explosives Safety Representatives. Duty Holders, commanders, managers, and HoE must make sure people under them are competent and resourced. Competence factors include education and experience, work environment, risk-assessment controls, arrangements requirements, legal and Defence policy and regulation requirements, consequences of non-compliance, duties of the role, and updating competence when context changes. Dir-DSS leads the professional development framework for the Safety and Environment profession.
- **Annex E letter-of-delegation safety checklist.** Eight topic groups for inclusion in letters of delegation or ToRs: leadership and Just Culture behaviour; safety management arrangements in the AoR; people competence, resources, auditable delegation records, and Trade Union consultation duties; governance support; risk (Duty Holding letters per Element 5; ALARP elevation per Element 4 / JSP 375 Chapter 8); open reporting and SMS-aligned reporting systems; assurance processes per Element 12; organisational change via Organisational Safety Assessment (OSA) per Element 2 / JSP 375 Chapter 35, with elevation if the organisation can no longer operate safely.

## Key Concepts

### Element 6 Expectations (6.1 to 6.5)

| Exp | Theme (paraphrase) | Performance Statement themes (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Sufficient resources aligned to the Area risk profile | Resource plans match risk; gaps funded or elevated |
| 6.2 | Defined responsibilities, accountabilities, and delegations for safety management | Roles mapped; delegations recorded and current |
| 6.3 | Plans for recruitment, deployment, career development, retention, and succession | Workforce plans cover safety-critical posts and succession |
| 6.4 | Training programmes include safety skills for Defence requirements | Training needs analysis, delivery, and periodic review |
| 6.5 | Competency process assesses and assures qualifications, behaviours, and skills | Assessment before and during tenure; records; refresh on change |

### Annex E: what a safety letter of delegation must cover

Summarised checklist (Annex E sections 1 to 8):

1. **Leadership and behaviour:** role-model positive safety behaviours; lead by example; maintain a Just Culture that lets people speak up and report openly.
2. **Safety management arrangements:** conduct AoR activity under Area arrangements and applicable law, policy, and regulation; clear local standards; communicate them; drive adherence; review arrangements in performance management; remediate non-conformance; articulate and record sub-delegated safety responsibilities.
3. **People:** competent, resourced sub-delegates and safety posts; auditable delegation record; meet consultation duties with recognised Trade Unions or other employee representatives.
4. **Governance:** support Area safety governance and formal engagement meetings on safety.
5. **Risk:** where Duty Holding applies, issue letters under the Element 5 construct; elevate residual risk that cannot be made ALARP through the chain of command.
6. **Reporting:** open near-miss and occurrence reporting; use SMS-aligned health, safety, and environmental protection reporting systems.
7. **Assurance:** processes for reporting, risk and issue identification, and assurance delivery per Element 12.
8. **Change:** OSA before organisational change with potential significant safety impact; elevate if the organisation is no longer able to operate safely.

## Mental Models

- **Role map before training catalogue.** Expectation 6.2 and 6.5 sit on knowing which posts carry safety duty; 6.4 fills the skill gap once roles are known.
- **Resource is a risk control.** Expectation 6.1 ties headcount and budget to the risk profile, not to a generic establishment figure.
- **Letter is the contract; competence is the proof.** Annex E content plus Expectation 6.5 and Element 5 Expectation 5.2 form one appointment chain.
- **Everyone has a duty floor.** Paragraphs 21 to 22 apply to all personnel even when they hold no formal safety post.

## Anti-patterns

- Safety-critical posts filled without SQEP criteria or without HR mapping (fails 6.1 to 6.3 and 6.5).
- Letters of delegation that omit Annex E risk, assurance, or change clauses, or that never get accepted and filed.
- Training programmes that teach generic induction only and skip role-specific safety skills (fails 6.4).
- Duty Holders or HoE appointed on paper without competence evidence or without resource for people under them.
- Treating HSE or DDSS competence frameworks as optional decoration rather than inputs to the Area process.

## Key Takeaways

1. Expectations 6.1 to 6.5: resource to risk; define and delegate; workforce plans; safety-inclusive training; competence assessment and assurance.
2. Dir-DSS owns profession-level gap analysis and the Safety and Environment professional development frame; Areas own local role maps, HR mapping, and delivery.
3. HoE, commanders and managers, and all personnel each have defined safety duties in Element 6 guidance.
4. Annex E is the mandatory safety content checklist for letters of delegation and leader ToRs, spanning leadership through change control.
5. Competence is role-specific and must be kept current when context, equipment, or regulation changes.
