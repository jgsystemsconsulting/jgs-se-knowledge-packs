# Glossary: jsp-815

Defence safety terms as the chapters use them: MOD and UK meanings first, drawn from the Framework and each Element's Terms and definitions, with the chapters that carry the detail.

- **4Cs**: Coordination, co-operation, communication, and control: the four duties Element 5 sets for managing visiting workers and contractors. Method detail is named to JSP 375 Chapter 34 and not carried here. → ch06
- **5x5 matrix**: The Defence safety risk matrix recommended as the common rating language for likelihood and severity, consistent in approach with JSP 892. → ch05
- **Accountable Person**: Term used across JSP 815 and JSP 375 for the HoE, Officer Commanding, Station Commander, and similar site or activity holders. Annex D standardises on Head of Establishment. → ch06, ch07
- **ALARP (as low as reasonably practicable)**: Defence's usual process term for lowering risk: a risk is ALARP when further reduction would be grossly disproportionate to the benefit gained. Paired with tolerability, which is judged separately; ALARP alone is not the whole test. → ch04, ch05, ch06
- **AoR (area of responsibility)**: The span of activity, people, and risk a leader, Duty Holder, or HoE controls. Delegated authority, competence, and Duty Holding duties all attach to the AoR. → ch06, ch07
- **ATL (Audit Team Leader)**: The auditor who plans the audit, leads fieldwork, owns the report, and reviews the auditee's Action Plan under the Annex H method. → ch10
- **Bow-tie method**: The risk assessment method Dir-DSS recommends for strategic safety risks: hazards in the middle, causes and consequences either side, controls on the ties. → ch05
- **CADMID/T**: The equipment lifecycle: Concept, Assessment, Demonstration, Manufacture, In-service, Disposal/Termination. Element 7 spreads equipment safety duties across all six phases. → ch08
- **Combat Immunity**: A court-applied concept that may shield combat activity from negligence claims. Element 3 treats it as decided by courts on facts, never as a pre-briefed waiver for peacetime or routine risk. → ch04
- **Crown enforcement notices**: Statutory regulators' Crown-specific tools: Crown Improvement Notices, Crown Prohibition Notices, and Crown Censure. HSWA prosecution of the Department differs from private parties, but individuals remain liable. → ch04
- **CSOC**: A joint command arrangement in Annex A's applicability principles: personnel under CSOC OPCOM or posted to it adhere to CSOC policy and arrangements rather than parent or host Defence Area rules. → ch03
- **DDH (Delivery Duty Holder)**: The Duty Holding tier that manages safety risk at the delivery level of the activity, demonstrating competence and control appropriate to that tier. → ch06
- **DDSS (Directorate of Defence Safety and Support)**: The directorate that owns JSP 815 feedback, develops Defence safety policy under Dir-DSS, collates function and principal risk updates, and advises sponsors on exemption cases. → ch01, ch04, ch05
- **DED (disapplication, exemption, or derogation)**: A defined gap in statute's reach over Defence. Where a DED applies, DDSS policy plus DSA regulation are the Defence arrangements, intended to be at least as good as UK legislation so far as practicable. → ch04
- **Defence Area**: One of the four top-level areas in Defence Safety Policy: Department of State; Armed Forces (MSHQ and the Military Commands); National Armaments Director Group; and the Defence Nuclear Enterprise. For the Armed Forces, responsibility also runs to the component Military Commands. Their senior leaders are CDS, PUS, NAD, and CDN. → ch01, ch03, ch06
- **Defence Safety Committee**: The principal Defence forum for safety governance. Each Defence Area must also establish its own governance; 2LOD and 3LOD audit programmes are submitted to the committee for ratification. → ch03, ch10
- **Defence Safety Function**: The structure and processes that let Defence deliver operational capability while minimising risk to personnel and equipment. Dir-DSS leads it and conducts second-line assurance against the SMS. → ch01, ch02
- **DG DSA**: Director General of the Defence Safety Authority: owns, develops, and maintains Defence safety regulations on behalf of SofS and, under the DSA Charter, empowers suitably qualified Crown servants to regulate Defence safety activity. → ch04
- **Dir-DSS (Director of Defence Safety and Support)**: Owner of JSP 815, leader of the Defence Safety Function, owner of the Function Strategy, Safety Operating Model, and overarching SMS, and developer of Defence safety policy. → ch01, ch02, ch04
- **Duty Holder Facing organisation**: An organisation whose activities and decisions affect a Duty Holder's risk picture. It must assure the Duty Holder, report failures to meet commitments, and support safe delivery. → ch06
- **Duty Holding**: An enhanced safety management construct for selected military activities with credible, reasonably foreseeable Risk to Life, introduced after the Nimrod Review in aviation. It enhances, never replaces, legal Duty of Care, spans four tiers (EDH, SDH, ODH, DDH), and must be recorded where applied other than by regulation mandate. → ch06
- **Duty of Care**: The legal obligation to take reasonable steps for the health, safety, and welfare of employees and others affected. In Defence it runs from SofS down the chain of command and follows the relationship, not geography, so it applies on deployments even where HSWA is UK-limited. → ch01, ch06
- **ECS (Exemption Case Submission)**: The evidence pack a sponsor builds for an exemption certificate: legislation cited, national security or operational capability justification, affected persons, residual-risk controls, action plan, and monitoring. → ch04
- **EDH (Executive Duty Holder)**: The most senior Duty Holding tier, formally appointed by letter; the Defence Area senior leader applies the construct and EDHs appoint SDHs. → ch06
- **EIH / FHP**: Environmental and industrial hazards, and force health protection: the assessment inputs Annex C requires on reconnaissance before a deploying force arrives. → ch06
- **Element**: One of the 12 topic divisions of the Defence SMS, from E1 Leadership, Governance and Culture to E12 Assurance. Some Elements may not apply fully to every activity. → ch02
- **Exemption certificate**: SofS's route for excusing a person or class from specified health and safety legislation provisions. A last resort after other avenues are exhausted, normally time-limited (maximum five years unless justified), conditioned, endorsed by Dir-DSS and a 3* senior manager, regulator-reviewed, and registered. → ch04
- **Expectation**: A numbered activity expected to be in place in a Defence Area's arrangements (E1.1 to E12.5, 72 in total). Expectation numbers are the cite key; Performance Statements only show what evidence looks like. → ch02
- **FULLCOM**: Full Command: the parent command relationship. Annex A keeps assigned, visiting, and lodging units under their FULLCOM parent's safety management arrangements, with host Standing Orders still to be followed. → ch03
- **GIAA (Government Internal Audit Agency)**: The body whose assessment criteria the Framework's assurance stages align with: are controls embedded, do they help manage key risks, do they operate as intended. Annex H findings rate against the same ladder. → ch02, ch10
- **Golden thread**: The elevation principle that risk records preserve decision rationale up the chain, keeping a visible line from the activity to the authority that can resource, direct, or accept the risk. → ch05
- **HoE (Head of Establishment)**: The formally appointed person responsible for resilient, efficient, safe, legal, secure, and compliant operation of a land-based site or establishment. Holds day-to-day access control and authority to stop activity when any part of the SSW is compromised; may also be appointed as a Duty Holder. → ch06, ch07
- **HSWA74**: Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974: the UK statute whose sections 2 and 3 set employer duties, whose section 6 reaches designers, manufacturers, importers, and suppliers of articles for use at work, and whose section 48 gives the Crown a distinct enforcement path. Defence more often says ALARP where the Act says SFAIRP. → ch01, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch08
- **ISO 19011**: The international standard giving guidelines for auditing management systems, on which the Annex H audit method is based. Areas may use other recognised methods if pan-Defence requirements are still evidenced. → ch10
- **ISO 45001**: The occupational health and safety management system standard the Defence SMS claims alignment with, plus additional and different Defence requirements. This pack carries clause numbers only, via the Annex F mapping. → ch02, ch10
- **JSP 375**: Management of Health and Safety in Defence: the minimum standard for day-to-day UK legislative compliance, named by Elements 3, 4, 5, and 6 for workplace risk assessment, SSW, 4Cs, inspections, and records. A separate publication; this pack names it without restating it. → ch01, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07
- **JSP 376**: Defence acquisition safety policy, named as the equipment safety case companion to Element 7. Named only. → ch01, ch05, ch08
- **JSP 814**: Policy and Regulations for MOD Sponsored Cadet Forces: the rulebook for cadet forces, which sit outside JSP 815's drafting scope. Named only. → ch01
- **JSP 816**: Defence Environmental Management System: the SMS's environmental twin with the same 12-Element structure. Areas may combine safety and environmental arrangements; the publications stay separate. Named only. → ch01, ch02, ch10
- **Just Culture**: The culture Element 1 and Annex E require: people can speak up and report openly without blame for good-faith reports. It underpins reporting culture and anonymous concern routes. → ch03, ch07, ch09
- **Leading, lagging, and cultural indicators**: The three families Element 9 requires for safety performance monitoring: predictive measures of process health, realised outcomes, and behaviours and reporting climate. One family alone is not a complete picture. → ch09
- **Letter of delegation**: The formal instrument by which delegated authority for control of activity is appointed (Expectation 5.4). Annex E lists the eight safety topic groups such letters and leader terms of reference must cover. → ch06, ch07
- **Lines of Defence (1LOD, 2LOD, 3LOD)**: The assurance structure from the HMT Orange Book. First line: self-assurance by those delivering the activity. Second line: function oversight, including Dir-DSS roles. Third line: independent review, including DSA. → ch02, ch05, ch10
- **Lodging unit**: A unit sharing a site under ADCON/LOGCON arrangements without HoE rights. Keeps its FULLCOM parent's arrangements and complies with host Standing Orders; cases outside Annex A's principles need a bilateral MOU covering audit and inspection. → ch03
- **MACR (major accident control regulations)**: Defence regulations for major accident hazards, sitting with the Defence Ordnance, Munitions and Explosives Safety Regulator's remit. → ch04
- **MHSWR 1999**: Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999: the regulations requiring employers to hold arrangements for planning, organisation, control, monitoring, and review, and requiring suitable and sufficient risk assessment (regulation 3). → ch03, ch05
- **MI (management information)**: The indicator and trend product Element 9 expects to be produced, reported, reviewed, and acted on in time, reaching decision forums before cost, schedule, and capability choices lock. → ch09
- **MOU (memorandum of understanding)**: The bilateral agreement covering audit and inspection for applicability cases that fall outside Annex A's principles. → ch03
- **Near miss**: An event that could have caused harm but did not. Element 10 puts near misses in the open reporting, recording, and investigation system as first-class inputs, not optional colour. → ch09
- **ODH (Operating Duty Holder)**: The Duty Holding tier operating the activity day to day between the senior and delivery tiers. → ch06
- **OPCOM**: Operational command. Annex A: units or people embedded under a host's OPCOM follow the host Defence Area's arrangements unless a formal inter-Area agreement says otherwise. → ch03
- **OSA (Organisational Safety Assessment)**: The assessment required before organisational changes with potential significant safety impact, linked to Element 2 and JSP 375 Chapter 35. Letters of delegation carry an OSA duty, with elevation if the organisation can no longer operate safely. → ch03, ch05, ch07
- **Parker-Hudson Ladder**: The culture maturity framework Element 1 names for assessing safety culture. Level definitions stay in the source; this pack does not reproduce them. → ch03
- **PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act)**: The four-stage cycle the Defence SMS is based on: Plan for policy, governance, and planning; Do to implement arrangements and controls; Check to measure performance and investigate failures; Act to review and improve. → ch02, ch03
- **Performance Statement**: The articulation of each Expectation showing how compliance and continual improvement are demonstrated, with example performance across the assurance levels. Summarised as themes in this pack, never quoted. → ch02
- **Principal Safety Risk**: The accumulation of strategic safety risks agreed at the Defence Board alongside Principal Risks. Areas give regular risk returns and review their top strategic safety risks at least quarterly. → ch05
- **Risk profile**: The picture of the nature and level of hazards, likelihood of harm, severity, and control effectiveness. Each Defence Area must establish and regularly review its own, aligned with activity planning. → ch05
- **RtL (Risk to Life)**: The outcome of an activity with a high probability of fatality. People should face such risk only where the benefit is clearly defined and risks are adequately controlled; credible, reasonably foreseeable RtL is the trigger condition for Duty Holding. → ch05, ch06
- **Safety case**: A structured argument, supported by a body of evidence, that a system is safe for a given application in a defined operating environment. Required where a single activity or workplace risk assessment cannot demonstrate adequate control because of complexity, novelty, scale, or risk profile; proportionate in scope, maintained and reviewed. → ch05, ch08
- **Safety management arrangements**: The Defence Area-specific arrangements Element 2 requires: either a subordinate SMS aligned to JSP 815 or a simpler statement of organisation and arrangements. JSP 815 calls both by this name. → ch02, ch03
- **Safety Operating Model**: The model owned by Dir-DSS alongside the Function Strategy and the overarching SMS, describing how the Defence Safety Function operates. → ch01, ch02
- **SDH (Senior Duty Holder)**: The Duty Holding tier that normally decides which activities in the AoR need Duty Holding (except regulation-mandated cases) and is personally responsible for their management; elevates or stops when risk cannot be held ALARP and tolerable. → ch06
- **SEMS**: Safety and environmental management system: shorthand for a Defence Area's combined safety management arrangements under JSP 815 and environmental arrangements under JSP 816. The chapters use it mainly for Annex A applicability: whose SEMS applies when units sit outside their parent command. → ch02, ch03
- **SFAIRP (so far as is reasonably practicable)**: The phrasing HSWA uses; the statutory root that ALARP restates as a process. Overseas, UK standards apply SFAIRP where host-nation requirements fall short. → ch04, ch05
- **SofS Policy Statement**: The Secretary of State for Defence's Policy Statement for Health and Safety in Defence: the personal leadership commitment and high-level requirements from which JSP 815 takes its authority, and the benchmark 3LOD review assesses arrangements against. → ch01, ch04, ch10
- **SQEP (Suitably Qualified and Experienced Personnel)**: The criteria set against which safety roles, especially safety-critical posts, are filled and evidenced on HR systems. → ch07, ch10
- **SRO (Senior Responsible Owner)**: The acquisition or project accountability point Element 8 names for elevating infrastructure residual risk, alongside Duty Holders, the HoE, and competent persons. → ch08
- **SSW (Safe System of Work)**: The wrapper every Defence activity runs inside: control measures from risk assessment grouped as safe person, safe practice, safe place, and safe equipment. The HoE's stop authority bites when any part of it is compromised. → ch06
- **Stop-work authority**: The duty and power of those controlling activity to pause or cease activity when risk is no longer ALARP and tolerable (Expectation 5.6), exercised without penalty for good-faith use. → ch06
- **Tolerability**: The separate judgement, especially on higher-risk activity, of whether the remaining risk is acceptable given the benefit. A risk can be ALARP and still intolerable; both tests must be met. → ch05
- **Waiver and concession**: The relief routes from Defence regulation or policy that Expectation 3.5 groups with statutory exemptions: all run through a documented process with records, conditions, and registers. Element 7 and 8 lifecycle compliance uses the same controlled relief. → ch04, ch08
