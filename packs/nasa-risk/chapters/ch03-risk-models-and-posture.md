# Risk models, analysis, and decision concepts

## Core Idea

Risk talk fails when words mean different things to different people, so this chapter fixes the technical vocabulary: risk as a distribution of possible performance against each objective, decomposed into individual risk scenarios, re-aggregated into the total risk the organization carries, and judged against tolerance levels that express the declared risk posture. The mathematical illustrations serve clarity, not a mandate for quantification: every concept has a qualitative counterpart, and the goal at any rigor level is a coherent, integrated risk process.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Aggregate risk as a distribution. All the ways an objective can be met or missed define a probability distribution over outcomes; the probability mass in the shortfall region is the aggregate risk to that objective. Objectives are usually binary (threshold performance that is met or not, as with verified requirements), but distributed, range, and open-ended objectives (maximize revenue) still yield a shortfall probability once a credible outcome range and its distribution are modeled. Shortfall magnitude matters for soft objectives, and achievement margin can itself create opportunity.
- The risk triplet. NPR 8000.4 characterizes risk operationally as a set of triplets: scenarios leading to degraded performance, their likelihoods, and the severity of the performance degradation, with uncertainties included in scenario identification and likelihood evaluation. Each triplet entry is an individual risk scenario (the general literature's "individual risk"), and the ensemble of triplets composes the aggregate. An individual risk scenario envelopes a class of finer scenarios sharing causes, pathways, and consequences; where to cut the envelope is a modeling judgment, so different analysts may legitimately produce different scenario sets for the same aggregate.
- Risk Scenario Diagrams. A scenario starts with a departure event from the intended path (technical or programmatic: cost overrun, mishap, malicious act, missed success criterion), propagates through pivotal events that mitigate or exacerbate, and ends in end states measured on the performance measures. End-state probability composes from departure and pivotal event probabilities. Consequences are stated as performance across the same measure set for all end states and all scenarios, never as the most dramatic event in the story. RSDs are supported by performance models of matching rigor.
- Aggregation forms. For uncorrelated scenarios sharing one consequence, the aggregate probability follows the standard union ("OR-gate") formula. When consequences differ in shortfall magnitude, aggregate characterization pairs the union probability with a representative shortfall value (median or mean). Two practical special cases get explicit treatment: mutually exclusive scenarios (one-shot missions where different fault types produce different miss distances), where assessed probabilities are adjusted for exclusivity; and cumulative-effect scenarios (cost or schedule overruns that stack), where the math can get complex enough to warrant Monte Carlo simulation and specialist support.
- The organizational risk model. The authoritative representation of an organization's risk links each scenario's RSD to a common success path, stacks the end-state distributions per performance measure, and augments the known-risk distribution by a U/U margin. That augmented distribution is what decisions should rest on.

## Key Concepts

- Aleatory versus epistemic uncertainty. Aleatory uncertainty is the randomness inherent in whether and how a departure propagates; epistemic uncertainty is imperfect knowledge about likelihoods and performance levels themselves. The two need separate treatment in analysis and communication.
- Hostile-agent scenarios. Accidental scenarios start from randomly occurring initiating conditions with roughly stable frequencies, so absolute probabilities work. Adversarial scenarios are driven by volatile political and conflict conditions, so the analysis assumes the attack occurs (probability one) and evaluates the conditional probability of the consequences given the attack; the conditional values express defense strength. Attack likelihood, if considered, is handled separately because it changes with the international context. NPR 2810.1 and NPD 2810.1 (with NIST SP 800-37 RMF and SP 800-53 controls) govern cybersecurity; NPD 1600.2, NPR 1600.1, and NPR 1620.3 govern physical security, including an Enterprise Security Risk Management Program. Cyber threats target data, control, and information systems; physical security covers direct attacks on physical, functional, and human assets; many real scenarios mix both.
- Unknown and underappreciated (U/U) risk. Identification is always incomplete, and risk characterized only from identified scenarios is systematically non-conservative. U/U risk cannot be written as event sequences, but its potential magnitude can be scoped from leading indicators: complexity, novelty, schedule pressure, quality-practice adherence, and, for AI/ML systems, algorithm complexity, training-data quality, and thin operational heritage (NIST's AI Risk Management Framework is named as a reference). Historical correlations, such as the finding that failed robotic missions tend to be underfunded for their design complexity, ground implied risk scenarios: scenarios whose event chains are unknown but whose existence and rough size are inferred from indicator-based evidence. Appendix B of the source collects leading indicators.
- Performance markers and Risk Tolerance Levels. Markers (PMKs) are thresholds in a performance measure: typically a performance requirement and a performance goal. The RTL is the limit probability of missing the marker. Requirement tolerances sit well below goal tolerances; a two-marker scheme yields green (both satisfied), yellow (requirement satisfied, goal not), red (requirement violated). CDFs communicate this directly for increasing-goodness measures; CCDFs (exceedance form) fit cost, schedule, and probability-of-loss-of-crew style measures where goodness decreases. Execution-stage markers must stay consistent with the planning-stage markers used in RIDM analysis, or the planning conclusions no longer apply.
- Individual-scenario tolerances (IRTLs). Because aggregate risk is managed through its scenarios, each significant scenario needs a tolerance derived from the aggregate one. The heuristic: estimate the number N of significant contributing scenarios (experience says roughly ten or fewer), set IRTL = ARTL / N per marker, assess each scenario's probability of pushing performance past the marker against that IRTL, and color green/yellow/red accordingly. The scheme distinguishes high-consequence scenarios (any single one can sink the objective, as with loss-of-crew) from cumulative-consequence ones (margin eroders that breach a cap only in numbers). N evolves as scenarios close and new ones appear, so IRTLs are not fixed for the life of the activity.
- Matrix display, re-anchored. The old practice of rating risks by placing them in a 5x5 matrix on qualitative judgment is no longer valid as an assessment method. Analysis and classification are distinct steps: assessment produces objective distributions; classification compares them to posture-derived tolerances. A 5x5 display may still be used for communication, but its bins must be anchored to the PMK and IRTL values, with extra thresholds chosen at managers' discretion. Deliberately more conservative local coloring is admissible, provided local practice never contradicts the leadership-declared aggregate posture; acceptance decisions ultimately follow the aggregate and individual tolerance tables.

## Mental Models

- Analysis before rating, always: objective determination of shortfall likelihoods first, subjective tolerability judgment second. Conflating them was the core defect of matrix-only practice.
- Individual scenarios are proxies constructed for manageability; the objective is the aggregate. Managing each scenario within its IRTL does not remove the obligation to assess aggregate risk on its own terms.
- Yellow risks are a communication duty: NPR 8000.4 lets an entity accept marginal risks but requires reporting them up the hierarchy.
- Formal tolerance expression varies: crew safety uses Agency-level safety goals and thresholds per NPR 8705.2 (probability of loss of crew), answering "how safe is safe enough" for developers.

## Anti-patterns

Judging consequence by the worst-case dramatic event instead of performance-measure degradation. Stovepiping risk by objective when scenarios commonly threaten several at once (schedule slips drive cost; catastrophes threaten crew and mission together). Treating an intentional attack as a random-frequency event. Certifying risk from identified scenarios alone, ignoring the U/U component. Re-binning old-style matrices without anchoring them to markers and tolerances.

## Key Takeaways

- Risk to an objective is a distribution, its scenarios are triplets, and the organization's risk model is the linked, U/U-augmented whole.
- Risk tolerance levels turn qualitative posture into decision rules; the requirement/goal two-marker scheme with its three-color logic is the default.
- The IRTL = ARTL / N heuristic makes individual management consistent with aggregate posture.
- Displays are free, foundations are not: any matrix or stoplight must be anchored to markers and tolerances.
