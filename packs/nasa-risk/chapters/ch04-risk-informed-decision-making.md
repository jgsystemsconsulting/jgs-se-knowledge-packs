# Risk-Informed Decision Making (RIDM)

## Core Idea

RIDM is NASA's process for choosing among alternatives that carry weight: activity paths, system designs, risk responses, or rebaselining packages. It makes each alternative's risk profile explicit in the performance measures that define success, then lets a deliberating authority select with eyes open. The process has three parts, Identification of Alternatives, Analysis of Alternatives (AoA), and Risk-Informed Alternative Selection, expanded into six implementation steps, and it closes by handing the selected alternative's risk profile to CRM as the starting inventory for implementation-phase management. Functional roles recur throughout: stakeholders (affected, outside the doing organization), risk analysts (probabilistic characterization of performance), subject matter experts, Technical Authorities (independently funded, authority traceable to the Administrator, in Engineering, Safety and Mission Assurance, and Health and Medical), and the decision-maker. The linear step presentation is instructional; in practice the parts overlap and iterate, analyses refine until the technical case is made, and decisions often arrive as a series of downselects by proxy decision-makers.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Three RIDM types. Activity-Planning RIDM is the broadest, run before execution to select the preferred alternative; its results belong in the Formulation Authorization Document, concluded before the Mission Concept Review for flight projects. Activity-Rebaseline RIDM is an exception-condition process, invoked by the Activity Decision Authority when CRM shows requirements cannot be met at acceptable risk and no feasible control exists; it re-trades risk against performance targets within the already-selected alternative only (a full re-open of alternatives would be an activity replan). Activity-Execution RIDM runs inside the CRM Plan step to select among risk-response alternatives; narrow in scope but potentially deep, it reuses the objectives, markers, and tolerances set at planning.
- The six steps. Part 1: Step 1 identify objectives and performance measures from stakeholder and upper-level management expectations; Step 2 identify decision alternatives. Part 2: Step 3 conduct integrated risk analysis of each alternative; Step 4 develop the Technical Basis for Deliberation (TBfD). Part 3: Step 5 deliberate (performance targets, risk tolerances, contending alternatives); Step 6 select an alternative, accept its risks, and document the rationale in a Risk-Informed Selection Report (RISR) or decision memo.
- Objectives hierarchy and performance measures. Qualitative, multifaceted expectations decompose into an objectives hierarchy, first partitioned across NPR 8000.4's mission execution domains (safety, technical, physical security, cybersecurity, cost, schedule), until quantifiable performance objectives exist. Decomposition uses fundamental objectives (what to accomplish), never means objectives (how), which form networks and would smuggle solutions into the evaluation; deterministic standards are means objectives. Good hierarchy sets are complete, operational, non-redundant, and solution-independent; a test of importance (would excluding this objective change the best course of action?) governs inclusion, with care that many small exclusions do not add up to a missed concern. Each performance objective gets at least one performance measure: natural scales where they exist, constructed scales for subjective objectives (a 1-to-5 stakeholder-support ladder), or proxies (meeting attendance as a stand-in for support, with adequacy caveats). Modeled metrics such as P(LOC) and P(LOM) fold their modeling protocols into the measure definition so values are computed consistently. Minimize Risk is explicitly not a performance objective: risk is an attribute of every objective, measured as the probability of missing its target.

## Key Concepts

- Alternatives and trade trees. Compile a conservatively broad initial set (novel solutions may earn waivers from deterministic standards), then prune with a trade tree or trade matrix, equivalent representations, removing alternatives that violate imposed constraints or are dominated (inferior on every measure). Pre-pruning uses point estimates skewed toward inclusion. Screening keeps only alternatives that respect constraints and are not known-dominated.
- Integrated risk analysis. A common analytical framework spans domains: shared uncertain performance parameters (one alternative's cost model consumes the same mass data as its lift model), consistent uncertainty treatment, preserved correlations, and transparency. The framework is configuration-controlled, as are the alternative definitions, so all parties analyze the same data set. Correlated measures (cost and schedule chiefly) can be preserved by running all analyses in a common Monte Carlo shell that samples shared parameters and accumulates performance-measure vectors.
- Uncertainty treatment. Aleatory uncertainty (stochastic variation, irreducible by study) and epistemic uncertainty (knowledge gaps, reducible by test and analysis) are named and may be kept separate, essential in CRM mitigation decisions, though RIDM may treat them jointly. Qualitative inputs (technology readiness levels) ride on quantitative probability scales; rank-dependent models handle constructed scales. U/U risk enters as a margin multiplier on known risk: a complex, novel crewed mission whose simulated P(LOC) is 0.01 may carry total risk several times higher per historical evidence, so tolerances apply to the margin-adjusted distribution.
- Robustness and sensitivity. Analysis is enough when uncertainty no longer blocks a confident discrimination between alternatives; overlapping pdfs mean the decision is not robust unless the alternatives' performances are strongly correlated, and a difference-distribution plot can reveal that near-identical alternatives have a known, certain ordering. Sensitivity studies over credible model forms and parameter ranges locate where the preferred alternative flips; robust regions need no more rigor. NASA-STD-7009's credibility assessment scale rates modeling and simulation credibility, giving deliberators perspective on the evidence.
- The TBfD. The document that risk-informs deliberation: candidate alternatives, methodologies, results. Without it a decision is not risk-informed; its presence alone does not make one. Imposed-constraint risk matrices (sorted, color-coded by probability of missing each constraint), band-aid charts (5th percentile, mean, median, 95th), and risk lists keyed to performance targets are its communication backbone, and raw pdfs go to the decision-maker on request.
- Deliberation mechanics. Forums mirror the study's major aspects, start small with critical mass, and include diverse partisans whose clashes surface issues early. Decision-maker-declared risk tolerances and performance markers implement risk posture. Risk-normalized performance targets (RPTs) anchor comparison: set each target at the same percentile of its measure's distribution across all alternatives, so failure probability at the target is equal by construction. Targets are set sequentially in a declared measure order, each conditioned on prior targets being met, which handles correlation; ordering heuristics favor low-tolerance measures first. Conflicts between tolerance and pre-existing constraints resolve three ways: cull the alternative, relax the tolerance, or renegotiate the constraint.
- Selection and acceptance. The decision-maker owns the choice and may keep or revise the deliberators' tolerances and targets; levied process requirements (best practices, legal or reputational concerns) get analyzed for their effect on performance risk, and negative net effects require justification through risk acceptance. Uncertainty beyond the target percentile matters: expected performance, upside potential at higher tolerances, downside severity, reducibility of wide distributions (partial prototyping of two finalists), risk-retirement burden, and institutional effects all inform the contender set. RIDM does not prescribe how to choose; it requires the rationale to be documented in light of the risk analysis, with accepted risks explicitly transmitted to the implementing organization.
- Graded approach by activity class. Generalizing NPR 8705.4's robotic-mission classes, the handbook defines Activity Classes A+ (crewed included), B, C, and D and grades every step: A+ demands fully quantitative methods, broad stakeholder decomposition, iterative deliberation, and report-format documentation; B trims decomposition and iteration; C favors readily modeled measures, considers simplified methods alongside rigorous ones, and documents in memo or briefing form; D restricts objectives to mission-essential and externally mandated constraints, prefers semi-qualitative expert judgment, minimizes alternatives and finalized targets. Class tailoring superimposes on type-based scoping (rebaseline mostly runs Part 1's steps out of scope; execution RIDM re-aims Part 1 at risk-response alternatives).

## Mental Models

- Risk-informed, not risk-driven: analysis equips the decision-maker, who still chooses by judgment, values, and deliberation.
- Risk normalization is the comparison trick: equal failure probability at the targets makes unlike alternatives commensurable.
- The analysis stops when the case is made; more rigor beyond robustness is waste, and less is gambling.
- Performance targets are indicators of achievable levels, not requirements; requirements come later and must not silently contradict what RIDM compared.

## Anti-patterns

Adding a standalone "minimize risk" objective with technology/cost/schedule risk scores instead of folding risk into each objective's shortfall probability. Selecting on distribution means while thresholds exist, hiding constraint-violation probability. Letting analysts cull alternatives on their own authority (clear infeasibility excepted). Defining execution-stage requirements that diverge from the planning-stage markers the selection was based on, invalidating the deliberation retroactively.

## Key Takeaways

- Six steps, three parts, three types (Planning, Rebaseline, Execution); one TBfD in, one RISR out, one risk profile handed to CRM.
- Fundamental-objective hierarchies plus natural, constructed, or proxy measures turn stakeholder expectations into comparable numbers.
- RPTs at uniform risk percentiles, set sequentially in a declared order, are the core deliberation instrument; conflicts with constraints resolve by culling, relaxing, or renegotiating.
- Grade the rigor to the activity class and the decision stakes; U/U margins apply before any tolerance comparison.
- The selected alternative's risk list, written in risk-statement form, is CRM's initial inventory; consistency between the processes is designed in.
