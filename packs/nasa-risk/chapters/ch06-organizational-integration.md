# Organizational and managerial integration

## Core Idea

Integrated risk management needs organizational scaffolding. The chapter assumes NASA's current structure: executive-level management and councils in the Enterprise Domain, mission directorates coordinating the Program/Project Domain, a mission support directorate for institutional efficiency, and Centers and facilities providing people and infrastructure; but the principles hold under any structure: day-to-day risk work happens inside units, while cross-cutting risk, life-cycle transitions, and cross-boundary coordination are where integration lives or dies. Three practical concerns organize the chapter: keeping RIDM and CRM connected across life-cycle stages, recognizing and routing cross-cutting risks, and planning risk management execution in RMPs that coordinate with each other instead of contradicting each other.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Three domains, one objectives tree. Enterprise (strategy, priorities, resources), Program/Project (technology and mission execution), Institutional (the human, physical, and technical support structure for the other two). Strategic objectives are the unifying focus; every lower unit executes an allocated portion, and support of those higher-level objectives is the prerequisite for integration. The barriers to integration are predictable, information flow, decision authority, and coordination gaps at interfaces, and the answer is explicit protocols, not goodwill.
- Life-cycle integration of RIDM and CRM. RIDM compares the risk profiles of alternative solutions (including acquisition strategies and Acquirer-Provider relationship types) and selects the optimum on risk-versus-benefit; CRM is the within-activity process that identifies and controls risks to the chosen path. The handbook maps both onto three conceptual stages: Activity and Activity Selection, Activity Execution Planning, and Activity Execution (cross-mappable to formal phases). Two continuity obligations follow. First, the pre-execution RIDM risk profile must be formally documented and transmitted across the pre-execution-to-execution gate, which matters because the staff executing the activity often differs from the teams and councils that selected it. Second, when controlling or mitigating a major risk requires choosing an optimal control among candidates, weighing benefit against implementation cost and induced risk, Activity-Execution RIDM is invoked from within CRM.
- LCR evaluation structure. With success criteria and their evidence specifications approved before the phase runs, each life-cycle review (conducted by the program/project with the independent Standing Review Board at selected reviews) asks four questions: Is the evidence consistent with the approved specifications (unexpected evidence creates uncertainty and smells of after-the-fact selection)? Does the evidence show the criteria met? Are the program's claims consistent with the evaluator's independent reading? How do identified deficiencies affect the judgment that the risk posture is being adhered to? Evaluators are told to watch for special pleading; mitigating circumstances asked to be taken on faith. Valid criteria effectively reduce the posture-adherence question to criteria satisfaction.
- Cross-cutting risk recognition. Two tests flag a risk as cross-cutting: it originates in activities a managerially distinct unit is responsible for executing, or it can significantly impact the objectives of such a unit (higher, lower, or parallel). Recognition can come from a higher unit reviewing sub-unit reports for common causes or multi-unit impacts, or from the identifying unit flagging upward; but the higher-level unit owns the official determination, and the whole mechanism depends on free vertical information flow, the RMTT report's key finding.
- Cross-cutting handling, two types. Type A: the risk cannot be effectively handled by a single unit, so handling itself must be coordinated; the higher entity runs part of the process and allocates control and tracking actions to the best-suited units, as with a multi-center recruiting shortage managed under headquarters coordination. Type B: multi-unit impacts but causes confined to one capable unit, as with launch vehicle delays threatening several spacecraft projects; handling stays with the responsible unit while integration reduces to timely notification of affected units and updates on outcomes, so they can seek alternatives if the handling plan slips.

## Key Concepts

- CORCP and CORHP. Cross-Organizational Risk Communication Protocols define trigger conditions for starting, updating, and terminating cross-cutting-risk communications, timeliness requirements, and default points of contact per unit type. Cross-Organizational Risk Handling Protocols add coordination criteria: which unit is the handling coordinator (who convenes contributors and leads the strategy meetings) and decision elevation criteria for when a higher authority must decide. Both belong in the RMPs of all levels, defined in cross-compatible fashion; contradictory criteria between interfacing RMPs is a named failure.
- RMP scope, expanded. Traditional plans covered purpose and scope, roles, communication avenues, likelihood/consequence categories, the CRM steps in brief, and risk registers with likelihood-versus-consequence matrices. NPR 8000.4 now requires much more: risk leadership principles, posture, tolerances, and acceptability criteria; stakeholder identification; risk types and sources; RIDM-CRM implementation and integration; applicable objectives and requirements; risks to each key objective; evidence commitments; the quantification-versus-qualitative decision per objective; aggregation from scenarios to objectives; coordination with higher-level RM plans and the SEMP; likelihood and severity categories consistent with performance markers and tolerance levels; assessment protocols; special provisions for cybersecurity and physical security; uncertainty treatment including U/U magnitude; elevation protocols; cross-organizational coordination protocols; communication protocols and display formats; decision documentation; review intervals; and management concurrence.
- RMP contents, tailored. The reference content list spans: introductory information (purpose, source directives, the subject activity, KDPs per NPR 7123.1, related activities); objectives, performance measures, markers, and tolerances with their justification against the Acquirer's posture; roles in responsible-accountable-consulted-informed form (risk manager, scenario owners, sign-off authority, boards and reviewers, informed stakeholders) with documented empanelment; identification processes (risk categories covered, taxonomy use, statement formats and validation, narratives); analysis processes (graded rigor per measure by class, complexity, novelty, and stage; model and data selection; heuristic and probabilistic methods; model verification and validation per NASA-HDBK-7009A; U/U margin determination from leading indicators; driver prioritization; display conventions); response processes (how the unit decides retire/watch/research/mitigate/elevate; when Activity-Execution RIDM needs a management petition; when rebaselining needs one; the execution-RIDM process itself; elevation communications); tracking and control processes (detecting changed conditions and realized departures, simple versus complex controls, RMP amendment rules, corrective action); cross-cutting communication protocols and disagreement documentation; KDP risk-acceptance criteria and evidence; and RMP review-and-update procedures at each key decision point.
- RMP authority and coordination. Any unit executing tasks for specified objectives produces an RMP, including support organizations whose interrelated institutional tasks amount to an "institutional project." An RMP authority (usually management, task possibly delegated to experts) produces it; the overall program, project, or activity manager retains approval and implementation responsibility. Authorities of interfacing units coordinate during production so assumptions agree, roles cover the space without duplication, and cross-organization consultation is assigned; then the coverage and tailoring goes through inter-organizational review per NPR 8000.4.

## Mental Models

- The objectives tree and the risk taxonomy tree are read together: taxonomy suggests scenarios and impact breadth; the objectives tree and its allocation map say whether those impacts cross unit boundaries.
- Continuity is a deliverable: risk information that does not survive a change of staff or a gate transition was never really transferred.
- A cross-cutting determination is a routing decision, not a label; Type A demands coordinated handling, Type B demands notification and update discipline.

## Anti-patterns

Planning risk management formally only in the Program/Project Domain while Enterprise and Institutional domains improvise; the historical gap that leaves cross-cutting risks unowned. Writing interfacing RMPs independently so their protocols contradict. Presenting review evidence not specified in advance, inviting both bias and erosion of assurance.

## Key Takeaways

- Integration is engineered through named protocols (CORCP, CORHP), coordinated RMPs, and gate-crossing documentation, not through exhortation.
- Cross-cutting recognition follows two simple questions and lands in two handling types; the higher unit decides, and communication flows are the enabling infrastructure.
- The modern RMP is the single document where posture, processes, roles, protocols, and U/U treatment become commitments, and it is reviewed and amended at every KDP.
- LCRs judge adherence to risk posture through pre-approved criteria and evidence, with evaluators trained on skepticism toward special pleading.
