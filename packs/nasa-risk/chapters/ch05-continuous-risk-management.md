# Continuous Risk Management (CRM)

## Core Idea

CRM manages the risk of a selected alternative during implementation: five cyclical steps, Identify, Analyze, Plan, Track, Control, supported end to end by Communicate and Document functions that run in parallel rather than in sequence. At any moment, scenarios are entering the risk database, the risk model is absorbing them, response plans are being built, and implemented responses are being tracked for effect. The unit of work is the individual risk scenario; the object of judgment is the aggregate risk to each objective, quantified by a scenario-based risk model inherited from Activity-Planning RIDM and refined continuously as design detail, test data, and new risks arrive. Early models lean qualitative and expert-judgment-based; mature ones become quantitative and data-based. CRM also manages opportunity alongside risk, and every element is graded across Activity Classes A+ through D, with the handbook presenting Class A+ guidance and describing lower classes as progressively culled versions of it.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Initialization. Each organizational level writes its own Risk Management Plan, every unit is a Provider to an Acquirer, with the RMP accepted in a negotiated handshake, and management owns it, with RIDM and CRM practitioners serving as subject-matter experts. A complete RMP names review-board stakeholders, documents the objectives tree top-down, sets performance markers and their tolerance levels plus elevation protocols, declares burn-down schedules, states per-objective whether assessment will be quantitative or qualitative (with rationale where qualitative), fixes communication protocols and reporting frequency, and defines cross-unit coordination. Small initiatives cover only the relevant elements.
- RIDM handoff. CRM receives the objectives and performance measures, the selected alternative's risk analysis, the marker set-points with tolerances, the initial risk list, and the RISR. The RIDM analysis covered only discriminator measures, so CRM completes it for the rest, expands the risk list, and shifts from parameter-based toward scenario-based modeling. Because CRM runs at every hierarchy level while RIDM ran pre-hierarchy, CRM models depend on upward information flow, shared assumptions, and negotiated model- and data-reporting protocols.
- Risk burn-down. Risk is expected to fall as drivers are controlled and knowledge improves, and the RMP records the expected trajectory (red at Milestone 1, yellow at 2, green at 3, notionally). Burn-down schedules become commitments assessed at life-cycle reviews, and budgets must carry cost and schedule reserves sized to the planned RM activities; a mismatch between reserves and realized impacts is itself a U/U signal. Schedules are holistic because responses shift risk between domains: safety and technical gains routinely paid for in cost and schedule.
- Risk database. NPR 8000.4 requires dispositions, acceptance decisions, and rationales to be documented in the unit's risk database. Integrated, taxonomy-standardized databases across units surface overlooked risks, expose other units' response rationales, align posture allocations, and reveal cross-cutting risks that need cooperative or higher-level handling; the systems view behind systemic responses to systemic risks.

## Key Concepts

- Identify. Scenarios, opportunities, and leading indicators come from the initial (RIDM) risk analysis, taxonomy-facilitated brainstorming (checklists, what-if, FMEA, HAZOP), implementation conditions (systems-engineering parameter data, response tracking data, inter-organizational alerts such as GIDEP, elevations from below, external market and supply shifts), and requirement rebaselining from above. Risk statements take two formats. Defined scenario: given a fact-based Condition, there is a possibility of a Departure from baseline affecting an Asset (an element of the organizational-unit portfolio), which can result in a Consequence adversely affecting stated Objective(s); each component a single phrase, grounded in evidence, with an actionability notation. Implied scenario: given a Leading Indicator known to correlate with U/U risk, a Condition has pushed it past its watch value, with the potential for a Departure to push it past its respond value, affecting an objective. Opportunity statements mirror this: within-plan actions yielding proximate benefits for existing objectives, or beyond-plan actions creating new objectives serving the mission. Seven validation questions gate entry (impact on an objectively describable objective, coherent scenario chain, documented basis, baseline change lacking adequate contingency, factual condition, credible departure, consequence written without regard to mitigations); a no or unknown answer invalidates the statement. A format-free narrative captures contributing factors, uncertainties, consequence ranges, and the identifier's recommended responses with rationale and expected risk shifting. Taxonomy categorization flags cross-cutting entries.
- Analyze. Build the RSD (departure event through pivotal events to end states expressed as performance on every measure), then choose probabilistic or heuristic analysis. Probabilistic: quantify event likelihoods (fault tree analysis, phenomenological modeling, data precedence running project-specific, then other-project, then surrogate, then expert elicitation; qualitative five-tier scales where data is thin); model end-state performance for every measure, leveraging nominal design and programmatic models modified for off-nominal branches; handle epistemic uncertainty by embedding the aleatory model in a Monte Carlo shell sampling parameter distributions with dependencies, producing distributions of risk results rather than points; classify each scenario per measure against its IRTLs (resolving uncertainty via declared confidence levels or mean values, safety demanding high confidence), and a scenario overall as the worst of its per-measure classes; integrate into the risk model accounting for competing, synergistic, and cumulative effects, recharacterize U/U per the current knowledge state, classify aggregate risk against aggregate RTLs, and find risk drivers, parameters, events, indicators, or combinations that move aggregate risk across acceptability boundaries, by sensitivity analysis, in two passes if needed (singles first, then combinations). Spider charts communicate: aggregate risk per top-level objective on one chart, top-risk charts per objective. Opportunity analysis runs the same machinery; an action is desirable if it turns red objectives green or yellow, or makes a new objective achievable, without pushing any other objective to red, and with implementation risk the benefit can justify. Heuristic: RSD pathways ranked by history and judgment, mapped onto conservative risk matrices anchored to IRTLs or RTLs (multiple marginal paths escalate to red absent a defensible cumulative argument; multiple acceptable paths escalate to yellow by default), one matrix per threatened objective, then aggregated per objective; explicitly a fallback when time, resources, or data make probabilistic methods impractical, with results that may differ from rigorous ones.
- Plan. Response options carry NPR 8000.4's six disposition types. Accept: the no-action disposition, applying to the totality of performance risk when aggregate risk sits within tolerances, documented with its assumptions, never combined into alternatives. Mitigate: positive action on risk drivers, subdivided into departure prevention (cut likelihood) and consequence reduction (cut severity); it may rebaseline derived objectives at lower levels (triggering Activity-Rebaseline RIDM with decision-authority approval) or, in extreme cases, reopen the planning trade space. Watch: a monitoring plan over driver observables with thresholds and contingent deferred decisions, updating the risk analysis on a batch schedule. Research: a plan to buy down epistemic uncertainty that blocks robust decisions, with scheduled results feeding the risk model. Elevate: transfer of a risk decision upward when no satisfactory combination of the other options exists at the current level; never an initial option, usually paired with Watch, and obligating the elevating unit to propose attractive alternatives beyond its own authority. Close: bookkeeping removal when drivers no longer exist, have become insignificant, or the event has happened and become a problem to track. Alternatives are synergistic combinations of options; from N options, 2-to-the-N combinations exist theoretically, pruned to those addressing multiple units' risks, most out-of-tolerance objectives, and least collateral risk. Formal Activity-Execution RIDM is needed only when unacceptable risk persists and fixes are not simple and obvious; otherwise the Plan step resolves day-to-day without it, and rebaselining remains the decision authority's call when no response alternative can restore tolerability.
- Track and Control. Tracking applies to Mitigate, Watch, and Research only. Mitigation tracking covers implementation progress inside the project schedule plus two effectiveness questions: actual risk reduction against the Plan-step forecast, and actual risk cost (the risk accepted elsewhere to buy the reduction) against its forecast. Watch and Research tracking monitor their observables as early-warning indicators feeding contingency execution. Requirement risk tracking charts show risk rising and falling as scenarios come and go. Control evaluates the tracking data: keep responses on track with minor in-scope corrections; execute contingencies for Watch and Research per pre-established criteria; and reinitiate the Plan step when a response's objective is clearly unattainable under the current plan. New controls from new risks must slot into the existing internal-control structure.
- Communicate and Document. Communication runs vertically and horizontally all five steps: every unit aware of the scenarios affecting it, consistent modeling assumptions, correct level-by-level aggregation, shared and current risk-driver lists, engagement of every affected unit in response planning, unambiguous elevation. Standard aids: risk and opportunity statements and narratives, burn-down schedules, populated taxonomies, RSDs, risk-driver lists, response-trade tables, and tracking charts against burn-down profiles. Protocols, periodic reporting plus trigger-based reporting past elevation thresholds, are negotiated and written into the RMP. Documentation: a risk response planning document in Plan and a risk response evaluation document in Track and Control, all archived in the relational risk database with its many-to-many links among scenarios, risks, drivers, and responses. Class A+ expects regular cross-organizational meetings weekly or biweekly; lower classes stretch the cadence.

## Mental Models

- Write the consequence as if nothing will be done: the Identify step captures the concern, the Plan step decides what to do about it, and mixing the two corrupts the database.
- The risk model is one integrated living artifact, not a pile of entries; each new scenario joins it, and U/U margin is re-estimated with it.
- Elevate is a confession of inability, not a delegation habit; the hierarchy exists so that someone at each level can manage, and only genuine impossibility moves a decision up.
- Quantitative where possible, heuristic where necessary, graded always; the class tables are the tuning knob.

## Anti-patterns

Letting risk statements presume their own mitigations. Rating speculative worries as risks by skipping the fact-based condition. Treating heuristic risk-matrix coloring as equivalent to probabilistic analysis rather than a conservative fallback. Proposing Elevate before exhausting Mitigate, Watch, and Research. Burning cost and schedule reserves on unanticipated mitigation impacts without re-baselining the reserves; the handbook reads that mismatch as underappreciated risk surfacing.

## Key Takeaways

- Five steps plus communicate-and-document, running concurrently, seeded by RIDM's risk list and bounded by the RMP's markers, tolerances, and elevation protocols.
- The six dispositions (Accept, Mitigate, Watch, Research, Elevate, Close) are the vocabulary of every response option; alternatives are combinations of options, analyzed for residual risk against tolerances before selection.
- Risk drivers, not scenarios, are the lever: sensitivity analysis finds them, and mitigation targets them.
- Burn-down schedules turn risk posture into schedule commitments, reviewed at life-cycle reviews alongside reserve adequacy.
- The relational risk database is the process memory: dispositions, rationales, and tracking evidence all live there, enabling cross-cutting recognition and aggregate roll-up.
