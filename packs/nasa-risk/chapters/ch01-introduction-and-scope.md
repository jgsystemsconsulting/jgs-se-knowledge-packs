# Introduction, purpose, and what changed in Version 2.0

## Core Idea

The handbook is NASA's in-depth reference for the practice of risk management, updating the 2011 Version 1 guidance and aligning it with the current Agency risk management procedural requirements, NPR 8000.4, and their parent governance directive, NPD 1000.0. NPD 1000.0 elevates Risk Leadership to a fundamental tenet of the risk culture NASA wants: decision authorities set limits of acceptable risk on top-level objectives, and managers inside those limits lead the management of risk themselves. The handbook's job is to turn that policy posture into implementable processes, analysis techniques, and organizational arrangements.

The scope is deliberately enterprise-wide. NPR 8000.4 treats NASA as an integrated enterprise and sets risk management requirements both for the enterprise as a whole and for the portfolio elements inside it: programs, projects, and the institutional activities that provide mission support. Version 2.0 extends coverage accordingly, adding explicit treatment of enterprise and institutional risk execution alongside the traditional program/project focus.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The two-part structure. Part 1 builds foundations in sequence: framework principles (ch02), risk concepts and analysis techniques (ch03), the two operational processes RIDM and CRM (ch04, ch05), and organizational integration including the Risk Management Plan (ch06). Part 2 then illustrates those processes with worked examples: process selection, an activity-planning RIDM execution, and CRM executions in both programmatic and institutional settings (ch07 here).
- A roles roadmap. Table 1-I maps generic risk management roles (deliberation and decision boards, high-level leaders, review boards, managers, risk management specialists, risk analysts) onto the two principal activity stages, Definition/Planning and Execution, and Appendix A turns that into a reading roadmap. The role names are generic, not official position titles; Definition/Planning corresponds roughly to Pre-Phase A and Phase A, Execution to the rest of the life cycle.
- Policy alignment. The requirements the handbook supplements sit inside a lattice of directives: NPD 1000.3, NPD 1000.5, NPD 7120.4, NPD 8700.1, NPD 8900.5, and NPD 2810.1, among domain-specific policy. This pack names these policies where the source does and does not reproduce their text.

## Key Concepts

Version 2.0 names what is new, and the list is effectively the pack's thematic index:

- Objectives-Driven Risk Management (ODRM) and Risk Leadership (RL). Risk is framed as the potential for shortfalls against established objectives. Decision authorities, consulting affected stakeholders, set risk tolerances (limits of acceptable risk) on top-level objectives; the set of tolerances is the risk posture. Managers hold authority and accountability to manage risk as they see fit within that posture, and tolerances flow down to supporting organizations in tandem with derived and allocated objectives.
- Aggregate risk. NPR 8000.4's definition of risk points assessment, communication, and management at the total risk to objectives, the aggregate, rather than at a list of individually rated items. Aggregate risk includes the full ensemble of individual risk scenarios; aggregation types are unpacked in ch03.
- Rigorous RIDM-CRM integration across life cycles. The two processes are treated as one integrated system with defined handoffs and re-entry points, not as two separate manuals.
- Cross-organizational integration. Objectives flow down from NASA Strategic Goals through programs, projects, and institutions; acceptable risk levels flow down with them; actual risks communicate back up along the same pathways, with horizontal communication and escalation covered in ch06.
- Alternative risk communication and display. Ratings tied to objective-specific tolerances replace across-the-board 5x5 matrix pinning: there are as many sets of rating criteria as there are top-level objectives. Two ground rules apply: risk analysis (an objective determination of shortfall likelihoods) must be completed before and separately from risk rating (a subjective tolerability judgment), and any display form, stoplight, 3x3, or 5x5, must be anchored to the declared risk posture.
- Risk, benefit, and opportunity management. Balancing risk against benefit and exploiting opportunities inside the adopted posture is part of risk management, not a separate discipline (introduced in ch02, expanded in ch05).
- Physical security and cybersecurity. NPR 1620.3B (physical security, responsible office OPS) and NPR 2810.1F (cybersecurity, responsible office OCIO) are named as the relevant requirements. Intentional-threat scenarios need special modeling treatment; dedicated sections in ch03 address the tailoring.
- Graded approach. A much more detailed account of how to scale scope, completeness, and the quantitative-versus-qualitative mix of RIDM and CRM analyses. The general introduction is ch02 Section 2.2.5; implementation detail sits in ch04 and ch05.
- Unknown and underappreciated (U/U) risk. Beyond managing known risks, the handbook treats the potential magnitude of unknown or underappreciated risks using historical experience as a guide, primarily in ch03.

## Mental Models

- Read Part 1 in order the first time: principles before concepts, concepts before processes, processes before organizational tailoring. The chapters are sequenced to reflect the priority in which a team organizing risk management would want to absorb the material.
- Always view local execution objectives inside the Agency's strategic and organizational context; risk relationships cross horizontal and hierarchical organizational borders and must be considered across them.
- The handbook supplements NPR 8000.4 with implementation guidance; it is not itself the requirement document. Nothing here replaces the policy directives it aligns to.

## Key Takeaways

- The center of gravity moved from rating individual risks on a standard matrix to setting objective-anchored risk tolerances, managing aggregate risk inside that posture, and exercising risk leadership at every level.
- RIDM (decision making before commitment) and CRM (risk management during execution) are integrated processes with explicit interfaces, including re-baselining and re-entry when conditions change.
- Enterprise, institutional, security, and opportunity perspectives are first-class citizens in this version, not appendices to the program/project view.
- The five named policy anchors are NPR 8000.4, NPD 1000.0, and the supporting directives above; they are named here, not quoted or rebuilt.
