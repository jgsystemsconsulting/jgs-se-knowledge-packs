# Chapter 2: Systems Engineering Criteria

## Core Idea
Section 4 groups airworthiness-relevant systems engineering criteria: design criteria discipline, tools/databases, materials selection, manufacturing/quality, operator and maintenance technical data, and configuration management. These establish the engineering management backbone domain sections assume.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Design criteria control**: Documented, approved design criteria as the technical law for the air system.
- **Tools and databases**: Qualified analytical tools and authoritative data supporting certification claims.
- **Materials / manufacturing / quality**: Selectable materials and controlled build processes that preserve airworthiness assumptions.
- **Tech data and CM**: Operator/maintenance manuals and configuration management so the certified product stays defined.

## Key Concepts
- Design criteria are controlled technical law for the air system.
- Tools and databases used in certification arguments need qualification.
- Materials, manufacturing, and quality evidence must match the certified configuration.
- Operator and maintenance technical data are airworthiness products.
- Configuration management keeps the as-built product aligned with the basis.

## Mental Models
- Airworthiness is inseparable from SE discipline — orphan domain analyses fail without CM and criteria control.
- Manuals and tech orders are airworthiness products, not after-the-fact publications.
- Manufacturing/quality evidence must match the as-certified configuration.

## Anti-patterns
- **Criteria folklore**: Design rules living only in tribal knowledge.
- **Tool worship**: Unvalidated models used as certification proof.
- **CM drift**: As-built diverging from the certification basis configuration.

## Key Takeaways
1. Lock design criteria and keep them current under CM.
2. Qualify tools/data used for certification arguments.
3. Bind materials, manufacturing, and quality evidence to the basis.
4. Treat operator/maintainer documentation as part of airworthiness.
5. Use SE criteria to integrate domain sections (structures, flight, and others).
