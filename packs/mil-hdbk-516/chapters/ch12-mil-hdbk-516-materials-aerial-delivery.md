# Chapter: Materials and aerial delivery

## Core Idea

This chapter covers section 19 (materials and material integrity) and section 20 (aerial delivery: air transport and airdrop). Materials criteria address selection, processing, and properties; corrosion prevention and control; non-destructive inspection planning; and special processes with process integrity.

Aerial delivery criteria address clearances; air-vehicle and cargo restraint structure; cargo and payload restraint provisions; weight and balance, center-of-gravity movement, and jettison; aerial delivery manuals; personnel airdrop system structure; and cargo ignition sources.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Materials integrity as AW

Material choice, processing, corrosion control, inspection, and special processes are design attributes that sustain strength and durability claims over life.

### Aerial delivery as a missionized structural and systems problem

Air transport and airdrop impose unique clearance, restraint, mass-properties, and ignition hazards on the air vehicle and its cargo or personnel systems.

### Manuals for delivery operations

Aerial delivery manuals are controlled products that must match the approved delivery configurations and limits.

## Key Concepts

- **Materials selection and processing.** Properties as designed and as processed, including allowables used in structural analysis.
- **Corrosion prevention and control.** Design and process measures that protect integrity in the operating environment.
- **Non-destructive inspection (NDI) plan.** Inspections that match damage-tolerance and materials assumptions.
- **Special processes.** Processes whose integrity is critical to material performance (for example heat treat or bonding as applicable).
- **Clearances and restraint.** Physical fit and structural restraint for cargo and personnel systems.
- **CG movement and jettison.** Mass properties during delivery operations, including emergency jettison paths.
- **Ignition sources.** Cargo-related fire ignition risks during delivery operations.

## Mental Models

- **Materials carry structures.** Section 19 evidence underwrites section 5 life and residual strength claims.
- **Delivery changes the vehicle.** Airdrop and transport configurations are AW configurations, not only mission tactics.
- **Restraint is structure.** Cargo and personnel restraint provisions are structural design attributes.

## Key Takeaways

- Section 19 covers materials, corrosion, NDI, and special processes.
- Section 20 covers air transport and airdrop clearances, restraint, mass properties, manuals, and ignition.
- Keep NDI and corrosion plans consistent with structures durability claims.
- Treat delivery configurations as first-class AW configurations.
