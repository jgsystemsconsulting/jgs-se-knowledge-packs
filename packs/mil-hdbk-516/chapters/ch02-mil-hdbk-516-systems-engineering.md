# Chapter: Systems engineering criteria

## Core Idea

Section 4 gives a disciplined systems engineering path so AW is achieved and kept through design, development, sustainment, and modification. Configuration management (CM) and system safety sit inside this section. The foreword records that prior-edition system safety content folds here.

Products from SE, CM, and system safety (architecture, requirements, baselines, verification, risks, problem tracking) define operational usage, configuration, and environment. Those products are the basis for AW assessment and subsequent approval.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Life-cycle SE for AW

Apply SE, CM, and system safety from initial design through maintenance and modification. Treat interactions and safety risks as first-class inputs to the certification basis.

### Operational usage, configuration, environment

Capture how the air system will be used, which configuration is assessed, and what environments apply. AW claims are meaningless without those three anchors.

### System safety inside SE

System safety practices generate hazards, controls, and residual risk that the AW authority accepts. Critical safety items and critical characteristics are called out as explicit SE products.

### Flight manuals as SE outputs

Flight manuals and related operator products are configuration-controlled SE outputs that must match the approved configuration and limits.

## Key Concepts

- **Configuration baselines.** Identify the exact design under assessment; drift without CM breaks the basis.
- **Verification activities.** Evidence that design attributes meet tailored standards.
- **Risk and problem reporting.** Track residual risk and in-service discoveries back into the basis.
- **Critical safety items (CSI) and critical characteristics.** Special control on items whose failure can compromise AW.

## Mental Models

- **SE products are the AW story.** Authority review reads architecture, requirements, baselines, and risk - not informal claims.
- **Safety is not a side letter.** Under D, system safety criteria live with SE, not a separate chapter.
- **Usage before hardware claims.** Wrong usage assumptions invalidate MoCs.

## Key Takeaways

- Section 4 covers SE, CM, system safety, CSI, and flight-manual control.
- Prior system safety cluster folds here per the foreword.
- Anchor every assessment on usage, configuration, and environment.
- Keep baselines and manuals aligned with the approved design.
