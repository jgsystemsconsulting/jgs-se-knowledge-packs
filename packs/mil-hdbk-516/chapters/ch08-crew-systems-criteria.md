# Chapter 8: Crew Systems Criteria

## Core Idea
Section 9 addresses crew systems airworthiness: pilot-vehicle interface, crew station accommodations/lighting/equipment, human-machine interface, UAS/ROA control-station operator accommodations, life support, emergency escape/survival, transparencies, crash survivability, and related human-system elements.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Crew systems scope**: Human interface, life support, escape/survival, and control-station accommodations as airworthiness elements.
- **UAS/ROA control stations**: Operator accommodations and interfaces included when applicable to the air system.

## Key Concepts
- Crew systems include HMI, crew station, life support, and escape/survival elements.
- UAS/ROA control-station accommodations can fall under crew-systems criteria.
- Crash survivability couples to structures and flight envelopes.
- Late geometry freezes often force crew-systems waivers.
- Escape and life-support changes need re-verification after cockpit redesigns.

## Mental Models
- Crew systems failures (escape, life support, HMI) are airworthiness failures, not only human-factors niceties.
- UAS moves some crew-system criteria to the control station without removing them.
- Crash survivability and escape couple to structures and flight envelopes.

## Anti-patterns
- **HMI last**: Fitting displays/controls after hard constraints freeze bad geometries.
- **Manned-only checklists on UAS**: Missing control-station criteria.
- **Escape system orphaned**: Not re-verified after seat/cockpit/structure changes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Include crew-systems criteria in the tailored basis whenever aircrew or remote operators are in scope.
2. Use human-factors and escape/life-support evidence listed in typical source data.
3. Re-verify after geometry, transparency, or control-station changes.
4. Link to flight technology for controllability/HMI (ch04).
5. Coordinate crashworthiness with structures (ch03).
