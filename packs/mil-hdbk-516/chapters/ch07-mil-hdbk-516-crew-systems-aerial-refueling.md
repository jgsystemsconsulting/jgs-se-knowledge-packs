# Chapter: Crew systems and aerial refueling

## Core Idea

This chapter covers section 9 (crew systems) and section 10 (aerial refueling). Crew systems criteria address life support; crew stations, control stations, and interiors; lighting; human performance; escape; emergency egress and bailout; crash survivability; and transparency integration. The foreword records that prior-edition passenger safety content folds into section 9.

Aerial refueling (AR) is its own section under D. AR is passing fuel between air vehicles in flight, including boom, receptacle, drogue, and probe equipment and the air-vehicle interfaces that make pairing safe.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Crew systems by function

Organize evidence around life support, station design, lighting, human performance, escape/egress, crash survivability, and transparencies. Cargo, fighter, and other vehicle categories may have different applicable subsets.

### Passenger safety fold

Passenger-related safety criteria that once stood apart now sit with crew systems when the re-fetch still shows that consolidation.

### AR compatibility stack

Physical, aerodynamic, mechanical, electrical, and operational compatibility with targeted tanker or receiver partners. Failure modes, built-in-test, technical manuals, wear/damage levels, and AR electronic hardware/software/firmware complete the stack.

## Key Concepts

- **Life support.** Oxygen, thermal, and related systems that keep occupants within safe physiological limits for the envelope.
- **Stations and interiors.** Layout, controls, and displays for crew and, where applicable, control-station operators for unmanned systems.
- **Human performance.** Workload and human factors that keep tasks acceptable across the envelope.
- **Escape and egress.** Ejection, bailout, and emergency exit design attributes.
- **Crash survivability.** Restraint, structure, and related design features for crash events.
- **Transparencies.** Birdstrike, structural, and thermal capability of windshields and canopies.
- **AR interface dimensions and engagement.** Safe engage/disengage without foreign-object damage, binding, or structural interference.
- **AR BIT and manuals.** Built-in-test and technical data that support safe AR operations.

## Mental Models

- **Occupant and operator are design attributes.** Human outcomes are not only training problems.
- **AR is a paired system.** Tanker and receiver claims must match the intended partner set.
- **Unmanned control stations count.** When the air system includes a control station, crew-systems thinking still applies.

## Key Takeaways

- Section 9 covers crew and passenger-related systems; section 10 is AR.
- Passenger safety folds into crew systems per the foreword.
- AR needs multi-domain compatibility plus BIT, manuals, and electronics evidence.
- Coordinate transparencies and restraints with structures.
