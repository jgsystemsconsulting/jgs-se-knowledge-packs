# Chapter: Maintenance criteria

## Core Idea

Section 16 covers maintenance criteria that keep the design airworthy through inspection and maintenance products. Although MIL-STD-1629 is canceled, the handbook still points to its FMEA, criticality analysis, and FMECA-maintainability information analysis tasks as a foundational guide for building useful FMECA artifacts that support section 16 claims.

Criteria link failure modes that hazard maintainers or the air vehicle to cautions, warnings, and tests in maintenance publications, and to inspection requirements that match detection methods and criticality.

## Frameworks Introduced

### FMECA-informed maintenance products

Use failure mode, effects, and criticality analysis - including maintainability information - to drive what appears in manuals, checklists, and inspection programs.

### Publications and inspections as design outputs

Maintenance manuals, checklists, and inspection requirements are configuration-controlled products of the design, not after-the-fact paperwork.

### Hazard to maintainer and air vehicle

Section 16 cares about failures that hurt people doing maintenance and failures whose end effects hazard the air vehicle if not detected or controlled.

## Key Concepts

- **Maintenance manuals and checklists.** Procedural products that encode cautions, warnings, and required tests.
- **Inspection requirements.** Scheduled and condition-based inspections tied to damage tolerance, corrosion, and other design assumptions.
- **Testable items.** Items whose failure effects warrant operational, functional, or system tests in the publications.
- **Detection methods.** How the design and the maintenance program discover latent failures before flight.

## Mental Models

- **Canceled standard, living method.** Citing MIL-STD-1629 as a guide is not the same as imposing it as a current mandate; the handbook explains the FMECA use.
- **Manuals are MoC evidence.** Weak publications undermine otherwise sound hardware claims.
- **Maintainer safety is AW-adjacent.** Hazards to maintainers that the design can create belong in the assessment.

## Key Takeaways

- Section 16 ties FMECA-quality analysis to manuals, tests, and inspections.
- Cover both maintainer hazards and air-vehicle end effects.
- Keep maintenance products under configuration control with the approved design.
- Coordinate inspections with structures and materials damage-tolerance and corrosion plans.
