# Chapter 3: Structures Criteria

## Core Idea
Section 5 provides qualitative airworthiness criteria for airframe structures — loads, strength, durability, damage tolerance, materials/processes, and related proof. Certification source-data lists point to analyses, tests, and mass-properties evidence typically used to show criteria are met.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Structural design criteria**: Loads, environments, and strength/durability/damage-tolerance expectations for the airframe.
- **Evidence pattern**: Analysis plus test plus configuration-consistent manufacturing data as a typical compliance set.

## Key Concepts
- Structures criteria cover loads, strength, durability, and damage-tolerance thinking.
- Compliance usually blends analysis, test, and configuration-consistent build data.
- Mass properties growth can invalidate a frozen strength story.
- Field repairs and modifications must stay inside the structural basis.
- Mission usage spectra should drive loads assumptions.

## Mental Models
- Structures airworthiness is criteria-plus-evidence, not a single static margin number.
- Weight/balance and as-built configuration feed structural validity.
- Damage tolerance and durability are life-cycle airworthiness concerns, not only first-flight gates.

## Anti-patterns
- **Analysis-only closure**: No test correlation where criteria imply empirical confirmation.
- **Ignoring mass properties growth**: Strength story frozen while weight climbs.
- **Repair unknown**: Field repairs outside certified structural basis.

## Key Takeaways
1. Map each structures criterion to analysis/test evidence owners.
2. Keep loads and spectra aligned with the real mission usage.
3. Control structural repairs and modifications under the certification basis.
4. Integrate materials/process changes with SE materials criteria (ch02).
5. Coordinate structural failure modes with system safety processes.
