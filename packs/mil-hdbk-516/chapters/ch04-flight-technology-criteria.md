# Chapter 4: Flight Technology Criteria

## Core Idea
Section 6 addresses flight technology airworthiness — flying qualities, performance, stability/control, guidance/path aspects, and related simulation/test evidence. Criteria remain qualitative; compliance typically blends analysis, simulation, and flight/ground test.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Flight technology criteria set**: Qualitative expectations for safe controllable flight across the envelope.
- **Simulation and test evidence**: Models and tests used to argue compliance, including special patterns where relevant.

## Key Concepts
- Flight technology spans flying qualities, performance, and stability/control.
- Evidence typically mixes analysis, simulation, and flight or ground test.
- Envelope-wide and degraded-mode cases matter when they are in scope.
- Flight-manual limits should trace to certification evidence.
- Control-law and software changes couple to software criteria.

## Mental Models
- Flight airworthiness is envelope-wide, including degraded and special-ops cases when in scope.
- Simulation supports but does not automatically replace flight evidence when criteria demand it.
- Crew-vehicle interaction links this section to crew systems (ch08).

## Anti-patterns
- **Point-in-envelope demo**: Showing one benign condition as full compliance.
- **Unvalidated sim**: Certification arguments from models without credibility evidence.
- **Orphan flight-manual limits**: Published limits not traced to criteria evidence.

## Key Takeaways
1. Tailor flight criteria to aircraft class and mission (ch01).
2. Plan combined analysis/simulation/flight evidence early.
3. Trace flight-manual envelopes to certification data.
4. Coordinate control-law/software changes with software criteria (ch07).
5. Include degraded-mode flying quality thinking where applicable.
