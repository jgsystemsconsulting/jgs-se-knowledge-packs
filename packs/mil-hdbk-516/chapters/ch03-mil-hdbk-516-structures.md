# Chapter: Structures criteria

## Core Idea

Section 5 covers air vehicle structure: parts and assemblies that carry and transfer loads for their primary functions. Scope includes wing, fuselage, empennage, control surfaces, mechanical controls, leading and trailing edges, radomes, inlets, nacelles, mounts, windshield and canopy structure, landing and arresting gear structure, shipboard launch/recovery structure, in-flight refueling structure, and structural attachments for seats, payload, and tie-down.

Criteria address loads, structural dynamics, strength, durability and damage tolerance, and weight and balance. MoCs use analysis, test, and examination appropriate to the claim. This pack describes the method, not criterion table cells.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Load to integrity chain

Define loads for intended usage, assess dynamics and strength, then durability and damage tolerance for the life of the design. Weight and balance close the structural usage story.

### Primary structure breadth

Structure is more than airframe skins. Gear, hooks, mounts, and attachments that carry load are in scope when they perform structural functions.

### Evidence patterns

Typical MoC patterns: loads derivation from usage, analysis of internal loads and margins, component and full-scale test where analysis is insufficient, and inspection planning tied to damage-tolerance assumptions.

## Key Concepts

- **Loads.** Flight, ground, shipboard, and special conditions tied to the approved envelope.
- **Structural dynamics.** Response including flutter, vibration, and dynamic landing or shipboard events as applicable.
- **Strength.** Static and related strength margins for limit and ultimate conditions as tailored.
- **Durability and damage tolerance.** Life and residual strength with assumed damage and inspection.
- **Weight and balance.** Mass properties that affect structural and flight claims.

## Mental Models

- **Usage drives loads.** Wrong envelope assumptions poison every later MoC.
- **Attachments are structure.** Seats, stores mounts, and tie-downs fail the air vehicle when they fail.
- **Method over cells.** Argue the standard with MoCs; do not copy handbook tables into the pack.

## Key Takeaways

- Section 5 is the structures domain for fixed- and rotary-wing designs.
- Cover loads, dynamics, strength, durability/damage tolerance, and weight and balance.
- Include gear, hooks, mounts, and attachment structure when they carry load.
- Keep mass properties consistent with flight technology and aerial delivery claims.
