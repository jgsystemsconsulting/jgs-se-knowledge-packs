## The unpublished Chapter 4

Chapter 4 (Non-Standard Acquisition) is unpublished in V1.1: the source marks it `[to follow]` with no page. It is not in this pack, and the pack does not invent it. Chapter 2 of this pack records the gap and the pointers Chapter 1 of the source gives for agile and adaptive acquisition, innovation and experimentation, programmable elements, cyber security, and autonomous systems and AI.

## Annex B: Programme Acquisition Safety Overview (described, not reproduced)

Annex B is a one-page figure titled Programme Acquisition Safety Overview, with no extractable prose. The figure itself is not reproduced here. Chapter 1 of the source says the figure presents the phased development of safety through the lifecycle and a graphical depiction of safety case and safety management system ownership.

The phases the figure spans are the seven acquisition phases: Pre-Concept, Concept, Assessment, Demonstration, Manufacture, In-Service, and Disposal/Termination. Ownership of the safety case and the safety management system sits with the SRO through procurement and moves to the User at entry to service, staying with the User through in-service and disposal.

## Annex C: Safety Evidence Summary Table (printed p. C-1)

The source's Annex C is an artefact guide for SROs and Users: it maps the evidence typically required at the end of each phase, and the Director of Acquisition and Programme Delivery PEAT lines of enquiry confirm the specific evidence requirements for each formal JSP 655 submission stage. A second header band in the source marks SOC over Pre-Concept, OBC over Concept, FBC over Assessment, and SRO/User over Demonstration through Disposal. Every programme differs: the SRO engages the ASC early to tailor, and a programme may evidence a requirement another way where the ASC agrees.
