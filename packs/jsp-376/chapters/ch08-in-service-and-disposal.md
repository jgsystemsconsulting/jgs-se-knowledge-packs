# Chapter 8: In-Service and Disposal/Termination

## The in-service key policy statement

The source states the in-service policy in one sentence, quoted here from the key policy statement:

> During the In-Service Phase, the User must make sure that the capability is operated in accordance with the safe operating envelope and that the safety risks remain ALARP and Tolerable.

## How the emphasis changes

The character of the safety work changes at entry. Before then, safety activity shaped the design and prepared the in-service arrangements; once in service, the focus is on implementing the controls already decided, monitoring performance, and learning from accidents, incidents, and near misses. If the controls are not implemented, the residual risk may rise above what was accepted, and the User must then assess the practicable measures that bring it back down.

## Safety management in service

The User may wrap several capabilities into a single set of arrangements, one SMP included, provided they can show how those arrangements apply to each capability and how each capability's characteristics were considered. As the Out of Service Date approaches, a specific disposal SMP is agreed with the disposal agency in advance.

On the OSA front, a final assessment after Full Operating Capability confirms that the identified mitigations landed and any additional ones were put in place; from then on, assurance follows the organisation's routine regime. A change to the capability or to the way it is used should prompt a fresh look at whether an OSA is needed before the change is made. Lessons learned from accidents, incidents, and near misses are reported and analysed so that risks stay ALARP and tolerable, and the resulting changes are documented in the safety case, communicated, and implemented.

## Requirements and compliance in service

The URD is maintained as the baseline. Changes arising from upkeep, update, organisational change, or the delivery of training must not compromise the safety elements of the user requirement. The context can move even when the requirement text does not, so legislation, regulation, and technology are monitored for their effect on the solution and its safety. The Legislative Compliance Assessment is reviewed twice over: against new or changed law, and against changes to the solution and its use. Sponsoring a new exemption falls to the User, supported by the delivery agent, and because the capability is non-compliant in the meantime it should come out of operation while the application is pending. A capability upgrade follows a tailored acquisition lifecycle; the source defers the detail as belonging to a later issue, and that limit is recorded here rather than filled in.

Certification is maintained through life. A modification can force full or partial recertification, and the DSA regulator or the certification body is asked first. Using a solution that should have been recertified and was not can prohibit use and undermine the safety case. Where certification was limited, the funded programme to remove the caveats is implemented, or the certification body is told that the limits are enduring.

## The in-service safety case

The safety case is maintained through life. Periodic reviews run at an interval the User sets and the SMP records, based on risk and no longer than every 2 years, each producing a signed in-service safety case report. Other occasions also trigger a review: a change in operating context; in-service design change or configuration drift away from the safety case; change from any contributing DLOD; transfer to a different operating authority; change to legislation, regulation, policy, or standards; a material change to the underlying safety argument; a major change made to the Statement of Operating Intent and Usage; any significant continuing safety concern, or a gap between actual performance and design intent; an accident or major incident, or the approach of return to service; a new higher-risk activity; a new technology recognised as good practice; or plans to change the Out of Service Date. On each occasion the User runs a pan-DLOD review, updates the safety case, signs a report, and holds the change back until the arrangements support an ALARP and tolerable judgement; temporary restrictions on use are one way to hold that line. Before the Out of Service Date the User signs an OSD safety case report covering the main equipment, supporting systems (simulators, for example), and spare parts, and shares it with the ASC if a submission is needed.

Operation stays inside the safe operating envelope, with each event risk-assessed. Temporary operation outside the envelope, on operations for example, needs a risk assessment accepted at the appropriate level, under JSP 815 Element 4 Expectation 4.3, which is named here and not restated. If the capability must permanently operate outside the envelope, that calls for a full safety case review plus enduring arrangements, not a standing waiver.

## The disposal key policy statement

The source states the disposal policy in one sentence, quoted here from the key policy statement:

> During the Disposal/Termination Phase, the User must make sure that the capability is disposed of in a safe manner.

## Planning and running disposal

Disposal planning starts early, so that the design can avoid materials that are hazardous to dispose of and reduce dismantling hazards. Disposal in this sense ranges further than end of life: it includes the through-life disposal of prototypes, test articles, and consumables, and the unplanned disposal that follows an accident. Routes include sales, gifting, and dismantling. The User is normally accountable, supported by the disposal agency; submarines remain the exception noted in Chapter 4 of this pack.

The disposal SMP is agreed before the Out of Service Date and followed during the phase. Through-life safety documentation is archived and retained beyond the life of the system, under JSP 441, JSP 375 Volume 1 Chapter 39, and the relevant Defence safety regulations. Disposal safety requirements are reviewed, because a capability that has lived a long time may have outlasted the requirements written for it. The disposal agent, the Defence Equipment Sales Authority, is told the safety issues, including hazardous materials and legal constraints, before the route is agreed.

## Selling, gifting, and certification

When elements are sold or gifted, MOD takes the role of supplier, with legal duties to provide something safe to operate, together with the information to operate, maintain, and dispose of it. The item is sold in a condition acceptable for continued use, or the new owner is told the condition before they decide. Exemptions relied on in Defence use may not be available to the new owner, and the disposal agency must say so before purchase. Certification for safe disposal is identified and completed; disposing without it may be illegal.

The safety case is maintained through the phase for scrap, sale, and loan. For loan or continuing use, the contractual and legal obligations are confirmed so that any later compensation claim has a known boundary. Transfer to a museum or display is a change of use and potentially of operating authority. A Disposal/Termination safety case report is signed before final disposal and shared with the ASC if required. The User stays accountable until formal disposal, all disposal activity is risk-assessed, and the Safety Assurance Model continues. No normal submission arises during the phase; the Annex C evidence is the assurance guide, and the ASC can give independent advice if engaged early.
