# Chapter 5: Supervision, assurance, and approvals

## The supervision key policy statement

The source states the supervision policy in one sentence, quoted here from the Supervision key policy statement:

> The SRO/User must make sure that the safety risks associated with all activity using the military capability are assessed and mitigation measures are implemented to reduce the residual safety risks to a level that is ALARP and Tolerable.

## Supervision in practice

Risk assessments take the safety case as their baseline. Suitable and sufficient assessments are completed in line with the methodology of JSP 815 Volume 2 Element 4, which is named here and not reproduced, and they demonstrate that supervision and control measures stand between the hazards and the people, leaving residual risk ALARP and tolerable. The activity itself is then run in a safe manner and in accordance with the appropriate instructions.

Test and evaluation draws particular supervisory attention. At that point the safety case may still be incomplete and the operating characteristics of the capability may not be fully understood, so the gap between what is assumed and what is known is at its widest.

## The assurance key policy statements

The source sets out three assurance key policy statements, quoted here as they appear:

> The SRO/User must make sure that assurance arrangements are put in place that provide confidence that the programme will deliver and maintain a safe military capability.

> Dir DS must put in place arrangements to provide the MOD Head Office Approving Authorities with independent analysis and advice of programme safety in support of key programme decision points.

> Heads of Defence organisations must make sure arrangements are put in place to provide delegated Approving Authorities with independent analysis and advice of programme safety in support of key programme decision points.

## The three lines of defence

Acquisition safety assurance reaches a programme from three directions. The first line comes from inside the programme itself, at element and at programme level. The second line comes from outside it: from the SRO or User's own organisation, from the delivery organisation, and from DDS, with the ASC contributing functional approvals decision support. The third line is normally the DSA. Some programmes also face external assurance bodies beyond these lines: the NAO, the MPRG, and the IPA are the named examples.

Responsibility for programme assurance arrangements sits with the SRO/User. During procurement the SRO establishes and runs pan-DLOD safety assurance, which builds confidence and surfaces delivery risk; the User then draws on that same assurance when deciding whether to accept the capability. Once the capability is in service and on the path to disposal, the User maintains and implements the assurance, gaining confidence that operation and disposal follow the requirements and the safety case.

## The Safety Assurance Model

A Safety Assurance Model is established and implemented as part of safety management, and it may sit inside the SMP. The model shows how assurance is delivered across all of the programme's projects and DLODs, and across all three lines of defence. It also gives the SRO a view of when assurance was last delivered and where the gaps are. For independent safety assurance and advice, the source names Dstl and the IPA as options, and points at IET factfiles on independent safety assurance providers; those are named here and not reproduced.

## Approvals decision support

The approval route is established under JSP 655: the SRO works with the Defence Portfolio and Approvals Secretariat to set the route and agree the evidence the Approvals Decision Support Community will need. The ASC sits as a functional member of the Head Office community and backs Dir DS in the IAC safety advisor role; from independent analysis of the submission and its supporting safety evidence, the ASC feeds the approvals decision support reports.

Delegated approvals need the same kind of independent safety advice, and the Heads of Defence organisations provide it for their own approving authorities, scaled to the size, complexity, and risk of the programme. Internal governance must account for safety at every level of submission, not only at the top.

## Tailoring the evidence: PEAT and PEAP

The SRO consults the ASC to develop the safety lines of enquiry in the Programme Evidence and Assurance Tailoring tool before those lines are agreed into the Programme Evidence and Assurance Plan. Annex C of the source is the guide to the evidence typically required at the end of each phase. Every programme differs, and a programme may evidence a requirement another way where the ASC agrees; the SRO engages the ASC early to tailor and confirm. Once agreed, the evidence is provided to the ASC to support the submission.

## Safety across the five cases

Safety is addressed across all five cases of the approval route. In the Strategic case, safety appears in the case for change. In the Economic case, safety sits in the net value to society, including safety risk and what it costs, with JSP 507 named as the related guidance. In the Commercial case, safety is built into the deal through levers and incentives. In the Financial case, safety is inside the financial model in both directions: the effect of safety changes on the financial position, and the effect of financial changes on safety. In the Management case, safety has been included in the programme management and delivery plans. (The source's Management bullet is defective as printed; it is synthesized here rather than copied.)

## Review Notes and Information Notes

Review Notes cover approvals outside the three-stage SOC, OBC, and FBC cycle. Safety coverage is tailored per submission, and the starting point is that the safety implications of the recommendations are covered and the supporting information resembles that of the previous business case; a Review Note produced during Assessment, for example, resembles OBC evidence. The SRO should seek ASC advice as soon as a Review Note is identified.

Information Notes normally sit outside formal decision support; only an exception agreed with the SRO or a request from the approving authority changes that. Even so, the SRO should seek ASC advice early on what safety information the note should carry.
