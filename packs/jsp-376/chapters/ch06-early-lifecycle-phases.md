# Chapter 6: The early lifecycle phases

## The Pre-Concept Phase

The source states the Pre-Concept policy in one sentence, quoted here from the key policy statement:

> During the Pre-Concept Phase, the SRO must make sure that safety is formally considered as a factor when deciding whether and how to proceed with the programme, and as a candidate KUR for high risk and complexity programmes.

The emphasis of the phase is whether the requirement can, in principle, be met sufficiently safely. The artefacts that carry safety through Pre-Concept are mostly programme furniture. Safety is explicitly addressed in the Annual Budget Cycle option approval. The SRO appointment letter sets out safety responsibilities, and the Programme Mandate carries the safety assumptions, constraints, boundaries, and dependencies; both are revisited as the programme develops. The Programme Management Plan identifies how each element will be delivered, assigns safety responsibility for each element to a competent individual, attends to internal and external interfaces, and contains a safety section, with safety tasks placed in the integrated master schedule. Key safety stakeholders appear in the stakeholder map and engagement plan: the User, the ASC, delivery agents, DSA regulators, statutory regulators, certification bodies, and command safety management teams. The ASC is invited to initiation meetings, and the Risk and Complexity Assessment tool carries a safety assessment and its rationale, discussed with the ASC and revisited at each phase start and on any material DLOD change. Programme-level safety governance is established, including a safety risk register whose key risks also sit beside the other programme risks. Safety intent is described in draft High-Level Characteristics drawn from the concept documents, safety enters as a candidate KUR for high risk and complexity programmes, and a Safety Case Strategy sets out how the safety case will develop through life. The assurance approach is documented, including an initial Safety Assurance Model to be implemented from Concept, safety sits in the Integrated Assurance and Approvals Plan, and the PEAP safety lines of enquiry are agreed with the ASC.

The Strategic Outline Case closes the phase, and safety evidence is owed across all five cases. Strategically, safety sits in the draft concept of employment and the High-Level Characteristics, alongside accident and incident history from legacy and comparable systems where that is available. Economically, safety enters the options appraisal and the first performance-cost-time trade. Commercially, the evidence shows that potential suppliers can safely design, produce, support, or dispose of what they may be contracted to deliver. Financially, initial cost modelling includes safety costs and benefits. For management, the case describes how the programme will be run from a safety perspective: governance, resources, interfaces, exemptions that might be needed, and a schedule that contains the planned safety activities.

## The Concept Phase

The source states the Concept policy in one sentence, quoted here from the key policy statement:

> During the Concept Phase, the SRO must make sure that safety is included in the user requirement list and the alternative concepts are assessed from a safety risk perspective.

The solution may still be unknown or only a conceptual outline, so the focus is on establishing requirements and testing whether the capability can be delivered without putting MOD personnel, contractors, or the public at unacceptable risk. The SMP is developed and implemented with an eye toward Assessment. An Organisational Safety Assessment is formally considered, and where one is required it establishes a safety baseline of current capabilities with the User. Safety requirements enter the URD in support of the safety KUR, and safety is built into the down-select method. A T&E Strategy shows how the requirements, safety among them, will be evidenced, and flags the elements needing certification; where certification is required, a through-life Certification Strategy is agreed with the certification bodies. The safety case provides evidence that the requirements are captured in the URD, that key risks are understood, and that the safety conclusions for each option are recorded. The User is consulted, lessons from similar capabilities are analysed, and any experimentation or concept demonstration runs under a suitable and sufficient risk assessment, including contractor-led activity where Defence reputational risk is only indirect. The Safety Assurance Model is developed and implemented, the PEAP lines are agreed with the ASC, and the SRO signs an Outline Business Case safety case report and shares it with the ASC.

The Outline Business Case closes the phase, again with safety evidence across all five cases. Strategically, the URD captures the safety user requirements and the benefits map carries safety and its design drivers. Economically, the expected safety performance of each design option informs the recommendation, including a safety ranking of the options. Commercially, supplier engagement shows safety will be designed in, and government-to-government routes are recognised as a source of non-compliance with UK safety legislation and of missing safety evidence. Financially, cost modelling holds the safety risks that could move the programme inside the cost envelope. For management, the SMP with its safety delegations is in place, significant safety risks are quantified, the OSA baseline is complete, the certification strategy is agreed, and the case offers a first view of which risks are not tolerable and how they might be mitigated.

## The Assessment Phase

The source states the Assessment policy in one sentence, quoted here from the key policy statement:

> During the Assessment Phase, the SRO must make sure that the safety perspective is included in the option assessment and preferred option recommendation.

The questions now are how the safety objectives in the URD can be achieved and which design option is safer, and an option with a fundamental shortcoming against legal or policy requirements is identified early and not adopted. The SMP is maintained, with strong pan-DLOD governance, and is extended to cover Demonstration, with governance and reporting lines for T&E put in place. Where required, the Organisational Safety Assessment is completed with the User and provided as Full Business Case evidence. The CONEMP develops into the CONUSE, and the URD is refined into the System Requirements Document, which the SRO makes sure carries all applicable system safety requirements. Safety requirements go into the invitation to tender and are weighted in the assessment; the contract carries the programme's safety requirements, compliance with the appropriate safety Defence Standards included (Def Stan 00-056 is named here and not quoted), together with safety performance indicators and incentives. A Legislative Compliance Assessment tests whether the preferred option can meet the statutory requirements, or whether an exemption will be needed; where an exemption will be relied on the SRO formally confirms before FBC submission that the legislative conditions are met. A full ITEAP captures every safety requirement and its acceptance strategy, a Certification Plan is agreed with the certification bodies, and each option receives its own pan-DLOD safety analysis. The SRO signs a Full Business Case safety case report, the User endorses it, and it goes to the ASC showing how safety shaped the options analysis and that the preferred option can feasibly meet the requirements. Supplier demonstrations run under risk assessment, and the Safety Assurance Model is maintained.

The Full Business Case closes the phase, with safety evidence across all five cases. Strategically, the feasibility of the URD safety objectives is examined, all system safety requirements are identified and verified by design analysis into a mature SRD, and the technical solutions undergo safety assessment. Economically, the case gives the analytic rationale for the preferred approach and how it achieves the safety requirements. Commercially, the recommended supplier has shown the capability and resources to build and test a safe capability safely, legal issues are addressed, and commercial incentives for safety are in place. Financially, cost and schedule modelling includes safety risk and its schedule impact. For management, the SMP is updated with measures against the significant safety risks, the remaining risks are actively managed and funded, and the certification plan sits inside the ITEAP.
