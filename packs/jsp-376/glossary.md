# Glossary: jsp-376

Acquisition safety terms as the chapters and cheatsheet use them: expansions follow the source's Annex A abbreviations list, definitions follow the chapters, and each entry names the chapters that carry the detail.

- **AA (Approving Authority)**: The person or committee with authority to approve an investment decision, per JSP 655; where approvals are delegated, Defence organisations put equivalent measures in place and the terms AA and ASC cover both Head Office and delegated arrangements. → ch01, ch02
- **ABC (Annual Budget Cycle)**: The budgeting cycle in whose option approval safety is explicitly addressed during Pre-Concept, before the Strategic Outline Case. → ch06
- **acquisition safety**: The management principles and techniques applied to deliver a capability that is safe to operate and can be operated safely through procurement, operation, and disposal. → ch01, ch02
- **ALARP (As Low As Reasonably Practicable)**: The legal test that risks are assessed and reduced until further reduction would be grossly disproportionate to the benefit, with recognised good practice as the usual starting point and any cost-benefit comparison required to favour safety. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch07, ch08
- **ASC (Acquisition Safety Cell)**: The cell inside the Directorate of Defence Safety that provides approvals decision support on acquisition safety, advises SROs and Users on requirements, exemptions, and evidence tailoring, and receives the safety case report as submission evidence. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **BC (Business Case)**: The approval document at each investment decision point; the three formal cases (SOC, OBC, FBC) fall at the ends of Pre-Concept, Concept, and Assessment, and each carries safety inside it. → ch02, ch05, ch06
- **CADMID/T**: Concept, Assessment, Demonstration, Manufacture, In-Service, Disposal/Termination: the six-phase acquisition lifecycle, which with Pre-Concept makes the seven phases the JSP applies safety across. → ch02
- **CONEMP (Concept of Employment)**: The statement of how the capability will be employed, set early under Concepts and Doctrine, whose operating context is where capability requirements, and then safety requirements, stem from. → ch03, ch06
- **CONUSE (Concept of Use)**: The refinement of the Concept of Employment that describes use in service; the User keeps operation inside the defined method of use, envelope, and configuration it sets. → ch03, ch06
- **DDS (Directorate of Defence Safety)**: The directorate (Policy, Profession and Function) that owns Defence safety policy including JSP 815, owns the Defence SMS on behalf of the Secretary of State, and houses the ASC. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch05
- **DED (Disapplication, Exemption and Derogation)**: A provision that lifts or relaxes legislation's application to Defence, where the associated Defence safety regulation is followed; exemptions, waivers, and concessions from Defence safety policy or regulation remain case-by-case and SRO/User-sponsored. → ch03, ch04
- **DE&S (Defence Equipment and Support)**: One of the four enabling organisations set up as a delivery agent; it delivers programme elements, including through industry contracts, and produces much of the safety evidence while accountability stays with the SRO/User. → ch02
- **DESA (Defence Equipment Sales Authority)**: The disposal agent, whom the User informs of safety issues, including hazardous materials and legal constraints, before a disposal route is agreed. → ch08
- **Dir DS (Director of Defence Safety)**: The owner of this JSP, holder of Safety Functional Leadership for the Chief Operating Officer and owner of JSP 815, and permanent safety advisor to the IAC, who must put in place arrangements giving Head Office Approving Authorities independent analysis and advice on programme safety. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch05
- **DLOD (Defence Lines of Development)**: The eight development lines a programme spans: Training, Equipment, Personnel, Information, Concepts and Doctrine, Organisation, Infrastructure, and Logistics, with Interoperability overarching; safety is considered across all of them. → ch01, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch08
- **DSA (Defence Safety Authority)**: The independent organisation that leads health, safety, and environmental protection regulation in Defence, develops and manages Defence safety regulations inside the SMS that DDS owns, assures the Secretary of State through the Permanent Secretary, and normally provides the third line of assurance. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **Dstl (Defence Science and Technology Laboratory)**: The Defence science and technology agency, named as an option for independent safety assurance and advice; the source's Annex A does not list this abbreviation, and the expansion follows the agency's official name. → ch05
- **FBC (Full Business Case)**: The business case at the end of the Assessment phase; its submission evidence includes safety in the case, the FBC safety case report, and User consultation. → ch05, ch06, ch07
- **FOC (Full Operating Capability)**: The staged growth point beyond Initial Operating Capability at which the User understands the safety position at each stage, and after which a final OSA confirms the identified mitigations landed. → ch07, ch08
- **HLC (High-Level Characteristics)**: The top-level characteristics, drafted from the concept documents and shaped with the Requirements Oversight Committees, in which safety is considered and from which safety requirements flow. → ch02, ch03, ch06
- **IAAP (Integrated Assurance and Approvals Plan)**: The plan that integrates the programme's assurance and approvals activity, in which safety sits from Pre-Concept. → ch06
- **IAC (Investment Approvals Committee)**: The Head Office approving authority for investment decisions, for which Dir DS acts as safety advisor supported by the ASC; references to the IAC include the IAC(Nuclear). → ch01, ch02, ch05
- **IOC (Initial Operating Capability)**: The first in-service milestone, for which all information needed for safe operation is transferred in time for any further test and evaluation. → ch07
- **ISD (In-Service Date)**: The two-way agreement at which the SRO shows the in-service safety arrangements exist and the User accepts the residual risk; an ISD safety case report is produced before the declaration, and safety ownership passes to the User. → ch07
- **ITEAP (Integrated Test, Evaluation and Acceptance Plan)**: The plan through which required safety test and evaluation activity is delivered to provide objective evidence that programme safety requirements are met; it grows alongside the safety requirements and carries the certification effort. → ch04, ch06, ch07
- **KUR (Key User Requirement)**: A key requirement in the URD; safety enters as a mandatory candidate safety KUR for high-risk and complex programmes, and a decision not to have one must be justifiable at SOC and OBC. → ch03, ch06
- **LCA (Legislative Compliance Assessment)**: The assessment of compliance with legislation and regulation; it starts in Assessment, is updated through Demonstration, completes at Manufacture, and continues to be updated in service. → ch06, ch07, ch08
- **LoD ((Assurance) Lines of Defence)**: The three assurance directions: the programme itself (first), the SRO or User's organisation, delivery organisations, DDS, and the ASC (second), and normally the DSA (third), with external bodies such as the NAO, MPRG, and IPA beyond them. → ch05
- **must**: Where the policy says must, the action is a compulsory requirement to be completed by the actioner. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **must make sure**: Where the term is used, the action may be delivered by an assigned individual, but the actioner remains accountable for the actions being conducted. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **OBC (Outline Business Case)**: The business case at the end of the Concept phase; its evidence includes safety in the case, the OBC safety case report, User consultation, the programme SMP, and the OSA baseline. → ch02, ch03, ch05, ch06
- **OSA (Organisational Safety Assessment)**: The assessment, directed by JSP 375 Volume 1 Chapter 35, of the safety risks of a proposed organisational change and the controls that prevent adverse impact; the SRO must formally consider one during procurement and the User for in-service changes. → ch03, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **OSD (Out of Service Date)**: The date the capability leaves service; an OSD safety case report is produced during In-Service, ahead of the disposal safety case report. → ch08
- **PCT (Performance, Cost and Time)**: The three sides of the programme delivery envelope, with safety a key element of Performance; a change to one side can force changes to the others and so move safety. → ch02
- **PEAP (Programme Evidence and Assurance Plan)**: The plan into which the safety lines of enquiry are agreed after tailoring; once agreed, the evidence it fixes is provided to support the submission. → ch05, ch06
- **PEAT (Programme Evidence and Assurance Tailoring)**: The tailoring tool whose safety lines of enquiry the SRO develops with the ASC before they are agreed into the PEAP; the Director of Acquisition and Programme Delivery PEAT lines of enquiry confirm the specific evidence for each formal JSP 655 submission stage. → ch05
- **programme**: The pan-DLOD construct within which the change outcome is delivered; a project or element is a part the SRO may assign while remaining accountable. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **RCA (Risk and Complexity Assessment)**: The assessment tool that carries the programme's safety assessment and its rationale, discussed with the ASC and revisited at each phase start and on any material DLOD change. → ch06
- **SAM (Safety Assurance Model)**: The model, established as part of safety management and possibly sitting inside the SMP, that shows how assurance is delivered across the programme's projects and DLODs and across all three lines of defence; it continues through in-service and disposal. → ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **SC (Safety Case)**: A structured argument, supported by a body of evidence, that provides a compelling, comprehensible, and valid case that a system is safe for a given application in a given operating environment; developed from Pre-Concept, through-life and pan-DLOD, with the SRO accountable in procurement and the User in service and disposal. → ch04, ch07, ch08
- **SCR (Safety Case Report)**: The summary of the safety case argument at a point in time (claim, argument, and evidence), signed by the SRO at procurement decision points and by the User in service and disposal, and shared with the ASC as submission evidence. → ch03, ch04, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **SEST (Safety Evidence Summary Table)**: The source's Annex C table, an artefact guide mapping the evidence typically required at the end of each phase; carried as a grid in `cheatsheet.md` and cited by the phase chapters. → ch06, ch07, ch08
- **should**: Where the policy says should, the action is not a compulsory requirement but is considered recommended good practice. → ch01, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch07, ch08
- **SMP (Safety Management Plan)**: The plan, one per programme, describing how the SRO/User intends a capability to be safely delivered, operated, and disposed of in accordance with the parent organisation's SMS; it is updated as phases progress, and a disposal SMP is agreed with the disposal agency. → ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **SOC (Strategic Outline Case)**: The business case at the end of Pre-Concept; its evidence includes safety in the case for change, the safety case strategy, and the SOC safety case report. → ch02, ch03, ch05, ch06
- **SRD (Systems Requirement Document)**: The systems-level requirements document carrying safety requirements from Concept onward, alongside the URD; the source prints the expansion as Systems Requirement Document. → ch06, ch07
- **SRO (Senior Responsible Owner)**: The individual accountable for delivery of the programme, per JSP 655; early on a Capability Sponsor or Transformation Manager may fill the seat, and the SRO is accountable for a capability that is safe to operate until entry to service. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07
- **SRO/User**: The construction used where the action applies to either role; which one is accountable usually depends on the stage, SRO up to entry to service and User after, and where it is unclear they consult each other, with the DDS ASC advising. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05
- **T&E (Test and Evaluation)**: The activity that assures a capability is safe, contractually compliant, and does what the User needs; often the first real-life exposure of the capability, and itself hazardous because a test may seek out the working tolerance of the system. → ch02, ch04, ch06, ch07
- **TEPIDOIL**: The mnemonic for the DLODs: Training, Equipment, Personnel, Information, Concepts and Doctrine, Organisation, Infrastructure, and Logistics. → ch02
- **tolerability**: The judgement, wider than ALARP, of whether the remaining risk is acceptable; a risk can be reduced as far as practicable and still be intolerable to wider society, so the SRO/User considers both. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **URD (User Requirements Document)**: The document that must capture all safety user requirements and reflect the need for the capability to be both safe to operate and operated safely for a given application in a given operating environment; safety requirements sit under Performance in it. → ch03, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **User**: The role accountable for the capability being maintained safe to operate and operated safely in service and through disposal: suitable and sufficient risk assessment inside a safe system of work, residual risk communicated to operators, operation inside the defined method of use, and incidents reported and investigated; where there is more than one User, a Lead User is appointed. → ch01, ch02, ch03, ch04, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
