# Glossary: fda-med-device

Terms as FDA uses them in the pinned sources, with the chapters that carry the detail.

- **510(k)**: Premarket notification under FD&C Act section 510(k): the path for devices relying on substantial equivalence to a legally marketed predicate. Software and firmware changes to a 510(k)-subject device run the S9 decision sequence before a new filing is chosen. eSTAR is the mandatory authoring format since 2023-10-01. → ch01, ch10, ch12
- **CADe/CADx**: Computer-assisted detection or diagnosis software that analyzes medical images or signals. Assessing the clinical implications of an image, signal, or pattern fails CDS Criterion 1, so these functions stay devices. → ch11
- **CDS (clinical decision support)**: Software that supports or provides recommendations to a health care professional about prevention, diagnosis, or treatment. Non-Device CDS must meet all four criteria of FD&C Act section 520(o)(1)(E); failing any one keeps the function a device. → ch11
- **CGMP**: Current good manufacturing practice: the device quality-system requirements FDA enforces. Under the QMSR, 21 CFR part 820 carries those requirements with ISO 13485:2016 incorporated by reference. → ch05
- **Component (part 820)**: QMSR definition that expressly includes software and firmware intended to be included in the finished, packaged, and labeled device. A software version manufactured under essentially the same conditions can also be a batch or lot. → ch05
- **CSA (Computer Software Assurance)**: FDA's risk-based approach for establishing and maintaining confidence that production and quality management system software is fit for its intended use and stays in a validated state. Assurance effort scales with process risk, and unscripted testing (ad-hoc, error guessing, exploratory) is a legitimate activity where risk allows. CSA supersedes GPSV Section 6 only; device software still follows GPSV principles and S1 premarket documentation. → ch04
- **Cyber device (section 524B(c))**: Software (including firmware and programmable logic) validated, installed, or authorized as a device or in a device, with the ability to connect to the internet and technological characteristics that could be vulnerable to cybersecurity threats. Cyber devices carry statutory duties: a postmarket vulnerability plan, reasonable-assurance processes, and an SBOM. → ch06, ch07, ch09
- **CVD (coordinated vulnerability disclosure)**: The process for monitoring, receiving, assessing, and communicating about vulnerabilities and exploits over the lifecycle. A plan including CVD procedures is mandatory content for cyber devices under section 524B(b)(1). → ch06, ch07
- **De Novo**: Classification request under FD&C Act section 513(f)(2) for a device with no predicate, seeking Class I or II classification instead of remaining in automatic Class III. eSTAR is mandatory for De Novo requests from 2025-10-01, and later changes are judged under 510(k) rules when applicable. → ch01, ch12
- **DHF (design history file)**: The quality-system record holding the fuller design, coding, review, and change evidence behind a device. At Basic Documentation Level the SDS and Enhanced-only evidence typically stay in the DHF unless FDA asks for them. → ch02, ch08
- **Documentation Level (Basic or Enhanced)**: The risk-based depth of software content recommended in a premarket submission, set for the device as a whole. Enhanced applies when a failure or flaw of any device software function could present a hazardous situation with a probable risk of death or serious injury, assessed before risk controls and including compromise of functionality through inadequate cybersecurity; Basic applies everywhere else. Enhanced adds the SDS in the submission, fuller configuration-management and maintenance evidence, and unit/integration test protocols and reports. → ch02
- **eSTAR**: Electronic Submission Template And Resource: FDA's guided PDF template that structures 510(k) and De Novo electronic submissions with fixed sections, embedded logic, and designated homes for Software/Firmware and Cybersecurity/Interoperability content. Mandatory for 510(k)s since 2023-10-01 and De Novos since 2025-10-01; the "eSTAR Complete" status is the author's readiness gate before transmit. → ch12
- **FD&C Act section 201(h)**: The device definition: instruments, machines, implants, in vitro reagents, and similar articles intended for diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or function of the body. Regulated software documentation starts only after a function meets this definition and no exclusion applies. → ch01, ch08, ch11
- **FD&C Act section 520(o)**: The software-function exclusion provisions. Section 520(o)(1)(E) holds the four non-device CDS criteria, and 520(o)(2) keeps excluded functions inside multi-function products outside device regulation. → ch01, ch11
- **FD&C Act section 524B**: The statutory overlay for cyber devices: submitters of 510(k), PMA, PDP, De Novo, or HDE must provide a postmarket vulnerability and update plan (b)(1), processes providing reasonable assurance of cybersecurity (b)(2), and an SBOM (b)(3). Regular-cycle updates cover known unacceptable vulnerabilities; critical vulnerabilities that could cause uncontrolled risks get out-of-cycle updates as soon as possible. → ch06, ch07
- **GPSV (General Principles of Software Validation)**: FDA's guidance on lifecycle validation of device software: validation confirms by objective evidence that software specifications conform to user needs and intended use, with coverage scaled to complexity and safety risk and re-validation after change. Section 6 (automated process equipment and quality system software) is superseded by CSA; the remaining sections stay current for device software. → ch03
- **HCP (health care professional)**: The intended recipient of Non-Device CDS recommendations. Criterion 3 requires recommendations to an HCP without replacing or directing HCP judgment, and patient- or caregiver-facing decision support stays in device territory under this guidance's frame. → ch11
- **IBR (incorporation by reference)**: The regulatory mechanism that pulls a standard into the CFR by pointing at it. The QMSR incorporates ISO 13485:2016 as the QMS requirements body and ISO 9000:2015 clause 3 for vocabulary; the CFR references the standards, it does not reprint them. → ch05
- **IDE**: Investigational Device Exemption: the pathway for clinical study of a device, listed by S1 among the premarket submissions that carry device software documentation. Cybersecurity architecture views and labeling are specifically recommended for IDE submissions. → ch01, ch09
- **ISO 13485:2016**: The device QMS standard incorporated by reference into 21 CFR part 820. Holding a third-party certificate is not compliance: manufacturers must meet the applicable requirements as part of US CGMP, and FDA still inspects. → ch05
- **KEV catalog**: CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog. FDA expects known vulnerabilities associated with the device and its software components, including KEV entries, to be identified and given safety and security risk assessments with applicable controls. → ch07
- **MDDS (medical device data systems)**: Software solely intended to transfer, store, convert formats, or display medical device data and results, without analyzing or interpreting that data or controlling connected devices, is a Non-Device-MDDS function outside the device definition. Hardware doing the same jobs remains a device (Device-MDDS) under an enforcement-discretion posture. Active patient monitoring paths aimed at immediate clinical intervention are device functions. → ch01, ch08
- **NTIA minimum elements**: The baseline SBOM attributes from Framing Software Component Transparency (October 2021). Premarket SBOMs should be machine-readable and meet these elements, plus per-component software level of support and end-of-support date. → ch07
- **OTS / SOUP**: Off-the-shelf software: generally available components (industry also says SOUP) that a manufacturer uses without complete software life-cycle control, such as operating systems and printer or display libraries. The manufacturer keeps responsibility for continued safe device performance, and the evidence submitted scales with Documentation Level. → ch08
- **PCCP (predetermined change control plan)**: A plan under FD&C Act section 515C describing planned device changes that would otherwise need a new 510(k) or PMA supplement. Changes consistent with an authorized PCCP do not need a separate supplemental submission. → ch01
- **PMA**: Premarket Approval under FD&C Act section 515: the Class III path requiring reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness. Class III devices generally warrant Enhanced Documentation unless the sponsor justifies Basic with a detailed rationale. → ch01, ch02
- **Predicate**: The legally marketed device a 510(k) submission compares against to show substantial equivalence. For software changes, the comparison baseline is the manufacturer's own most recently cleared configuration, not a new predicate choice. → ch01, ch10
- **Pre-Submission (Q-Sub)**: A voluntary FDA feedback mechanism before filing. Sponsors may use it to get FDA input on the planned Documentation Level and documentation before the marketing submission. → ch02
- **QMSR (Quality Management System Regulation)**: 21 CFR part 820 as amended by the 2024 final rule (89 FR 7496), effective 2026-02-02. It incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference, adds FDA definitions and clause bridges to UDI, tracking, MDR, and advisory-notice programs, and binds devices automated with computer software to Design and Development Clause 7.3. Noncompliance adulterates the device under section 501(h). → ch01, ch05
- **SBOM (software bill of materials)**: The record of software components a device contains, maintained as a QMS configuration-management artifact and updated when marketed software changes. Premarket SBOMs are machine-readable, cover manufacturer-developed, purchased, open-source, and upstream components, and carry support level and end-of-support data per component; for cyber devices an SBOM is a statutory submission element. → ch06, ch07, ch09
- **SDS / SRS**: Software Design Specification and Software Requirements Specification, two of the S1 Table 1 elements. The SRS is recommended at both Documentation Levels; SDS detail is an Enhanced submission item and stays in the DHF at Basic. → ch02
- **SPDF (Secure Product Development Framework)**: A set of processes across design, development, release, support, and decommission that reduces the number and severity of vulnerabilities. Using an SPDF is one way to help satisfy QMSR expectations for cybersecurity-related design and risk work; other aligned frameworks can also satisfy them. → ch06, ch07, ch09
- **Substantial equivalence (SE)**: The 510(k) demonstration that a device is as safe and effective as a legally marketed predicate. Following the software-change guidance and filing a new 510(k) does not guarantee an SE determination. → ch01, ch10, ch12
- **Threat modeling**: The process of identifying security objectives, risks, and vulnerabilities across the medical device system and defining countermeasures across the lifecycle. It is system-scoped and continuous in design, with assumptions stated explicitly (FDA's example posture: treat hospital networks as hostile). → ch07
- **UDI**: Unique Device Identification, assigned under 21 CFR part 830. The QMSR bridges ISO 13485 Clause 7.5.8 to the UDI system and requires UDI in complaint, servicing, and batch or lot records. → ch05
- **Unresolved software anomalies**: Remaining software anomalies, disclosed with an impact evaluation on safety and effectiveness and aligned to the risk management plan. Anomalies also get a security-impact assessment, because a flaw that looks sporadic in normal use may be continuously exploitable under adversarial use. → ch02, ch07, ch09
- **Validation vs verification**: Verification confirms that outputs of a life-cycle phase meet that phase's requirements; validation confirms by objective evidence that software specifications meet user needs and intended use. Validation spans the lifecycle, and any software change reopens impact analysis and regression before the software is again considered validated. → ch03
