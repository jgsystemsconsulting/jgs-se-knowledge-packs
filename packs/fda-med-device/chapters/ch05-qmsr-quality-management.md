# Chapter 5: QMSR Quality Management

Sources: S4 Medical Devices; Quality System Regulation Amendments, 89 FR 7496 (published 2024-02-02, effective 2026-02-02), purpose, IBR of ISO 13485:2016 and Clause 3 of ISO 9000:2015, supplemental provisions, two-year implementation, enforcement framing; S4a FDA Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) overview page (content current as of 2026-02-02), applicability, IBR conflict rule, CGMP exemptions, QMSR inspection program change; S5 21 CFR Part 820 Quality Management System Regulation (eCFR text sourced from 89 FR 7523, Feb. 2, 2024, as amended), §§ 820.1, 820.3, 820.7, 820.10, 820.35, 820.45.

## Core Idea

The Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) is 21 CFR Part 820 after the February 2, 2026 effective date: FDA device CGMP rule rewritten so the foundation is ISO 13485:2016 incorporated by reference, plus a short set of FDA-only definitions, clause-to-U.S.-law bridges, and supplemental record and labeling controls. For device software organisations the practical meaning is not getting an ISO certificate and walking away. It is: document and run a QMS that meets the applicable requirements of ISO 13485 and the rest of part 820; treat risk management as a product-realization obligation under that system; apply Design and Development (Clause 7.3 and its subclauses) to class II, class III, and listed class I devices, including **devices automated with computer software**; keep complaint, servicing, UDI, and labeling records at the FDA-specified detail; and expect FDA inspections under the QMSR-aligned compliance program rather than legacy QSIT. Until 2026-02-02 the former QS regulation still governed; on and after that date the QMSR is the binding CGMP text. Failure to comply adulterates the device under section 501(h) of the FD&C Act.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **QMSR architecture (S4, S5):** Subpart A general provisions (§§ 820.1-820.10) plus Subpart B supplemental provisions (§§ 820.35, 820.45). Former QS regulation subparts C-O are removed and reserved.
- **Incorporation by reference (IBR) stack (§ 820.7):** ISO 13485:2016(E) for the QMS requirements body (approved for §§ 820.1, 820.3, 820.10, 820.35, 820.45); ISO 9000:2015(E) Clause 3 terms and definitions (approved for § 820.3). The standards remain copyrighted ISO publications; the CFR points to them, it does not reprint them.
- **Document-and-comply duty (§ 820.10(a)-(b)):** manufacturers subject to § 820.1(a) must document a QMS that complies with applicable ISO 13485 requirements and other applicable requirements of part 820, and must satisfy named U.S. regulatory links when fulfilling listed ISO 13485 clauses (UDI under part 830 for Clause 7.5.8; device tracking under part 821 for Clause 7.5.9.1 when applicable; MDR notification under part 803 for Clause 8.2.3; advisory notices under part 806 for Clauses 7.2.3, 8.2.3, and 8.3.3).
- **Design-and-development applicability gate (§ 820.10(c)):** Clause 7.3 and its subclauses bind class II and class III devices and specified class I devices, expressly including devices automated with computer software, plus the table-listed class I devices.
- **Life-supporting / life-sustaining traceability gate (§ 820.10(d)):** manufacturers of devices that support or sustain life, where failure under labeled use can reasonably be expected to cause significant injury, must also meet Clause 7.5.9.2 (Traceability for Implantable Devices) as applicable, on top of other part 820 duties.
- **Supplemental FDA record and labeling overlays (§§ 820.35, 820.45):** complaint-investigation fields, servicing-activity fields, UDI-in-records, confidentiality marking; labeling/packaging examination, release documentation, and mix-up prevention beyond Clause 7.5.1.
- **Transition clock (S4 DATES; S4a):** final rule published 2024-02-02 (89 FR 7496); effective and IBR approval date 2026-02-02 (two-year implementation). FDA rejected a phased effective date to avoid running two inspection programs at once.
- **Inspection program shift (S4a):** as of 2026-02-02 FDA uses Inspection of Medical Device Manufacturers Compliance Program 7382.850 and no longer uses QSIT-era 7382.845 or PMA inspection program 7383.001 for that purpose.

## Key Concepts

- **Why the rule exists (S4 SUMMARY / purpose).** FDA amended device CGMP requirements to harmonize and modernize part 820 by converging with the international device QMS consensus used by other regulatory authorities, incorporating by reference the device-specific QMS standard, and adding requirements and conforming edits so the IBR does not create inconsistencies with other FDA law. The qualitative goal is consistent regulation and timelier patient access to safe, effective, high-quality devices; the Agency also estimated large net cost savings from reduced dual-system burden.
- **What IBR means for a software organisation.** You must **comply with the obligations** of ISO 13485:2016 as they apply to your operations, because § 820.10 makes those applicable requirements part of U.S. CGMP. You do not satisfy the QMSR merely by holding a third-party certificate of conformance. FDA inspections do not issue ISO 13485 certificates, and an ISO 13485 certificate does not exempt a manufacturer from FDA inspection (S4 implementation responses; S4a).
- **Conflict hierarchy (S5 § 820.1(b); S4a).** Part 820 supplements other device regulations unless a more specific requirement says otherwise. If any clause of ISO 13485 conflicts with the FD&C Act or its other implementing regulations, the Act and those regulations control.
- **Scope: finished-device manufacturers (S5 § 820.1; S4a).** QMSR applies to finished devices intended for human use (including accessories treated as finished devices). It covers methods and controls for design, manufacture, packaging, labeling, storage, installation, and servicing. Component or part manufacturers are outside the mandatory scope but are encouraged to consider the regulation as appropriate; FDA retains inspection authority under the Act if needed. Blood and blood components for transfusion or further manufacturing follow subchapter F, not part 820. Certain HCT/Ps regulated as devices are in scope with part 1271 overlays.
- **Who is a manufacturer (S5 § 820.3(b)).** Any person who designs, manufactures, fabricates, assembles, or processes a finished device, including contract sterilization, installation, relabeling, remanufacturing, repacking, specification development, and initial distributors of foreign entities performing those functions. **Organization** in the incorporated vocabulary is tied to this manufacturer definition for part 820 purposes.
- **Software inside the definitions (S5 § 820.3).** **Component** expressly includes software and firmware intended to be included in the finished, packaged, and labeled device. **Batch or lot** can be a software version manufactured under essentially the same conditions. **Finished device** is any device or accessory suitable for use or capable of functioning, whether or not packaged, labeled, or sterilized. FDA preamble rejects games that treat not-final-form (for example, software insertion still pending) as a way to escape CGMP.
- **Definitions stack (S5 § 820.3).** Baseline terms come from ISO 13485 and Clause 3 of ISO 9000, except where § 820.3 says otherwise. Section 201 FD&C Act definitions (including device and labeling) supersede correlating ISO terms. Additional FDA terms (batch/lot, component, finished device, remanufacturer, and others) fill gaps. **Safety and performance** is tied to safety and effectiveness language in Clause 0.1 of ISO 13485 and does **not** relieve the manufacturer from controls that provide reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness.
- **The core QMS duty (§ 820.10(a)).** Document a quality management system that complies with applicable ISO 13485 requirements and other applicable requirements of part 820. Operations-limited manufacturers comply only with requirements applicable to the operations they perform (§ 820.1(a)).
- **Clause bridges to U.S. programs (§ 820.10(b)).** Meeting the named ISO 13485 clauses is not complete for FDA purposes until the linked Title 21 duties are met: UDI assignment system (part 830) for Clause 7.5.8; tracking procedures (part 821) when applicable for Clause 7.5.9.1; complaint reporting to FDA (part 803) for Clause 8.2.3; corrections and removals / advisory-notice handling (part 806) for Clauses 7.2.3, 8.2.3, and 8.3.3.
- **Design and development for software-automated devices (§ 820.10(c)).** Class II, class III, and listed class I devices must comply with Design and Development, Clause 7.3 and its subclauses. Class I devices automated with computer software are in that set even when not on the historical table. For a software-heavy product this is the regulatory hook that pulls design planning, inputs, outputs, review, verification, validation, transfer, and design changes into the mandatory QMS path. S6 later reads the same hook for cybersecurity-related design and risk work (ch06).
- **Risk management as an explicit product-realization obligation (S4 preamble; S4a; S6 clause pointers).** FDA own explanation of the rule stresses that incorporating ISO 13485 promotes consistency in design, production, and lifecycle controls and **now specifically requires risk management**. The preamble treats embedded risk-management concepts across ISO 13485 as making risk management throughout the total product life cycle more explicit, without changing FDA primary objective of reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness. S6 points software manufacturers to Subclause 7.1 (risk management in product realization) and Subclause 7.3.7 (design and development validation, including software validation) as the QMSR anchors for cybersecurity risk management and validation where appropriate. This chapter names those clause numbers as obligations; it does not quote ISO standard text.
- **Control of records overlay (§ 820.35).** In addition to Clause 4.2.5 (Control of Records), FDA requires structured content for certain records. Complaint records that are investigated (including MDR-reportable complaints and complaints the firm investigates anyway) must capture device name; date received; UDI/UPC and other IDs; complainant identity/contact; nature and details; correction or corrective action; reply to complainant. If a similar complaint was already investigated, document the justification for not re-investigating. Servicing records must capture device name, UDI/UPC, date, who serviced, service performed, and test/inspection data (Clause 7.5.4 path). UDI must be recorded for each device or batch (Clauses 7.5.1, 7.5.8, 7.5.9 context). Confidentiality marking is allowed to aid FDA public-information decisions.
- **Device labeling and packaging controls (§ 820.45).** In addition to Clause 7.5.1 production/service controls, manufacturers must document and maintain procedures detailing integrity, inspection, storage, and operations for labeling and packaging. Examine labeling/packaging for accuracy before release or storage where applicable (correct UDI/UPC or other ID, expiration date, storage and handling instructions, additional processing instructions). Document labeling release under Clause 4.2.5. Establish and maintain operations that prevent mix-ups, including inspection before use against the medical device file, with results documented under Clause 4.2.5. The preamble explains these FDA-only strengthens because labeling and packaging errors drive many recalls and FDA kept stronger inspection controls than the incorporated labeling language alone provided.
- **CGMP exemptions and IDE nuance (S4a).** Some devices are CGMP-exempt by classification regulation, but exemption does not remove complaint-file and general record duties under § 820.35. Devices under IDE are **not** exempt from design and development requirements under § 820.10(c) and ISO 13485 Design and Development Clause 7 and its subclauses.
- **Combination products (S4).** Conforming edits to 21 CFR part 4 clarify device QMS requirements for combination products without rewriting drug/biologic CGMP regimes.
- **Enforcement (S5 § 820.10(e); S4).** Noncompliance with any applicable part 820 requirement adulterates the device under section 501(h); the device and responsible persons are subject to regulatory action. Foreign devices offered for import remain subject to section 801(a) refusal when they appear adulterated.
- **Transition and enforcement context (S4 section VI; S4a).** Published 2024-02-02; effective 2026-02-02. Until the effective date, manufacturers complied with the former QS regulation. FDA kept a single cutover (no staggered class-based effective dates) so inspection programs would not fork. Implementation work FDA described includes IT updates, personnel training, finalizing the inspection approach, and assessing related regulations and documents. On 2026-02-02 the Agency began the 7382.850 inspection process and stopped QSIT (7382.845) and the listed PMA inspection program document for that transition. MDSAP participation and ISO certification status are not substitutes for FDA inspection authority; FDA indicated it would align MDSAP materials with the QMSR as a participating authority.
- **What this chapter is not.** It is not a substitute for purchasing ISO 13485:2016 or ISO 9000:2015. Clause numbers identify **where** obligations live; the binding wording of those clauses is in the IBR standards and must be read there. It is also not a premarket software documentation checklist (ch02) or a CSA method for production/QMS software tools (ch04).

## Mental Models

- **Foundation plus overlays.** ISO 13485:2016 is the QMS foundation by IBR; §§ 820.3, 820.10(b)-(d), 820.35, and 820.45 are the FDA overlays and bridges. Design your system to the foundation, then prove the overlays.
- **Certificate is not compliance.** Third-party ISO 13485 certification can be useful evidence of a system, but FDA judges CGMP compliance under part 820 and still inspects.
- **Software is in the finished-device story.** Software as component, software version as lot, and devices automated with computer software under Clause 7.3 are first-class QMSR facts, not side letters.
- **Clause number is a pointer, not a quotation.** Name Clause 7.3, 7.1, 4.2.5, 7.5.8, and the rest when tracing duties; open the standard for the actual requirement text.
- **One effective date, one inspection program.** The two-year clock was the transition plan; after 2026-02-02 the QMSR and 7382.850 are the operating reality.

## Anti-patterns

- **Quoting or paraphrasing ISO standard body text into SOPs from memory and calling it QMSR.** Use the purchased standard; this pack deliberately refuses ISO prose.
- **Treating MDSAP or ISO certificates as a shield against FDA inspection or Form FDA 483 findings.**
- **Skipping Clause 7.3 because the product is only class I while it is automated with computer software.**
- **Building a dual QMS (old part 820 narrative plus a separate ISO binder) after the effective date.** The rule point is one converged system.
- **Ignoring § 820.35 complaint and servicing field lists because ISO already has complaint handling.** FDA supplemental fields still bind.
- **Weak labeling/packaging inspection under the theory that Clause 7.5.1 is enough.** § 820.45 exists because FDA kept stronger labeling controls.
- **Assuming component suppliers are fully outside FDA reach.** They are outside mandatory QMSR scope, not outside all FD&C Act authority.
- **Reading safety and performance wording as a lower bar than safety and effectiveness.** § 820.3 says it is not.

## Key Takeaways

1. QMSR is part 820 effective 2026-02-02: ISO 13485:2016 IBR foundation plus FDA definitions, clause bridges, and supplemental record/labeling rules.
2. Manufacturers must document a QMS that meets applicable ISO 13485 and part 820 requirements; FD&C Act and specific device regulations win conflicts.
3. Devices automated with computer software (and other § 820.10(c) devices) must meet Design and Development Clause 7.3 and its subclauses.
4. Risk management in product realization and design/development validation (including software validation) are central QMSR hooks for software organisations; cybersecurity builds on them in ch06.
5. Complete named clauses only when UDI, tracking, MDR, and advisory-notice links in § 820.10(b) are also satisfied.
6. §§ 820.35 and 820.45 add mandatory complaint, servicing, UDI-in-record, and labeling/packaging controls on top of the incorporated clauses.
7. Noncompliance adulterates under section 501(h); inspections follow the QMSR-aligned program (7382.850), not QSIT.
8. Transition was publication 2024-02-02 to effectiveness 2026-02-02; no ISO standard text is reproduced in this pack.
