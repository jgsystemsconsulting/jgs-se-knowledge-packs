# Chapter 9: Premarket Cybersecurity Documentation Package

Sources: S6 Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality Management System Considerations and Content of Premarket Submissions (2026-02-03), §§ IV.D (submission documentation scaling), V (SPDF-generated artifacts as submission inputs), VI (labeling and cybersecurity management plans in the file), VII (section 524B documentation touchpoints), Appendix 2 (architecture-view submission notes), Appendix 3 (IDE), Appendix 4 and Table 1 (recommended premarket documentation elements and IDE scaling).

## Core Idea

S6 is both a secure-development playbook and a premarket packing list. This chapter is the packing list: how to structure the cybersecurity section of a premarket submission, which artifact types belong in the file, how depth scales with cybersecurity risk, and how IDE expectations differ from marketing submissions. The Secure Product Development Framework (SPDF), threat modeling method, SBOM field content, control selection, and postmarket management machinery are generated by design and QMS work described elsewhere in this pack (ch06 for QMS/SPDF processes, ch07 for technical premarket content). Here the job is assembly order and completeness: put a coherent cybersecurity risk management story in the file, attach the architecture and testing evidence that story needs, include labeling and management-plan material users and FDA rely on, and scale breadth to connectivity and harm potential without turning Appendix 4 into a checkbox ritual.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Submission documentation scales with cybersecurity risk (IV.D, Appendix 4)**: more connected, safety-critical, or widely interoperable devices need broader threat modeling, more architecture views, and richer controls and tests. A simple non-connected thermometer and a networked multi-patient system do not ship the same volume of cyber evidence, but both still face the same **types** of documentation when cybersecurity risk is in scope.
- **Appendix 4 / Table 1 packing list**: recommended premarket documentation types mapped to guidance sections, with a separate IDE column ("recommended" vs "could be helpful but not specifically recommended").
- **SPDF as the generator, submission as the slice**: an SPDF is a set of lifecycle processes that reduce number and severity of vulnerabilities. Using an SPDF is one way to satisfy QMSR expectations for cybersecurity-related design and risk work; the premarket file receives selected outputs, not the entire process archive.
- **Cybersecurity Risk Management Report as the spine**: threat model (often including architecture views), cybersecurity risk assessment, third-party/SBOM-related material, vulnerability assessment and software supportability, unresolved anomalies assessment, and traceability measures and metrics are commonly organized under or with that report.
- **Architecture Views package**: global system view; multi-patient harm view; updateability/patchability view; security use case views for functionality with safety risks (Appendix 2 details what those views should communicate).
- **Transparency block (VI)**: cybersecurity labeling for users, and cybersecurity management plans, sit in the submission as well as in the field-facing design.
- **Section 524B overlay for cyber devices**: statutory plans/procedures, reasonable-assurance processes, and SBOM obligations add binding documentation expectations on top of the general recommendations; ch06/ch07 carry the duty detail, this chapter notes where those artifacts land in the file.

## Key Concepts

- **Who this package is for.** S6 premarket documentation recommendations apply to premarket submissions for devices with cybersecurity considerations, including but not limited to statutory cyber devices. Cybersecurity is part of safety and effectiveness review (including substantial equivalence analysis in the 510(k) context). Inadequate cybersecurity labeling can support misbranding concerns under section 502 when directions for use or safety claims fail.
- **Not a bare checklist.** Appendix 4 states Table 1 is one possible organization, not a deliverable checklist divorced from the processes in Sections V–VI. Reviewers read a risk story; artifacts should interlock.
- **Cybersecurity Risk Management Report (Sections V, VI.B).** Central narrative: how risks were identified, assessed, controlled, and will be monitored. May house or point to the threat model, risk assessment, SBOM-related support, vulnerability and supportability analysis, unresolved security anomalies, and traceability measures.
- **Threat model (V.A.1, with ties to V.A.3–V.A.5, V.B.2, Appendices 1–2).** Submission-facing threat model shows assets, adversaries, interfaces, and abuse paths across the system in which the device operates. Breadth scales hard with connectivity. Full method and examples belong in ch07; the package rule is: include a threat model that matches system scope, and let architecture views either live inside it or travel beside it.
- **Cybersecurity risk assessment (V.A.2 and related).** Links threat scenarios to safety and effectiveness impact and to risk controls. Distinct from, but coordinated with, general medical-device risk management.
- **SBOM (V.A.4; section 524B(b)(3) for cyber devices).** Software bill of materials identifying commercial, open-source, and off-the-shelf components. For cyber devices an SBOM is a statutory submission element; for other devices S6 still treats SBOM documentation as part of the recommended third-party software story. Element definitions and maintenance process live in ch07/ch06; here: put the SBOM (or clearly referenced SBOM package) in the premarket cyber section.
- **Vulnerability assessment and software supportability (V.A.4, VI.A).** Evidence that known vulnerabilities and support windows were considered, including third-party components, and that update/patch expectations are credible.
- **Unresolved anomalies assessment (V.A.5).** Security-relevant unresolved anomalies, impact, and disposition, coordinated with the general software anomaly list in the S1 package (ch02) when both apply.
- **Traceability measures and metrics (V.A and related).** How security requirements, risks, controls, architecture, and tests connect. Call out the method; do not assume reviewers will reverse-engineer links.
- **Architecture views (V.B.2, Appendix 2).** At marketing depth, expect global, multi-patient harm, and updateability/patchability views, plus security use case views for safety-risk functionality. Low-complexity devices may need only a single view in each major category; high-complexity networked or cloud-backed devices often need multiple views per category. Appendix 2 covers diagrams and information details for a view.
- **Security requirements and control implementation evidence (V.B.1, Appendix 1).** Requirements and implemented controls (authentication, authorization, cryptography, integrity, confidentiality, logging, resiliency, update mechanisms, and related categories in Appendix 1) should be findable from the architecture and risk story. Control catalogs and design guidance are ch07 material; the package must show they exist and map to risks.
- **Cybersecurity testing (V.C).** Security testing evidence that design and development activities worked: the submission carries plans/results at a depth matching risk. Method detail is ch07; assembly rule is include the testing record the risk story relies on.
- **Labeling (VI.A).** Premarket labeling content that informs users about connectivity, cybersecurity risks, update and patch expectations, and transferred risk. Usability/human-factors consideration applies when users must perform security tasks. IDE submissions still carry informed-consent and general cybersecurity labeling expectations per Appendix 4.
- **Cybersecurity management plans (VI.B).** Plans for vulnerability intake, assessment, disclosure, and updates over the lifecycle. For cyber devices these plans interlock with section 524B(b)(1)–(2) process duties (ch06). In the file: include the plan (or submission-appropriate summary plus controlled full plan) so FDA can see postmarket readiness, not only day-zero design.
- **IDE scaling (Appendix 3 and Appendix 4 IDE column).** Architecture views and labeling are specifically recommended for IDE; many risk-report internals, full management plans, and some assessments are "could be helpful but not specifically recommended." Do not assume marketing-file bulk is required on day one of an IDE, and do not assume IDE thinness is enough for a 510(k)/De Novo/PMA.
- **524B documentation touchpoints (VII.C).** For cyber devices, plans and procedures (b)(1), processes providing reasonable assurance of cybersecurity (b)(2), and SBOM (b)(3) are statutory. S6's recommended elements help show those duties; missing SBOM or empty management planning is not a documentation style choice for a cyber device.
- **Coordination with the S1 software package (ch02).** Cybersecurity documentation sits beside, not inside, the Basic/Enhanced device-software package. Shared themes (unresolved anomalies, architecture, risk) should not contradict across sections. eSTAR provides designated software and cybersecurity homes (ch12).

## Mental Models

- **Generator vs binder.** SPDF and QMS activities generate evidence (ch06). ch07 defines what good threat models, SBOMs, controls, and tests contain. This chapter binds selected outputs into a reviewable section.
- **Risk scales breadth, not element types.** Appendix 4: every documentation **type** remains in play for devices with cybersecurity risk; threat modeling and architecture views are where page count and diagram count grow first.
- **Report spine, views ribs, tests legs, labeling face.** Risk management report holds the argument; architecture views show structure; testing shows the argument survives contact with analysis and attack surface; labeling and management plans show the argument survives contact with users and time.
- **IDE is a thinner cut of the same animal.** Same taxonomy, fewer "recommended" cells. Plan the marketing package early so IDE artifacts are upward-compatible.
- **Cyber device is a duty flag, not a separate template.** 524B adds must-submit items and standing processes; it does not replace Table 1.

## Anti-patterns

- **Shipping a control catalog with no threat model or risk assessment.** Controls without a risk story read as decoration.
- **One network cloud diagram reused as every architecture view.** Global, multi-patient harm, patchability, and security use cases answer different questions.
- **SBOM as a spreadsheet orphan.** Components must connect to vulnerability handling, supportability, and update plans.
- **Marketing-file silence on management plans.** Transparency section VI is part of premarket content, not only a postmarket binder.
- **Copying device software V&V and labeling it cybersecurity testing.** Security testing has its own scope in S6 Section V.C; general software tests may not exercise adversarial assumptions.
- **Contradicting ch02 anomaly or architecture statements.** Reviewers read both sections.
- **Treating Appendix 4 as pass/fail tick boxes with empty processes behind them.** S6 rejects checklist-only reading.
- **Dumping entire QMS procedures instead of submission-appropriate reports.** Process depth belongs in ch06 evidence retention; the file needs navigable, device-specific cybersecurity documentation.

## Key Takeaways

1. Structure the premarket cybersecurity section around Appendix 4 element types: risk management report (with threat model, risk assessment, SBOM-related material, vulnerability/supportability, unresolved anomalies, traceability), architecture views, security requirements/controls, cybersecurity testing, labeling, and cybersecurity management plans.
2. Scale breadth with cybersecurity risk and system complexity; keep element types in play even for lower-risk connected devices.
3. Use architecture views (global, multi-patient harm, updateability/patchability, security use cases) as first-class submission artifacts, detailed per Appendix 2.
4. For IDE, prioritize recommended architecture views and labeling; treat other Table 1 cells as optional helpers unless your risk story needs them.
5. For cyber devices, section 524B makes plans/procedures, reasonable-assurance processes, and SBOM binding; align the package to those duties.
6. Keep this package consistent with the S1 software documentation package (ch02) and place both in their eSTAR homes (ch12).
7. Do not reinvent SPDF process or SBOM field definitions here: ch06 owns QMS/SPDF process, ch07 owns technical content depth.
