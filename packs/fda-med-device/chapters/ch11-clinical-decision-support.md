# Chapter 11: Clinical Decision Support

Sources: S7 Clinical Decision Support Software (2026-01-29), §§ II–V (four statutory criteria under section 520(o)(1)(E), criterion-by-criterion interpretation, Non-Device CDS and device software function examples, Criterion 4 transparency and time-criticality).

## Core Idea

Clinical decision support is not automatically a device and not automatically outside FDA oversight. Section 520(o)(1)(E) of the FD&C Act excludes a software function from the device definition only when it meets **all four** statutory criteria. Fail any one criterion and the function remains a device (unless another exclusion applies). Non-Device CDS, in FDA's working label, is decision-support software aimed at health care professionals (HCPs) that clears that four-part gate: it does not acquire/process/analyze medical images, IVD signals, or patterns/signals from a signal acquisition system; it displays, analyzes, or prints medical information; it supports or provides recommendations to an HCP about prevention, diagnosis, or treatment without replacing or directing HCP judgment; and it enables the HCP to independently review the basis for those recommendations so the HCP is not intended to rely primarily on them. Time-criticality and automation level sit inside Criterion 4, not as a separate fifth test. Pathway and Documentation Level questions for functions that remain devices are ch01 and ch02.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Four criteria of section 520(o)(1)(E)**: (1) not intended to acquire, process, or analyze a medical image or a signal from an IVD or a pattern or signal from a signal acquisition system; (2) intended to display, analyze, or print medical information about a patient or other medical information; (3) intended to support or provide recommendations to an HCP about prevention, diagnosis, or treatment of a disease or condition; (4) intended to enable that HCP to independently review the basis for the recommendations so the HCP is not intended to rely primarily on them for an individual patient's diagnosis or treatment decision.
- **Criterion 1 vs Criterion 2 input split**: Criterion 1 names device-side inputs (images, IVD signals, patterns/signals from acquisition systems); Criterion 2 names Non-Device CDS inputs (medical information such as demographics, symptoms, certain test results, discharge summaries, guidelines, peer-reviewed studies, labeling, agency recommendations).
- **Criterion 3 recommendation shape**: condition-, disease-, and/or patient-specific information and options that enhance, inform, or influence an HCP decision; not a specific preventive, diagnostic, or treatment output or directive; not intended to replace or direct HCP judgment. Lists, prioritized lists, and follow-up/next-step option lists can qualify when they stay recommendations.
- **Criterion 4 independent-review package**: purpose/intended use (HCP user and patient population); required inputs and how to obtain them; plain-language algorithm development and validation summary; patient-specific outputs and knowns/unknowns. Automation level and time-critical nature of the decision feed whether independent review is realistic.
- **Single clinically appropriate option**: when only one option is clinically appropriate and the function otherwise meets all four criteria, FDA intends to exercise enforcement discretion; time-critical or image/signal-driven variants of the same story stay devices.

## Key Concepts

- **All four criteria, every time.** Non-Device CDS is an exclusion, not a product category brand. Software can look like "CDS" in marketing and still be a device if it fails Criterion 1 (image/signal/pattern analysis), Criterion 3 (directive output), or Criterion 4 (no real independent review path).
- **Criterion 1, excluded inputs.** Medical images include imaging-system outputs and images acquired or later processed for a medical purpose. Signals include IVD electrochemical/photometric responses used to generate clinical results and parameters measured by body-attached or external acquisition systems (ECG generation, streaming physiologic measurement, radiology image generation, and similar). Patterns are multiple, sequential, or repeated measurements of a signal (ECG waveform/QRS, NGS sequence/variant datasets, CGM repeated glucose values). Discrete point-in-time vitals at ordinary encounters generally are not, by themselves, a pattern. Assessing clinical implications of an image, signal, or pattern fails Criterion 1. CADe/CADx-style image work, arrhythmia detection on ECG waveforms, NGS variant interpretation, and assay-signal-to-result computation are device-side examples.
- **Criterion 2, medical information inputs.** Display, analyze, or print patient-specific medical information and/or other medical information from well-understood, accepted sources. Device results used consistent with FDA-required labeling can count as medical information once Criterion 1 is satisfied (for example a single lab glucose value in the EHR). Continuous sampling of the same measurand can be a pattern and fail Criterion 1. Sole display/print under other 520(o) provisions is not CDS; this criterion is about decision-support that uses medical information.
- **Criterion 3, recommendations to an HCP.** Outputs should present options or informational support the HCP can take into a decision with other knowledge. Specific directives (the one treatment plan the software generates as the answer, a definitive diagnosis from signal-level inputs, an immediate escalation order) fail Criterion 3. Patient- or caregiver-facing decision support that is not for an HCP meets the device definition under this criterion's HCP addressee; FDA points to existing digital-health policies for non-HCP products rather than this exclusion.
- **Enforcement discretion for a single clinically appropriate recommendation.** Examples FDA describes (cardiovascular risk estimate from ordinary clinical inputs for HCP consideration; cognitive-impairment treatment plan for HCP review/revision; antibiotic suggestion from symptoms and exposure history; radiology-report summary from the radiologist's findings; differential diagnosis from symptoms/vitals/labs; chronic low-back-pain care pathway; longer-horizon transplant risk estimates) stay in the discretion bucket only with HCP review and without time-critical or image/signal/non-validated-input add-ons. Add PET analysis, spectroscopy diagnosis, 24-hour event prediction, trauma/emergent pathway timing, or near-real-time physiologic escalation and the function is a device FDA intends to oversee.
- **Criterion 4, independent review in practice.** Labeling or software should state purpose, intended HCP, and population; identify inputs, relevance, and data-quality needs; describe in plain language the general approach (meta-analysis, expert panel, statistical model, AI/ML, and so on), the data relied on (representativeness, development vs validation practice), and validation results/limitations (including weak sub-populations); and surface patient-specific information plus missing/corrupted/unexpected inputs. Sources should be identifiable and understandable to the intended HCP. Usability testing may be needed to show the implementation actually enables independent review. **FDA does not consider software intended for a critical, time-sensitive task or decision to meet Criterion 4**, because the HCP is unlikely to have time to review the basis.
- **Automation bias and time-criticality.** Automation bias is over-reliance on automated suggestion (commission or omission errors). Urgency raises that bias because there is less time to consider other information. FDA therefore reads Non-Device CDS as software that still lets the HCP review the basis and not rely primarily on the output. High automation plus little time cuts against Criterion 4 even when the UI looks "transparent."
- **Worked Non-Device CDS shapes (still need Criterion 4).** Evidence-based order sets; matching patient record data to practice guidelines or peer-reviewed literature; contextually relevant reference information consistent with labeling or guidelines; drug-drug, drug-allergy, and drug-disease interaction notifications from up-to-date accepted sources; formulary lists and alerts; duplicate testing or medication-error prevention flags; preventive-care or order reminders; discharge/report summaries assembled for HCP use; lists or prioritized lists of preventive/diagnostic/treatment options; follow-up or next-step option lists; identification that a patient falls in an indicated population for an approved agent from record-level medical information. Criterion 4 examples emphasize non-time-critical ESRD treatment-option lists from guidelines, prioritized chemotherapeutic options from record demographics/diagnosis, and COPD step-count follow-up options where the HCP has time and training to read the basis.
- **Worked device CDS / device software shapes.** Image-set treatment planning, image manipulation for device use, CT interpolation into a treatment directive, multi-signal time-critical diagnostic outputs, skin-lesion malignant/benign calls from images, ECG or waveform acquisition/analysis for diagnosis or time-critical treatment, NGS pattern analysis for clinical variants, continuous or high-frequency physiologic pattern analysis that yields a specific diagnostic or treatment directive, and non-HCP time-critical decision tools. Many fail Criteria 1 and 3 together; time-critical or opaque-basis tools fail Criterion 4 even when inputs look like medical information.
- **Statutory backstops.** Software described in 520(o)(1)(E) is not excluded if it meets section 513(a)(1)(C) criteria, if it is used in manufacture and transfusion of blood and blood components to assist disease prevention, or if HHS issues a final order that the function would be reasonably likely to have serious adverse health consequences. Multi-function products still separate device functions from excluded functions under section 520(o)(2) (ch01).
- **Device CDS after the gate fails.** Functions that remain devices take ordinary premarket pathways (ch01) and software Documentation Level Basic or Enhanced (ch02). S7 also points to IMDRF SaMD risk-categorization thinking for additional risk framing of regulated software functions; it does not replace the four statutory criteria.

## Mental Models

- **Exclusion gate, not a CDS badge.** Marketing "CDS" is irrelevant; the four criteria are the test.
- **Inputs first.** Image/signal/pattern in → Criterion 1 fails → device. Medical information in → keep walking Criteria 2–4.
- **Options vs orders.** Lists and prioritized options can be Non-Device CDS; a single directed answer usually is not (discretion only in narrow, non-time-critical, otherwise-qualifying cases).
- **Time is part of Criterion 4.** If the clinical moment leaves no room to read the basis, independent review fails even with a perfect methods appendix.
- **Transparency is necessary, not sufficient.** Plain-language methods plus bad timing or high automation still fail the "not rely primarily" intent.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating any EHR alert as Non-Device CDS without walking all four criteria.** Interaction checkers can qualify; arrhythmia engines and image CADx cannot on Criterion 1 alone.
- **Hiding image or signal analysis behind a "recommendation" label.** Criterion 1 does not care about the output chrome.
- **Shipping a single definitive diagnosis or treatment directive to the HCP and calling it support.** That fails Criterion 3 (and often 4).
- **Omitting basis, sources, validation limits, or input quality needs.** Criterion 4 is about enabling independent review, not dumping a black-box score.
- **Time-critical crash-cart or OR escalation tools claiming Non-Device CDS via a methods PDF.** FDA's Criterion 4 reading rejects critical time-sensitive tasks.
- **Patient/caregiver apps assuming the HCP-only exclusion.** Criterion 3's addressee is an HCP; non-HCP CDS stays in device territory under this guidance's frame.
- **Skipping Documentation Level and pathway work after correctly concluding the function is a device.** Non-exclusion means ordinary device software rules (ch01, ch02).

## Key Takeaways

1. Non-Device CDS requires meeting all four section 520(o)(1)(E) criteria; one miss keeps the function a device.
2. Criterion 1 bars acquire/process/analyze of medical images, IVD signals, and patterns/signals from acquisition systems.
3. Criterion 2 requires display/analyze/print of medical information (patient-specific and/or other accepted medical information).
4. Criterion 3 is HCP-facing recommendations that inform decisions without replacing or directing HCP judgment; specific directives fail.
5. Criterion 4 requires an independent-review path (purpose, inputs, methods/data/validation in plain language, patient-specific knowns/unknowns); time-critical tasks fail it.
6. Automation bias rises with urgency; high automation plus little review time undercuts Non-Device CDS claims.
7. Order sets, guideline matching, labeling-consistent references, interaction checks, formulary and duplicate-order tools, reminders, summaries, and option lists are common Non-Device shapes when Criterion 4 also holds.
8. Image/signal analysis, definitive diagnostic or treatment outputs, and time-critical decision tools are common device shapes.
9. Narrow enforcement discretion can cover a single clinically appropriate recommendation that otherwise meets the criteria and is not time-critical or signal/image-driven.
10. Regulated device CDS still needs pathway selection (ch01) and Documentation Level Basic/Enhanced (ch02).
