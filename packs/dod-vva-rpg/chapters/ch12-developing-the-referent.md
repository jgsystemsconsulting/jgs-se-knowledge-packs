# Chapter 12: Developing the Referent

## Core Idea
Validation is comparison: M&S, its data, and its results are judged against a **validation referent** — the best *adequate* codified body of information about the simuland for the intended use. Weak, gold-plated, or unspecified referents make accreditation decisions non-defensible.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Development referent vs validation referent**: the same word covers (a) knowledge used to *build* the M&S and (b) the standard used to *assess* it. This chapter is about the assessment/validation referent.
- **Expanded validation-referent definition**: the most appropriate available, organized information describing characteristics and behavior of the represented reality, chosen from the perspective of validation for a stated intended use.
- **Identify → select → specify → approve → use**: find candidate information; choose by direction, convenience, economics, proxy, coverage, community acceptance, or a mix; describe domain, attributes, and uncertainties; obtain Accreditation Authority acceptance; then compare data-rich, data-poor, or inadequate referents honestly.
- **Adequacy triad**: (1) “best possible” may be unaffordable — use an *adequate* referent for this use; (2) referent fidelity must exceed the fidelity you claim for the M&S; (3) pick the cheapest set that still has that fidelity (do not gold-plate).

## Key Concepts
- **Information kinds**: observations/tests, theories/algorithms (preferably already checked against the simuland), results from independently validated simulations, SME estimates, or combinations. Quality varies across and inside a referent.
- **Codified and authoritative**: organize sources; prefer credible authorities; expect gaps and contradictions and record how they are reconciled.
- **Simuland coverage**: actors/systems/entities, processes, interactions, and environments — including the human operator when the M&S is interactive.
- **Selection drivers**: direction (mandated source), convenience (what is already in hand), economics, proxy (stand-in phenomenon), coverage extent, and community acceptance. Combine them; do not hide a convenience choice as “community standard.”
- **Specification contents**: domain coverage and overlap; attribute distinctiveness (only decision-driving traits, with their variations); parameter uncertainty on the *referent* side as well as the M&S side; coherence across redundant/supplementary/disjoint sources.
- **Use classes**: data-rich (statistical comparison possible), data-poor (theory/SME fill gaps; inference must match intended use), inadequate (cannot support the claimed use — constrain or reject, do not pretend).
- **Authority**: the Accreditation Authority accepts the referent. Vague intended use inflates Type I/II decision risk.

## Mental Models
- **You cannot out-validate your referent**: claimed M&S fidelity cannot exceed the fidelity of what you compared against.
- **Adequate ≠ sloppy**: schedule/cost may force less than laboratory-best data; they do not excuse an unspecified or contradictory body of knowledge.
- **Emergent or future simulands iterate**: if the M&S creates new knowledge or portrays hard-to-predict futures, the referent specification itself may have to iterate — and that hazard must be visible to the Authority.

## Anti-patterns
- Redefining “validation” as “comparison to high-quality test data only,” then discarding SME/theory cases that DoD validation still covers (label those *data-based validation* if you must narrow).
- Using a development referent (what we built from) as if it were an independent assessment referent.
- Selecting by convenience while advertising community acceptance.
- Comparing results to a referent whose uncertainty was never characterized, then blaming all residual error on the M&S.
- Gold-plating a ballpark-estimate use with a full test-data campaign.

## Key Takeaways
1. Name the validation referent explicitly; do not treat “the real world” as a source you already have.
2. Select and specify it for intended use: coverage, attributes, uncertainties, and source reconciliation.
3. Referent fidelity must beat claimed M&S fidelity; spend only what adequacy requires.
4. Authority approval of the referent is part of accreditation, especially when the referent is data-poor or partial.
