# Chapter 6: Accreditation Agent's Role in VV&A of New Simulations

## Core Idea
The Accreditation Agent assembles and assesses the accreditation case—intended use, evidence, limitations, and residual risk—so the Accreditation Authority can accept, accept with constraints, or reject use of the M&S.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Accreditation package**: structured evidence dossier, not a single certificate without context.
- **Recommendation → decision**: Agent recommends; Authority decides (roles must stay distinct in accountability).
- **Constraints as first-class outcomes**: accredited for use only under stated conditions, configurations, and data regimes.

## Key Concepts
- **Accreditation criteria**: derived from User intended use and acceptability thresholds.
- **Evidence completeness**: V&V results, CM identity, data pedigree, prior accreditations (if any), and known defects.
- **Gap analysis**: what is untested, weakly validated, or extrapolated beyond the referent.
- **Residual risk statement**: what could still go wrong if the M&S is used as proposed (ch10).
- **Re-accreditation triggers**: intended-use change, major configuration change, failed operational feedback, expired assumptions.

## Mental Models
- **Decision under uncertainty**: accreditation is risk acceptance with eyes open, not proof of perfection.
- **Configuration lock**: the accredited article is a versioned system, including key data and environment assumptions.
- **Trace the claim**: every accreditation claim maps to evidence or an explicit residual risk.

## Anti-patterns
- Accrediting “the model” generically without intended use.
- Equating “V&V complete” with “accredited.”
- Hiding limitations in appendices the Authority never sees.
- Reusing old accreditation letters after silent software or data changes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Accreditation is an official fitness-for-use decision supported by a coherent package.
2. Constraints and residual risk are legitimate, often necessary, outcomes.
3. Configuration and intended-use identity define what the decision covers.
4. Re-accreditation is part of lifecycle management, not failure.
