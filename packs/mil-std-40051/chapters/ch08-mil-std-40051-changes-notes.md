# Chapter: Change packages, revisions, and notes

## Core Idea

Change control and the non-mandatory notes section close the standard practice. Section 4 carries the mechanics for change markings and revisions. Section 6 holds intended use, acquisition guidance, tailoring pointers, and keyword listing. Together they keep fielded manuals aligned with the configuration the acquiring activity bought.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Changes and revisions

- **Change packages.** Mark changed content so users can see what moved between issues. Structured change references link related objects.
- **Revision implementation.** A revision is a controlled replacement path, not an informal rewrite. Title-page and supersession data must track the revision state.
- **ETM/IETM visibility.** Lists of effective pages or work packages, and change-transmittal material where required, keep electronic deliveries as accountable as paper.

### Section 6 notes (non-mandatory)

Section 6 is explicitly general or explanatory and not mandatory. It still matters for acquisition hygiene:

1. **Intended use.** The standard prescribes requirements for technical publication types and their revisions.
2. **Acquisition requirements.** Cite the title, number, and date of the standard, and include a filled-out content selection matrix.
3. **Tailoring guidance.** The acquiring activity, with user representatives, tailors options through Appendix A.
4. **Subject terms.** Keyword listing supports discovery and cataloging; it is not a second requirements set.

## Key Concepts

- **Effectivity is user-visible.** If a maintainer cannot tell which WP is live, the change system failed.
- **Matrix rides the contract.** Notes restate that acquisition documents should carry the filled matrix with the standard citation.
- **Revision versus change.** Changes update an issue; revisions re-baseline. Both need markings and notices consistent with section 5 front-matter rules.
- **No sample change-page images.** This pack describes the control idea. It does not ship scanned change sheets.

## Mental Models

- **Configuration first.** Treat every fielded TM as a configuration item with change history.
- **Notes guide buyers, not authors rewriting shall statements.** Section 6 helps acquisition; it does not soften mandatory sections 4 and 5.
- **Tailoring is recorded.** If it is not in the matrix and the contract trail, it is not tailored.

## Key Takeaways

- Change markings and revision rules live primarily in the general-requirements spine.
- Section 6 restates intended use, acquisition citation, and Appendix A tailoring.
- Always pair the standard citation with a filled content selection matrix in acquisition documents.
- Electronic deliveries still need effective-page/WP accountability.
- Sample change images stay out of this pack.
