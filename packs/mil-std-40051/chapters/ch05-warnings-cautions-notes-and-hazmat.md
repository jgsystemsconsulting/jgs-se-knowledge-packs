# Chapter 5: Warnings, Cautions, Notes, and HAZMAT Presentation

## Core Idea
Warnings, cautions, and notes are controlled information types with format, placement, and HAZMAT iconography rules. They protect personnel and equipment and are central to both **training documentation quality** and **ops/maintenance safety**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Warning vs caution vs note** — warning = personnel hazard; caution = equipment damage; note = advisory/essential-but-not-hazard emphasis.
- **Placement and indentation** — immediate to the step they govern; standardized text indentation/format.
- **HAZMAT warnings** — hazardous materials icons and text patterns, including alpha designators for icon sets where specified.
- **Warning summary relationship** — front-matter summary plus in-step warnings; neither replaces the other.
- **Safety warnings in graphics** — figures may carry hazard cues consistent with procedure text.
- **Nuclear/ordnance/special hazards** — when those TM types apply, specialized hazard content rules stack on the base system.

## Key Concepts
- **Comprehensibility of hazard text** — plain language, specific consequence, specific avoidance action.
- **Icon + text pairing** — icons do not stand alone without required text.
- **HAP-free and environmental notes** — environmental/HAP-related cautions appear when materials require them.
- **Electrostatic/HCI and similar** — equipment-protection cautions for sensitive items.
- **Emergency procedures** — emergency markings + warning severity appropriate to the event.
- **Training use** — warnings in TMs are teachable content for operator/maintainer courses, not fine print.

## Mental Models
- **Hazard first, step second** — users must see the warning before the dangerous action.
- **One meaning per signal word** — never inflate notes to warnings or downgrade true warnings.
- **HAZMAT is a documentation deliverable** — missing icons/text is a TM defect, not a supply problem alone.

## Anti-patterns
- Clustering all warnings in an appendix instead of at the step.
- Using 'WARNING' for equipment-only risks (should be caution).
- Icon-only HAZMAT callouts without text.
- Copy-pasting generic warnings that do not match the actual task chemicals/energy sources.

## Key Takeaways
1. Signal words and formats are standardized and safety-critical.
2. HAZMAT presentation combines icons, text, and summaries.
3. Placement is immediate to the governed step.
4. Hazard content doubles as training material for ops/maintenance crews.
