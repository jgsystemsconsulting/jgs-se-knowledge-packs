# Chapter 4: Style, Format, and Layout Rules

## Core Idea
General requirements define how page-based content must look and read: typography/layout envelopes, headers/footers, tables, figures/diagrams, references, and formatting conventions that keep multi-vendor TM output interoperable for Training & Documentation production lines.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Style and trim size table** — pick authorized manual style; layout follows the trim.
- **Headers, footers, and continuation headers** — TM number, WP IDs, and continuation labeling so photocopies and extracts remain identifiable.
- **Tables** — numbering, titles, ruled structure, and abbreviation rules inside tables.
- **Figures and diagrams** — title format, placement, foldouts, emergency page markings, and diagram types (wiring, schematic, etc.) with callout disciplines.
- **References and xrefs** — within-TM and cross-TM citation formats for WPs, figures, tables, and external docs.
- **Typography/comprehensibility aids** — consistent step numbering, indentation for notes, and readability suited to the maintainer audience.

## Key Concepts
- **Landscape vs portrait** — landscape allowed for specific figure/table cases with rules.
- **Foldout pages** — large graphics with prescribed handling in page-based sets.
- **Emergency page markings** — distinctive marking for emergency procedures.
- **Callouts and legends** — graphics must be usable without tribal knowledge.
- **Metric/US customary** — follow acquiring activity measurement policy as specified in TM requirements.
- **Software manual specifics** — when software TMs are selected, screen/data presentation still obeys page-based modularity.

## Mental Models
- **Format is part of safety and training quality** — inconsistent layout increases procedure error rates.
- **Every graphic is a maintainable CI** — titles/numbers enable change bars and citations.
- **Write for the worst lighting and gloves** — TM pages are field artifacts.

## Anti-patterns
- Publisher-unique styles that ignore the trim/style table.
- Figures without numbers used as 'the picture above'.
- Tables as screenshots without textual equivalents when data must be searchable/citable.
- Cross-references by vague prose ('see earlier') instead of WP/figure IDs.

## Key Takeaways
1. Style/trim, headers, tables, figures, and xrefs are normative production rules.
2. Continuation and emergency markings preserve usability under real conditions.
3. Graphics and tables are controlled, numbered objects.
4. Consistent format supports Training & Documentation throughput.
