# Chapter 8: Plate Exemplars and Production Patterns

## Core Idea
Much of the PDF is example plates—sample title pages, WP layouts, foldouts, emergency markings, and DMWR/NMWR covers. Use them as **production patterns** for Training & Documentation shops and as quick visual checks during Ops & Maintenance TM validation, not as free-text to retype.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Sample maintenance WP layouts** — single- and multi-column procedure patterns with initial setup blocks.
- **Sample title/cover families** — operator, combined operator/field, phased maintenance checklists, DMWR, NMWR, Marine Corps variants.
- **Foldout and emergency page examples** — how large graphics and emergency markings appear in page-based sets.
- **Change-page examples** — revision bar placement on text and graphics.
- **Diagram type gallery** — wiring/schematic/illustration patterns called up from general requirements.
- **How to use exemplars** — copy structure and labeling discipline; replace all dummy TM numbers, NINs, and prose with program data.

## Key Concepts
- **Dummy data is not authority** — example TM numbers and part names are illustrative.
- **Match the exemplar to the matrix row** — operator cover ≠ DMWR cover.
- **Accessibility of graphics** — callouts and contrast suitable for reproduction.
- **Volume stack examples** — multi-volume identity repetition patterns.
- **Checklist products** — PMI/PMC style pages differ from full narrative WPs.
- **Validation use** — field reviewers compare draft TMs to exemplar completeness (setup present? warnings placed? TOC typed?).

## Mental Models
- **Exemplars are acceptance hints** — if your page cannot map to a pattern family, you may be off-standard.
- **Production line thinking** — templates in the authoring tool should mirror exemplar zones.
- **Ops feedback loop** — maintainers mark where exemplars' clarity exceeds your draft.

## Anti-patterns
- Shipping exemplar placeholder text into a released TM.
- Using an operator cover layout on a DMWR.
- Ignoring foldout rules then binding unreadable charts.
- No visual QA against exemplar checklist before release.

## Key Takeaways
1. Plates are normative examples for layout and identity patterns.
2. Map each deliverable page type to an exemplar family.
3. Replace all dummy content; keep structural discipline.
4. Exemplars support both documentation production and maintenance validation.
