# Chapter: Applicable document classes

## Core Idea

Section 2 lists government and non-government documents that form part of the standard to the extent specified in later sections. The list is a class map for acquisition and authoring, not a dump to paste into every TM. Users must still meet every document cited in sections 3, 4, and 5 even if a title is missing from section 2.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Four buckets

1. **General rule.** Section 2 holds documents specified by later normative sections. It does not claim to list every citation used only as example or background.
2. **Government documents.** Specifications, standards, handbooks, and other government publications named for DoD TM work.
3. **Non-government publications.** Industry and consensus documents adopted to the extent the standard cites them.
4. **Order of precedence.** When documents conflict, the order-of-precedence rule in the standard decides which text wins. Do not invent a local order.

### How to use the list in practice

- Name the class a requirement pulls from (performance specification, military standard, handbook, other government publication, non-government publication).
- Cite the exact title only when the acquiring activity or the source paragraph demands it.
- Do not quote long passages from those documents inside this pack.

## Key Concepts

- **Specifications.** Performance and preparation specifications for lubricants, modification work orders, demilitarization manuals, aircraft operator manuals and checklists, lists of applicable publications, and related TM products appear as named classes.
- **Standards.** Marking, system safety, test terminology, vehicle preparation, and other military standards are referenced as external constraints on TM content.
- **Handbooks.** DoD handbooks that guide TM practice are in the government set when cited.
- **Other government documents.** Directives, instructions, and publications outside the specification/standard/handbook triad still bind when section 4 or 5 points to them.
- **Issue control.** Unless the solicitation or contract says otherwise, the issue in force is the one cited there, not an arbitrary local choice.

## Mental Models

- **Class before catalog.** Start from the kind of document the requirement needs, then resolve the current issue through the contract.
- **Citation is not license to paste.** This pack names classes and representative titles; it does not reproduce their text.
- **Gaps are not exemptions.** A missing row in section 2 does not waive a citation that appears in sections 3 through 5.

## Key Takeaways

- Section 2 is a controlled bibliography for normative use, not a free-reading list.
- Keep government and non-government classes distinct.
- Resolve conflicts with the standard's order-of-precedence rule.
- Do not dump or quote the bibliography in synthesized notes.
- Acquisition documents still name the issue of each cited source.
