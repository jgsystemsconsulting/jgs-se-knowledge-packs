# Chapter 1: WBS Principles and Definitions

## Core Idea
MIL-STD-881F (13 May 2022) is the DoD standard practice for preparing, understanding, and presenting Work Breakdown Structures for defense materiel items. It gives Program Managers a uniform product-oriented framework and tells contractors how to extend Program and Contract WBS detail for planning, cost, schedule, technical, and reporting use across the Adaptive Acquisition Framework.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Product-oriented family tree** — hardware, software, services, data, and facilities arranged so parent/child relationships preserve 100% of the work at each level.
- **Program WBS vs Contract WBS vs Subcontract WBS** — Government-owned program structure, contractor-extended contract structure, and lower-tier extensions under the same rules.
- **Common Elements (Appendix K)** — cross-commodity elements (SE/PM, system test, training, data, support equipment, etc.) applied to Appendices A–J commodity structures (not L/M the same way).
- **Mandatory ACAT applicability** — direction for programs under DoDI 5000.02 Adaptive Acquisition Framework; supersedes MIL-STD-881E (6 Oct 2020).

## Key Concepts
- **Defense materiel item / Program Element** — the PE or designated item that owns the Program WBS.
- **Level of identification** — define deep enough to manage high-cost/high-risk/high-technical-interest items; reporting level is negotiated separately from definition depth.
- **100% rule** — children of a parent must sum to 100% of that parent's work and resources; never promote a child beside its parent if that severs the relationship.
- **Recurring vs nonrecurring** — cost classifications, not WBS elements; do not invent WBS nodes solely to split R/NR.
- **WBS dictionary** — element definitions, content, and boundaries that keep estimators, schedulers, and engineers aligned.
- **Evolution** — WBS matures from pre-award Program WBS through contract award extensions and post-award control.

## Mental Models
- **Structure first, data second** — primary purpose is to define the product and work; historic cost files are valuable but must not distort the structure.
- **Definition ≠ reporting** — you may define lower than you report; high-visibility items still need definition at the level where risk appears.
- **Parent/child integrity over PM convenience** — visibility hacks that orphan transmitters from fire-control parents break cost and technical truth.

## Anti-patterns
- Building a functional or org-chart WBS instead of a product-oriented one.
- Creating WBS elements named after cost categories (recurring, fee, G&A).
- Pulling a subsystem to a sibling level of its former parent for 'visibility'.
- Treating Appendix commodity templates as untailorable copy-paste without dictionary work.

## Key Takeaways
1. 881F standardizes WBS language for all ACAT defense materiel programs.
2. Program/Contract/Subcontract WBS layers share one product-oriented logic.
3. 100% rule and dictionary discipline prevent broken cost/technical rollups.
4. Common Elements bind commodities; Appendices A–J supply commodity templates.
