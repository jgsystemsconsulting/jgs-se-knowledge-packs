# Chapter 6: Common Elements (Appendix K)

## Core Idea
Appendix K defines WBS elements common across commodities in Appendices A–J (prior to Operations and Support structures handled in L). These elements—systems engineering, program management, system test and evaluation, training, data, support equipment, odd support, facilities, initial spares, and related—attach at the correct product levels so enabling work is visible without becoming a dumping ground.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **SE/PM (and SEIT/PM variants)** — systems engineering, integration, and program management effort associated with a product parent; space and some commodities use SEIT/PM wording.
- **System Test and Evaluation** — DT/OT planning and execution elements associated to the system/product, with Appendix M refining Government ST&E breakouts.
- **Training** — equipment, services, and facilities for operator/maintainer training as WBS products/services.
- **Data** — technical data, engineering data, and related data products as elements—not an afterthought CDRL pile outside the WBS.
- **Support equipment / operational/site activation / facilities / initial spares** — enabling products and services with clear parents.
- **Applicability boundary** — K common elements apply to A–J constructions; Appendices L and M have their own sustainment and GST&E regimes.

## Key Concepts
- **Attach commons to the product they enable** — system-level SE/PM vs subsystem SE/PM must match the integration scope actually performed.
- **Unique commodity commons** — some appendices point to K.3/K.4/K.7 unique applications (e.g., space); follow the commodity pointer.
- **Test equipment vs support equipment** — keep definitions straight so ST&E and SE elements do not double-count gear.
- **Training as product** — devices, courseware, and services can be WBS elements when they are deliverables.
- **Data rights vs data element** — legal rights are contractual; the data product still needs a WBS home for work/cost.
- **Do not use SE/PM as a trash drawer** — hardware that is a product belongs under product parents.

## Mental Models
- **Commons complete the 100% rule** — product-only trees understate real program work.
- **Level matching** — subsystem SE under subsystem parents; system integration under system parents.
- **L and M are siblings, not K clones** — sustainment CES and Government ST&E have dedicated appendices in 881F.

## Anti-patterns
- Putting all engineering hours in one Level-2 SE/PM regardless of product.
- Omitting training/data/support equipment because 'CLIN structure covers it'.
- Applying K commons unchanged to Appendix L sustainment reporting.
- Double-booking the same lab equipment under ST&E and support equipment.

## Key Takeaways
1. Appendix K is mandatory literacy for every A–J WBS.
2. SE/PM, ST&E, training, data, SE, facilities, and spares are first-class elements.
3. Attachment level must match integration/ownership scope.
4. L/M handle sustainment and Government ST&E beyond K's A–J role.
