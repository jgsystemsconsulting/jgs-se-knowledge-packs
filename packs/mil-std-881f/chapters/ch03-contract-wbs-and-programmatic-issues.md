# Chapter 3: Contract WBS Extension and Programmatic Issues

## Core Idea
Section 3 tells contractors (and Government performers under agreements) how to extend the Program WBS into a Contract WBS and how to handle modern programmatic complications—System of Systems, Family of Systems, cybersecurity, software-intensive systems, and IMP/IMS linkage—without breaking product orientation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Contract WBS extension** — add detail under approved Program WBS parents; maintain 100% rule; keep dictionary current.
- **SoS and Family of Systems** — preserve identity of constituent systems while showing integration/enterprise elements at the right parent.
- **Cybersecurity** — place cyber work with the product or common element it protects; avoid a disconnected 'cyber bucket' that hides product risk.
- **Software dual path** — distinguish embedded weapon-system software from Information Systems/Defense Business Systems (Appendix J) and report development visibility accordingly.
- **IMP/IMS linkage** — events/accomplishments/criteria and schedules trace to WBS products, not to a parallel shadow structure.

## Key Concepts
- **Software on specific equipment** — normally child of the equipment/product it enables; elevate only when functionality cannot be partitioned (per 100% rule guidance).
- **IS/DBS** — use Appendix J patterns; software-intensive business systems are not forced into air-vehicle trees.
- **Visibility into software processes** — WBS supports management visibility; it is not a substitute for software engineering plans.
- **Subcontract WBS** — flow the same product parents to subs; do not let each sub invent a unique top-level.
- **Reporting vs management detail** — contractor internal management may go deeper than CSDR/report levels.
- **Post-award change control** — WBS changes need configuration discipline tied to technical baseline changes.

## Mental Models
- **Put software where the product lives** — orphan software rollups destroy cost estimating relationships.
- **Cyber is a property of products and enabling elements** — structure follows attack surface ownership.
- **IMP/IMS tells when; WBS tells what** — both required; neither replaces the other.

## Anti-patterns
- One giant 'Software' Level-2 for an entire weapon system with no product parents.
- Separate cyber WBS that never maps to configuration items under test.
- IMS tasks with no WBS product tags.
- Subs delivering WBSs that do not roll to the Contract WBS.

## Key Takeaways
1. Contract WBS extends Program WBS under 100% rule and dictionary control.
2. SoS/FoS, cyber, and software each have placement rules—do not improvise buckets.
3. IMP/IMS must reference WBS products.
4. Subcontract structures inherit product parents.
