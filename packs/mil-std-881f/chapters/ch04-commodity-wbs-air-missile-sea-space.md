# Chapter 4: Commodity WBS Templates — Air, Missile, Sea, and Space

## Core Idea
Appendices A–F (and related) supply standardized Level structures and element definitions for major commodity families. Use them as the starting taxonomy for Program/Contract WBS element selection, then tailor with documented dictionary entries rather than free-form invention.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Appendix A Aircraft Systems** — air vehicle (airframe, propulsion, avionics, armament, furnishings, etc.), system engineering/program management, system test, training, data, support equipment, facilities, and initial spares patterns.
- **Appendix B Electronics / Avionics / Generic Systems** — sensor, comms, and generic electronic product breakouts when the materiel item is electronics-centric rather than a full air vehicle.
- **Appendix C Missile/Ordnance** — missile air vehicle/munition, propulsion, guidance, payload, launchers, and support chains.
- **Appendix D Strategic Missile Systems** — strategic unique parents and SEIT/PM applications.
- **Appendix E Sea Systems** — ship/sea platform product structure and maritime support elements.
- **Appendix F Space Systems** — space vehicle, ground segments, launch interfaces, and space-unique common-element applications (see also K.4 notes in source).

## Key Concepts
- **Air vehicle vs system level** — keep platform product parents distinct from SE/PM and ST&E common elements.
- **Propulsion, avionics, armament** — typical Level-3/4 product families under air/missile vehicles; extend only with 100% rule intact.
- **Power and distribution / environmental / fuel** — product subsystems, not overhead pools.
- **Ordnance vs strategic missile** — choose C vs D based on strategic unique structure needs.
- **Sea and space ground segments** — ground-based equipment and facilities belong in defined elements, not buried in 'other'.
- **Tailoring** — rename 'Other … 1…n (Specify)' slots with clear identities; do not leave unspecified buckets in the managed baseline.

## Mental Models
- **Pick one primary commodity spine** — hybrids need an explicit primary appendix plus justified grafts.
- **Vehicle first, enablers second** — common elements hang off the product structure, they do not replace it.
- **Specify every 'Other'** — anonymous other-elements destroy estimating and CSDR usefulness.

## Anti-patterns
- Copying Appendix A onto a pure ground-radar program.
- Parking all avionics under SE/PM because 'it is engineering'.
- Using strategic-missile parents for tactical munitions without rationale.
- Leaving multiple 'Other Subsystems' with no dictionary definitions.

## Key Takeaways
1. A–F appendices are the authorized starting taxonomies for their commodities.
2. Product vehicle/platform parents stay above SE/PM and test commons.
3. Tailor by specification and dictionary, not by silent omission.
4. Sea/space ground and launch interfaces need explicit elements.
