# HFI and systems engineering, DLODs and SRLs (Part 2)

## Core Idea

Part 2 positions HFI as an integral part of Systems Engineering: without the user the system is incomplete, and it is the user who carries most of a system's emergent properties. Because many projects do not consider the user as part of the system, HFI exists to prompt consideration of the human elements. The HFI process reflects agreed systems engineering standards, citing ISO/IEC 15288, and follows accepted HFI good practice in managing and mitigating people-related considerations. Anchoring HFI inside SE buys two things: process stages associated with typical project phases, which gives a mechanism for monitoring performance, and explicit recognition of Learning from Experience as the route to improving future acquisitions.

The same section gives HFI two complementary maps of the human contribution. The Defence Lines of Development (DLODs) surface high-level issues at the programme and capability level, managed through programme boards and the Capability Integration Working Group; the five HFI domains are of greater utility at the DE&S project level, supporting detailed design and development. The Human Component of Capability touches every DLOD and belongs in trade-off considerations.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The DLOD considerations table: for each line of development the source poses people-side checks. Training: personnel trained to operate the new or updated capability, training effectiveness, and wider training policy. Equipment: design complies with relevant people-related standards, all users identified, equipment fit for purpose, and human capability not reduced by added technology. Personnel: characteristics necessary for safe operation, maintenance and support identified and documented; implications for recruitment, selection and retention considered; personnel levels determined and planned. Information: all users in the generation, transmission, receipt, assimilation, manipulation, sharing, storage and maintenance of information identified, and all users have the information they need; information assurance dealt with. Doctrine and Concepts: implications of doctrine considered, the importance of doctrine to command understood, and the capability matches the reality of operations. Organisation: structure matched to environment and task, hierarchy considered within the overall structure, national cultures identified, and processes, procedures and ways of working considered. Infrastructure: how changes in accommodation standards and base locations affect capability, duty of care and personnel expectations. Logistics: the capability has the necessary people support and equipment support.
- System Readiness Levels (SRLs): a project management tool, part of MOD technology management, that captures evidence and assesses and communicates system maturity consistently, feeding project assurance at key decision points. The SRLs define nine maturity steps from User Requirements understood (SRL 1) to Proven System (SRL 9), mapped onto the SE and CADMID lifecycles. Maturity at each step is assessed across nine SE disciplines or drivers, such as Training, Software and Information Systems, with HFI one of the drivers; one driver is project specific, at the Project Team's discretion.

## Key Concepts

The SRL(HFI) scale, reproduced in the source from a self-assessment tool, walks the same ladder from the HFI side: understand the HF issues implicit within the URD and the associated risks (levels 1 to 2); achieve clear definition of the human roles within the system, exploring staffing reductions, job design, workload, performance shortfalls and safety management; provide HFI input to the SRD with responsibilities aligned to other disciplines; conduct and test initial evaluation of HFI; verify sub-systems within a representative (simulated laboratory) environment; demonstrate a system prototype within an integration environment such as a vehicle integration test rig; achieve and accept the human-related aspects of design and function, with the In-Service Date declared after effective integration of the equipment with the Human Component, that is personnel, procedures, support and training regimes, under operational conditions; and finally monitor and manage any future HFI issues through the Through Life Management Plan. Tender evaluation appears mid-scale: contractor offerings are evaluated to predict operability, maintainability and supportability, and HFI concerns must be included in down-selection criteria.

HFI must be considered throughout the system lifecycle, taking account of both engineering and support aspects, including mid-life updates, procurement of off-the-shelf products, and definition and management of an in-service organisation. The unique characteristics of the Human Component of Capability are the reason systems engineers need this additional guidance.

## Mental Models

- Two lenses, two altitudes: DLODs for programme-level trade-off, HFI domains for project-level design; neither replaces the other.
- Readiness is graded evidence, not opinion: HFI maturity is scored as one driver among nine, so human-side progress is visible in the same assurance reviews as software and training.
- A good practice applied without professional judgement is not automatically successful; the source notes practices must be appropriate to their application, and professional HFI/HFE input may be required.

## Key Takeaways

- HFI is part of SE, not an adjunct; the process deliberately mirrors ISO/IEC 15288-style lifecycles.
- Every DLOD has named human-side checks; use the table as trade-off prompts at capability level.
- SRL(HFI) gives nine graded steps from understood URD issues to monitored in-service performance, with acceptance tied to demonstrated equipment-people integration.
