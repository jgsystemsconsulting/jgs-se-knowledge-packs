# Roles and responsibilities for HFI (Parts 1 and 2)

## Core Idea

Six named MOD staff responsibility areas carry HFI through an acquisition: Project Team Leader, Human Factors Integration Focus, Requirements Management, Capability Sponsor, Capability Integration Working Group, and HFI Support Function. Part 1 section 3 states the directive versions of each duty and Part 2 section 5 restates them as guidance; the names match across both parts. One naming note: the third heading in both parts is Requirements Management, the responsibility area; the individual who discharges it inside the project team is the Requirements Manager (RM). This chapter uses both names for that one role.

The load-bearing design of the role set is the split of the Human Factors Integration Focus into two people: HFI Focus within the Front Line Command / Capability community, abbreviated HFIF(Cap), and HFI Focus within the DE&S Project Team or Delivery Team, abbreviated HFIF(PT). Part 2 is explicit that in practice these are two roles performed by two people, with a close working relationship, especially early in the project; by the end of the Concept Phase the HFIF(PT) would be expected to take the lead.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Prime responsibility anchors on the Project Team Leader: Part 1 gives the PT Leader prime responsibility for ensuring HFI is successfully managed and that satisfactory HFI outcomes are achieved, including nominating a PT member as HFIF(PT) (Part 2), ensuring staff doing HFI management have sufficient information and training, and ensuring the System Requirements Document used by the Solution Provider includes sufficient Human Factors System Requirements.
- The two-halves HFI Focus model: HFIF(Cap) manages HFI during Pre-Concept and early Concept, centred on defining people-related User Requirements and scanning every DLOD for HFI issues and risks; the role is unlikely to be exclusive or full-time and may sit with the Capability Development Desk Officer. HFIF(PT) owns day-to-day management of HFI activities carried out by DE&S or on its behalf, and is responsible for tailoring DE&S HFI activities.
- Requirements Management as translation chain: FLC/Cap owns the pan-DLOD Capability Requirements and develops them into User Requirements; the RM, the FLC's representative in the project team, translates User Requirements into System Requirements and is custodian of the Golden Thread that links requirements unbroken from Defence Policy down through to DLOD outputs, possibly supported by a Requirements Engineer. For HFI, the RM derives HFI requirements from the URD into the SRD, which should carry a dedicated HFI section, seeking guidance from the HFIF roles because the RM is unlikely to have detailed HFI knowledge.
- Integration and assurance machinery: the Capability Integration Working Group ensures integration across the DLODs, with its chairperson accountable that the Human Component of Capability is adequately captured, defined, analysed and tested; the HFI Support Function is the DE&S Engineering Group HFI Policy team, which provides information, guidance and support, agrees tailoring, and provides assurance that the HFI process is applied appropriately.

## Key Concepts

The Capability Sponsor is responsible for capability at programme level: leading the Capability Change Planning Process, identifying equipment and support requirements, and acting as sponsor for new and enhanced equipment and support programmes. Part 1 adds that the Sponsor operates as the decision-maker providing new equipment and support on behalf of MOD, and that on major programmes the Senior Responsible Owner ensures the programme addresses all DLODs through life, including process and culture or behavioural change.

The CIWG is DLOD-focussed, its membership typically representing each DLOD, and one individual may represent more than one, in which case the differing roles should be made explicit in Terms of Reference. Part 1 recommends that the HFIF(Cap) and HFIF(PT) are members of the CIWG; Part 2 recommends HFIF(Cap) membership.

The HFI Support Function has honest limits: although DE&S is responsible only for Equipment and Logistics at project level, HFI must be applied across all DLODs through life, so the HFI Policy team works from both Engineering and Support perspectives, addressing Support Solutions Envelope Core Development Area 2 and the Guide to Engineering Activities and Reviews. Part 2 is candid about the limits: beyond an initial consultation the EG HFI Policy team has no resource to assist projects, and its core business is managing HFI policy, standards and guidance. SQEP support to project teams comes from the HFI Internal Technical Support team, the Dstl Human Systems Group, or the Engineering Delivery Partner, accessed through the Engineering Services Single Front Door. Part 1 directs that Project and Delivery Teams engage the HFI Policy team early and throughout the programme, and that any tailoring of the HFI Process must be agreed with it.

The PT and Cap Branch must agree a method to generate the requirements set (HFURs, HFSRs and HFPRs) that addresses the Human Component of Capability, with EG HFI team advice where appropriate; Part 1 calls generating this set essential to successful realisation of the project capability.

## Mental Models

- Two focuses, one relay: capability-side HFI runs Pre-Concept and Concept and hands over to project-side HFI, which carries the work under contract; the handover is planned, not assumed.
- Evidence versus freedom: the HFIF(PT) must strike a balance between having tangible evidence of the Solution Provider's work, sufficient for assurance and audit, and giving the Solution Provider freedom to get on with the job.
- Where in-house expertise falls short, the route is a SQEP support request, not silent muddling through.

## Anti-patterns

Part 2 names one directly: production of plans and reports is costly and time-consuming, and over-specifying the requirement turns the effort into valueless paperwork instead of analysis anyone can use. Demanding documents as proof of HFI, rather than evidence proportionate to risk, is the stated failure.

## Key Takeaways

- Six responsibility areas, and the HFI Focus is deliberately two posts: HFIF(Cap) before contract, HFIF(PT) from the project team.
- The RM owns the URD-to-SRD translation and the Golden Thread; the SRD should have a dedicated HFI section.
- The CIWG chair answers for the Human Component of Capability being captured, defined, analysed and tested.
- The HFI Policy team agrees all tailoring and assures application, but does not staff projects; SQEP support flows through the Single Front Door.
