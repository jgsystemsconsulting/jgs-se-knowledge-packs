# Assess tenders, HFI-3.0 (Part 2)

## Core Idea

The goal of the Assess Tenders stage is to ensure the Solution Provider's tender suitably addresses all HFI requirements. It is completed at the end of the Assessment phase to support selection of a Solution Provider, but it may also run at other points in acquisition to support risk-reduction or concept-maturation activities; Part 2 notes the process may be needed several times to support contracts placed before the Demonstration and Manufacture phases. The stage consists of three core activities: assess the tenderer's response, liaise with the CIWG and RWG, and support tenderer selection.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The tenderer's side of the exchange: in response to the ITT, the tenderer must generate an HFI Plan and any other materials the Statement of Requirement specifies, which may include compliance justifications and inputs to documents such as the Integrated Test, Evaluation and Acceptance Plan. Under it, the HFI Plan a tenderer produces has to set out every management and technical HFI activity that will be run to meet the HFPRs and HFSRs and to cover the identified HFI considerations; the HFI-PD03 Product Description fixes the plan's minimum contents. The plan should also describe supply-chain HFI activities, and its focus should be on management activities, with technical activities covering all five domains included at relatively high level.
- The HFE exception: the Human Factors Engineering domain is treated differently from the other four. Its technical detail cannot stay high-level; the tenderer shall provide a complete, itemised list of HFE activities, each described in detail, identify the methods, tools and techniques to be applied with justifications for their selection, and provide a schedule of HFE activities mapped against key project milestones such as Preliminary Design Review, Critical Design Review, Factory Acceptance Test and System Acceptance Test. Where HFE activity is significant, a Human Engineering Programme Plan may be provided in addition to, or annexed to, the HFI Plan.
- Assessment criteria fixed upstream: the criteria applied here are those the HFIF(PT) determined in activity HFI-2.10, and may combine HFSRs and HFPRs, with performance levels and evidence quality defining scoring.

## Key Concepts

The HFIF(PT) shall scrutinise the HFI Plan and all other HFI-related tender documents, including statements of compliance against HFSRs, against five criteria: compliance with the HFI Plan Product Description; the extent to which the HFPRs in the SOR have been addressed; the extent to which the HFI Plan addresses the identified HFI considerations; the extent to which it is integrated with other project plans; and the extent of claimed compliance with the HFSRs. Within the assessment the HFIF(PT) may also conduct design analysis, or contract an independent third party such as the Internal Technical Support HFI Team, Dstl or an external contractor, to analyse design elements and test whether the compliance levels claimed are achievable within the constraints of the proposed design. Part 2 flags this as particularly important for COTS and MOTS equipment, where the opportunity for design change is limited but there may be an opportunity to test real equipment during evaluation.

Tender assessment usually surfaces issues: requirements or process non-compliances whose impact must be understood before down-selection. Requirements trades need assessment for impact from all perspectives including HFI, and the trades and issues must be presented to the Requirements Working Group and the Capability Integration Working Group so decisions on handling are made there. Although the HFIF(PT) may not sit in contract negotiations directly, the team must be ready to respond to requirement clarifications and potential changes as they arise, whether trades or changes to the process and timescales proposed in the tender response. Selection support closes the stage: considerations arising from the tenderer's proposed HFI management and technical activities, and level of HFI competence, are assessed against the specified criteria from HFI-2.10.

## Mental Models

- Compliance claims are hypotheses: the assessment tests whether claimed compliance is achievable within the proposed design, not whether the words are present.
- Depth is asymmetric by design: management plans stay high-level while HFE activity is scheduled and detailed, because HFE is where the design work actually happens.

## Key Takeaways

- HFI-3.0 can recur; any pre-Demonstration contract may need its own tender assessment round.
- Five fixed criteria anchor the scrutiny, and independent design analysis is the sanctioned way to test COTS claims.
- Non-compliance and trades route through the RWG and CIWG; HFI impact must be understood before down-selection decisions.
