# Test and acceptance and in-service feedback, HFI-5.0 and HFI-6.0 (Part 2)

## Core Idea

HFI-5.0, Test and Acceptance, ensures all HFI requirements are tested and accepted in accordance with the Integrated Test, Evaluation and Acceptance Plan. Test and evaluation activities are typically conducted by the Solution Provider under close scrutiny by MOD, with results presented to MOD for acceptance through the agreed process in the ITEA Plan; the stage carries eleven core activities. HFI-6.0, In-Service Feedback, ensures the delivered system meets the customer's requirements through life and that people-related considerations attached to design changes are suitably managed; it is principally run by MOD with support from the Solution Provider and Front Line Command, and carries four core activities: capture HFI lessons from procurement, capture information from in-service feedback, update and maintain the HFI RAIDO Register, and assess the impact of design changes across the HFI domains and DLODs.

## Frameworks Introduced

- MOD's four watch duties in testing: the PT Leader is ultimately responsible for the system being accepted into service, while the HFIF(PT) assesses test specifications to confirm they will generate the required evidence, observes HFI test and evaluation activities for conformance with the test plans, reviews test reports for a fair reflection of the testing conducted and the results claimed, and provides the PT Leader and CIWG with an understanding of the impact of any non-compliances and advice on acceptability.
- The acceptance evidence chain: the Solution Provider's ITEA Lead manages and schedules test activities and engages the engineering disciplines; the Provider's HFI Manager plans the HFI test activities and generates Detailed Test Specifications, integrating them into the larger ITEA Plan, including HFI aspects of tests primarily aimed at non-HFI requirements such as logistics, maintainability and equipment performance. The Verification and Validation Requirements Matrix records evidence against each SRD requirement, is normally owned by the Provider's ITEA Lead, and the HFI Manager is responsible for providing evidence against each HFSR in it.
- The in-service lessons machinery: DLIMS is the default system across Defence for identifying, tracking and managing lessons, and is the ultimate repository for issues captured through local mechanisms such as briefings and reviews, equipment failure reports, and safety or aviation hazard logs; Flight Safety matters are recorded in ASIMS, and the Navy Lessons Process, based on DLIMS, sits behind the maritime route. Feedback from the in-service HFI activities is made available to subsequent acquisition projects, MOD subject matter experts and specialist functions through established Learning from Experience processes.

## Key Concepts

Before any test activity, the HFIF(PT) reviews the HFI RAIDO Register with the Provider's HFI Manager to identify considerations that can be addressed within it. Results go to the HFI Working Group, where key findings are agreed, residual considerations are captured in the register linked to both the requirement and the trials report that raised them, giving an audit trail, and considerations that the trials resolved are closed with the group's agreement. Outputs must be fully assessed for HFI compliance and new considerations; the HFIF(PT) ensures Front Line Commands see the analyses and agree with findings and recommendations, with significant issues raised to the programme RAIDO, RWG and CIWG. New considerations during testing usually stem from requirement non-compliance but may come from test procedures or other factors, and timely register management protects the schedule; the register is jointly managed through this stage, with the Provider presenting emerging considerations and proposed responses for endorsement.

Design freezes constrain the stage: the design is progressively frozen, held under version control, and changes run through formal change control with a change log or database, status tracking, and assessment for unintended consequences and impact on HF compliance, feeding the HFI Log as audit trail for the HFI Case. Requirements management becomes evidence collation: compliance against each HFI requirement is gathered, and where compliance cannot be achieved the Provider's HFI Manager quantifies and justifies the non-compliance so a way forward can be agreed with HFI stakeholders before presentation to the CIWG or RWG. HFI also inputs MOD project documentation across the stage, including the Through Life Management Plan, hazard log, safety case, project risk register, lessons databases, and the MOD's own HFI Case, HFI Log and HFI Plan.

In service, entry to service is a greater test than any acceptance testing: widespread use exposes the capability to a broader audience and surfaces issues that slipped through design and testing, particularly where operational conditions differ from the originally envisaged context. Feedback should cover all aspects, drawing on operational, maintenance, training and support staff; even where equipment design cannot change, processes and tooling can be optimised and mid-life updates or future spirals improved. The source highlights maintenance in particular: check the alignment between work as imagined, work as prescribed and work as done, so that deviations from prescribed procedures are not normalised, and formalise any procedural changes to prevent maintenance errors and incidents. After acceptance the project transfers to an in-service team, where the HFIF(PT) role is unlikely to be full-time; lessons from procurement are captured before the team ramps down, using the HFI Working Group and Steering Group as the mechanism. For significant modifications or upgrades, acquisition procedures from earlier phases are re-enacted at appropriate scale, drawing on stored HFI data, with the HFI elements of the safety case revisited; a major change may run a full, compressed HFI development cycle from requirements definition through design, testing and acceptance into service, tailored to the update.

## Mental Models

- Evidence or it did not happen: every test result, closure and non-compliance needs its register entry and its referenced evidence.
- Design freezes raise the stakes: the later the stage, the more HFI must work through change control and trade-off bodies rather than redesign.
- Service is the real laboratory: the acceptance test is a sample; in-service feedback is the population study, fed through LfE into the next acquisition.

## Key Takeaways

- HFI-5.0 runs on the ITEA Plan, with MOD scrutinising rather than testing, and acceptance resting with the PT Leader.
- The VVRM binds each HFSR to formal evidence; the HFI Working Group gates all closures.
- HFI-6.0 keeps the RAIDO register alive through life and forces every design change to be assessed across domains and DLODs.
- Lessons flow through DLIMS and LfE into successor projects, and major upgrades re-enter the cycle.
