# User need and system requirements definition, HFI-1.0 and HFI-2.0 (Part 2)

## Core Idea

HFI-1.0, User Need Definition, ensures appropriate human considerations enter the User Requirements and that the Acquirer gets sufficient information to develop Human Factors System Requirements. It is addressed during the Pre-Concept and Concept phases in support of the Outline Business Case, and aligns best with Programme Identification activities after a draft Programme Mandate, when a capability need is identified, funding for an option approved, and a Senior Responsible Owner appointed. HFI-2.0, System Requirements Definition, ensures appropriate HFPRs and HFSRs are developed and incorporated into the Invitation to Tender or Invitation to Negotiate documentation, completed during the latter Concept phase and through the Assessment phase in support of the SRD, Statement of Requirement and ITT/ITN materials. The first stage comprises seven core activities, the second ten; each stage has defined inputs and outputs, with detailed guidance including tailoring guidance and user checklists available alongside the process description.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The HFI-1.0 activity chain: appoint the HFI Focus (Capability), who shall have at minimum Awareness-level HFI knowledge, ideally Practitioner, and who ensures completion of the activities that follow; define the HFI Plan, describing the pre-contract FLC approach to people-related User requirements, identifying HFI stakeholders across all DLODs, conforming with GEAR and the HFI-PD01 Product Description, with any tailoring agreed with the Defence Authority (HFI Policy Team); analyse legacy system data and feedback; and identify the Human Component of Capability, with sub-activities providing HFI input to the URD, input to the CONEMP, and identification of the Target Audience.
- The legacy analysis baseline: in-service feedback from Front Line Commands, HFI RAIDO registers from predecessor systems where available, and pan-Defence lessons sources such as DLIMS, ASIMS and the Navy Lessons Process. The exercise should capture the predecessor's good features as well as its problems, since good features carried forward can reduce the training burden. The captured data forms the HFI baseline dataset that underpins an Early Comparability Analysis, revisited within the Early Human Factors Analysis in HFI-2.0, and normally yields an HFI RAIDO Register.
- The HFI-2.0 activity chain: appoint the HFIF(PT), a SQEP with at minimum Awareness and preferably Practitioner level, who should be a good influencer since much HFI work means persuading stakeholders whose responsibilities overlap; plan the approach to HFI in a project HFI Plan meeting the HFI-PD02 description and GEAR quality criteria, aligned with the FLC's HFI Plan; establish a MOD HFI Steering Group before contract award as a forum for the EHFA, Early Comparability Analysis and development of HFPRs and HFSRs; define the Target Audience; conduct HFI analyses; maintain the HFI RAIDO Register; engage the Steering Group; identify the HFI requirements; compile HFI contract documentation; and determine the Tender Assessment Criteria for HFI.

## Key Concepts

Identifying the Human Component means working with the Head of Capability to make explicit where people are or might be involved, even when their contribution is buried in general statements, because this avoids later difficulty when the SRD is derived from the URD. The URD plays the pivotal role in the acquisition, driving all later requirements and plans, and needs high-level hooks to which more specific HFSRs can be traced. Defence Standard 00-251 provides candidate HF User Requirements for populating the URD; they are intentionally generic, may not all apply, and do not preclude capability-specific alternatives where HFI knowledge supports them. The HFIF(Cap) shall ensure the URD adequately covers HFI, either by including the generic HFURs tailored to context or by confirming the generic set is adequately covered by other User Requirements. The CONEMP describes how the capability will be employed and provides the context document for deriving system requirements; the later CONUSE, derived from the CONEMP for Full Business Case, describes employment across activities, operations and scenarios and lives through the platform's life. Early Target Audience Description work may be limited to identifying who the users, maintainers and trainers will be, maturing in HFI-2.4 into the detailed TAD covering physical, psychological and sociological characteristics, organisations, selection criteria, career paths and training requirements.

The EHFA is central: Part 2 requires every programme to conduct one at some level of detail, recommends workshops fed with the HFI baseline dataset, and points to the HFI domains and DLODs as prompts for identifying considerations, which are then assessed for probability and impact with response plans formulated. Follow-on analyses may include task analysis and use-case modelling, human performance modelling of workload, error and situation awareness, initial training needs analysis, personnel analysis such as setting ship or submarine complements, environmental analysis for habitability, and workspace analysis from user-population percentiles. The HFI RAIDO Register is the management tool for tracking considerations through the programme, with entries carrying at least a unique number, title, description, priority, source, owner, mitigation strategy and status; its maintenance responsibility transitions from HFIF(Cap) to HFIF(PT) at the end of the Concept phase.

Three requirement types structure the outputs. HFURs, identified in HFI-1.0, go in the URD. HFSRs go in the SRD, derived from the generic set in Defence Standard 00-251 together with measures of performance, each carrying agreed ownership, threshold and objective measures, priority including tradability, tailoring, and a proposed means of demonstrating compliance. HFPRs are invoked by the contract through the Statement of Requirement, selected by the HFIF(PT) who must justify exclusions, with derogations from the Defence Standard 00-251 set agreed with the HFI Policy Team. Requirements should be SMART; Part 2 cautions the generic HFSRs will not be SMART until reviewed against the needs of the given system. Contract compilation as a minimum includes HFPRs for the SOR/SOW, HFSRs for the SRD, HFI input to the CONEMP and the TAD, and often the RAIDO Register; tenderers shall commit to an HFI Plan and compliance statements against all defined HFPRs and HFSRs, and staged contracting is recommended where uncertainty is significant.

## Mental Models

- Hooks before detail: early, generic HFURs in the URD exist so that later, specific HFSRs have something to trace back to.
- One register, one truth: the HFI RAIDO Register is the single tracked account of people-related considerations, reviewed with stakeholders through the Steering Group.
- Requirements three ways: user-level (HFUR), system-level (HFSR), and process-level (HFPR) answer who the people must be, what the system must do for them, and how the work must be run.

## Key Takeaways

- HFI-1.0 is a capability-side stage ending at OBC; HFI-2.0 is a project-side stage ending at tender issue, with a planned handover of both focus role and RAIDO ownership.
- The EHFA is the risk-based rationale underpinning later HFI investment; no project skips it, only its depth varies.
- Contract documentation and tender assessment criteria are HFI products: if compliance statements and criteria are not specified here, HFI-3.0 has nothing to assess.
