# Chapter 5: DT&E Sufficiency, Contractor Testing, and Evidence

## Core Idea
MOTRC judgments rest on developmental and contractor test evidence as much as on OT plans. DT sufficiency assessments at major milestones, government oversight of contractor testing, and production- or operational-representative articles determine whether dedicated OT can be honest and efficient.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **DT sufficiency (MDAP)**: Mandatory MDA assessments at MS B and MS C (T-0)—plans/resources/risk mitigation/production-entry criteria early; completed DT + remaining work + dedicated-OT readiness later. Service vs USD(A&S) MDA changes who performs the assessment (DD(DTE&A) vs AF/TE).
- **Contractor testing in the enterprise**: CDT coordinates DT including contractor testing; LDTO helps oversee contractor DT results. COTS/NDI contracts should force early access to legacy-type test data for strategy building (T-3).
- **Template cluster (design/performance & test execution)**: Attachment 13 (contractor testing), Attachment 14 (government DT&E), Attachment 15 (software maturity), Attachment 20 (production/operationally representative articles), Attachment 21 (system performance) are primary evidence homes—use with cross-refs rather than in isolation.
- **OT adequacy practices for integrated data credit**: Characterize integrated events; trace to OT data needs; argue operational relevance (article + environment); define scoring/controls; maintain configuration surveillance and data management that limits inappropriate contractor influence on OT integrity.

## Key Concepts
- **Article representativeness**: Production-representative articles are the traditional OT adequacy baseline. When unavailable, operational-representative articles may support adequacy if the intent is to field the current configuration even if later redesign is planned.
- **End-to-end DT as OT predictor**: Prefer end-to-end integrated system testing before OT; if impossible, document risk and the path to exit criteria (T-3).
- **Safety releases**: PM ensures system-safety process and required safety release before events with known hazards to people, equipment, or environment (T-3).
- **Software/agile evidence**: Frequent releases blur DT/OT; use templates to keep OT readiness visible and run full certification thinking on major end-to-end/capacity events.
- **Deficiency linkage**: Attachment 19-style deficiency identification/resolution feeds candid not-ready lists and exit criteria in the memo.
- **M&S and resources**: Accredited models/simulators and funded instrumentation (template family around A17/A32) are part of sufficiency, not afterthoughts.

## Mental Models
- **DT proves buildability and technical maturity; OT proves mission utility under realistic conditions—MOTRC stitches them.**
- **No data rights / no early contractor data ⇒ weak strategy and weak certification.**
- **Representativeness is a claim that must be argued**, not assumed from a lab serial number.

## Anti-patterns
- Waiting for OT to discover basic technical shortfalls DT should have closed.
- Planning integrated OT credit without traceability or scoring controls.
- Certifying toward OT on non-representative articles without documenting fielding intent and limitations.
- Ignoring contractor-test quality until the final briefing.

## Key Takeaways
1. MS B/C DT sufficiency and continuous MOTRC reinforce each other.
2. CDT + LDTO + contractor-test oversight create the DT evidence spine.
3. Article representativeness and integrated-event credit rules determine OT adequacy risk.
4. Safety, deficiency closure, software maturity, and M&S accreditation are certification inputs.
