# Chapter 4: Certification Memorandum, Gates, and Decertification

## Core Idea
The certification memorandum is the auditable product of MOTRC: it records participant agreement, states readiness for OT within constraints, and proves the process was followed. It is due at least 15 calendar days before scheduled OT&E start (or as mutually agreed) and becomes a benchmark for comparing OT results to projected capabilities.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Memo as gate artifact**: Signed by MDA or PEO by ACAT (MDA is waiver authority on signer). OTO commander acknowledges before OT begins (T-1)—concur or non-concur with reservations.
- **Escalation**: Unresolved Official–OTO disagreement elevates to SAF/AQ and AF/TE.
- **Decertification / recertification**: Official may decertify and return system to DT on written OTO or PM recommendation after stop-test for poor performance; recertify via full memo process after fixes (T-3); revisit relevant templates.
- **Pause alternative**: Short OTO pauses for lesser/temporary issues; repeated pauses may signal need to stop-test and decertify.

## Key Concepts
- **Agile tailoring of the memo**: Embedded/iterative OT and just-in-time mission-thread capstone evaluations need memos that certify readiness for the specific dedicated OT event without blocking external-resource windows. Distinguish integrated DT/OT inside iterative cycles from dedicated OT touchpoints (T-3). OTO must stay velocity-responsive while stating dedicated-OT readiness honestly (T-3).
- **Required memo content (minimum)**: (1) OT description; entrance/exit criteria; which acquisition decision/increment; anticipated OT dates (PEO waiver). (2) How certification was structured/executed (PEO waiver). (3) Templates/line items not ready or qualified—why, elevated risks, action plans/workarounds/exit criteria (T-1). (4) Test limitations/deferrals, rationale, clear-later plans; deferred items still require later OT or requirements change (T-1). (5) Other attributes/mission characteristics not ready or not expected to meet requirements (PEO waiver). (6) Major disagreements with OTO/users/others + rationale (T-1).
- **Addressees**: Memo to OTO commander; info copies tailored by program level across SAF/AQ, AF/TE, AF/A3/A4/A5/8, AFMC A-staff slices or USSF S-staff/TE, capability director, PEO, LDTO, operating/using commands, other participants (T-1; PEO waiver on list).
- **OTO acknowledgment memo**: Sent to the same tailored addressee set (T-1).
- **Pre-brief attendance**: Operating command, OTOs, LDTO, and other participants attend the Official’s briefing at appropriate levels (T-1).

## Mental Models
- **The memo is a contract of candor** among PM, Official, and OTO—not a marketing brochure.
- **15-day (or agreed) lead time** protects OT resource lock-in.
- **Non-concur is a designed path**, not a process failure—escalation exists.

## Anti-patterns
- Enumerating ready/not-ready without summarizing critical processes and residual risk.
- Starting dedicated OT before OTO acknowledgment.
- Decertifying without revisiting templates that failed.
- Using endless 'pauses' to avoid a stop-test decision.

## Key Takeaways
1. Memo documents agreement, constraints, process compliance; ≥15 days before OT (or agreed).
2. Signer = MDA/PEO by ACAT; OTO must acknowledge (concur/non-concur).
3. Content covers structure, not-ready items, limitations/deferrals, disagreements.
4. Decertify → fix → recertify; pauses are for minor/temporary issues only.
