# Chapter 7: Template Structure, Documentation, and Tracking

## Core Idea
Attachments 2–32 are an interlocking checklist matrix spanning the acquisition/modification life cycle. They structure executive briefings, assign suggested OPRs, and consolidate multi-source guidance into action items phrased as statements of fact—not yes/no trivia. Documentation products (TEMP strategy text, briefs, memo, tracking tool) make the process auditable.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Interlocking matrix**: Overlap and cross-references are intentional—risk in one area propagates. Closely associated templates cite each other (e.g., software maturity links).
- **Ends not means**: Templates stress what must be true for readiness; PMs keep flexibility on how. Best practices: full open disclosure, consistent routine reviews, routine multi-party communication.
- **Answering action items**: Brief status narratives, individually or grouped; templates double as future roadmaps toward OT.
- **OPR focus**: One suggested lead OPR per many line items to preserve accountability; final OPR selection is local. PM (+ ITT + CDT/TM) remains OPR for executing the whole process. Higher approval authorities may sit above the working OPR.
- **Tracking tool**: AF/TE maintains an automated certification-template tracking workbook on the AF Portal; modify it when templates are tailored.

## Key Concepts
- **Notional groups**:
  - *Test planning & documentation* — e.g., acquisition strategy/schedule, AoA, CRDs, threat/intel, ITT charter, CONOPS, LCSP, IT/NSS, TEMP, integrated test planning.
  - *System design & performance* — cyber resiliency, contractor testing, government DT&E, software maturity, LFT&E, M&S, CM, deficiencies, representative articles, system performance, OT plan, ESOH reviews.
  - *Test assets & support* — OT team training, support equipment, spares, support agreements, PHS&T, personnel, contractor support, technical data, T&E resources.
- **TEMP Part III**: PM documents the certification-process strategy here (T-3)—the institutional hook between MOTRC and the program’s test strategy artifact.
- **Briefing craft**: Keep template order in the final briefing; executive altitude with supporting detail on demand; allow OTO alternate status views.
- **Template updates**: Process matures via AF Form 847 feedback to AF/TEP as policy, technology, and T&E methods evolve.
- **Glossary/references**: Attachment 1 holds references and abbreviations used across templates—use it rather than inventing local acronyms in briefs.
- **Compliance posture**: Publication states compliance with attachments is mandatory; tiered waiver rules follow DAFI 33-360 patterns, with acquisition-execution-chain mandates handled per AFI 63-101/20-101 (not always classic tier elevation).

## Mental Models
- **Status narrative > checkbox** — executives need risk story, not green dots alone.
- **Cross-refs are navigation**, not optional reading.
- **Tracking tool prevents last-week archaeology.**

## Anti-patterns
- Answering every line item 'yes' without evidence pointers.
- Reordering the final briefing away from the template matrix so seniors lose the map.
- Leaving TEMP Part III silent on how MOTRC will run.
- Tailoring templates in slides but never updating the tracking tool or stakeholder agreements.

## Key Takeaways
1. 31 templates in three chronological groups form the MOTRC checklist matrix.
2. Action items want status narratives; single OPRs keep accountability sharp.
3. TEMP Part III + tracking tool + ordered executive brief + memo = documentation set.
4. Feed improvements back through AF Form 847 to AF/TEP.
