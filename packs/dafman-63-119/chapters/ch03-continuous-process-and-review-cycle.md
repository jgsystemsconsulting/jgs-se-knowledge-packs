# Chapter 3: Continuous Process and Certification Review Cycle

## Core Idea
Certification is a series of systematic template reviews—not a single calendar event tied to one milestone. The PM, users, DT/OT communities, and oversight coordinate regularly; the Certification Official receives candid readiness assessments. Risk management uses lessons learned and historical decision criteria; the PM owns T&E risk-management execution.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three template groups (notional chronology)**: Test Planning and Documentation; System Design and Performance; Test Assets and Support (matrix in Figure 3.1; details in Attachments 2–32).
- **Tailoring early**: Fit information need, size, and complexity; repeat the review cycle as often as needed. Templates are not program-specific—ITT dialogue with the program office is continuous. Tailor/negate only with serious multi-stakeholder consideration.
- **Continuous process**: Not locked to a milestone. Schedule a certification briefing to capture completed integrated test, validate OT done so far, and list remaining OT objectives before dedicated OT (PM–OTO mutual agreement).
- **Review cycle stages**: Pre-certification reviews → assessment (incl. exit criteria) → negotiation of workarounds/limitations → reporting (DT report, final briefing, memo path).

## Key Concepts
- **Start early / series of reviews**: Initial pass marks every template for applicability; set-asides can return if requirements change. Schedule reviews from the integrated master schedule (PM, CDT/TM, OTO) (T-1). Frequency generally rises toward final certification; plan reviews before OT activities such as operational assessments.
- **Integrated-testing tailoring**: OT success partly depends on DT and other data—reviews examine which DT data can support OT. Agile/DevSecOps: more frequent integrated testing; OT fully engaged in release planning (T-3); certification must incorporate adaptive test that informs subsequent cycles (T-3).
- **Cyber resiliency thread**: Detect/recover/continue mission under intentional or accidental cyber events via system security engineering, threat-informed requirements, and iterative cyber T&E. Early MRAP-C / MBCRA prioritizes attack surface and cyber risks. From initiation, integrate cyber testing into DT and OT (T-1); resource and document in TEMP and CRDs (T-3). Architecture/environment/threat changes can force more frequent review of IT/NSS, cyber resilience, and deficiency-resolution templates (A9, A12, A19).
- **Assessment standard**: For each line item—will the system be ready to deliver planned operational capability?—against current requirements, relevant specs/standards, and risk assessment. Cumulative judgments vs exit criteria decide OT readiness.
- **Exit criteria discipline**: For each operational shortfall/deficiency/issue, define specific testable performance-based exit criteria; prefer demonstrated, stabilized performance in an operationally representative environment. Incomplete testing → PEO risk-based impact judgment and completion plan (T-2). Prefer end-to-end integrated system test as OT predictor; else risk-assess alternate approaches and plan of action (T-3).
- **When ideal standards are missed**: e.g., late/incomplete technical orders—manage long-term; compare departures to prior programs’ OT outcomes both individually and in aggregate.
- **Action / get-well plans**: PM coordinates deliberate courses of action for discrepancies blocking smooth OT transition (T-3).
- **Deferred requirements**: Incremental strategies may defer OT of some requirements—coordinate/document with user, reflect in requirements docs, assess operational impacts/dependencies, summarize in final briefing and memo (T-3).
- **Negotiation**: Persistently high/medium risks that affect OT need mission-critical risk treatment (T-3), negotiated workarounds or agreed OT limitations without compromising OT independence.
- **Reporting products**: PM consolidates inputs for Certification Official. LDTO supplies unbiased DT briefing/report with independent OT-completion risk view. Final briefing keeps template order; executive-level; OTO may present differing template status views. Incremental/limited deployment: final cert per release. Agile: DT/OT handover is dynamic—velocity-aware.
- **Final certification minimum**: After all integrated test complete, schedule event covering completed IT, OT sufficiency to date, remaining OT objectives before dedicated OT (T-1), with enough lead time for OT resources/schedules.
- **USD(R&E) / DD(DTE&A)**: Oversight-list programs may receive program or DT sufficiency assessments that must support MOTRC (T-0).

## Mental Models
- **Find problems while they are still cheap** — continuous reviews beat end-of-DT surprises.
- **Exit criteria turn complaints into testable closure conditions.**
- **Cyber is not a late specialty review** — it rides every DT/OT wave.

## Anti-patterns
- One big pre-OT review with no intermediate feedback.
- Setting templates aside permanently without change-control when requirements shift.
- Accepting 'yes/no' template answers without status narrative or risk level.
- Deferring requirements silently without user coordination or later OT plan.

## Key Takeaways
1. Continuous series of template-driven reviews, tailored and scheduled from the IMS.
2. Assessment standard = readiness to deliver planned operational capability vs exit criteria.
3. Negotiation preserves OT independence while enabling workable risk treatment.
4. Cyber resiliency and integrated-test data credit are first-class continuous threads.
