# Chapter: NASA DE acquisition guiding principles

## Core Idea

The digital engineering acquisition framework is a set of approaches for acquiring data products and services that support DE processes. It is not itself an operational system. The framework covers how programs acquire, receive, and control interoperable flow of product definition information (PDI) and product configuration information (PCI), including metadata that spans systems engineering, design, test, procurement, manufacturing planning, operations, maintenance, and sustainment.

Guiding principles tell a buyer what "good" looks like before clause selection: early visibility, FAIR-oriented tagging, enterprise-capable ecosystems, minimum essential data, and future competition kept open.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **PDI and PCI flow.** Product definition and product configuration information, plus related metadata, move across the product and data lifecycles under controlled acquisition approaches.
- **KDP-aligned flow.** Programs review and reuse applicable NPRs, standards, DRDs, and DE enterprise solutions so information crosses milestone deliveries at key decision points from Pre-Phase A through Phase F.
- **FAIR-oriented assets.** Data assets carry attributes and metadata that support findability, accessibility, interoperability, and reuse, within security and access limits.
- **Center-agnostic ecosystem preference.** Prefer Agency enterprise solutions when they fit; accept local program deployments when they must.

## Key Concepts

- **Early and continuous access.** Data should be visible, accessible, and understandable to potential users as early as security allows, and stay that way through the lifecycle.
- **Metadata interoperability.** Metadata standards should align with applicable national and international consensus practice so Center systems can retrieve the tags.
- **Shared-space access.** Assets live in shared spaces at the appropriate Centers except where law, policy, or classification limits access.
- **Publication methods.** Accessible assets conform to Agency data publication methods consistent with current and planned information systems.
- **Trust anchors.** Assets carry information-assurance and security metadata and an identified authoritative contractor or Agency source.
- **Process reuse.** Interoperability rides business and mission processes that reuse Agency information-system capabilities, reinforced by producer-consumer communities and training.
- **Minimum essential data.** Acquire the contractor-originated data required to meet lifecycle needs, with traceability, not a maximal dump.
- **Future competition.** Managers and Technical Authorities weigh data requirements that keep later procurements competitive.

## Mental Models

- **Principles before RCL.** Use section 6 as a filter; only then open Appendix A selection.
- **Tagging is delivery.** Untagged data that cannot be found is not delivered in practice.
- **Enterprise default, local exception.** Start Agency-wide; justify local forks.

## Key Takeaways

- Framework means acquisition approaches, not a product SKU.
- PDI/PCI interoperable flow is the object of control.
- FAIR-oriented metadata and authoritative sources enable trust.
- Buy minimum essential data with future re-compete in mind.
- Principles constrain sections 7 and 8 choices.
