# Chapter: RCL and selection matrix method

## Core Idea

Appendix A holds Recommended Contract Language (RCL) and a selection matrix. RCL statements are numbered guidance lines a program can select and tailor. The matrix is a selection method that maps planning needs to candidate statements. This pack describes the method. It does not copy matrix cells, RCL list rows, or clause prose.

RCL becomes binding only after incorporation into a DRD, SOW/PWS, solicitation, contract, or other acquisition instrument. Until then it remains handbook guidance.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Numbered RCL pool.** Statements carry identifiers for selection and traceability across reviews and tailoring cycles.
- **Selection matrix.** A structured way to mark which statements fit a given acquisition profile after the section 7 question pass.
- **Tailor-then-incorporate.** Select, adjust to program risk and data shape, then place into binding instruments with acquisition and contracting support.
- **Traceability loop.** Keep a record of why each statement was chosen so later baseline changes can drop or extend language deliberately.

## Key Concepts

- **Guidance ceiling.** RCL does not independently establish a requirement inside the handbook.
- **Coverage families.** Matrix rows group language around themes such as data management, rights and access, interoperability and exchange, configuration and data management, and related acquisition controls. Exact cell text stays in the source.
- **Fit to criticality.** Higher criticality and deeper digital-thread dependence justify denser RCL sets; low-risk commercial slices may take a thinner set.
- **No silent full-paste.** Selecting every row "to be safe" creates unpriced scope and unenforceable noise.
- **Link to DRDs.** Many RCL statements exist to point work at DRDs that carry contents, format, and timing detail.

## Mental Models

- **Matrix is a filter, not a dump.** Empty columns after a real question pass mean "do not buy that language."
- **ID before edit.** Keep statement identifiers while tailoring so configuration control can see lineage.
- **Pair with cost.** Every retained RCL cluster should have a cost-volume home.

## Anti-patterns

- Copying the matrix into a proposal as if it were the contract.
- Selecting RCL without answering section 7 questions.
- Dropping identifiers during tailoring so traceability dies.
- Treating Appendix A as optional reading after award.

## Key Takeaways

- Appendix A is a selectable RCL library plus a selection matrix method.
- Use after guiding questions; incorporate only what the buy needs.
- Numbered IDs support traceability through tailoring.
- Binding force comes from the contract instrument, not the handbook page.
- No matrix cells or RCL rows are reproduced in this pack.
