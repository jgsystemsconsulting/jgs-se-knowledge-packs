# Chapter: DE data acquisition guiding questions

## Core Idea

Section 7 is a planning method, not a checklist to paste into a solicitation. It steers which Recommended Contract Language (RCL) statements from Appendix A belong in the buy. It assumes the make/buy analysis under the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) has already run. The questions force the team to name what is being bought, what data rights matter, what deliverables exist, and what heritage or commercial path applies before anyone drafts clauses.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Question-led RCL selection.** Answer planning questions first; open the selection matrix second.
- **Procurement-strategy conversation.** Questions are for initial planning sessions that shape strategy, not for after-the-fact compliance theater.
- **Heritage vs new.** Follow-on buys carry explicit change, lesson, and "what worked" probes so prior gaps do not repeat.
- **Criticality and schedule coupling.** Effort criticality (including safety-criticality concepts referenced via MIL-STD-882E) and period-of-performance risk sit beside data-rights questions.

## Key Concepts

Kinds of questions the method asks (paraphrased; cells not copied):

- **Buy identity.** What is being acquired? End item, service, or research and development? Overarching goals?
- **Heritage path.** Done before? New or follow-on? What changed? What lessons apply? What worked and what failed?
- **Commercial path.** Does FAR Part 12 commercial acquisition apply? Other items or services to fold in?
- **Deliverable and technology shape.** What are the deliverables? Is new technology in play?
- **Rights and scope.** What data-rights considerations apply? Which needs are mission-critical versus optional or alternate?
- **Cost and customer map.** Rough order of magnitude? Who is supported? Who are the customers?
- **Vehicle shape.** For indefinite-delivery vehicles, can scope and ceiling absorb other Centers later?
- **Criticality and time.** Criticality level? Required schedule? Cost of missing it?
- **Later sections expand.** Further question groups cover data definition needs, interoperability, security and access, repository targets, and closeout expectations so RCL picks stay traceable to a stated need.

## Mental Models

- **Answer then select.** An RCL row without a answered planning question is decoration.
- **Follow-on is not free.** Heritage without a change/lessons pass imports yesterday's data gaps.
- **Options are scope.** Nice-to-haves belong in explicit options, not silent scope growth inside data calls.

## Key Takeaways

- Guiding questions gate Appendix A selection.
- Cover buy identity, heritage, commercial path, deliverables, rights, cost, vehicle, criticality, and schedule.
- Method assumes make/buy already decided.
- Do not copy question tables into contracts; use them in planning.
- Trace each selected RCL back to at least one answered need.
