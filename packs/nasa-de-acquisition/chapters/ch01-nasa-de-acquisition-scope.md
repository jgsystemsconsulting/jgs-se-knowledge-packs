# Chapter: Scope and cited-document classes

## Core Idea

NASA-HDBK-1004A is a NASA Technical Handbook. It guides how programs and projects plan a Digital Engineering (DE) acquisition framework: which data and models to buy, how to make them interoperable, and how to place those choices into solicitations and contracts. It does not, by itself, create binding requirements. Requirements bind only when a directive, standard, contract, grant, or agreement invokes them.

The handbook treats Recommended Contract Language (RCL) as selectable guidance. Teams tailor chosen RCL into a Data Requirements Description (DRD), Statement of Work or Performance Work Statement (SOW/PWS), solicitation, or other binding instrument. After incorporation, acquisition and contracting staff may use shall-language as needed for enforceability.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Handbook vs standard.** A NASA Technical Standard states what must be accomplished. A handbook states how an objective may be accomplished: practices, methods, examples, and options. This pack follows that distinction.
- **DE acquisition framework.** An approach set for acquiring data products and services that feed DE processes, not an operational system bought as a product.
- **Applicability window.** Guidance is aimed at NASA programs and projects that perform or acquire digital engineering work across Pre-Phase A through Phase F, sized to the program's scope, risk, data needs, and contractual environment.
- **Cited-document classes.** Section 2 groups references that support the guidance. Classes include Agency directives and NPRs, NASA standards and handbooks, consensus and industry standards, and acquisition regulations. Names stay with their publishers; this pack does not quote them.

## Key Concepts

- **Guidance status.** RCL statements do not independently establish a requirement. Selection and tailoring come first; binding force comes only after incorporation into a contract instrument.
- **Discretion.** Programs exercise judgment on which RCL and DRD templates fit the buy. One-size language is not the point.
- **Order of precedence.** Handbook guidance does not supersede or waive other applicable Agency documentation. Conflicts resolve through the delegated NASA Technical Authority.
- **Companion practitioner depth.** Where the handbook defers deeper digital-engineering practice detail, it points to a companion handbook (NASA-HDBK-1009). This pack records the pairing and does not reproduce that companion.
- **Public release.** Cover line on the vetted edition is APPROVED FOR PUBLIC RELEASE--DISTRIBUTION IS UNLIMITED. No printed DOPSR case appears on the cover text layer.

## Mental Models

- **Select then bind.** Choose RCL and DRD content as guidance, then place tailored text into instruments that actually bind the contractor.
- **Name, do not dump.** Section 2 is a map of document classes, not a bibliography to paste into a pack chapter.
- **Handbook ceiling.** If a need requires a hard requirement, look to the governing directive, standard, or contract clause family, not to this handbook alone.

## Key Takeaways

- Pin is NASA-HDBK-1004A, approved 2026-10-26.
- Scope is DE acquisition planning and implementation guidance for NASA programs and projects.
- RCL and DRD material become binding only after incorporation into a contract instrument.
- Cited standards and regulations are named, not quoted.
- Third-party works stay with their publishers.
