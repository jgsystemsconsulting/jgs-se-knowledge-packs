# Chapter: Lessons learned for DE data acquisition

## Core Idea

Section 5 records failure patterns from large NASA programs when digital data acquisition was weak or missing in the contract. The primary pattern is simple: omit data rights and delivery requirements up front, and access during performance and at closeout becomes a second, expensive buy. Tailored template language reduces that risk for the life of the data.

Programs cited include the Space Shuttle Program (SSP), International Space Station (ISS), Launch Services Program (LSP), and large efforts such as Commercial Crew, Constellation, and Orion. Use the patterns when reading guiding principles, guiding questions, and contract-language sections.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Up-front rights vs end-of-contract scramble.** Access planned in the solicitation beats a closeout negotiation for lost artifacts.
- **Media and stewardship debt.** Mixed paper, microfiche, and digital media without stewardship rules create long-term translation and records-management cost.
- **Security classification lag.** Export control, CUI, and related markings decided late produce backlogs that cannot be dispositioned cleanly.
- **Multi-system lock-in.** When no single system holds the needed functions and data shapes, programs keep parallel systems and pay conversion or proprietary-code fees later.

## Key Concepts

- **SSP pattern.** Design data and drawings were often not contractual deliverables; multiple designs across the program left Centers without shared reference material. End-of-contract ownership was not defined, so recovery spent large sums.
- **ISS pattern.** Multiple parts and disposition systems persist because none alone covers required function and data form. Both SSP and ISS faced large payments for proprietary code needed to run formerly contractor-owned software; without the code, data was unusable or conversion-heavy.
- **Closeout surprise.** Asking a payload-processing contractor at end-of-contract for work procedures that were never deliverables is a planning failure, not a contractor surprise.
- **LSP and commercial-line pattern.** Similar access and format gaps appear when DE data needs are treated as afterthoughts on commercial and launch-service buys.

## Mental Models

- **Silence is a purchase order.** What the contract does not say about data becomes a future line item.
- **Format is a rights problem.** Rights without a usable encoding still leave the Agency blind.
- **Mark early.** Classification and export markings decided at creation beat bulk remediation.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating data rights as optional boilerplate to drop under schedule pressure.
- Accepting contractor-native formats with no neutral-exchange path into NASA systems.
- Deferring records stewardship and classification until closeout.
- Assuming heritage programs already "own" the digital thread without checking deliverable lists.

## Key Takeaways

- Primary lesson: missing data-rights language means paid re-access later.
- Mixed media and weak stewardship raise lifecycle cost.
- Late security markings create disposition backlogs.
- Proprietary runtime dependence can strand otherwise delivered data.
- Use these patterns to stress-test sections 6 through 8 before source selection.
