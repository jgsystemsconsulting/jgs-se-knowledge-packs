# Systems Engineering Resources

## Core Idea

The source closes its teaching with a curated shelf: publications specific to ITS, general systems engineering references, training providers focused on transportation, and US DOT software tools. Every resource flagged by the laptop icon across the document collects here, so the chapter functions as the pack's bibliography; this chapter records what the shelf contains, named but not linked, per pack policy.

## Frameworks Introduced

- A four-way resource taxonomy: ITS-specific publications, general SE references, training, and tools.
- The tool family as a coherent set: the national reference architecture as template source, a regional customization tool, and a project-level drawing and definition tool that stay compatible with each other.

## Key Concepts

ITS-specific publications include the regulation and policy pages for 23 CFR 940 and the FTA architecture consistency policy; the Caltrans Local Assistance Procedures Manual's Systems Engineering Review Form; the national cooperative research report on contracting ITS projects; a state DOT's published SEMP and systems engineering templates; the July 2022 federal memorandum on systems engineering for ITS projects; and the Model Systems Engineering Documents series covering traffic signal systems, dynamic message signs, closed-circuit television, and (forthcoming at publication) transportation sensors and detection systems.

General references named: INCOSE as the professional society; ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2015 as the life cycle processes standard; a federal risk-based asset management report; the NASA systems engineering handbook; the Department of Defense systems engineering plan preparation guide; a systems engineering design textbook (Buede); the FHWA final report on applying Scrum methods to ITS projects; and the federal configuration management publication page.

Training options concentrate on transportation: the National Highway Institute (instructor-led and web courses), the federal professional capacity building program, a university consortium for transportation education, training attached to the national ITS reference architecture (web-based through facilitated regional workshops), and INCOSE for the general discipline. Tools: ARC-IT supplies physical objects, interfaces, service packages, functional objects, and requirements as building blocks; RAD-IT personalizes ARC-IT for a region; SET-IT produces project architectures and supports Concept of Operations and requirements definition, based on and compatible with RAD-IT.

## Mental Models

- Start from the shelf, not from scratch: the model documents, templates, and forms let an agency adopt artifacts already in circulation.
- Tools implement the architecture chain: regional planning tools and project tools share a data lineage, so regional choices flow into project drawings.

## Anti-patterns

None stated. The source notes for its tool mentions that describing a tool does not constitute endorsement; that caution applies to the resource list as a whole.

## Key Takeaways

- The model SE documents series is the fastest route to compliant analysis for common field-device projects.
- Training for transportation-specific systems engineering runs through federal programs and the national architecture's training pages; INCOSE covers the general discipline.
- The ARC-IT, RAD-IT, and SET-IT trio carries the architecture from regional to project level.
