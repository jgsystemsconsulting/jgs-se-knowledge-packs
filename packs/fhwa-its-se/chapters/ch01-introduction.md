# Introduction: purpose, audience, navigation

## Core Idea

The document introduces transportation professionals to a needs-focused, requirements-driven engineering process, known in the broader industry as systems engineering, so that the risk of procuring technology that does not meet their needs goes down. It describes the systems engineering project life cycle, how to manage a systems engineering process, and how to start applying the approach on an agency's next ITS project and then fold it into business processes more broadly. This is the fourth generation of the Systems Engineering for ITS content, published December 2022, and it merges and updates two earlier FHWA publications: the Systems Engineering Guidebook and the Systems Engineering Handbook.

The edition is aligned with the fourth edition of the INCOSE Systems Engineering Handbook, which in turn is consistent with ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2015, the international standard for system life cycle processes. The source is explicitly a resource and learning tool, not formal US DOT guidance on meeting the systems engineering requirements of 23 CFR part 940 (ITS Architecture and Standards) or the parallel FTA policy for transit projects; compliance is determined by each FHWA Division Office and FTA Regional Office, and readers are told to contact their federal representative for state-specific requirements.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Fourth-generation consolidation: guidebook and handbook content combined into one document, aligned to INCOSE and ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 rather than to a transportation-only process definition.
- A stated scope boundary: the document teaches the process; it does not rule on regulatory compliance.
- An icon system that tags content by kind: suggestions from experience, warnings about approaches that have failed, resource pointers collected in chapter 5, tailoring notes for smaller projects, software tool mentions (no endorsement), regulation and policy references, and terminology definitions.

## Key Concepts

The primary audience is public sector managers and staff who deploy ITS projects; system owners, operators, maintainers, and private sector professionals wanting a primer also fit. The reader is assumed to have a transportation background and some knowledge of ITS, but no prior systems engineering knowledge. Systems engineers are deliberately not the target audience, though they may still value the ITS application and tailoring discussion; the document is for professionals who must manage contractor or agency teams developing an ITS project.

Navigation follows the project story: chapter 2 defines terms, principles, life cycle models, and the federal regulation and policy background; chapter 3 walks an ITS system from operations planning through retirement and adds the cross-cutting processes; chapter 4 gives the agency perspective on risk evaluation, tailoring, procurement, and implementation of the regulation; chapter 5 lists resources; chapter 6 provides documentation templates; chapter 7 is the glossary and acronym list.

## Mental Models

- Learning tool over rulebook: the introduction states the document will not make anyone a systems engineering expert, and it points to the wide set of external resources it flags along the way.
- Icons as a reading filter: a reader can chase only warnings, only tailoring notes, or only regulation references, depending on the question at hand.
- Federal-aid context from the start: the regulation and its FTA counterpart are treated as standing constraints on ITS projects funded from the Highway Trust Fund, with the document as preparation rather than substitute.

## Anti-patterns

The source refuses one role outright: it is not formal guidance from US DOT on how to meet the systems engineering requirements in 23 CFR part 940, and treating it as compliance authority is flagged as a mistake. Compliance questions belong with the FHWA Division Office or FTA Regional Office.

## Key Takeaways

- The pack's source is the December 2022 fourth generation, aligned with INCOSE and ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2015.
- Aim the content at project managers and agency staff, not at systems engineering specialists.
- Every later chapter assumes the frame set here: needs-focused, requirements-driven, tailored to project risk.
- The regulation and policy references throughout are pointers, not compliance determinations.
