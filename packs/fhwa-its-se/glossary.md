# Glossary

Terms the source defines or characterises, alphabetical. Each entry names the chapter that uses it.

- **Acceptance**: action by the acquirer's authorized representative taking ownership of products, in whole or part performance of the contract. ch03, ch07
- **Acquirer**: organization that procures products for itself or another. ch07
- **Baseline**: an approved product at a point in time; changes go through a formal change process. ch02, ch03, ch07
- **Concept of Operations (ConOps)**: document defining the way the system is envisioned to work from multiple stakeholder viewpoints. ch02, ch03, ch06, ch07
- **Configuration item (CI)**: a product (document, hardware unit, software unit) chosen for change control; a baseline is a configuration item. ch03, ch07
- **Configuration management (CM)**: discipline of technical and administrative direction that identifies and documents CI characteristics, audits conformance, controls changes, and records status. ch03, ch06, ch07
- **Control (decision) gate**: formal decision point in the life cycle where owner and stakeholders judge a phase complete before the next begins. ch02, ch03, ch07
- **Decomposition**: successively breaking the system into buildable or procurable components, functional and physical. ch02, ch03, ch07
- **Elicitation**: drawing out and discovering knowledge, used in defining needs and requirements. ch03, ch07
- **Enabling products**: products that let the end product be developed, supported, and maintained (compilers, test environments, training materials). ch03, ch07
- **End products**: the hardware, software, communications, and databases that perform the desired capability. ch03, ch07
- **Gap analysis**: technique comparing current (legacy) capability against identified needs, used to prioritize development and replacement decisions. ch03, ch07
- **Incremental development**: building a planned, fully specified system through successive deployment increments. ch02, ch07
- **Intelligent Transportation System (ITS)**: electronics, communications, or information processing used to improve the efficiency or safety of a surface transportation system. ch01, ch07
- **ITS project**: any project that funds, wholly or partly, acquisition of technologies providing or significantly contributing to ITS user services. ch02, ch07
- **Life cycle**: end-to-end process from system conception to retirement or disposal. ch02, ch07
- **Life cycle model**: representation of the steps in development and the other phases of a project (sequential, incremental, iterative). ch02, ch07
- **Operational concept**: in ITS usage, the roles and responsibilities of participating agencies and stakeholders in operating and implementing the systems of a regional architecture. ch03, ch07
- **Regional ITS Architecture**: regional framework for ensuring institutional agreement and technical integration of ITS projects in a region. ch02, ch03, ch04, ch07
- **Regression testing**: testing not only the changed area but also the unchanged areas the change could affect. ch03, ch07
- **Requirement**: governs what (functional), how well (performance), and under what conditions (environmental, non-functional) a product achieves its purpose. ch02, ch03, ch07
- **Risk**: undesirable situation with both a likelihood of occurring and a potentially negative consequence for the project. ch03, ch04, ch07
- **Service packages**: service-oriented building blocks of the national ITS reference architecture, tailored to real-world transportation needs and implemented through projects. ch04, ch05, ch07
- **Stakeholders**: the people the system is built for, plus anyone who manages, develops, operates, maintains, uses, or is affected by it. ch03, ch07
- **System**: integrated composite of people, products, and processes providing a capability to satisfy a stated need or objective. ch02, ch07
- **System of systems**: a system whose elements are themselves systems, achieving a mission none could achieve alone. ch07
- **Systems Engineering Analysis (SEA)**: the documented analysis required for ITS projects, covering architecture mapping, agency roles, requirements, alternatives, procurement options, standards, and operations provisions. ch02, ch04
- **Systems Engineering Management Plan (SEMP)**: top-level plan for managing the systems engineering effort; framework written by management, completed by performing teams. ch03, ch04, ch06, ch07
- **Tailoring**: planning SE activities appropriate and cost-effective for project size and complexity, based on cost, size, stakeholders, interfaces, ownership, resources, and risks. ch03, ch04, ch07
- **Trade-off study**: objective evaluation of alternative requirements, architectures, designs, or solutions against identical criteria. ch03, ch04, ch07
- **User needs**: expressions of the operational needs the system must meet; uniquely identifiable, major desired capabilities, solution free, with rationale. ch03
- **Validation**: confirming the system fulfills its intended use and meets the user's needs; also the in-process confirmation of step outputs by stakeholders. ch03, ch07
- **Verification**: confirming a product meets its specified requirements; ensures the product was "built right". ch03, ch07
- **Work breakdown structure (WBS)**: product-oriented, hierarchical listing of hardware, software, services, and work tasks defining a program. ch06, ch07
