# Chapter 1: Purpose, Scope, and General Principles

## Core Idea
OMB Circular A-94 (revised Nov. 9, 2023) promotes social welfare through well-informed federal decision-making by giving general guidance for benefit-cost and cost-effectiveness analyses and specific discount-rate policy for programs whose benefits and costs unfold over time. It rescinds the 1992 circular of the same number.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Dual guidance**: General BCA/CEA good-practice elements plus mandatory discount-rate rules for analyses submitted to OMB.
- **Authority**: Issued under 31 U.S.C. 1111 and the Budget and Accounting Act of 1921, as amended.
- **Binding vs suggested**: Suggested for internal Executive Branch planning; must be followed for analyses submitted to OMB under Circulars A-11 and A-19 and for estimates under the 1992 Presidential Memorandum on benefits and costs of legislative proposals.
- **Law/EO carve-out**: Does not supersede agency practices prescribed by law, Executive Order, or other relevant circulars.

## Key Concepts
- **Decision support goal**: Inform whether to initiate, renew, expand, contract, or stop programs/projects that use economic analysis.
- **Discounted net benefits**: Primary outcome measure comparing benefits and costs in present-value terms.
- **Cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA)**: Used when benefits are fixed or hard to monetize; still requires careful cost measurement and discounting.
- **Analysis elements**: Clear baseline, alternatives, time horizon, benefit/cost identification and measurement, inflation treatment, discounting, uncertainty, distributional incidence, and transparent reporting.
- **Exceptions/scope limits**: Circular lists categories and contexts where other guidance or statutory rules control; always check the circular's scope section against the decision type.

## Mental Models
- **A-94 is a decision-quality checklist for federal economic analysis, not a substitute for program-specific statutes.**
- **If OMB will see the package, treat A-94 as mandatory process discipline.**

## Anti-patterns
- Using internal-only shortcuts when the same analysis will later be submitted to OMB.
- Ignoring law/EO-prescribed methods that override circular defaults.
- Treating CEA and BCA as interchangeable without stating why benefits are not monetized.

## Key Takeaways
1. A-94 exists to improve social-welfare-informed federal decisions via BCA/CEA and discount-rate policy.
2. 2023 revision replaces the 1992 circular.
3. Mandatory for specified OMB submissions; suggested for internal planning.
4. Discounted net benefits and structured analysis elements are the core principle set.
