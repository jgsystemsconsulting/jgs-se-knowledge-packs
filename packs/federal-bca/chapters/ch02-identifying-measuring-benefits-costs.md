# Chapter 2: Identifying and Measuring Benefits and Costs

## Core Idea
Sound BCA starts by identifying the full set of social benefits and costs relative to a clear baseline, then measuring them in commensurate units—preferably monetized—while avoiding double-counting and omitted external effects.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Baseline discipline**: Define the world without the policy/program alternative; benefits and costs are differences from that baseline.
- **Comprehensive identification**: Include direct and indirect effects, externalities, and opportunity costs—not only budgetary outlays.
- **Measurement hierarchy**: Prefer transparent, evidence-based monetization; when monetization fails, use rigorous non-monetized measures and/or CEA framing.

## Key Concepts
- **Opportunity cost**: Value of resources in their next-best use; central to both public investment and regulatory-style analyses.
- **Transfers vs real resource effects**: Distinguish pure transfers from social resource costs/benefits so net social measures are not distorted.
- **With/without vs before/after**: Attribute changes to the alternative, not to unrelated trends.
- **Time profile**: Date benefits and costs when they occur; later periods feed discounting (ch03) and uncertainty (ch04).
- **Inflation consistency**: Real vs nominal measurement must match the discount-rate basis (ch03); A-94 treats inflation explicitly so analysts do not mix bases.
- **Avoid double counting**: Example patterns include counting both asset purchase costs and the full stream of services without adjusting capital accounting.

## Mental Models
- **Budget cost is not social cost.**
- **If you cannot explain the baseline, you cannot defend the delta.**

## Anti-patterns
- Listing only agency budget line items as 'costs.'
- Mixing real and nominal dollars in one present-value sum.
- Counting the same benefit in two categories (e.g., willingness-to-pay and separate consumer surplus add-ons incorrectly).

## Key Takeaways
1. Identify benefits/costs as baseline deltas, including externalities and opportunity costs.
2. Measure consistently (real/nominal) with transparent evidence.
3. Separate transfers from real resource effects.
4. Inflation treatment must match discounting basis.
