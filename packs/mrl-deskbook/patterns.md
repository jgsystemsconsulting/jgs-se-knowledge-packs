# Patterns: Manufacturing Readiness Level Deskbook (Version 2025)

Reusable Version 2025 practice patterns. Each has When / How / Trade-offs.

## Pattern 1: Target before score

**When:** A team wants to "assign an MRL" to an element without a pathway context.

**How:** Pick pathway and decision point first (MCA phase, MTA path, UCA urgency). Name the target criteria set. Then assess evidence against that set. Record residual risk if short.

**Trade-offs:** Slower than a single number. Prevents false precision and grade-chasing.

## Pattern 2: Nine-thread coverage pass

**When:** An assessment report discusses only design or process maturity.

**How:** Walk Technology and Industrial Base; Design; Cost and Funding; Materials; Process Capability and Control; Quality; Manufacturing Workforce (Engineering and Production); Facilities; Manufacturing Management. Mark each as covered, tailored, or residual.

**Trade-offs:** More SME time. Surfaces supplier, workforce, and facilities gaps early.

## Pattern 3: Self-assessment then independent test

**When:** Site time is limited and the assessed organization already has shop knowledge.

**How:** Require a tailored self-assessment against target level before the visit. Use the visit to test claims, not to discover the basic story cold. Keep independent judgment in the report.

**Trade-offs:** Depends on assessed-organization honesty and skill. Still beats a cold walk with no prep.

## Pattern 4: Assessment-to-MMP loop

**When:** Any element misses its target.

**How:** Write an MMP with problem, options, schedule, funding, actions, prototype or test linkage, thresholds, and open criteria. Responsible organization reviews. Fund and monitor through SETRs and management reviews.

**Trade-offs:** Paper load rises. Converts score theater into executable risk work.

## Pattern 5: Contract the verbs

**When:** A program hopes criteria language alone will drive contractor behavior.

**How:** Put maturity expectations in acquisition planning, RFP Sections L and M, SOW tasks, and DIDs for assessments and MMPs. Align quality and AS6500A clauses. Flow requirements to suppliers.

**Trade-offs:** Contracting complexity. Creates enforceable tasking criteria cannot create alone.

## Pattern 6: Adapt without erasing threads

**When:** S and T, depot, ship, single-system, or industry gate contexts do not match high-rate MCA imagery.

**How:** Keep the thread lens. Change environment expectations and vocabulary. Record conscious disposition for thin threads. Map company gates to manufacturing questions.

**Trade-offs:** Requires judgment. Avoids fake pilot-line claims and silent thread deletion.

## Pattern 7: OT screening inside manufacturing risk

**When:** Shop-floor networks and equipment can stop lines or corrupt quality.

**How:** Ask basic OT cybersecurity questions during the MRL Assessment. Treat gaps as manufacturing residual risk. Escalate technical depth to cyber specialists. Do not relabel the assessment as a full cyber audit.

**Trade-offs:** Adds agenda time. Catches major OT blind spots without running a certification program.

## Pattern 8: Workbook as console

**When:** Teams need interactive navigation of threads, levels, and questions.

**How:** Use the external Excel Users Guide worksheets. Copy and trim the questionnaire to target level and item. Leave matrix cells and pop-up text in the workbook.

**Trade-offs:** Depends on workbook availability. Keeps this pack free of cell reproduction.

## Pattern 9: Environment honesty

**When:** Evidence from a lab is offered against a pilot-line or production target.

**How:** Name the demonstration environment explicitly. Accept lab evidence for early targets only. Require production-relevant, production-representative, pilot-line, or production-line evidence as targets rise.

**Trade-offs:** May delay claimed maturity. Protects decision makers from environment inflation.
