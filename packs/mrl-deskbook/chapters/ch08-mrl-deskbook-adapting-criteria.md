# Chapter: Effectively Adapting and Using MRL Criteria

## Core Idea

Appendix C explains how to adapt MRL criteria when the default major-system production story does not fit. Adaptation changes emphasis, timing, and vocabulary. It does not invent a second matrix. Threads that look inapplicable still need a conscious disposition: tailor them, defer them, or record why residual risk is accepted. Ignoring a thread without that judgment is not adaptation.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Science and technology environment

S and T work lives earlier than full acquisition baselines. Applied research and Advanced Technology Development need manufacturing risk visibility without pretending a pilot line already exists.

- Early research focuses on manufacturing implications, material and process concepts, and industrial-base signals.
- Advanced Technology Development raises the bar on prototype realism and on which threads must move before transition into a program of record.
- Examples in the deskbook show how teams keep criteria language while shrinking scope to the technology under test.

Summary pattern: keep the thread lens, lower environment expectations to match the S and T stage, and document the transition target the technology must hit before handoff.

### Sustainment, product support, and depot activity

MRL thinking supports sustainment maturity and depot work, not only new production.

- Product support management can use criteria to judge whether repair, overhaul, and supply strategies rest on real manufacturing and repair capability.
- Logistics assessments gain a manufacturing-risk vocabulary for organic and contractor support postures.
- Depot activation scenarios, including unplanned capability stand-up, benefit from explicit maturity targets so facilities, workforce, materials, and process control are not assumed.

Relationship talk between manufacturing readiness ideas and sustainment maturity labels is orientation. Teams still map local sustainment gates to the manufacturing evidence they need.

### Single or limited system acquisition, including ships

Some acquisitions produce one or few articles. Satellites, ships, and other limited-quantity systems still need manufacturing maturity, but rate-centric full-rate production imagery misleads.

- Single or limited systems except ships: emphasize design maturity, special handling, facilities capacity for the actual build count, and supplier fragility when no second source exists.
- Ship programs: multiple yards, long build spans, and modular integration change how pilot-line and production-representative ideas apply. Functional assemblies may mature on different clocks.
- System-of-systems limits: assessing every subsystem the same way may be impossible; choose the units that carry manufacturing risk for the limited buy.

### Industry vocabulary and gated product development

Industry partners often speak in company stage-gate language rather than DoD milestone names. Adaptation maps deskbook targets onto those gates without losing thread coverage. A company may say business decision gate where a program office says milestone. The manufacturing questions remain.

Quality management systems such as ISO 9000/9001 families and AS6500-series manufacturing management practice often host the adapted activities. They do not replace MRL criteria. They give industry a process home for the work.

## Key Concepts

- **Conscious disposition of threads.** If a sub-thread cannot apply, record why and what residual risk remains.
- **Environment honesty.** S and T labs are not pilot lines. Limited builds are not high-rate ammunition lines.
- **Depot as manufacturing.** Stand-up and repair capability have facilities, workforce, materials, and process-control risks of their own.
- **Vocabulary bridge.** Keep criteria substance when swapping DoD labels for industry gate names.

## Mental Models

**Same lens, different distance.** Adaptation changes how close the evidence sits to full production, not whether manufacturing risk matters.

**Ships and singles break rate metaphors.** Ask what "enough maturity" means for the actual quantity and integration path.

**Standards host, criteria judge.** ISO and AS6500-class systems implement process. MRL criteria still judge manufacturing maturity and risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Appendix C covers S and T, sustainment and depot, single or limited systems including ships, and industry gated development.
- Adaptation is deliberate tailoring with residual-risk notes, not silent deletion of threads.
- Limited-quantity and ship contexts rewrite how rate and pilot-line ideas apply.
- Industry vocabulary can carry the same manufacturing questions across company gates.
- No matrix cells are reproduced; adaptation still points at Appendix A structure.
