# Chapter: Manufacturing Maturation Plans and Risk Management

## Core Idea

An MRL Assessment exists to surface manufacturing risk so the program or project can reduce or remove it. The primary follow-on artifact is the Manufacturing Maturation Plan. Every assessment element that misses its target MRL should carry an associated MMP. A low label early in acquisition is not automatically failure. It is a signal for investment and workaround planning before the next phase gate.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Assessment to MMP loop

1. Assess current conditions against target criteria.
2. Name elements below target (technologies, components, items, subsystems, processes, suppliers).
3. Judge impact if the element fails to mature, including difficulty, time, and cost to recover or work around.
4. Write an MMP for each material shortfall.
5. Fund, execute, and monitor the plan through reviews and later assessments.

Technology transition plans and broader risk, issue, and opportunity management sit beside MMPs. The deskbook points readers to the DoD risk management guide for acquisition programs for the wider process.

### MMP outline essentials

The assessed organization prepares the plan. The responsible organization reviews and guides. Typical contents the deskbook expects in an MMP:

- Title line for the element under recovery
- Problem framing: what the element is, how it sits in the system, where maturity lags the target, driving issues, and cost/schedule/performance risk class
- Option set: preferred path benefits plus fallback paths and their consequences
- Calendar and money: maturation schedule with funding breakout
- Work on the preferred path (key activities)
- Ready-state for the alternate path
- Last responsible moment to switch paths
- Whether money is actually available to run the manufacturing recovery plan
- Named actions and owners
- Hardware or test articles built, and how that test setting maps to the real manufacturing setting
- Performance thresholds the recovery must hit
- Which MRL criteria are already closed and which remain open

### Risk-management best-practice bands

1. **Recognize manufacturing risk as first-class.** Treat it on par with other program risk. See manufacturing maturity as part of cost, schedule, and performance success, especially at technology transition.
2. **Manage it inside the program fabric.** Assess, plan, budget, and manage toward maturity and cost targets. Fold assessment results into broader programmatic risk work.
3. **Monitor mitigation.** Know the MRL picture for every technology or product under consideration. Baseline early in each phase. Put support tasking in statements of work. Do not rely only on assessed-organization self-views. Bring manufacturing progress into management reviews, systems engineering technical reviews, and status reporting.
4. **Use outside SMEs.** Draw on product-center manufacturing expertise, manufacturing technology programs, trained external SMEs, and Defense Contract Management Agency insight on shop-floor strengths and weaknesses.
5. **Build staff skill.** Use available deskbook tools and training so responsible-organization staff can spot and mitigate manufacturing risk.

## Key Concepts

- **MMP as attachment.** The plan follows the assessment report. It is not optional decoration when targets are missed.
- **Supplier depth.** Plans cover supplier and sub-tier shortfalls, not only prime-line gaps.
- **Early signal value.** Naming a low MRL early can channel investment before the shortfall becomes a phase-exit crisis.
- **Contract link.** Section 6 of the deskbook carries suggested statement-of-work language and related data item descriptions for assessments and MMPs.

## Mental Models

**Shortfall plus impact plus path.** A useful MMP states what is immature, why it matters, and how maturity will be grown or worked around under time and money constraints.

**Preferred path and escape hatch.** Keep an alternative approach and the last responsible moment to switch. Manufacturing recovery often needs lead time the calendar does not forgive.

**Ownership split.** Assessed organization drafts. Responsible organization accepts residual risk into the program baseline when a path cannot fully close the gap inside schedule.

## Key Takeaways

- Assessment purpose is risk identification in service of maturation, not score theater.
- Every missed target needs an MMP.
- MMP content covers problem, options, schedule, funding, actions, prototypes, thresholds, and criteria still open.
- Best practices span recognition, management, monitoring, outside expertise, and staff skill.
- Contract language in Chapter 6 can bind assessment and MMP support into the deal.
