# Chapter: Applying MRLs in Contract Language

## Core Idea

Section 6 shows how to put manufacturing maturity and quality expectations into acquisition planning, solicitations, and contracts. MRL criteria stay descriptive. They state what maturity looks like. They do not, by themselves, order a contractor to perform work. Tasking comes from the contract documents this chapter names: acquisition plans, Sections L and M of a request for proposal, statements of work, deliverables and data item descriptions, quality clauses, and relationships to AS6500A.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Acquisition planning

During planning, set manufacturing and quality expectations for the applicable milestone or phase (pre-Milestone A through full-rate production and operations and support). The responsible organization identifies required maturity, documents risk through assessments ahead of major milestones and technical reviews, and brings results to those reviews. Elements still below target need Manufacturing Maturation Plans visible to decision makers.

Examples the deskbook sketches:

- Pre-Milestone A: manufacturing feasibility across competing design alternatives; critical technologies and processes that must mature by later gates.
- Post-Milestone A: maturity assessments and gap analysis toward Milestone B demonstrations.
- Programs entering at Milestone C: production readiness reviews fed by manufacturing maturity and risk assessment before the production decision.

Systems engineering plans should carry target entry and exit MRL levels for the development phase. Industry supply-chain contracts should flow manufacturing expectations down tiers.

### RFP language

- **Section L (instructions to offerors).** Ask offerors to describe manufacturing maturity, recognized risks, and the quality effort proposed. Prefer responses that include self-assessment or independent assessment against MRL criteria.
- **Section M (evaluation factors).** Weight manufacturing maturity and risk so source selection can discriminate on producibility and industrial realism, not only on paper performance.

### Statement of work and deliverables

Put assessments and MMP support into the statement of work. Pair them with data item descriptions so reports and plans are formal deliverables. From the government side, L and M language plus SOW tasking makes manufacturing maturity part of both selection and execution. Minimum impact still reaches requirements and contract structure even when the full suggested set is tailored.

### Quality considerations and AS6500A

Additional quality topics sit beside MRL language: process control, supplier quality, and continuous improvement expectations that align with the Quality thread. AS6500A relates manufacturing management requirements to MRL threads. Organizations plan actions that address manufacturing risks and opportunities in ways that map to those threads. Quality standards and MRL criteria reinforce each other. Neither replaces the other. Criteria describe maturity. Standards and contract text create enforceable process obligations.

## Key Concepts

- **Descriptive versus tasking.** A criteria cell is not a SOW verb. Write the verb in the contract documents.
- **Selection and execution.** Section L and M shape who wins. SOW and deliverables shape what the winner must produce.
- **Flow-down.** Prime commitments without supplier flow-down leave Materials, Quality, and Facilities threads exposed.
- **DID pairing.** Assessment reports and MMPs need data item descriptions when the government wants enforceable delivery and format.

## Mental Models

**Plan, solicit, bind, deliver.** Planning sets targets. Solicitation evaluates industrial story. Contract binds assessment and maturation work. Deliverables make the paper trail.

**AS6500A as bridge.** Use the standard's manufacturing management structure to host MRL-aligned activities without pretending the criteria matrix is itself a standard clause set.

**No silent FAR or DFARS rebuild.** Name the FAR and DFARS when discussing regulatory backdrop. Do not quote or reconstruct their text in this pack. Real clause selection stays with contracting officers.

## Key Takeaways

- Put manufacturing maturity into acquisition planning, RFP Sections L and M, SOW tasking, and deliverables.
- Criteria remain descriptive; contracts create tasking.
- MMPs and assessment reports should be planned deliverables when targets matter to the deal.
- AS6500A and quality standards complement MRL criteria.
- Supply-chain flow-down keeps multi-tier risk visible.
