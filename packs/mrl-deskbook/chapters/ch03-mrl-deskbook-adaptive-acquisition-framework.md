# Chapter: MRLs in the Adaptive Acquisition Framework

## Core Idea

The Adaptive Acquisition Framework gives milestone decision authorities, other decision authorities, and program managers room to match pathway and process to the capability being bought. Manufacturing risk management belongs across weapon-system life cycles. MRL Assessments are treated as best practice on Major Capability Acquisition, Middle Tier Acquisition, and Urgent Capability Acquisition pathways. They are not generally required for Business Systems or Defense Acquisition of Services routes.

Figures in the source that map MRL targets to pathway events trace to DoDI 5000.88 for engineering of defense systems. Name that instruction. Do not quote or rebuild its text. Related pathway instructions (DoDI 5000.02 for framework operation, DoDI 5000.85 for major capability, DoDI 5000.80 for middle tier, DoDI 5000.81 for urgent capability) are named the same way.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Pathway set

- Major Capability Acquisition (MCA)
- Middle Tier Acquisition (MTA), with Rapid Prototyping and Rapid Fielding paths
- Urgent Capability Acquisition (UCA)
- Business Systems
- Defense Acquisition of Services

MRL practice concentrates on MCA, MTA, and UCA. Independent Technical Risk Assessments for major programs can draw on MRL Assessment results as supporting best practice. Pathway differences may require the adaptations Appendix C describes.

### MCA phase targets (section 3 language)

During Materiel Solution Analysis, MRL Assessments (MRL 4) should be run on competing materiel solutions in the analysis of alternatives. Manufacturing feasibility and required resources get special weight. Alternative Systems Review manufacturing risk is judged against MRL 4 criteria and against the difficulty of reaching MRL 6 by the end of Technology Maturation and Risk Reduction. Teams should also be at least MRL 6 at that later gate when the program intends a clean transition into engineering and manufacturing development.

In Technology Maturation and Risk Reduction, baseline manufacturing maturity from the prior phase, watch technologies that entered below TRL 4, and drive manufacturing maturity toward the Milestone B target. Engineering and Manufacturing Development uses criteria and metrics at the appropriate level of maturity for LRIP. Full-rate work uses criteria and metrics at the appropriate level of maturity for FRP. Best practices call for a target of MRL 8 for LRIP entry evidence on the pilot line. Production and deployment drive remaining gaps closed so full-rate decisions rest on demonstrated rate, quality, and supportability.

### MTA targets

Middle Tier programs still need manufacturing risk visibility even on compressed schedules. Rapid Prototyping work should hold a minimum target of MRL 4 so laboratory and early production-relevant evidence exists before prototype commitments harden. Rapid Fielding efforts that intend near-term production will target MRL 8 so pilot-line proof is in view before fielding volume grows. Where a prototype path is meant to seed later production, teams document the gap of meeting MRL 8 criteria and the work still required.

### UCA targets

Urgent Capability Acquisition compresses decision space. Even so, manufacturing maturity should be MRL 7 when production-representative evidence is expected before fielding under urgency. Programs that must sustain and expand fielded articles carry an objective of meeting MRL 9 so full-rate quality and rate are not left as afterthoughts. When industrial capacity or process control fall short of MRL 6, decision authorities treat that shortfall as explicit residual risk against the urgent timeline.

### Pre-acquisition and early MCA notes

The Materiel Development Decision is the mandatory MCA entry. Technology and ManTech work before that point should be mature enough to transition into Materiel Solutions Analysis with manageable manufacturing risk. Manufacturing subject-matter experts belong in systems engineering technical reviews. Initial assessments set priorities and feed Manufacturing Maturation Plans that either reach the target or hold residual risk inside schedule. Fund and execute those plans. Run a final assessment before the next phase transition.

## Key Concepts

- **ITRA relationship.** Independent Technical Risk Assessment frameworks can consume MRL Assessment outputs as manufacturing-risk evidence. The MRL work informs the ITRA; it does not replace it.
- **Acquisition Strategy and baseline.** Target MRLs belong in strategy and baseline thinking so cost, schedule, and manufacturing maturity stay coupled.
- **SETR coupling.** Reviews such as Alternative Systems Review, System Requirements Review, System Functional Review, Preliminary Design Review, and Critical Design Review are natural assessment waypoints.
- **Adaptation.** Pathway speed and product type may require the tailoring patterns in Chapter 8.

## Mental Models

**Pathway first, target second.** Choose MCA, MTA, or UCA logic before locking a number. A Rapid Prototyping minimum is not a Full-Rate Production objective.

**Anchor phrases from section 3.** Keep these deskbook target phrases in view when writing plans and reports: MRL Assessments (MRL 4); should also be at least MRL 6; appropriate level of maturity for LRIP; appropriate level of maturity for FRP; Best practices call for a target of MRL 8 for LRIP; minimum target of MRL 4; will target MRL 8; of meeting MRL 8 criteria; manufacturing maturity should be MRL 7; objective of meeting MRL 9; fall short of MRL 6.

**Risk residual is a decision input.** Missing a target is not automatic program stop. It is a named residual that decision authorities weigh with cost, schedule, and performance.

## Key Takeaways

- Best-practice pathways for MRL Assessments: MCA, MTA, and UCA.
- MCA moves from MRL 4 in early solution analysis toward MRL 6 by the end of technology maturation, then toward LRIP and FRP maturity in later phases.
- MTA Rapid Prototyping leans on early targets; Rapid Fielding leans on pilot-line proof.
- UCA still needs production-representative and full-rate objectives when fielding and sustainment demand them.
- DoDI 5000.88 and sibling pathway instructions are named only. Quote none of them.
