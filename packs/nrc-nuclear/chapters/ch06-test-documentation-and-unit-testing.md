## Core Idea

RG 1.170 covers test documentation for digital computer software used in nuclear power plant safety systems. IEEE Std. 829-2008 is the staff-accepted approach for software and system test documentation, subject to the exceptions and additions in the guide's regulatory positions. Staff positions address the test program and Master Test Plan content, control of software and test documentation as configuration and quality records, how test documents may be combined without lowering integrity, system testing of all associated safety features including recovery paths, traceability from functions to test cases, integrity level 4 for safety system software, testing tasks drawn from the standard's life-cycle process steps, test-tool documentation, secure-analysis testing tasks, and which annexes are endorsed.

RG 1.171 covers software unit testing of that same safety system software. ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 is the staff-accepted unit-testing approach, subject to the guide's exceptions and additions. Staff positions set the minimum documentation content for unit testing, require justified coverage of requirements and module structure beyond statement coverage alone, place test products under configuration control, point independence questions to RG 1.168, and redirect 1008's stale 829-1983 references to IEEE Std. 829-2008 as endorsed by RG 1.170.

BTP 7-14 B.3.1.12 is the staff review of the Software Test Plan (STP). It scores management, implementation, and resource characteristics and checks that testing spans unit through installation, that retest after failure and after modification is planned, and that final system testing is assigned to the V&V group.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **IEEE Std. 829-2008 test documentation method (S3 C lead-in).** IEEE Std. 829-2008 is acceptable for test documentation of safety system software with the listed exceptions and additions. Informative annexes may be useful; the staff does not endorse them except as noted. Cited criteria are Appendix B to 10 CFR Part 50 unless noted.
- **Test program built on integrity level and life-cycle processes (S3 C.1).** The standard does not mandate every document in every test phase; it recommends integrity-level and life-cycle process practice that yields the needed documentation. A chosen subset for a phase must still carry the information needed to meet regulatory requirements on software and system test documentation, demonstrate minimum testing tasks for the designated integrity level with each associated life-cycle phase, and document to the highest level of documentation in the Clause 8 Master Test Plan (MTP) plus the highest selected test documentation from Clauses 9 through 17. The MTP follows those clauses with the Clause 5 life-cycle test processes and Table 3 required testing activities by integrity level. For integrity level 4, Table 2 calls for the MTP and associated documents. As a minimum, MTP information additions that provide an acceptable planning approach for system safety requirements include: (a) qualifications, duties, responsibilities, and skills of persons and organizations assigned to testing (Clause 8), with further training support in the Level Test Plan (LTP) under Clause 9.3.6; (b) refinement of test items, testing approach, and acceptance criteria in the LTP (Clause 9.1.5); (c) environmental conditions and special controls, equipment, tools, and instrumentation under LTP Clause 9.3.2 and Level Test Design (LTD) Clause 10.2.2; (d) system, software, and hardware test instructions and procedures incorporating requirements and acceptance limits from applicable design documents, supported in the LTD, with acceptance criteria further in the Level Test Case (LTC) Clauses 11.2.5/6 and procedural steps in the Level Test Procedure (LTPr) Clause 12.2.2; (e) test prerequisites and criteria for meeting requirements and acceptance limits under LTPr Clause 12.2.1; (f) Level Test Log (LTL) Clause 13.2.1 details on logs and records identifying the tester, observation type, results/acceptability, and deficiency actions; (g) further direction for test data and interim result reporting in the LTL Clause 13, with life-cycle processes demonstrating adequate testing and error-resolution documentation with retesting sustained by an Anomaly Report (AR) Clause 14. The licensee incorporates those items with the normal MTP outline. Overall test documentation provides an overview history that the software safety analysis and its test tasks and activities completed successfully.
- **Software documentation as controlled quality records (S3 C.2).** Criteria VI and XVII and 10 CFR 21.51 require retention of documents and records that affect quality. Because design control applies to test acceptance criteria and because some test documentation is reused and evolves (for example regression test documentation), that documentation is controlled as one or more configuration items under a software configuration management system. Test records such as test reports are maintained as quality records under that SCM control. Section 5.11(5) of IEEE Std. 603-1991 and Section 4.22 of IEEE Std. 279-1971 require distinct identification of documentation, including test records, associated with safety systems. Under IEEE Std. 829 the MTP identifies documents such as LTLs, ARs, Level Interim Test Status Reports (LITSR), Level Test Reports (LTR), and the Master Test Report (MTR). The MTR summarizes testing activities, test task results, anomalies and resolutions, quality, and final metrics; it details project suitability and sustainability and reflects the MTP. Variations follow the established deviation policy in Clause 8.2.3.3.
- **Test documentation set, combination rules, and pre-implementation completion (S3 C.3).** Clause 7 describes software and system test documentation as a set of individual documents. Types and levels from Clauses 8 through 17 may be combined or eliminated per Clause 6. For each type, the licensee documents software and system test content decisions and rationale at the highest testing level, with sign-off by all designated stakeholders. To claim conformance to IEEE Std. 829-2008, that documentation is completed before implementation. Combining documents into larger test documents is acceptable when each component document keeps its identity and proper test planning documentation is maintained through the life cycle for a traceability matrix in V&V. Lowering the integrity level as suggested in Clause 6.4 is not acceptable. Some acceptance test procedures may keep an open entry location for data and testing status; an acceptable combine/eliminate method for Clause 13 Level Test Log activity is to use procedure and log as one document (for example Factory Acceptance Test Procedures), or to keep the log as an appendix or attachment when the procedure repeats many times (for example surveillance procedures).
- **System testing of all associated features (S3 C.4).** Criterion XI requires testing that demonstrates systems and components will perform satisfactorily in service. LTD Clause 10.2.1 "Features to be tested" addresses other features exercised in a design that may not be the specific LTD objective. The licensee formally tests all associated features in the safety system and follows the Clause 5 testing activity process. Clause 9 examples may yield more than one testing level (for example recovery processing software with normal and failure recovery levels). Normal or failure recovery sits above unit testing at component or system level and is included as a test requirement. When transaction decisions may not find the normal return path, whether failure is software- or hardware-initiated by control flow or data flow, a traceable baseline for failure-recovery software testing is included in the LTD and LTP.
- **Traceability of functions to test cases (S3 C.5).** Criterion XI again drives the need to show satisfactory in-service performance. Traceability analyses relating functions and test cases ensure all functions are tested (IEEE Std. 1012-2004); software V&V planning addresses those analyses. Clause 8.1.3 requires listing all applicable reference documentation as part of the life-cycle process with the test documentation from Clauses 8 through 17. That test documentation includes those references unless equivalent traceability information is maintained elsewhere in the V&V records.
- **Integrity levels for test documentation (S3 C.6).** Clause 4 of IEEE Std. 829-2008 defines the same four-level integrity scheme (level 4 highest). The applicant or licensee uses that method or another method mapped to it. The staff takes exception to Annex B Table B.3 risk-assessment illustration that would justify integrity below level 4. Safety system software is assigned integrity level 4 or equivalent by mapping to level 4 as defined in IEEE Std. 829-2008.
- **Testing tasks and Annex C (S3 C.7).** Clause 5 lists minimum testing tasks for each life-cycle process step usable in preparing required test plans. Annex C Table C.1 outlines Clause 5 by testing tasks, inputs, and outputs and amplifies body requirements. For safety system software, applicants and licensees should consider the recommendations in that annex.
- **Test tool documentation exception (S3 C.8).** Clause 6.3 would allow no repetition of test information when an automated tool completely manages it with tracing references. The staff takes exception: in particular cases electronic validation methods with repetition keep test information easily accessible as the basis for a safety conclusion, and that information remains available in the record management system. Tools used to develop safety system software follow IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 as endorsed by RG 1.152.
- **Secure analysis as a required test task (S3 C.9).** Clause 5 and Table 3 list "Identifying security issues (test)" as a required test-effort task in the development life-cycle process and as a minimum recommended test activity in Requirements, Design, Implementation, Test, Installation/Checkout, and Operation Test. That identification task is also included in the Acquisition, Supply, Planning, and Concept life-cycle phases in Table 3 and Clause 5.
- **Annex endorsement map for IEEE Std. 829-2008 (S3 C.10).** Eight informative annexes are listed as information sources and not endorsed unless noted. Annex A bibliography is not endorsed but may be useful. Annex B example integrity level scheme is endorsed as described with the C.6 exceptions. Annex C testing tasks is endorsed as described in C.7. Annex D optional testing tasks is not endorsed but may be useful. Annex E metrics is not endorsed but may be useful. Annex F independence is not endorsed but may be useful. Annex G tailoring documentation examples is not endorsed as examples rather than regulatory guidelines; repackaging document contents is permitted if no information is lost. Annex H guidelines for compliance with IEEE/EIA Std. 12207.1-1997 is not endorsed because the staff has not reviewed that standard for endorsement.
- **ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 unit testing method (S4 C lead-in).** The requirements in ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 provide an acceptable approach for unit testing of safety system software with the listed exceptions and additions. Appendices are not endorsed except as noted.
- **Software testing documentation minimum set for unit testing (S4 C.1).** Section 1.1 of ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 mandates test design specifications and test summary report documents, found in IEEE Std. 829-2008 Clauses 10 (Level Test Design) and 17 (Master Test Report). The 1008 standard either folds additional information into those two documents or indicates need for more documentation. Whatever formats the licensee uses, documentation supporting software unit testing (unit-test activity documents or overall testing-effort documents) includes the information needed for regulatory requirements on software test documentation. As a minimum that information covers: (a) qualifications, duties, responsibilities, and skills of persons and organizations assigned to testing; (b) special conditions and controls, equipment, tools, and instrumentation needed for testing; (c) test instructions and procedures incorporating requirements and acceptance limits from applicable design documents; (d) test prerequisites and criteria for meeting those requirements and acceptance limits; (e) test items and the testing-program approach; (f) test logs, test data, and test results; (g) acceptance criteria; and (h) test records identifying the tester, observation type, results and acceptability, and deficiency actions. Missing items among that list are added to the selected unit-test support documentation.
- **Test program coverage of requirements and module structure (S4 C.2).** Two coverage aspects are particularly important for unit testing of safety system software: (a) Coverage of requirements: testing includes all features and associated procedures, states, state transitions, and associated data characteristics essential to the safety determination. (b) Coverage of module structure: Section 3.1.2(2) of ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 specifies statement coverage as a completeness criterion. The staff judges statement coverage insufficient for measuring test completeness and does not endorse it as a sufficient criterion for software unit testing. For safety system software, the licensee identifies and justifies the unit testing coverage criteria it will use.
- **Test program records under CM (S4 C.3).** Criteria VI and XVII and 10 CFR 21.51 require control and retention of quality-affecting documents and records. Criterion III requires design changes under design control commensurate with the original design. Section 3.8.2(4) of ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 discusses preservation of testing products, including test iterations caused by task, procedure, and design-criteria variations or deviations from IEEE Std. 829-2008 Clauses 8 and 9 (Master Test Plan and Level Test Plans). Because design control applies to testing acceptance criteria and because some testing materials are reused and evolve (for example regression test materials), those materials are configuration items under software configuration management change control.
- **Independence points to RG 1.168 (S4 C.4).** Criterion III imposes independence for verification and checking of design adequacy. IEEE Std. 1008-1987 does not include a requirement for independent software verification. RG 1.168 provides additional guidance on testing independence.
- **Update 1008's 829-1983 references (S4 C.5).** ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 still references ANSI/IEEE Std. 829-1983, which has been revised. IEEE Std. 829-2008 adds levels of test documentation, including unit test documentation. IEEE Std. 829-2008 as endorsed by RG 1.170 is the edition to use.
- **1008 appendix endorsement map (S4 C.6).** Appendix A implementation and usage guidelines is not endorsed (insufficient how-to). Appendix B concepts and assumptions is helpful but out of date and not endorsed. Appendix C sources for techniques and tools is out of date and not endorsed. Appendix D general references is out of date and not endorsed.
- **B.3.1.12 STP staff review (S7).** RG 1.170 / IEEE Std 829 (with noted exceptions) and RG 1.171 / ANSI/IEEE Std 1008 (with noted exceptions) frame acceptable test documentation and unit testing methods. Management characteristics: purpose and scope of software testing and the general functions testing organizations perform; organization with boundaries, interfaces, and reporting channels between testing organizations and other company organizations; responsibilities naming who completes each testing task; security as testing of SDOE design features per RG 1.152. Implementation: measurement indicators and systematic collection and analysis of testing data to judge testing effectiveness, plus completion criteria per testing task; procedures for testing items, keeping test cases and procedures synchronized with software design, associating released code with associated testing documentation, tracking problem reports, and ensuring each reported problem was correctly resolved; record keeping that identifies required testing records, handles testing items, and tracks test cases and procedures so revision history and latest revision of each item are retrievable. Resources: facilities, methods, techniques, and tools for each testing task; tool-selection process; hardware and software test environment and necessary controls; listed international, national, industry, and company standards and guidelines for the software testing organization. Review guidance: the STP covers all testing done to the software, including unit, integration, factory acceptance, site acceptance, and installation testing. The reviewer checks that test planning is understandable, testing responsibilities sit with appropriate personnel, and adequate provisions exist for retest after original-test failure. Because modifying software after an error can introduce a new error, the STP requires the full set of tests after any software modification. Final system testing is a V&V test, so the STP assigns definition, test design, and performance of that testing to the V&V group.

## Key Concepts

- **MTP anchors the documentation stack.** Integrity level 4 pulls the MTP and associated Clause 9-17 documents; subset choices still meet the regulatory information floor.
- **People, environment, procedures, logs, and anomaly closure are first-class content.** Qualifications through AR-supported retest are explicit MTP additions, not optional prose.
- **Test documents are CM items and quality records.** Regression materials evolve and still stay controlled; safety-system test records are distinctly identified.
- **Combination keeps identity; integrity level does not drop.** Clause 6.4 integrity lowering is rejected; procedure-plus-log combining can be acceptable.
- **Conformance documentation is finished before implementation.** Stakeholder sign-off and highest-level content decisions are part of the claim.
- **Every associated safety feature is formally tested.** Recovery normal and failure paths above unit level belong in LTD and LTP baselines.
- **Traceability closes Criterion XI.** Functions map to test cases; references live in the 829 document set or equivalent V&V records.
- **Integrity level 4 again.** Same nuclear floor as V&V, with the same rejection of Table B.3 downgrades.
- **Annex C testing tasks are in; most other 829 annexes are out.** Optional-task and metrics annexes stay informational only.
- **Tool-managed records still need accessible repeated safety evidence when staff need it.** Automated tracing does not erase record-system availability.
- **Security-issue identification is a required test task across more phases than Table 3's minimum list alone.** Acquisition through concept are included.
- **Unit testing borrows 829 LTD and MTR shapes and still lists eight minimum information classes.** Format choice does not erase content duty.
- **Statement coverage is not enough.** Licensees justify the structural coverage criteria they actually use.
- **Unit-test products stay under CM with the MTP/LTP baseline.** Deviations and iterations are preserved testing products.
- **1008 is silent on independence; RG 1.168 is not.** Unit testing still sits inside the broader V&V independence regime.
- **STP review spans the full test ladder and locks system test to V&V.** Retest after failure and full regression after modification are plan requirements.

## Mental Models

- **RG 1.170 writes the documents; RG 1.171 writes the unit-test practice; B.3.1.12 grades the STP.** Three views on one testing system.
- **Integrity level selects documentation weight; nuclear use locks level 4.** Mapping is allowed; silent thinning is not.
- **MTP is the map; LTP/LTD/LTC/LTPr/LTL/AR/LTR/MTR are the terrain.** Combination is packaging, not deletion of required content.
- **CM is how test truth survives change.** Without configuration control, regression sets and acceptance criteria drift.
- **System test is still V&V-owned final proof.** Development may run many levels; the STP still assigns final system testing definition, design, and performance to V&V.
- **Coverage has two axes.** Requirements/features/states/data on one axis; justified module-structure criteria stronger than statement coverage on the other.
- **Security testing is scheduled work, not a late checklist.** Identify security issues in planning and concept as well as in build and install phases.
- **Unit testing is not a documentation orphan.** It consumes 829 designs and summary reports and feeds the same anomaly and CM machinery.

## Anti-patterns

- **Integrity level 4 safety software with a thinned document subset that cannot show minimum tasks or MTP-level information.** S3 C.1.
- **MTP that never states tester qualifications, environments, acceptance criteria, logs, or anomaly-driven retest paths.** S3 C.1.a-g.
- **Test reports and regression materials outside SCM and quality-record control.** S3 C.2; S4 C.3.
- **Claiming 829 conformance while finishing documentation after implementation or without stakeholder sign-off of content decisions.** S3 C.3.
- **Combining documents in a way that loses component identity or lowers integrity level per Clause 6.4.** S3 C.3.
- **System tests that skip associated features or omit normal/failure recovery baselines from LTD and LTP.** S3 C.4.
- **No function-to-test-case traceability in the test set or equivalent V&V records.** S3 C.5.
- **Assigning safety software below integrity level 4 via Table B.3 likelihood wording.** S3 C.6.
- **Ignoring Annex C task/input/output recommendations for safety system software test planning.** S3 C.7.
- **Relying on tool-only storage with no repeated, record-system-accessible safety test evidence when needed.** S3 C.8.
- **Security-issue identification missing from acquisition, supply, planning, or concept phases.** S3 C.9.
- **Unit-test packs missing any of the eight minimum information classes.** S4 C.1.
- **Declaring unit testing complete on statement coverage alone.** S4 C.2.
- **Unit-test products and iterations not preserved under CM after design or procedure deviations.** S4 C.3.
- **Treating 1008's silence on independence as permission to skip RG 1.168 independence rules.** S4 C.4.
- **Building unit-test docs against 829-1983 instead of 829-2008 as endorsed by RG 1.170.** S4 C.5.
- **STP that covers only unit or factory tests and omits integration, site acceptance, or installation testing.** B.3.1.12 review guidance.
- **No planned retest after failure, or modifications without requiring the full test set again.** B.3.1.12.
- **Final system testing defined, designed, or performed outside the V&V group.** B.3.1.12.
- **Test cases and procedures that drift from design with no synchronization or revision-history retrieval.** B.3.1.12 implementation and record-keeping characteristics.

## Key Takeaways

1. RG 1.170 endorses IEEE Std. 829-2008 for software and system test documentation of safety system software with nuclear exceptions and additions; standard body text is not reproduced here.
2. Integrity level 4 pulls the Master Test Plan and associated documents; any phase subset still carries regulatory information, minimum tasks, and highest selected documentation levels, including people, environment, procedures, logs, and anomaly-supported retest content.
3. Test documentation is controlled under SCM as configuration items and quality records, distinctly identified for safety systems; the Master Test Report summarizes activities, anomalies, quality, and metrics against the MTP.
4. 829 conformance requires pre-implementation completion and stakeholder sign-off of content decisions; documents may combine if identities remain, but integrity level may not be lowered.
5. System testing formally covers all associated safety features, including normal and failure recovery above unit level, with function-to-test-case traceability in the test set or equivalent V&V records.
6. Annex B (with C.6 exceptions) and Annex C are endorsed; most other 829 annexes are not; automated tools follow RG 1.152 / IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003, and staff may still require accessible repeated test evidence in the records system; identifying security issues is a required test task across development and earlier life-cycle phases.
7. RG 1.171 endorses ANSI/IEEE Std. 1008-1987 for unit testing with exceptions; unit-test documentation meets an eight-item minimum and uses 829-2008 Level Test Design and Master Test Report shapes as the mandated document anchors.
8. Unit testing covers safety-essential requirements features, states, transitions, and data, and uses justified module-structure coverage criteria stronger than statement coverage alone; test products stay under CM; independence follows RG 1.168; 829-2008 replaces 1008's 829-1983 references.
9. B.3.1.12 reviews STP purpose, testing organization and responsibilities, SDOE feature testing, measurement, synchronization and problem-tracking procedures, record keeping, tools and standards, and full-ladder coverage from unit through installation.
10. Critical staff checks are understandable planning, correct responsibility assignment, retest after failure, full regression after any modification, and V&V ownership of final system test definition, design, and performance.
