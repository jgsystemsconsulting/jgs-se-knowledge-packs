## Core Idea

RG 1.169 covers configuration management plans for digital computer software used in nuclear power plant safety systems across the software life cycle inside the system life cycle. IEEE Std. 828-2005 is the staff-accepted approach for satisfying regulatory requirements on those plans, subject to the exceptions and additions in the guide's regulatory positions. The minimal CM activity set is configuration identification and control of designs and code, identification and control of design interfaces, control of design changes, control of user/operating/maintenance documentation, control of vendors supplying safety system software, control and retrieval of qualification information, software configuration audits, status accounting, and control of building, release, and delivery.

BTP 7-14 B.3.1.11 is the staff review of the Software Configuration Management Plan (SCMP). It scores management, implementation, and resource characteristics, enumerates what must be controlled, and stresses librarian ownership, broad item coverage beyond operational code, verification of modifications, and dual vendor/licensee plans when both exist.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **IEEE Std. 828-2005 life cycle CM method (S2 C lead-in).** The RG applies to all software life cycle aspects in the system life cycle context. The standard is acceptable for safety system software CM plans with the listed exceptions and additions. Cited criteria are Appendix B to 10 CFR Part 50 unless noted.
- **Definitions clarifications (S2 C.1).** Technical terms enumerated in Clause 2 of IEEE Std. 828-2005 draw on IEEE/EIA 12207.0-1996 and are accepted with clarifications. Baseline uses meaning (1); "formal review and agreement" means responsible management reviewed and approved; baselines are under change control; acceptable baseline change approval authority is in Regulatory Position 2. Interface uses all four meanings by context; meaning (1) shared boundary is read broadly under Criterion III to include design interfaces between participating design organizations. Configuration audit in a delivery context includes both functional and physical configuration audits.
- **Authority levels scaled to stage and safety (S2 C.2).** Hierarchies of change approval authority are allowed when required authority matches life cycle stage (nearness to release) and product importance to safety. Promoting a product to a higher safety-significance level can change both control level (Clause 3.3.2.3) and responsible individual.
- **Control points tied to baselines and milestones (S2 C.3).** The SCM plan describes criteria for selecting control points (Clause 2.1.1) and maps those points to baselines, project milestones, and life cycle milestones.
- **Minimal safety system SCM activity set (S2 C.4).** The plan covers: (a) identification and control of all software designs and code; (b) identification and control of all software design interfaces; (c) control of all software design changes; (d) control of software documentation (user, operating, and maintenance); (e) control of software vendors supplying safety system software; (f) control and retrieval of qualification information associated with software designs and code; (g) software configuration audits; (h) status accounting; and (i) control of building, release, and delivery of products. Other QA activities may own some of these functions; the plan then states the division of responsibility. Contractually developed or qualified commercial safety system software products enter the same control: exact version identified and controlled like other configuration items, usage tracked and reported.
- **Corrective action end-to-end (S2 C.5).** Criterion XVI requires identifying conditions adverse to quality, finding causes, correcting them, and documenting the process. Because development and maintenance often split these duties across organizations, Clause 3.3.2's correction process description (change request, evaluation, approval, implementation, verification, changed-version release) is required, plus the preliminary steps into a change request: anomaly reports, problem analyses, and statistical monitoring of software performance, by description or by reference.
- **Configuration items and controlled documents (S2 C.6).** Clause 3.3.1.1's minimum of listing all configuration items to be delivered meets Criterion VIII when all software deliverables are identified and controlled as configuration items, and supports Criteria III, VI, and VII. For safety system software, configuration items or controlled documents include: software requirements, designs, and code; data files used or called directly or indirectly; exact versions of support software used in development; libraries of software components essential to safety; software plans that could affect quality; test software requirements, designs, or code used in testing; test results used to qualify software; analyses and results used to qualify software; software documentation; test cases; databases and software configuration data; and software change documentation. Items that could change from design change, review, or audit take formal change control. Items that may not change but are needed for correct production (for example compilers) are still configuration items so every factor in the executable is controlled, supporting maintenance, future development, error-impact tracing, and regression analysis. Historical or statistical retentions may be controlled documents rather than full configuration items.
- **Purchased and commercial materials (S2 C.7).** The SCM program takes control of contractually developed or qualified commercial safety system software (Criteria VII and VIII): exact version and build number identified and controlled, usage tracked and reported. Commercial-grade software in safety-related digital systems points to the commercial-grade acceptance path the source names (EPRI TR-106439 as accepted by the July 17, 1997 SER).
- **Development tools as configuration items (S2 C.8).** Tools used to develop safety system software follow IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 as endorsed by RG 1.152. An SCM program compliant with IEEE Std. 828-2005 takes control of those tools as configuration items; SCM plans identify production tools as configuration items.
- **No downward adaptation escape (S2 C.9).** Clause 4.2 "Downward Adaptation," which allows omitting some standard requirements for limited scope or complexity, is not endorsed for safety system software, so acceptance criteria and controlled conditions under Criteria V, II, and VIII stay intact.
- **Design verification of changes (S2 C.10).** Clause 3.3.2(d) definitions of verification, implementation, and release of a change must meet Criterion III: design changes receive design control commensurate with the original design, including reexamination of appropriate safety analysis related to the change.
- **Cost and schedule assumptions bounded by safety (S2 C.11).** Clause 3.1(g) plan assumptions that affect cost and schedule remain consistent with 10 CFR 50.57(a)(3) reasonable assurance that authorized activities do not endanger public health and safety.
- **Release management and delivery (S2 C.12).** Clause 3.3.7 building, release, delivery, change control, master copies, and documentation control includes preserving materials used to deliver the software and a change control mechanism sufficient to correct faults found in the software, not only organizational policy statements.
- **Backfit clarification (S2 C.13).** Standard wording that it also applies to noncritical or already-developed software is not read as a 10 CFR 50.109 backfit requirement; Section D of the RG states the staff backfitting position.
- **Annex endorsement map (S2 C.14).** Annex A bibliography is informational; treatment of other standards follows Section B of the RG. Annex B relating IEEE Std. 828-2005 to IEEE/EIA 12207.1-1997 and ISO/IEC TR-19759:2005 is not endorsed because those documents are not endorsed.
- **B.3.1.11 SCMP staff review (S7).** RG 1.173 / IEEE Std 1074 planning for configuration management, RG 1.169 / IEEE Std 828, and RG 1.152 / IEEE Std 7-4.3.2 CM clauses (including COTS CM controls) frame the review. Management characteristics: purpose and scope of CM functions; CM organization boundaries, interfaces, and reporting; responsibilities including configuration control board duties and named authorities who release software, data, or documents for revision and who release them for operation after revision; security as configuration control measures supporting SDOE per RG 1.152. Implementation: CM effectiveness metrics and completion criteria per CM task; procedures for identifying and naming items, placing items under control, keeping data files and tables synchronized with using software, keeping software and documentation synchronized, associating source with object and executables, managing software libraries, controlling and retrieving qualification information, confirmation audits, and status accounting; controlled-item list spanning requirements, designs, code, support software versions, safety-essential component libraries, quality-affecting plans, test software and qualifying test results and analyses, documentation, databases and configuration data, pre-developed safety system software, change documentation, and management/development/assurance tools; problem-report tracking to correct resolution; change-request approval information and control of all design changes; relationship of software CM to V&V anomaly handling and maintenance change control; periodic baseline reviews and physical audits; purchased-item and tool tracking with qualification, bug lists, and errata so affected design outputs can be found; vendor control procedures; record keeping with required CM records, protection, storage, handling, retention, shipping, revision-history retrieval, latest-revision identification, and backup/disaster recovery. Resources: CM facilities, methods, techniques, tools, tool-selection process, and environment controls; listed standards for the CM organization. Review guidance: CM errors often come from changing the wrong version or under-testing changes; CM starts when design first releases software; a software librarian or group owns versions, issues current versions for test or modification, and receives modified tested software back; control covers all software and software information that can affect safety software, not only operational code; the plan states how modified software or documentation is tested and verified and by whom; vendor development CM and applicant/licensee operational CM may be separate, and a plant-level CM plan must still address software-specific issues.

## Key Concepts

- **Four classical CM elements, nuclearized.** Identification, control (including design changes and interfaces), status accounting, and audits sit inside the minimal activity list with vendor control, qualification-information control, and build/release/delivery control.
- **Baseline is a management-approved, change-controlled object.** Authority to change it scales with release proximity and safety importance.
- **Control points are planned, not improvised.** They correspond to baselines and to project and life cycle milestones.
- **Division of responsibility with QA is explicit.** If another QA activity owns part of CM, the SCM plan says so.
- **Commercial and contracted software join the same regime.** Exact version (and build number where stated) and usage reporting are mandatory.
- **Corrective action is one process, even across organizations.** Anomaly report through changed-version release is described end to end.
- **Compilers and support tools are configuration items.** Everything that shapes the executable is controlled, not only source the team wrote.
- **No safety-system downward adaptation.** Omitting standard requirements for "simple" projects is not endorsed here.
- **Change verification equals original design control.** Safety analysis tied to the change is reexamined.
- **Release control preserves the deliverable and the fix path.** Master copies and fault-correction change control are part of delivery CM.
- **SCMP review cares about the librarian.** Named control of who issues and receives versions is a critical staff check.
- **Breadth of controlled items is a safety property.** Wrong support-software, test, plan, or tool versions can corrupt the product as surely as wrong application code.
- **Problem reports close under CM.** Tracking to correct resolution links CM to V&V anomaly handling and maintenance.
- **Dual plans are normal.** Vendor development CM and licensee operational CM can both exist; plant CM plans must still cover software-specific controls.
- **SDOE-supporting configuration controls.** Security characteristic in B.3.1.11 is configuration control that supports the secure development and operational environment.

## Mental Models

- **CM is the spine connecting requirements, design, code, test, and tools.** If it is not identified, versioned, and retrievable, later V&V and maintenance have nothing solid to stand on.
- **Status accounting answers "what is current?"** Audits answer "does the baseline match reality?" Control answers "who may change it?" Identification answers "what exists?"
- **Authority rises as release nears and safety rises.** Shallow approval early does not justify shallow approval on safety-significant product near release.
- **RG 1.169 writes the plan content; B.3.1.11 grades the plan in review.** Use both when asking what good SCMP work looks like.
- **Wrong version is a common-cause ready failure mode.** Librarian discipline and broad item coverage exist to stop silent version drift.
- **Tools are product ancestors.** A buggy compiler or outdated analyzer without errata tracking is a configuration defect, not merely a convenience issue.
- **Licensee operational CM does not erase vendor CM duties.** Both ends of the chain need software-aware controls.

## Anti-patterns

- **CM plan that lists operational code only.** S2 C.4/C.6 and B.3.1.11 both demand designs, interfaces, documentation, vendors, qualification data, tests, analyses, support software, tools, and related items.
- **Baselines changed without management-level approval authority scaled to safety and stage.** C.1-C.2.
- **Control points with no map to baselines or milestones.** C.3.
- **Commercial safety software used without exact version/build control and usage tracking.** C.4, C.7.
- **Fragmented corrective action with no described path from anomaly to verified release.** C.5.
- **Compilers and other production-essential tools left outside configuration control.** C.6, C.8.
- **Invoking downward adaptation to skip CM requirements on safety system software.** C.9 not endorsed.
- **Design changes verified more lightly than the original design, without rechecking related safety analysis.** C.10.
- **Cost/schedule CM assumptions that undercut reasonable assurance of safety.** C.11.
- **Release process with no preserved delivery materials or fault-fix change path.** C.12.
- **Reading the standard's noncritical/already-developed scope line as automatic backfit.** C.13.
- **No software librarian and no clear revision versus operation release authorities.** B.3.1.11 review guidance.
- **Modified software returned to service without stated test/verification ownership.** B.3.1.11.
- **Plant CM plan that never addresses software-specific identification, synchronization, or tool control.** B.3.1.11 dual-plan note.
- **Problem reports that never reconverge under CM to a verified resolution.** Implementation procedures in B.3.1.11.

## Key Takeaways

1. RG 1.169 endorses IEEE Std. 828-2005 for safety system software CM plans with nuclear clarifications; standard body text is not reproduced here.
2. Minimal CM coverage is identification and control of designs, code, and design interfaces; control of design changes and of user/operating/maintenance documentation; vendor control; qualification-information control and retrieval; configuration audits; status accounting; and build/release/delivery control.
3. Baselines are management-approved and change-controlled; approval authority scales with life cycle stage and importance to safety; control points map to baselines and milestones.
4. Configuration items include requirements, designs, code, data files, exact support-software versions, safety-essential libraries, quality-affecting plans, test artifacts and qualifying results and analyses, documentation, test cases, databases and configuration data, change documentation, production tools, and contracted or commercial safety software at exact version/build.
5. Corrective action runs from anomaly and problem analysis through change request, evaluation, approval, implementation, verification, and changed-version release, even when organizations are split.
6. Downward adaptation of IEEE Std. 828-2005 is not endorsed for safety system software; change verification meets original design-control rigor including related safety analysis.
7. Release management preserves delivery materials and keeps a change path strong enough to correct fielded faults.
8. B.3.1.11 reviews SCMP purpose, CM organization, CCB and release authorities, SDOE-supporting controls, metrics, identification and synchronization procedures, broad controlled-item lists, problem and change control, baseline audits, purchased-item/tool deficiency tracking, vendor control, records and disaster recovery, and CM tools/standards.
9. Critical review checks are librarian ownership of versions, control of every software work product that can affect safety software, and explicit test/verification of modifications.
10. Vendor development CM and licensee operational CM may both apply; software-specific issues must still appear when CM lives inside a plant-level plan.
