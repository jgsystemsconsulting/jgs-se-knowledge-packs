## Core Idea

After planning documents are accepted, BTP 7-14 turns to execution and products. B.3.2 asks whether the developer's plans were actually carried out for safety analysis, V&V, configuration management, and testing. B.3.3 asks whether each design output shows the functional and process characteristics important to safety system software. B.4 tells the reviewer how to sequence inspection and analysis across plans, implementation samples, and design-output samples. Plan-level acceptance criteria stay in ch02 and ch04-ch06; this chapter is implementation evidence and design-output scoring.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Implementation acceptance purpose (S7 B.3.2 lead-in).** Implementation criteria address life cycle process activities and documentation. When those activities and products are acceptable, the reviewer gains confidence the plans were carried out. The staff confirms the developer followed the plans; detailed acceptance criteria come from the developer's own plans as the staff accepted them. Beyond plan-following, the reviewer pays particular attention to the four activity areas below (shown as process implementation in Figure 7-A-1 of the BTP).
- **Safety analysis implementation (S7 B.3.2.1).** The Software Safety Plan describes the safety analysis tasks. Acceptance means those tasks were carried out in their entirety. Documentation must show successful accomplishment for each life cycle activity group and that the proposed digital system software is safe: system safety requirements adequately addressed; no new hazards introduced; software requirements, design elements, and code elements that can affect safety identified; all other requirements, design, and code elements shown not to affect safety adversely. Safety analysis also covers SDOE risk and measures ensuring design products lack undocumented code and other unwanted or undocumented functions or applications.
- **V&V implementation (S7 B.3.2.2).** RG 1.168 endorses IEEE Std 1012 for V&V methods and IEEE Std 1028 for reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, and audits, each subject to listed exceptions. IEEE Std 7-4.3.2 Clauses 5.3.3 and 5.3.4 (via RG 1.152) add V&V and independent V&V guidance the BTP names. Acceptance means SVVP tasks were carried out in their entirety. Documentation must show successful V&V for each activity group, and that requirements, design, code, integration, and installation design outputs satisfy the functional and process characteristics in B.3.3. Problems found in verification are documented with action items; actions taken are recorded and configuration management is applied. Traceability analysis is required and bidirectional: system requirements and design to SRS requirements, organized so design, implementation, and validation trace can be added, updated at each activity group completion, and finally able to trace from system requirements and design through software requirements, design, implementation, integration, validation, and installation. Integration V&V shows unit and subsystem tests required by the SVVP completed successfully, anomalies resolved and documented, final integration tests completed, per-run reports with anomalies and recommended actions, and a final integration V&V report consistent with the Software Integration Plan. Validation shows every SRS requirement has one or more tests with acceptance criteria, each result clearly meeting the requirement, procedures with setup, inputs, expected outputs, and completion time, error and anomaly handling including correction under configuration management and re-test until resolved, and a final validation report summarizing problems and corrections with a statement that validation succeeded and the software met all SRS requirements. Installation (acceptance) testing documents configuration, required inputs, expected outputs, execution steps, and acceptance criteria; extends into post-installation testing during initial startup and power ascension; documents problems and action items; produces an acceptance test report stating successful plan execution and readiness for operation, demonstrating correct operation identical to the validated system, summarizing results after problem resolution, and showing execution per the acceptance test procedure.
- **Thread audit as V&V check (S7 B.3.2.2 continued).** One accepted method is a thread audit: sample plant parameters and trace software implementation from purchase specification and functional requirements through writing and testing of code. Sample size must be representative (may be as many as 5 percent of requirements) and at least statistically significant. The review includes: sampling actual code sections (design engineer walkthrough if the reviewer is not expert in the language; escalate to more experienced staff or independent contractor if the explanation is unfollowable); comparing development documents to code; examining problem reports and test plans for selected requirements and verifying corrections (absence of problem reports is unusual and triggers careful examination of test and review thoroughness; completed reports show resolution and how it was tested; check configuration management of revised code); examining engineering cross-discipline interfaces for nuclear-specific needs in code; examining applicant or licensee interface for plant-specific requirements; ensuring the V&V process follows the vendor plan; reviewing final process results; and other items driven by tools and methods. The reviewer may ask why work was done a particular way and judge the answer. If the reviewer finds errors the V&V team missed after claimed verification and validation, a serious quality problem may exist; check more requirements to separate systematic from isolated failure. Several such problems can mean the development process is inadequate for safety-related nuclear use; discuss with management whether the request to use the software should be rejected. A successful thread audit requires familiarity with the developer's plans and procedures and with the NRC requirements, NUREGs, RGs, and industry standards those plans claim; it is one of the few times the reviewer sees the actual development process rather than only the documented plans.
- **Configuration management implementation (S7 B.3.2.3).** RG 1.169 endorses IEEE Std 828 subject to RG provisions. CM partitions into management and control of software and the development environment, and document control. Tools are selected, evaluated, and used properly. The Software Development Plan describes what software and documents enter configuration control (and relationships if separate systems). The SCMP describes implementation tasks. Acceptance means SCMP tasks were carried out in their entirety. Documentation must show configuration items identified, baselines established for the activity group, adequate change control on the product baseline, and appropriate configuration audits for items created or modified. Items are labeled unambiguously. Baselines are established per activity group before the set of activities is complete, then protected from change; derivative baselines follow change control; baselines trace to the baseline they came from and to the design outputs or activity they identify. A configuration control board authorizes baseline changes. Problem reports describe anomalous or inconsistent software and documentation and invoke change control when corrective action is needed. Change control preserves integrity; any configuration-item change changes its identification (version or change date); baseline and controlled-item changes are recorded, approved, and tracked; problem-driven changes trace to the problem report; software changes trace to origin and affected processes repeat from change point to discovery point; the CCB reviews proposed changes for system safety impact. Status accounting before activity-group completion documents identifications, baselines, problem-report status, change history, and release status. The CM organization audits life cycle activities to confirm CM procedures were carried out.
- **Testing implementation (S7 B.3.2.4).** RG 1.152 / IEEE Std 7-4.3.2 Clause 5.4.1 addresses computer system qualification testing. RG 1.168 positions the BTP labels C.7.b (regression analysis and testing) and C.7.d (test evaluation) apply. RG 1.170 / IEEE Std 829 covers test documentation; RG 1.171 / ANSI/IEEE Std 1008 covers unit testing. Thorough testing builds from smallest testable units through larger integrated units to post-installation system test. Unit, module, and system lack a single precise industry definition; RG 1.171 applies to all testing before integrated system testing. Testing activities must show completeness (all required functionality for all operating modes, including error recovery), consistency (with safety system requirements and design, SRS, SDS, and documented operational environment; uniform terminology), and traceability (SRS, SDS, and test documentation show how each requirement is tested).
- **Design-output acceptance structure (S7 B.3.3 lead-in).** Criteria determine whether the software has each characteristic important to safety system software, organized by life cycle activity group, then design output, then characteristic. Formal or semiformal methods may prepare some outputs; SRP Appendix 7.0-A Subsection C.3 discusses benefits and review precautions. Acceptance splits into functional characteristics (accuracy, functionality, reliability, robustness, safety, security, timing) and process characteristics (completeness, consistency, correctness, style, traceability, unambiguity, verifiability). Not every characteristic applies to every output. Functional criteria required by 10 CFR 50.55a(h) must be supported by the software. Lead-in definitions: accuracy values feed setpoint calculations; functionality is compared to plant requirements with overall system, hardware, and software architecture evaluated for feasibility; software reliability prediction has no consensus method so credited values need care; robustness needs tests specifically analyzed to validate the claimed characteristic (invalid inputs, environmental stressors); safety is explicitly considered and documented in design outputs; security is SDOE per RG 1.152; timing is analyzed for deterministic behavior. Process lead-in: completeness is hard and experience-dependent (a requirements compliance matrix across hardware, software, code, test, and V&V helps; review the applicant's completeness method); consistency needs careful component examination and I/O understanding (commercial-grade dedication may need design-process insight; plans internally and mutually consistent); correctness includes simplicity (no unused or unneeded functions or code in safety-related software even for convenience or future use; only features needed for safety functions and for system and software testing); style is consistent and understandable within the project more than any particular fashion, with coding standards examined for understandability and resulting code meeting them; traceability uses an RTM from system requirement through software requirement, design description, code, and test; unambiguity is hard because software and nuclear communities may share words with different meanings (discuss dual readings with vendor and applicant or licensee; if unclear to the reviewer, others likely share the gap); verifiability means the artifact supports setting verification criteria and performing analysis, review, or test (conformance to a standard is a good example; "meets the intent of" or "consistent with" without naming followed requirements is unverifiable).
- **SRS design output (S7 B.3.3.1).** RG 1.172 / IEEE Std 830 is the acceptable SRS approach subject to exceptions. An SRS must show the functional and process characteristics below. Functional: accuracy (numeric requirements per input and output variable with units, error bounds, data type, and size); functionality (operations per mode completely specified as inputs, transformations, outputs); reliability (fault tolerance and failure modes per operating mode; hardware and software failure handling including analysis and recovery; on-line in-service testing and diagnostics); robustness (behavior under unexpected, incorrect, anomalous, or improper input, hardware behavior, or software behavior, especially unexpected message-traffic rates); safety (functions, procedures, inputs, and outputs classified by safety importance; safety-important requirements identified as such, including safety analysis report requirements and abnormal conditions and events as RG 1.152 describes); security (SDOE per RG 1.152); timing (functions with timing constraints identified; criteria per mode; goals distinguished from requirements; deterministic stimulus-to-response delay for safety actions under normal and anticipated failure conditions; SRP BTP 7-21 for real-time performance). Process: completeness (all computer-system actions for all modes and all possible input values, including full instrument spans; prohibited actions described; operational environment and monitored/controlled physical variables specified; functional requirements describe initiation, I/O variables, task sequences and events, termination and system status; user interfaces per user category; safety functions must not rely on calendar date, and calendar-dependent actions must account for date concerns such as two-digit year); consistency (with safety system requirements and design and operational environment; no contradictory requirements; timing consistent with thermo-hydraulic safety analysis; uniform terminology); correctness (fault-free action descriptions; no extra requirements; accurate environment and variables; accurate initiation, I/O, sequences, termination); style (understandable; distinguish software requirements from design constraints, platforms, coding standards; precise technical terms in SRS or glossary; each requirement unique and complete in one location); traceability (two-way to safety system requirements and design; two-way to software design; forward to confirming inspections, analyses, or tests); unambiguity (one interpretation each and together); verifiability (specific analysis, review, or test constructible per requirement). Review guidance: requirements errors and misunderstood intent are a major software-error source; each functional characteristic should appear in each requirement; unclear requirements will be unclear to designers. Thread audit checks completeness, consistency with overall safety system requirements, absence of conflict, understandability, unambiguity, trace to safety system requirements, RTM location in software, and RTM test location.
- **SAD design output (S7 B.3.3.2).** A Software Architecture Description is required, reviewed with hardware architecture. Functional characteristics: reliability (combined architecture so individual software element failure does not compromise safety; actions on error detection; modular design controlling error propagation); safety (no new hazards; safety functions separated from normal operating and overhead functions with well-defined strictly controlled interfaces; online maintenance features included; no violation of single failure, channel separation, or Class 1E / non-1E separation; architecture configuration introduces no new hazards); security (SDOE per RG 1.152); timing (all timing limitations, handling strategy, required margins, measurement method; min/max execution times per architectural element; scheduling and inter-process communication described; correct sequence; SRP BTP 7-21 and SRP Section 7.9 as the BTP points). Process: completeness (all SRS requirements; all SRS operating modes including initialization, operational, shut-down, maintenance, test); consistency (each element compatible with SRS, hardware architecture, environment, and other software elements; interactive timing consistent; uniform terminology); style (understandable; developer style guide; rationale for architectural decisions); traceability (two-way SRS to architecture; two-way architecture to detailed design; forward to confirming inspections, analyses, or tests); verifiability (analyses, reviews, tests can show architecture satisfies software requirements). Review guidance: the architecture must let the reviewer understand how the software works, data flow, and deterministic nature, in enough detail to understand operation.
- **SDS design output (S7 B.3.3.3).** A Software Design Specification is required. Functional: accuracy (calculations specified to meet accuracy requirements; floating point avoided when possible, otherwise special care; input-range determination, out-of-range detection, and actions specified; calculations analyzed for convergence, round-off, precision, accuracy as appropriate); reliability (detailed design so single element failures do not cause safety system failure); robustness (correct operation under unexpected, incorrect, anomalous, or improper input, hardware, or software behavior; no failure and no incorrect outputs under those conditions; attention to physically possible device values even if logically impossible in the application, for sensor error and line noise); safety (no new hazards); security (unauthorized changes prevented, detected, or mitigated per RG 1.152); timing (deterministic stimulus-to-response delay; SRP BTP 7-21). Process: completeness (actions of each software unit for entire input domain; complete enough for implementation; all SRS-anticipated situations; equipment, human, hardware, software interfaces; equations, algorithms, control logic fully specified); consistency (with architecture; mutual consistency of elements; operational environment; I/O consistent with hardware or pre-developed software interfaces; detailed timing consistent with parent architectural element; models and numerical techniques agree with standard references where applicable; uniform terminology; mathematical mutual compatibility); correctness (equations, algorithms, control logic evaluated for potential errors and detailed enough to code; data structures ensure correct initialization, access, scale, and dimension; no use before init, no unanticipated value change, no change by unanticipated element); style (developer style guide; each element specified; design-decision rationale; programming language standards identified; language features disallowed without justification identified); traceability (two-way detailed design to architecture; two-way to code; forward to confirming inspections, analyses, or tests); verifiability (analyses, reviews, tests can show design satisfies architecture). Review guidance: SDS ensures code reflects requirements; thread audit follows several requirements into final code; read the whole SDS for understandability and sufficiency; carefully review the V&V report on the SDS; reviewer should find no problems the V&V team missed; after V&V completion the reviewer should find no errors.
- **Code listings (S7 B.3.3.4).** Implementation produces code showing required characteristics. Functional: accuracy (source meets accuracy requirements and design; care on floating point, round-off, precision; math library accuracy known, documented, and shown adequate); robustness (corrupted data does not fail the safety system; avoid data corruption; check input data identity and format; check message identity and content format; corrective actions on violation); safety (no new hazards); security (unnecessary code not introduced per RG 1.152); timing (deterministic execution; SRP BTP 7-21). Process: completeness (meets all design specifications and implementation constraints; compatible with hardware environment); consistency (variable names, types, locations, array sizes consistent; equations correspond to SDS models and techniques; parameters passed consistent in number, type, structure, physical units, direction; min/max execution times consistent with expected performance); correctness (implements all SDS requirements; no features or functions not required); style (design-document programming style constraints followed; data structures protected against simultaneous change; arrays fixed predefined length; no global variables; no dynamic memory allocation); traceability (two-way detailed design to code; two-way code to containing subsystem or system at factory build and test; forward design element to confirming inspections, analyses, or tests); verifiability (analyses, reviews, tests can show code correctly implements detailed design). Review guidance: sufficient comments and annotations so developer intent is clear for the reviewer and for future modification; undocumented code is not suitable for safety-related nuclear use; documentation enough for a qualified software engineer; escalate language or tool questions to other NRC staff or independent contractors as needed.
- **System build documents (S7 B.3.3.5).** One or more SBDs required. Functional: robustness (methods to detect incorrectly built releases; identify errors and anomalies found during build); safety (build introduces no new hazards); security (build does not introduce unintended changes per RG 1.152). Process: completeness (all build procedures fully specified; all required software units, code, and data included); consistency (with SRS, design description, and code; uniform terminology); correctness (correct versions of all required software elements and documents identified; verify correct elements including library units actually used); style (developer standards; technical terms defined in document or glossary); traceability (integrated build elements back to code elements; forward to field installation); verifiability (analyze, review, or test each integrated build against product functional requirements; methods to detect bad builds; identify build errors and anomalies). Review guidance: SBD proves delivered and installed software is the V&V'd and tested software; future maintenance needs correct version identity; check version, revision, and date; verify that identity matches what was tested; configuration management group should hold this information.
- **Installation configuration tables (S7 B.3.3.6).** ICTs configure installed software correctly. Functional: functionality (tables configure the installed system to required plant functionality); safety (no new hazards); security (no unneeded functionality; protected from unauthorized changes per RG 1.152). Process: completeness (all information necessary for correct operation); consistency (with SRS, design description, code, and build documents); correctness (all plant-specific data); traceability (each installed program element back to the integrated software elements that created it); verifiability (analyze, review, or test each installed system on initial install, subsequent installs, and periodically in operation). Review guidance: options, variable setpoints, and operating methods need plant-specific configuration recorded in the ICT; sample changeable items for correctness; verify V&V already made the determination then sample (for example setpoints versus setpoint calculations; other items back to system specification); ICT is critical for vendor and applicant or licensee configuration management.
- **Operations manuals (S7 B.3.3.7).** One or more software OMs (may sit inside a system operations manual). Functional characteristics are not directly scored because OMs do not impose requirements on the software; process characteristics apply. Process: completeness (all operator-available actions for all modes including error recovery and backup; operator actions as inputs, initiated actions, responses; purpose and operation of each function and interfaces; operational environment with precautions and limitations against personnel or plant hazards and security vulnerabilities; monitored/controlled physical variables; user interfaces per category); consistency (with system operations, safety system requirements and design, SRS, SDS, operational environment; no contradictory instructions; uniform terminology); style (understandable to users; technical terms defined; may be reference-manual style assuming trained users); traceability (forward from SRS and operations plan to OM showing how each requirement is carried out by operators or automatically without operator action and how results display; forward from code-generated error messages to OM descriptions); unambiguity (user instructions have one interpretation). Review guidance: primary concern is unusual or emergency operation for digital-system safety; normal-operation portions are reviewed at less depth.
- **Software maintenance manuals (S7 B.3.3.8).** One or more SMMs (may sit inside a system maintenance manual). Functional characteristics not directly scored. Process: completeness (maintenance procedures fully defined; precautions and limitations against hazards and security vulnerabilities; field trouble reports collected and analyzed for needed software changes; configuration management procedures described or referenced; procedures to verify changes carried out correctly without introducing faults, to return software correctly to service, and to perform field upgrades); style (understandable; terms defined; may be reference-manual style for trained users); traceability (forward from maintenance plan to manual showing how each requirement is carried out). Review guidance: determine whether applicant/licensee or vendor performs maintenance; board-swap-only licensee maintenance needs less manual depth than board-level repair; judge adequacy of detail accordingly.
- **Software training manuals (S7 B.3.3.9).** One or more STMs (may sit inside a system training manual). Functional characteristics not directly scored. Process: completeness (all operator-available actions for all modes including error recovery; user and equipment inputs, initiated actions, responses; operational environment with precautions and limitations; monitored/controlled variables; user interfaces per category); consistency (with safety system requirements and design, SRS, SDS, operational environment; no contradictory instructions; uniform terminology); style (understandable; terms defined; may be tutorial style assuming users also have the operations manual); traceability (forward from SRS and training plan to manual showing how each requirement is carried out by users or automatically and how results display). Review guidance: manuals must be understandable and useful; taking the training courses that use them is one method; aim may be technician or software engineer, so judge against intended use; escalate training-documentation questions to other NRC staff or independent contractors as needed.
- **Review procedures (S7 B.4).** Reviews combine document inspection and analysis. Confirm software life cycle plans incorporate appropriate commitments (B.3.1). New software or an unproven development team needs greater planning-phase emphasis. Audit a sample of V&V, safety analysis, and configuration management documentation across activity groups to confirm proper implementation (B.3.2 criteria derive the per-activity inspection). Review a sample of design outputs to confirm they address functional requirements allocated to software and show expected process characteristics (B.3.3 derives per-output inspection). SRP Appendix 7.0-A holds additional detail on software review process and its relationship to system reviews.

## Key Concepts

- **Plans supply the yardstick; implementation supplies the evidence.** B.3.2 acceptance is "tasks in the plan carried out in their entirety," documented.
- **Four implementation pillars.** Safety analysis, V&V, configuration management, testing.
- **Thread audit finds process truth.** Sampling requirements into code and tests is how staff check that V&V actually worked.
- **Missed errors after claimed V&V are a process alarm.** Isolated misses differ from systematic ones; systematic ones can reject the software path.
- **CM is identification, baseline, change control, status accounting, audit.** CCB authority and safety-impact review on changes are not optional decorations.
- **Test bottom-up to install.** RG 1.171 covers pre-integrated-system testing; documentation and evaluation follow RG 1.170 and RG 1.168 positions the BTP cites.
- **Two characteristic families on every scored output.** Functional (what the product must do) and process (whether the artifact is complete, consistent, correct, styled, traceable, unambiguous, verifiable as applicable).
- **Design-output set is fixed vocabulary.** SRS, SAD, SDS, CL, SBDs, ICTs, OMs, SMMs, STMs.
- **OM, SMM, STM skip functional scoring.** They still need process characteristics because operators, maintainers, and trainees use them.
- **Simplicity is a correctness property.** Unused convenience features do not belong in safety-related software.
- **Unverifiable conformance slogans fail.** "Consistent with" without named clauses is not verifiable.
- **Deterministic timing is recurring.** SRS, architecture, design, and code all carry timing expectations; SRP BTP 7-21 is the real-time pointer.
- **SDOE is the recurring security hook.** RG 1.152 as the BTP cites it for undocumented code, unauthorized change, and unneeded functionality controls.
- **B.4 is sample-based.** Plans get commitment checks; implementation and outputs get representative samples, with heavier planning emphasis for new software or new teams.

## Mental Models

- **ch02-ch06 accept the contracts; ch07 grades performance and deliverables.**
- **RTM is the spine through B.3.2 and B.3.3.** If the matrix cannot walk system need to test evidence, the review stalls.
- **Thread audit is a biopsy, not a census.** Representative and statistically minded sampling, then escalate if the tissue is bad.
- **Build and install identity must match V&V identity.** SBD and ICT exist so the plant does not run a cousin of the tested software.
- **Emergency OM depth beats routine OM polish.** Safety review weight follows hazard, not page count.
- **Maintenance manual depth follows who turns the screwdriver.** Vendor repair versus licensee board-swap changes the adequacy bar.

## Anti-patterns

- **Claiming plan compliance without life cycle activity-group evidence.** B.3.2 wants documentation per group, not a cover letter.
- **Validation tests that do not map one-to-one onto SRS requirements.** Every requirement needs test coverage and clear pass evidence.
- **Empty problem-report history treated as success without checking test thoroughness.** Unusual cleanliness is a review flag.
- **Reviewer-found defects already past V&V signoff, left unexplained.** Systematic misses threaten rejection.
- **Baselines changed without CCB and without identification bump.** Change control integrity fails.
- **Crediting software reliability numbers without method scrutiny.** Lead-in warns there is no consensus prediction method.
- **Shipping unused features "for later" in safety code.** Correctness forbids unneeded functions.
- **"Meets the intent of RG X" with no clause list.** Unverifiable.
- **Undocumented source accepted for safety use.** CL review rejects it.
- **Installed version that is not the tested build.** SBD/ICT failure.
- **ICT setpoints that disagree with setpoint calculations.** Sample check is expected to catch that.
- **Same review depth on normal OM chapters as on emergency chapters.** Mis-weights the safety question.
- **One maintenance-manual depth for vendor-only repair and for licensee software maintenance.** B.3.3.8 says judge by who maintains.

## Key Takeaways

1. B.3.2 accepts implementation when safety analysis, V&V, CM, and testing tasks from the accepted plans are fully carried out and documented, including bidirectional traceability and integration, validation, and installation test evidence.
2. Thread audit samples requirements into code and tests; repeated reviewer-found escapes past V&V can show the process is unfit for safety-related nuclear software.
3. CM implementation needs unambiguous identification, per-group baselines, CCB-authorized change control with safety-impact review, status accounting, and CM audits.
4. Testing must be complete across modes including error recovery, consistent with requirements and design artifacts, and traceable from SRS and SDS into test documentation; RG 1.171 covers pre-integrated-system testing.
5. B.3.3 scores design outputs with functional characteristics (accuracy, functionality, reliability, robustness, safety, security, timing) and process characteristics (completeness, consistency, correctness, style, traceability, unambiguity, verifiability) as applicable per output.
6. SRS, SAD, and SDS carry the requirements-to-architecture-to-detail chain with two-way traces and deterministic timing; CL must implement only required features with protected data structures, fixed arrays, and no globals or dynamic allocation as the BTP states.
7. SBDs and ICTs lock build and plant-configuration identity to the V&V'd product; OMs, SMMs, and STMs are scored on process characteristics with review depth matched to emergency use and to who maintains or trains.
8. B.4 sequences plan commitment review, sampled implementation audits, and sampled design-output reviews, with extra planning emphasis for new software or unproven teams, and points to SRP Appendix 7.0-A for software-to-system review relationship.
