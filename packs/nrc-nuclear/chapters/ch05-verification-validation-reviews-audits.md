## Core Idea

RG 1.168 covers verification, validation, reviews, and audits for digital computer software used in nuclear power plant safety systems across the software life cycle inside the system life cycle. IEEE Std. 1012-2004 is the staff-accepted approach for V&V of safety system software, and IEEE Std. 1028-2008 is the staff-accepted approach for software reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, and audits, each subject to the exceptions and additions in the guide's regulatory positions. Staff positions in C address software integrity, software reliability, independence of software V&V, conformance of materials, quality assurance, tools for software development, and V&V tasks (including audits, regression analysis and testing, secure analysis, test evaluation, and evaluation of user documentation), then map which IEEE annexes are endorsed, partially endorsed, or not endorsed.

BTP 7-14 B.3.1.10 is the staff review of the Software Verification and Validation Plan (SVVP). It scores management, implementation, and resource characteristics, requires independence of the V&V organization, and treats a disciplined SVVP plus the V&V reports as essential evidence that the development process will produce high-quality safety software.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **IEEE Std. 1012-2004 V&V method and IEEE Std. 1028-2008 reviews/audits method (S1 C lead-in).** The RG applies to all software life cycle aspects in the system life cycle context. IEEE Std. 1012-2004 is acceptable for V&V of safety system software with the listed exceptions and additions. IEEE Std. 1028-2008 is acceptable for reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, and audits, often used with software quality assurance, again with listed exceptions and additions. Cited criteria are Appendix B to 10 CFR Part 50 unless noted. Informative annexes to both standards may be useful, but the staff does not endorse those annexes except where noted below.
- **Software integrity level 4 for safety system software (S1 C.1).** Clause 4 of IEEE Std. 1012-2004 defines a four-level integrity scheme (level 4 highest, level 1 lowest) used to set the minimum V&V task set. The applicant or licensee may use that method or another method mapped to it. Software used in nuclear power plant safety systems is assigned integrity level 4 or the equivalent by that mapping. The staff takes exception to Annex B Table B.1 assignment wording that uses "critical consequences" as the level-4 task description and to Table B.3 guidance that would justify integrity below level 4 from likelihood. The phrase "no mitigation possible" in Clause 4 is read as mitigation within the given safety system's safety function only; a diverse system may still support overall plant-level safety. The assignment rule remains: level 4 or equivalent.
- **Software reliability verification and quantitative goals (S1 C.2).** Criterion III requires verifying or checking design adequacy. Table 1 of IEEE Std. 1012-2004 is an acceptable approach for software reliability verification or testing; its component and integration test-plan tasks (Design V&V Tasks 5 and 6) describe methods for measuring software reliability as criteria that software elements correctly implement requirements. Staff acceptance of quantitative reliability goals for computer-based safety systems still rests on deterministic criteria for the computer system in its entirety (hardware, system software, firmware, application, and interconnections), consistent with other agency guidance on digital safety system reliability measures.
- **Independence of software V&V (S1 C.3).** Criterion I requires QA persons and organizations to report high enough to have authority and freedom, including independence from cost and schedule when those oppose quality. QA functions include verifying by checking, auditing, and inspecting that safety-related activities were performed correctly. Criterion III requires design verification and checking by persons other than the designers. Reviewers performing verification therefore are not part of the design organization's development effort and use an independent structure for technical, financial, and managerial independence that is not under design or project-management budget and schedule constraints. Organizational mapping must show how staff reviewers are independent and exclusive to the tasking areas. Figure F.1 in Annex F of IEEE Std. 1012-2004 is acceptable only for the top three relationship boxes (Acquire Program Management, V&V Effort, Development Effort); the bottom three Staff boxes and relationship lines are excluded. The applicant or licensee retains ultimate responsibility for V&V adequacy and software quality, including when an external organization performed V&V (for example commercial-grade product later used in a safety-related system). Financial, managerial, and technical independence must be strong enough that design-process schedule and resource pressure do not compromise V&V. Criterion II requires indoctrination and training so proficiency is achieved and maintained; independent verifiers should be at least as proficient, and preferably more proficient, in both software engineering and I&C safety system functionality and design. IEEE Std. 1012-2004 guidance on financial and managerial independence supports Criterion I budget and schedule freedom; its technical independence guidance supports Criterion III design-check separation. Clause C.4.1 wording that V&V responsibility is vested in an organization separate from development does not mean a separate company is required, but Criteria I and III must still be met.
- **Conformance of materials and preexisting software (S1 C.4).** Criteria III and VII address selection and review of materials, parts, equipment, and processes essential to safety-related functions, and conformance of purchased material to procurement documents. IEEE Std. 1012-2004 guidance on retrospective V&V of unverified or preexisting (pre-developed, including commercial off-the-shelf) software is not endorsed by this RG for acceptance of preexisting safety system software. RG 1.152 addresses acceptance of preexisting software; the source also names the commercial-grade evaluation path in EPRI TR-106439 as accepted by the July 17, 1997 SER.
- **Quality assurance records for V&V (S1 C.5).** Criterion I identifies QA functions of assuring an appropriate QA program is established and executed and of verifying that safety-related activities were performed correctly. Criterion III requires design changes under design control commensurate with the original design. Beyond IEEE Std. 1012-2004 Clause 7.7.4 control procedures, V&V materials needed to verify V&V program effectiveness or to evidence activities affecting quality are maintained as quality assurance records. Records needed to verify changes follow Criterion XVII retention of sufficient records of activities affecting quality.
- **Tools for software development (S1 C.6).** Tools used to develop safety system software follow IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 as endorsed by RG 1.152. V&V tasks of witnessing, reviewing, and testing are not required for the tools themselves when the software produced with the tools is subject to V&V activities that will detect tool-introduced flaws. If that cannot be shown, the provisions of this RG apply to tool development.
- **V&V tasks treated as minimum, not optional (S1 C.7).** Table 3 of IEEE Std. 1012-2004 lists "optional" V&V tasks further described in informative Annex G. The staff rejects optional status for some of those tasks for safety system software under Appendices A and B to 10 CFR Part 50, whether or not the V&V organization performs them. Unless incorporated into other SVVP V&V tasks or performed outside the software V&V organization as duties of another organization, the minimum set includes: (a) Audits, including functional, in-process, and physical audits of software (Criterion I verification functions; Criterion III design-check measures). Even when QA or configuration management commonly owns audits, the V&V organization performs and relies on them and describes them in the SVVP when it does; IEEE Std. 1028-2008 is an acceptable audit method. (b) Regression analysis and testing after software modifications (Criterion III change control commensurate with original design), as an element of V&V of software changes. (c) Secure analysis: IEEE Std. 1012-2004 security-analysis V&V tasks and Clause 7.7.4 controls protecting products and V&V results from unauthorized alteration. Tasks test secure operational environment design features, verify and validate SDOE feature criteria and functions, and protect the test environment and results from inadvertent manipulation. Security analysis may live in a separate document or inside other documents. Measures securing the test environment address V&V test-phase vulnerabilities from the vulnerability assessment. Staff may review and audit that documentation and testing to conclude the digital safety system was developed in a secure environment and will be protected from inadvertent operational actions. RG 1.152 contains SDOE guidance to meet IEEE Std. 603-1991 and 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B. (d) Test evaluation confirming technical adequacy of test plans, designs, and results against Criterion II controlled conditions and Criterion XI evaluation of test results. (e) Evaluation of user documentation as part of translating the design basis into specifications, procedures, drawings, and instructions under Criterion III, because user documentation supports safe operation and proper maintenance.
- **Annex endorsement map (S1 C.8).** IEEE Std. 1012-2004 has eight informative annexes and IEEE Std. 1028-2008 has two; listed as information sources and not endorsed unless noted. 1012 Annex A (mapping V&V activities to ISO/IEC 12207:1995, IEEE Std. 1074-1997, and CMMI process areas) is not endorsed as implementation guidance but may help integration. Annex B (risk-based integrity scheme) is endorsed as described with the C.1 exceptions. Annex C (IV&V definitions), including Table C.1 conditional independence, is not endorsed; BTP 7-14 gives additional guidance in this area. Annex D (reuse software V&V) is not endorsed; SRP Appendix 7.0-A and BTP 7-14 give additional guidance. Annex E (V&V measures) is not endorsed for lack of consensus on quality and coverage measures, though feedback use is allowed. Annex F organizational diagram is endorsed with the C.3 exception excluding the bottom Staff boxes. Annex G optional tasks is not endorsed, though C.7 treats some listed tasks as necessary. Annex H bibliography is informational. 1028 Annex A (comparison of review types) is not endorsed as implementation guidance but may clarify review-type differences. 1028 Annex B bibliography is informational.
- **B.3.1.10 SVVP staff review (S7).** RG 1.152 Section C.2 SDOE testing guidance, RG 1.168 / IEEE Std 1012 for V&V methods (with exceptions), RG 1.168 / IEEE Std 1028 for reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, and audits (with exceptions), and RG 1.168 Section C.7.c secure-analysis position (SDOE design-feature assessment as part of the minimum V&V set) frame the review. Management characteristics: purpose and scope of software V&V and a general V&V process description; organization with boundaries and interfaces to other company organizations, reporting channels, relationships among V&V tasks, independence from the development organization, and documented formal communication between V&V and design; oversight showing how software V&V integrates with system V&V, coordinates with development, and interacts with the software safety organization; responsibilities naming who completes each V&V task, who approves successful completion, and who approves release of reviewed and tested software design outputs; risks with methods to evaluate safety risk per software item and per V&V task, plus contingency plans when a V&V task may fail and recovery from that failure. Implementation: measurement indicators and completion criteria per V&V task; evaluation criteria for test plans, specifications, procedures, and cases and for review plans, specifications, and procedures; systematic collection and analysis of V&V analysis, review, and testing data, including measured, recorded, analyzed, and reported error rates from reviews and testing; procedures describing review and testing strategy, management of V&V activities, the V&V task set and planning assumptions, methods to evaluate each software design output and each development activity against system and software requirements, analysis/report/correct/reassess paths when errors are detected, risk evaluation of project development activities, and evaluation of proposed software changes on planned reviews and tests; anomaly report generation and dissemination, discrepancy resolution methods, test-case selection procedures, and software review activity procedures; V&V reporting that documents all activities (personnel, procedures, results), including review documentation, evaluation criteria, error reporting, and anomaly resolution, summarizing positive and negative practices and findings plus actions, methods, and tools used; description of required testing plans, specifications, procedures, and cases for unit, integration (subsystem), system validation, installation (acceptance), and regression testing of modifications, with test documentation requirements, readiness and evaluation criteria, error reporting, and anomaly resolution, and with test records covering item descriptions, data, logs, tester identities, observation types, results and acceptability, deficiency actions, and expected versus actual results for cases. RG 1.170 / IEEE Std 829 and RG 1.171 / ANSI/IEEE Std 1008 are named as the acceptable documentation and unit-test method pointers. Resources: methods, equipment, instrumentation, and tools per V&V task; specified unit, integration, validation, installation, and regression test methods; tool-selection process; hardware and software environment for V&V tools and necessary controls; listed international, national, industry, and company standards and guidelines for the V&V organization. Review guidance: the SVVP is a key planning document; high-quality process and rigorous V&V are essential to demonstrating high-quality software development, so staff carefully review the SVVP and the V&V reports. The most critical item is independence of the V&V organization in management, schedule, and finance per RG 1.168: V&V personnel are not subject to designer or project-manager scheduling pressure for reports or review effort, and the team reports to a management level not exerting direct pressure for a favorable V&V report. The reviewer judges whether the V&V effort is independent enough to perform its tasks without bowing to schedule and financial pressure.

## Key Concepts

- **Two endorsed process standards, nuclearized.** 1012 for V&V tasking and integrity-driven minimum sets; 1028 for how reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, and audits are conducted.
- **Integrity level 4 is the nuclear floor.** Mapping to another scheme is allowed; assigning below level 4 for safety system software is not.
- **Reliability measures sit under deterministic system criteria.** Table 1 methods help verify requirements implementation; quantitative goals alone do not replace whole-system deterministic acceptance.
- **Independence is technical, financial, and managerial.** Separate company is not required; separate development control and protected V&V authority are.
- **Licensee keeps V&V responsibility.** External or commercial V&V does not transfer Appendix B accountability.
- **Verifier proficiency matches or exceeds design proficiency.** Training and skill are part of independence, not optional polish.
- **Preexisting software acceptance is outside this RG's 1012 reuse path.** Point to RG 1.152 and the named commercial-grade path instead of retrospective V&V annex guidance.
- **V&V products are QA records.** Effectiveness evidence and change-verification records are retained under Criteria I, III, and XVII.
- **Tools ride on product V&V when tool flaws would be detected there.** Otherwise tool development itself takes the RG 1.168 path.
- **Several "optional" 1012 tasks are mandatory here.** Audits, regression analysis and testing, secure analysis, test evaluation, and user-documentation evaluation join the minimum set unless covered elsewhere by plan.
- **Secure analysis protects SDOE features and the test bed.** Vulnerability-driven environment controls and staff audit rights sit with RG 1.152 SDOE guidance.
- **Annex map is surgical.** B and F (with exceptions) are in; C conditional independence, D reuse V&V, E measures, G optional-task catalog, and most other annexes are out as endorsement.
- **SVVP review grades plan quality and independence first.** Reports matter, but a non-independent V&V team fails the central B.3.1.10 check.

## Mental Models

- **RG 1.168 writes the V&V and review rules; B.3.1.10 grades the SVVP in staff review.** Use both when asking what acceptable V&V planning looks like.
- **Integrity level selects the task floor; nuclear use locks the floor at 4.** Tailoring is mapping and incorporation, not silent deletion of safety-critical tasks.
- **Independence walls protect finding defects from shipping pressure.** Budget, schedule, and reporting lines are the mechanism; a poster statement is not.
- **Audits are shared instruments.** QA or CM may own them operationally, but V&V still relies on them and records that reliance in the SVVP when it does.
- **Change reopens V&V.** Regression analysis and testing are how Criterion III change control shows up in the V&V minimum set.
- **Security is a V&V task family, not only a cyber plan annex.** SDOE features, test-environment protection, and vulnerability-linked controls are in scope here and in RG 1.152.
- **Test evaluation closes the loop on Criterion XI.** Plans and designs are not enough; results get technical adequacy review under controlled conditions.
- **User documentation is a safety interface.** Criterion III translation duties reach the manuals operators and maintainers use.

## Anti-patterns

- **Safety system software assigned below integrity level 4 without a demonstrated level-4 mapping.** C.1 exceptions block Table B.3 likelihood downgrades and reject "critical consequences" as the level-4 task description.
- **V&V team inside development budget, schedule, or reporting control.** C.3 and B.3.1.10 both treat that as a primary failure.
- **Assuming a separate company is required, or assuming any separate letterhead is enough without Criteria I and III substance.** C.3.
- **Relying on external V&V and treating licensee responsibility as transferred.** C.3 ultimate-responsibility paragraph.
- **Independent verifiers less skilled than designers in software engineering or I&C safety functions.** C.3 proficiency rule.
- **Accepting preexisting safety software solely through 1012 retrospective/reuse V&V annex guidance.** C.4 does not endorse that path; use RG 1.152 and the named commercial-grade route.
- **V&V materials and change-verification records not kept as QA records.** C.5.
- **Development tools neither covered by product V&V that would catch tool flaws nor developed under this RG.** C.6.
- **Treating audits, regression testing, secure analysis, test evaluation, or user-documentation evaluation as skippable "optional" 1012 tasks with no alternate owner.** C.7.
- **SVVP silent on functional, in-process, and physical software audits the V&V organization relies on.** C.7.a.
- **Modifications without regression analysis and testing in the V&V minimum set.** C.7.b.
- **Security analysis that never tests SDOE features or never protects the test environment and results.** C.7.c.
- **Test materials never evaluated for technical adequacy against controlled-conditions and test-result criteria.** C.7.d.
- **User documentation left outside design-basis translation checks.** C.7.e.
- **Using Annex C conditional independence or unendorsed reuse/measures annexes as the regulatory basis.** C.8.
- **SVVP that never names task completion criteria, error-rate measurement, anomaly resolution, or the full unit-through-regression test documentation set.** B.3.1.10 implementation characteristics.
- **V&V reporting that records only negative findings or omits methods, tools, and personnel.** B.3.1.10 reporting requirements.
- **Staff review that accepts an SVVP without checking management, schedule, and financial independence of the V&V team.** B.3.1.10 review guidance.

## Key Takeaways

1. RG 1.168 endorses IEEE Std. 1012-2004 for V&V and IEEE Std. 1028-2008 for reviews, inspections, walkthroughs, and audits of safety system software, each with nuclear exceptions and additions; standard body text is not reproduced here.
2. Safety system software is integrity level 4 or equivalent; staff reject Annex B paths that would assign lower integrity from likelihood wording.
3. Software reliability verification may use Table 1 component and integration test-plan methods, while quantitative reliability goals still require deterministic criteria for the whole computer system.
4. V&V independence is technical, financial, and managerial under Criteria I and III; a separate company is not mandatory; the licensee retains ultimate V&V and quality responsibility; verifiers should meet or exceed designer proficiency in software engineering and I&C safety design.
5. 1012 guidance on retrospective V&V of preexisting software is not endorsed for accepting preexisting safety system software; RG 1.152 and the named commercial-grade path apply instead.
6. V&V effectiveness and change-verification materials are QA records; development tools follow RG 1.152 / IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003, with tool V&V required only when product V&V would not detect tool-introduced flaws.
7. Audits, regression analysis and testing, secure analysis (including SDOE feature and test-environment protection), test evaluation, and user-documentation evaluation are part of the minimum V&V set unless incorporated elsewhere by plan or owned by another organization.
8. Annex endorsement is limited: 1012 Annex B and Annex F (top boxes only) are in with stated exceptions; Annex C conditional independence, D, E, G, and most other annex material are not endorsed as regulatory methods.
9. B.3.1.10 reviews SVVP purpose, independent organization and reporting, oversight interfaces to system V&V, development, and software safety, named task and release authorities, risk and contingency methods, measurement and procedures, anomaly handling, full-level test documentation, tools and standards, and the V&V reports.
10. The critical staff check is whether the V&V team can perform its work without schedule or financial pressure from design or project management for a favorable report.
