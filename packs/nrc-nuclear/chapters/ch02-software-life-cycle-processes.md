# Chapter 2: Software life cycle processes

## Core Idea

RG 1.173 tells applicants how to build software life cycle processes for digital safety system software. The staff position is that IEEE Std. 1074-2006 is an acceptable organizing method for those processes, but only with the exceptions and additions in the guide's regulatory positions. The standard orders inputs, activities, outputs, preconditions, and postconditions so that quality-critical work is performed and related correctly; it does not prescribe detailed technique inside each activity.

BTP 7-14 is the staff's review lens on the same ground. Acceptance of safety system software rests on three checks: acceptable plans controlled the work, the plans were followed through the life cycle, and the process produced acceptable design outputs. B.2 lists what information the reviewer expects in planning, implementation, and design-output packages. B.3.1 then sets acceptance criteria for the planning documents themselves, including the nine software plans from the Software Management Plan through the Software Safety Plan (B.3.1.1-B.3.1.9). SVVP, SCMP, and STP reviews sit later in the BTP and are covered in other chapters.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **IEEE Std. 1074-2006 as organizing method (S6 C lead-in; S7 B.3).** RG 1.173 states that the standard provides an approach the staff considers acceptable for 10 CFR Part 50 and RG 1.152 development-process expectations, subject to the guide's exceptions and additions. S7 points reviewers to the same RG/IEEE pair for an appropriate set of life cycle activities.
- **Clarifications for nuclear use (S6 C.1).** Because the standard was not written specifically for nuclear safety, five clarifications apply: identify applicable regulatory requirements and design bases in input, activity, and output descriptions (C.1.a); keep cost/schedule contingency actions consistent with Appendix B Criterion I independence from cost and schedule when those oppose safety software quality (C.1.b); include an acceptance process for preexisting or commercial off-the-shelf software under Criterion III and VII, with RG 1.152 and the commercial-grade path the source names (C.1.c); treat security planning as secure software development environment plus cyber security, pointing to RG 1.152 and RG 5.71 rather than the standard's security-objectives wording (C.1.d); use the guide's definitions of accident and hazard (C.1.e).
- **Compliance means full mandatory coverage (S6 C.2).** Claiming compliance with IEEE Std. 1074-2006 means all mandatory activities are performed, every "shall" is met, and every input, output, activity, precondition, and postcondition the standard mentions is described or accounted for in the licensee's or applicant's life cycle model. The standard organizes; it does not replace detailed implementation guidance.
- **Software Safety Analysis Activity Group (S6 C.3).** Beyond the standard, a Software Safety Analysis Activity Group is required so development stays consistent with system safety analyses. Analyses run for each life cycle phase with defined inputs (regulatory requirements and guidance, system safety analysis, prior-phase safety analysis, and current design information), activity checks (system safety requirements addressed, no new hazards, safety-affecting software elements identified, other elements shown not to affect safety, problems and resolutions documented), and outputs feeding design, later safety analysis, configuration management, and V&V. Those activities run under a software safety plan covering organization, safety officer responsibilities, management of the work, and per-phase hazard and abnormal-condition analyses.
- **New or modified software installation and operation rules (S6 C.4).** Temporary work-arounds during installation are allowed only when every software change follows configuration control and the changed software is checked offline before installation. Installation proceeds only when all functions affected by the software are declared inoperable under plant technical specifications; interconnected systems are taken out of service; subtle data couplings count. Before return to operable status, currently approved configuration-managed software is installed per the installation process so work-arounds are removed. Operation and maintenance remap the life cycle as development iterations under licensing-basis configuration control and V&V. System transitions and other safety system changes are evaluated under 10 CFR 50.59, with out-of-scope changes going in as license amendment requests.
- **Tailoring limits (S6 C.5).** Customer data tailoring at install must match the packaged installation plan information. Tailoring that is a design change, including configurations outside the original design, is not permitted unless it receives the full design and quality assurance treatment of safety system software development.
- **Annex endorsement map (S6 C.6).** Annex A (detailed life cycle activities) is normative and endorsed with the Part C clarifications. Annexes B (mapping example), C (mapping template), and D (life cycle model examples) are not endorsed. Annex E (glossary) is useful but not endorsed without further review. Annex F bibliography treatment follows the guide's Section B discussion of other standards.
- **B.2 information triad (S7 B.2).** Review material splits into planning documents (B.2.1), implementation evidence (B.2.2), and design outputs (B.2.3). Planning may include SMP, SDP, SQAP, SIntP, SInstP, SMaintP, STrngP, SOP, SSP, SVVP, SCMP, and STP. Implementation evidence includes safety analyses, V&V analysis and test reports, configuration management reports, and testing activities across requirements, design, implementation, integration, validation, installation, and operations and maintenance. Design outputs include SRS, hardware and software architecture descriptions (SAD), SDS, code listings, build documents, installation configuration tables, and operations, maintenance, and training manuals, with system requirements documents read for context.
- **Conformance claims must be specific (S7 B.3 lead-in).** Format conformance and content conformance are distinct. Complete conformance lets the reviewer check that the claimed method was used and the system is safe. Partial or qualified conformance requires identifying which guidance was followed; vague claims such as processes "consistent with RG 1.173" without naming the followed parts trigger requests for additional information before the process can be judged.
- **Planning acceptance structure (S7 B.3.1).** Acceptable plans become additional criteria for later process and product review. Plans are coherent views assigned to defined organizations; alternative plan sets are allowed if they still carry all required information and meet the criteria. Plans may layer (for example a test plan coordinating development-team and V&V-team testing). Every planning document is judged on process characteristics: consistency, style, traceability, unambiguity, and verifiability. Characteristic families are management (purpose, organization, oversight, responsibilities, risks, security), implementation (measurement, procedures, record keeping, schedule), and resource (budget, methods/tools, personnel, standards). Incomplete early plans for future plants are acceptable if missing budget or schedule data is filled before it is needed. Staff review targets safety and quality, not cost-effectiveness; budget and schedule matter only insofar as they support thorough design and V&V. Multi-vendor work is coordinated by the principal plant vendor, with interface and integration emphasis. When both applicant/licensee and vendor plans exist, they are reviewed together; Appendix B allows delegation of QA work but the licensee retains responsibility and must verify activities affecting safety-related functions.
- **Software Management Plan (S7 B.3.1.1).** The SMP covers management aspects of the software project. Management traits: purpose (intent, functions traceable to system requirements, system and project overview, assumptions, scope, product and process goals), organization (structure, interfaces, reporting, subcontractor management, and independence of QA, software safety, and V&V from development reporting and financial control), oversight (priorities, progress monitoring, deviation detection), responsibilities (duties; development owners primary for design-output quality), and security (SDOE per RG 1.152). Implementation: management indicators and metrics collection; procedures for priorities, assumptions, monitoring, key-decision rationale, deliverable lists, management reviews, corrective action and process improvement, reports, production and change of the remaining plans under configuration management. Resources: project budget with protected QA/safety/V&V budgets; management methods and tools; personnel numbers, qualifications, and phase loading, with safety and V&V staff competent in software engineering. Review focus: vendor-licensee interface and quality judgment, independence of assurance groups, effective Appendix B oversight by the applicant or licensee, duty-holder competence, and effective SDOE methods. Management-process detail is secondary except where safety is at stake; assurance budgets and V&V qualification get real scrutiny (V&V at least as qualified as design is the stated rule of thumb).
- **Software Development Plan (S7 B.3.1.2).** The SDP is the technical development plan. Management: purpose of each life cycle activity group; documented life cycle with uniquely identifiable development, verification, and support processes and well-defined inputs and outputs; technical oversight and ordered software quality factors; risk identification and management with attention to safety-compromising risks (system, hardware integration, size and complexity, pre-developed software, cost and schedule, technology, program interfaces), plus key design issues and studies needed to resolve them. Implementation: technical quality indicators including development-phase error rates; procedures that divide activities into tasks with identified inputs, sources, start conditions, outputs, and destinations, plus end-of-activity review (RG 1.173 acceptable for defining life cycle inputs and outputs); schedule with work packages, milestones, hold points, and reviews as milestones. Resources: development methods, reuse approach, environment (design aids, compilers, loaders, libraries), tool qualification graded to safety significance of the software produced, prohibition of unverifiable or safety-incompatible methods unless analysis shows the alternative is less safe; listed standards and guidelines including requirements, design, and coding standards. Review focus: whether the SDP yields careful development and whether other plans (SVVP, SCMP, and related) are actually applied through effective oversight and timely deviation detection; size/complexity and commercial pre-developed software risks; clear life cycle tasks and I/O; tool-output verification (defects tools miss must be caught by V&V, or the tool itself is safety-related); and standards previously staff-approved or else reviewed for regulatory fit.
- **Software Quality Assurance Plan (S7 B.3.1.3).** The SQAP typically sits under an NRC-approved QA program and must conform to Appendix B and the overall QA program. It identifies which QA procedures apply to which software processes, which methods implement them, and what software-specific supplements are needed. Management: QA process purpose and goals; QA organization boundaries and reporting; responsibilities including assessment of safety, reliability, and maintainability characteristics; security via periodic SDOE audits per RG 1.152. Implementation: QA measurement data; life cycle QA procedures, participation in standards/methods/tools assessment, documentation accuracy controls, adverse-condition tracking, life cycle traceability, required quality factors, QA reports, and formal reviews and audits (RG 1.168 / IEEE Std 1028 acceptable for QA reviews and audits); record keeping for documents under QA oversight. Resources: QA facilities, methods, and tools; methods to establish and maintain standards for software QA, V&V, and configuration management. Review focus: following the SQAP must produce high-quality software that performs the safety function; staff samples process and product rather than 100 percent code check, expecting the thread audit not to find errors the QA or V&V teams missed; if the licensee delegates QA to a vendor, the retention of responsibility and the method of judging software quality must be clear; QA needs authority and freedom from cost and schedule pressure.
- **Software Integration Plan (S7 B.3.1.4).** Management covers purpose (software and hardware/software integration goals), organization (boundaries and reporting; integration may report into development), and responsibilities. Implementation: integration adequacy indicators and error-rate reporting; procedures and controls for software, hardware/software, and multi-vendor systems integration, plus documented integration tests and expected results. Resources: integration tools qualified to the safety significance of the software created with them. Review weight is lower unless multiple developer groups, commercial dedication, or reuse raise integration criticality.
- **Software Installation Plan (S7 B.3.1.5).** Management: installation purpose and qualified operating environment (temperature, humidity, vibration, rack space); organization relative to broader safety system installation (development or customer may install); clear responsibility split when the customer installs. Implementation: installation measurement and error reporting; procedures for software, hardware/software, and systems installation with functional checks (computer, sensors and actuators, card presence and slots, communications, correct software versions on correct computers), anomaly reporting to the developer before operation, and adequate testing in the SInstP or SVVP; for operating plants, declare affected functions inoperable under technical specifications before install and complete return-to-service testing before operable declaration. Resources: installation tools qualified to the safety significance of the installed software. Review notes written approved procedures, configuration-controlled correction of procedure errors, mixed software/hardware installers, and system test as a V&V responsibility under IEEE Std 1012 as the BTP cites it.
- **Software Maintenance Plan (S7 B.3.1.6).** Maintenance means repairing nonconforming items or implementing pre-planned actions to maintain performance; performance improvements or environment adaptations are design changes. Management: maintenance purpose and field trouble-report path; organization with formal customer communication so operational failures are not ignored; responsibilities; safety-risk management during maintenance; SDOE controls against unintended functionality and unauthorized changes during repair, testing, and calibration (RG 1.152). Implementation: maintenance effectiveness metrics; applicant/licensee procedures and optional vendor procedures; problem reporting content (time, date, state, description, correction) feeding Appendix B XV/XVI nonconformance and corrective action, with 10 CFR 50.59 and Part 21 evaluation as appropriate; design-control treatment for "use-as-is" or "repair" dispositions; as-built records of accepted deviations; timely common-cause evaluation for operability; periodic problem analysis and improvement recommendations. Resources: maintenance facilities, software, hardware, documentation, and tools qualified to safety significance. Review stress: same deliberate controls after delivery when the original team is gone and schedule pressure is high; correct version control with the SCMP; regression testing using original acceptance tests or a justified subset; confirmation that proposed work is maintenance not new function; maintainer and tool qualification equal to the original design, with provision to qualify replacement tool revisions; dual vendor and licensee plans when maintenance is contracted, with the licensee retaining Appendix B responsibility.
- **Software Training Plan (S7 B.3.1.7).** Management: training needs for plant staff including operators and I&C engineers and technicians, plus training facilities; training organization interfaces and trainer knowledge of software operation; responsibilities including customer training. Implementation: training effectiveness metrics including end-of-training error rate; job-function-specific training procedures, products, and reports. Resources: methods and tools; training on a system equivalent to the actual hardware/software. Review scales with who operates and maintains: vendor-maintained systems need less deep licensee software skill than licensee-performed software maintenance.
- **Software Operations Plan (S7 B.3.1.8).** Management: operational purpose and functions; organization for operator stations and supporting actions; operator responsibilities; physical, logical, and administrative controls against unauthorized changes per RG 1.152. Implementation: operational error-rate measurement; start/operate/stop procedures for all modes, software state consistent with plant mode, backup of data and code, and error-message lists with interpretation and resolution steps. Resources: facilities, software, hardware, and documentation needed to operate. Review prioritizes completeness and unauthorized-change controls.
- **Software Safety Plan (S7 B.3.1.9; ties to S6 C.3).** The SSP plans software safety analysis across the life cycle and must conform to the design basis. Management: purpose and scope and interaction with system safety; safety organization boundaries, integration with system safety and coordination with development and V&V, and a single safety officer with clear responsibility for software safety qualities; responsibilities and authority to enforce safety requirements in SRS, design, and implementation and to reject unsafe pre-developed software or unsafe tools; methods to reduce software-failure safety risks, resolve software-controlled hazards, and document safety analysis on requirements, design descriptions, and source code, including abnormal events and malicious modifications. Implementation: safety-effort metrics; safety strategy, issue resolution escalated to the project manager, unacceptable-risk resolution, per-task methods, hazard identification (software-caused and software-controlled), safety requirements in the SRS, life cycle safety activities through operation and maintenance, required safe-operation documentation, operational monitoring of safety functions, and traceability from plant/system safety analysis and security vulnerability assessment into software safety analysis. Resources: tool selection and controls against tool-introduced hazards; listed standards for the safety organization. Review accepts safety content folded into the SMP or another plan if the concepts are covered; expects a group that weighs digital-system safety and security risk; checks that considered risks match the stated strategy and that safety activities will resolve the issues.

## Key Concepts

- **Organizing standard versus implementing detail.** IEEE Std. 1074-2006 ensures important activities exist and relate; detailed how-to lives in the plans and implementing procedures.
- **Mandatory completeness for claimed compliance.** Omitting mandatory activities voids a compliance claim even if the standard's own conformance clause would allow elimination.
- **Safety analysis is an added activity group.** Nuclear use requires phase-by-phase software safety analysis beyond the base standard, under a safety plan with a responsible safety officer.
- **Configuration control gates installation work-arounds.** Offline check plus full CM is the price of any temporary change during install.
- **Inoperability before install.** Affected and interconnected functions leave service under technical specifications; subtle shared-data couplings still count.
- **Maintenance is remapped development.** Field changes re-enter the life cycle under licensing-basis CM and V&V; they are not informal patches.
- **10 CFR 50.59 on transitions.** Safety system changes get the 50.59 evaluation; out-of-scope changes become license amendment requests.
- **No free design-change tailoring at install.** Packaged install tailoring is bounded; new configurations take full design control.
- **Annex A only among 1074 annexes.** Mapping examples, templates, model catalogs, and the unreviewed glossary are not staff-endorsed shortcuts.
- **Three review bags: plans, implementation, outputs.** B.2 enumerates the documents and reports; B.3.1 scores the plans before B.3.2/B.3.3 judge execution and products.
- **Named plan set is a view, not a mandatory file list.** Other packagings work if every required content item and criterion is still met.
- **Five plan quality attributes.** Consistency (internal and across the set), understandable style, traceability (system management to SMP to other plans to implementation and outputs), unambiguity, and verifiability that the plans were followed.
- **Independence of assurance from development.** QA, software safety, and V&V do not report to development and are not under development financial control; their budgets are protected.
- **Licensee retains QA responsibility.** Delegation to vendors is allowed; verification that safety-affecting work was done correctly is not.
- **Tool outputs that cannot be shown correct.** Compilers and similar tools either have their defects caught by V&V or the tools themselves are developed or dedicated as safety-related.
- **Maintenance versus design change.** Repair and pre-planned performance maintenance stay in SMaintP; attribute improvements and environment adaptations are design changes under full controls.
- **Common-cause urgency.** Any safety software error can hit redundant functions; problem evaluation must support timely operability determinations.
- **Single software safety officer.** Clear responsibility and authority to enforce safety requirements and reject unsafe reused software or tools.

## Mental Models

- **RG builds the process; BTP grades the plans.** ch02 pairs applicant-facing life cycle construction (RG 1.173) with reviewer-facing plan acceptance (B.3.1.1-B.3.1.9).
- **Plans are contracts for later evidence.** An accepted plan supplies the yardstick for B.3.2 implementation and B.3.3 design-output reviews.
- **Independence is structural, not ceremonial.** Reporting lines and budget walls for QA, safety, and V&V are the mechanism, not a poster statement.
- **Life cycle I/O is first-class data.** Every activity names inputs, sources, start gates, outputs, and destinations; RG 1.173 is the acceptable method for that definition.
- **Commercial and preexisting software enter through acceptance, not hope.** Suitability is established inside the model at a defined point, not assumed from marketplace presence.
- **Install is a controlled outage, not a copy step.** Inoperability, interconnection analysis, CM, offline checks, and clean final load are one chain.
- **Field maintenance reopens the factory.** Same design control, V&V, CM, and regression discipline apply when the original authors are gone.
- **Safety analysis threads every phase.** Requirements through operations and maintenance each produce safety analysis that feeds design, CM, and V&V.
- **Specific conformance beats slogans.** Reviewers need the followed clauses named before they can accept a "consistent with RG 1.173" story.

## Anti-patterns

- **Claiming IEEE Std. 1074-2006 compliance after dropping mandatory activities.** S6 C.2 rejects partial elimination while still flying the compliance flag.
- **Letting cost or schedule contingency override safety software quality independence.** C.1.b ties contingency actions back to Appendix B Criterion I.
- **Dropping commercial or reusable software into the safety system without a life cycle acceptance process.** C.1.c and commercial-grade evaluation expectations block that path.
- **Relying on the standard's security-objectives text instead of SDOE and cyber guidance.** C.1.d points to RG 1.152 and RG 5.71.
- **Installing with live affected functions or uncleared work-arounds.** C.4 requires inoperability declarations, interconnection takedown, offline-checked CM changes, and final approved load before operable status.
- **Calling a design-changing install tailor a simple data tweak.** C.5 demands full design and QA measures for design-change tailoring.
- **Vague "consistent with RG 1.173" process claims.** S7 B.3 expects identified guidance and will RAI the rest.
- **Assurance organizations reporting to or funded by development.** B.3.1.1 flags lost independence as a core SMP defect.
- **Applicant oversight that cannot show Appendix B retention of responsibility over vendor work.** Dual plan sets without a verification method fail the B.3.1 general and SMP review guidance.
- **SDP that lists no life cycle I/O, no risk method for complexity or pre-developed software, or no tool-output verification story.** B.3.1.2 review guidance treats those as primary gaps.
- **SQAP that exists on paper while staff sampling finds errors QA and V&V never recorded.** B.3.1.3 treats that as evidence the assurance system is weak.
- **Maintenance treated as emergency hacking without regression testing, version control, or design-change screening.** B.3.1.6 exists to stop that pattern.
- **No software safety officer and no phase safety analyses.** S6 C.3 and B.3.1.9 both require planned, owned safety analysis work.
- **Training only on mismatched hardware/software.** B.3.1.7 wants equivalence to the delivered system.

## Key Takeaways

1. RG 1.173 endorses IEEE Std. 1074-2006 as an organizing life cycle method for safety system software, with nuclear-specific clarifications on regulatory inputs, cost/schedule independence, commercial software acceptance, security planning, and hazard/accident definitions.
2. Compliance means every mandatory activity and every stated I/O, precondition, and postcondition is present in the applicant's model; the standard does not supply detailed technique.
3. A Software Safety Analysis Activity Group, run under a software safety plan, is required in addition to the base standard and feeds design, CM, and V&V each phase.
4. Installation of new or modified safety software demands technical-specification inoperability of affected and interconnected functions, CM-controlled offline-checked changes, removal of work-arounds, and licensing-basis CM/V&V for maintenance iterations; 10 CFR 50.59 governs transitions.
5. BTP 7-14 B.2 structures review information as planning documents, implementation reports, and design outputs; B.3.1 scores plans before later sections score execution and products.
6. Planning documents share five process attributes and three characteristic families; alternative plan packaging is fine if content and criteria are complete; licensee and vendor plans are read together under retained licensee QA responsibility.
7. The SMP establishes independent QA, safety, and V&V, protected assurance budgets, SDOE, and the machinery that produces and controls the other plans.
8. The SDP documents the life cycle model, task-level I/O, technical metrics, safety-minded risk management, graded tool qualification, and the standards set in use.
9. SQAP, SIntP, SInstP, SMaintP, STrngP, SOP, and SSP each carry purpose-built management, implementation, and resource criteria; maintenance is not design change, installation honors technical specifications, and the SSP puts a single safety officer over phase safety analyses.
10. Staff review concentrates on safety and quality outcomes: specific conformance claims, structural independence, effective oversight, verifiable tool chains, and life cycle discipline that survives delivery into operations and maintenance.
