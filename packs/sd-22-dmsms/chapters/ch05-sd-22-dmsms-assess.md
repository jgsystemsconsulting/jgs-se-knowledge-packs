# Chapter: Assess resolution need, timing, and level

## Core Idea

Assess answers three questions for each issue Identify surfaces: whether a resolution is warranted, when work should start, and at what level of assembly the fix should land. Section 5 of SD-22, 06-JAN-2026 gathers programmatic, availability, criticality, and logistics data, then runs health-assessment logic. Appendix L holds the health-assessment methodology. Templates and risk-grid cells are not reproduced.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Data needed for assessment

- **Programmatic.** Schedule, funding windows, configuration baseline, planned modifications, and mission context.
- **Availability.** Stock, pipeline, and supplier status at item, assembly, and unit levels.
- **Criticality.** Effect of loss on safety, mission, and supportability.
- **Logistics.** Organic and contractor repair paths, demand history, and depot posture. FMS-unique and common equipment belong in the picture when partner gear shares the item.

### Whether, when, and level

- **Whether.** A predictive hit or PDN is not automatic proof that a resolution must launch now. Stock, alternate sources, and demand may absorb the event.
- **When.** Health views project demand against inventory over time so the team can see the impact date and the funding lead time behind it.
- **Level.** Fixing at piece-part, assembly, or system level changes cost, schedule, and collateral redesign. Choose the level that matches risk and opportunity.

### Health-assessment method (Appendix L)

A health assessment projects item demand for a subsystem against inventory to estimate depletion timing, then rolls results up the hierarchy. Hardware health assessments break the picture into layers the DMT can brief. Describe the method; skip template cells and notional calculation tables.

## Key Concepts

- **Triggers into Assess.** New PDNs, forecast crossings, design-risk findings, or a change in demand for already-obsolete stock.
- **Preliminary health view at end of Identify.** Section 4 notes that a preliminary health report can brief leadership before full Assess work finishes.
- **Impact date vs funding lead time.** Knowing when stock runs out is useless if the budget cycle cannot meet that date.
- **No automatic resolution.** Assess can close an issue as watch-only when risk is tolerable.

## Mental Models

- **Triage, not panic.** Identify finds smoke; Assess decides which fires need crews.
- **Hierarchy roll-up.** Piece-part shortages matter when they starve higher assemblies the mission needs.
- **Time is a resource.** Early Assess preserves cheap Analyze options.

## Key Takeaways

- Collect programmatic, availability, criticality, and logistics data before deciding.
- Separate whether, when, and level; do not collapse them into a single binary.
- Use Appendix L method for health projections; do not copy templates.
- Include FMS-common and unique equipment in availability and criticality views.
- Feed clear Assess outputs into Analyze so optioneering starts from a decided need.
