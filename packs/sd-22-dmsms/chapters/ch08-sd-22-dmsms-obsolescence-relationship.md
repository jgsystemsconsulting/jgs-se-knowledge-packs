# Chapter: Obsolescence and its relationship to DMSMS

## Core Idea

Appendix A of SD-22, 06-JAN-2026 clarifies how obsolescence and DMSMS relate. They are often spoken together, and they share the same five-step management process, but they are not synonyms. Obsolescence is a state that depends on context. A DMSMS issue is the event where supply cannot meet demand for an item the system needs.

Most DMSMS problems grow from obsolescence, yet not every obsolete item is a DMSMS problem, and not every DMSMS problem starts as classical obsolescence.

## Frameworks Introduced

### State versus event

- **Obsolescence (state).** An item may be out of production, out of mainstream support, or functionally outdated relative to current practice. Whether that state hurts the program depends on stock, alternates, and remaining demand.
- **DMSMS issue (event).** Demand exists, and manufacturing sources or material availability cannot satisfy it on the required timeline. That event triggers Identify through Implement.

### Shared process

The guidebook runs both concerns through Prepare, Identify, Assess, Analyze, and Implement. Appendix A exists so teams do not treat every obsolete line item as an automatic resolution case, and so they do not ignore non-obsolescence shortages (for example a still-current item whose only qualified source exits).

## Key Concepts

- **Adequate stock exception.** An obsolete item with enough inventory for remaining life may need watch status, not a redesign.
- **Current-item shortage.** A living product can still create a DMSMS issue when capacity, materials, or quality escapes collapse.
- **Hardware and software.** Both domains show first-order and lower-order mechanisms introduced in ch01.
- **Relationship diagram (concept).** The source figures a notional overlap between DMSMS and obsolescence. This pack keeps the idea and skips the figure cells.

## Mental Models

- **Venn, not identity.** Overlap is large; identity is false.
- **Stock changes the label.** Yesterday's obsolete-but-safe item becomes tomorrow's DMSMS issue when demand spikes or shelf stock fails.
- **Process reuse.** Do not invent a parallel obsolescence bureaucracy. Use the same DMT and DMP spine.

## Key Takeaways

- Define obsolescence as context-dependent state; define DMSMS as supply-versus-demand failure.
- Route both through the five steps already covered in ch03 through ch07.
- Do not auto-fund a resolution solely because an item is marked obsolete.
- Do not ignore shortages on items that are still in production.
- Draw Appendix A ideas in own words; leave FY24 skill sentences behind.
