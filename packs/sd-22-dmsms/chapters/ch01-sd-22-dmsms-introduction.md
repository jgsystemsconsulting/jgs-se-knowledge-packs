# Chapter: Introduction to SD-22 DMSMS management

## Core Idea

DMSMS is the loss, or impending loss, of the manufacturers and suppliers that provide the items, materials, and software a system needs. Defense systems outlive commercial product cycles, so those losses are expected rather than rare. SD-22, 06-JAN-2026 (cover dated December 2025) frames DMSMS work as risk-based, multidisciplinary, and continuous across the life cycle. The goal is early discovery of issues and selection of cost-effective resolutions while options remain open.

This edition replaces the March 2024 version. The foreword adds FMS practice for DoD systems sold to allies and partners under security-cooperation terms. It also stresses that programming and budgeting must fund both day-to-day DMSMS operations and the resolutions themselves. A leadership-approved DMP is the usual first step for that funding case.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Five-step management process

The guidebook is built around five steps. There is no separate Strategize step in this edition.

1. **Prepare**  -  foundations, DMT, DMP, operations funding, contracts, workforce, and FMS communication paths.
2. **Identify**  -  monitoring and surveillance that turn item data into near-term and forecast issues.
3. **Assess**  -  decide whether a resolution is needed, when it should start, and at what assembly level.
4. **Analyze**  -  define viable options, cost them, and choose the preferred path (often through BCA or AoA).
5. **Implement**  -  fund, field, and close the chosen resolution, including FMS-specific follow-through.

### First-order and lower-order mechanisms

- **First-order** obsolescence comes from market, regulatory, or supplier decisions that cut off buy, license, or support paths.
- **Lower-order (derived)** obsolescence is functional loss created by the program's own changes elsewhere in the design or support chain.

Both apply to hardware and software. Electronics are the classic case, but MaSME items and software packages are in scope.

## Key Concepts

- **Scope.** Best practices for a complete DMSMS management program on DoD systems, including FMS equipment sold by DoD. The pack synthesizes those practices; it does not restate statute or policy text.
- **Why a program exists.** Unmanaged issues raise cost, slip schedule, and erode readiness and availability. Proactive work widens the window of viable resolutions.
- **Document map.** Sections 1 and 2 set context and policy. Sections 3 through 7 are the five steps. Appendices A through O hold methods and supporting detail. Appendix P is abbreviations, Appendix Q is references, Appendix R is definitions.
- **New in this edition (high level).** FMS communication and FMS notes inside Identify, Analyze, and Implement; tighter DMP integration with program capability levels (Appendix D); a new DMP checklist and example (Appendix E); later appendices renumbered after E.

## Mental Models

- **Inevitability, not anomaly.** Treat DMSMS as a permanent management load, not a one-time cleanup.
- **Time buys options.** Early notice preserves low-cost paths (for example life-of-need buys or modest redesign). Late notice collapses the set to expensive field fixes.
- **Process spine.** Every later chapter hangs off the same five steps. Do not invent a sixth overlay unless a later extract names it.

## Key Takeaways

- Pin this pack to SD-22, 06-JAN-2026 (cover dated December 2025), not the prior April 2024 working-draft pin.
- Run Prepare through Implement as a continuous cadence.
- Fund operations and resolutions; a DMP is the usual leadership hook.
- Include FMS equipment and communication when the program sells or supports partner configurations.
