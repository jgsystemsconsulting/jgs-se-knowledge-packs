# Chapter: DMSMS policy and life-cycle guidance

## Core Idea

Section 2 of SD-22, 06-JAN-2026 places DMSMS management inside DoD acquisition and sustainment policy, then walks the same concerns through development and sustainment. Policy is named so practitioners know which issuances bind them. This pack names those issuances; it does not quote their text.

DMSMS work is not optional ornamentation. High-level and detailed policy both expect a funded plan, proactive risk-based monitoring, and resolution of issues that threaten cost, schedule, performance, or readiness.

## Frameworks Introduced

### High-level and detailed policy layers

- **High-level policy** sets the expectation that programs manage DMSMS across the life cycle and keep leadership accountable for the plan and its funding.
- **Detailed policy** points into DoD instructions and manuals that govern acquisition pathways, systems engineering products, logistics assessments, and contracting hooks. Common anchors named in this edition include DoDI 5000.02 family material, DoDI 5000.85 major capability acquisition, DoDI 5000.91 product-support management, and related logistics and engineering guidance. Names come from the re-fetch; exact clause text stays out of the pack.

### Life-cycle guidance

- **Development.** Systems engineering products such as the Systems Engineering Plan and SETR events carry DMSMS checkpoints. Early design choices lock later sustainment cost.
- **Sustainment.** Organic and contractor support paths still need monitoring, health views, and funded resolutions. Product-support managers stay inside the DMT picture.
- **Pathway tailoring.** Different acquisition pathways change timing and depth, not the need for the five steps. Tailor intensity; do not drop the spine.

## Key Concepts

- **SEP and SETR hooks.** Lead systems engineers and program managers use the SEP and technical reviews to surface DMSMS risk before production decisions freeze options.
- **ILA and SR questions.** Appendix C holds review questions for independent logistics assessments and sustainment reviews. Name the appendix; do not copy the question cells.
- **SETR question set.** Appendix B holds DMSMS questions for SETRs by step. Same rule: name it, skip the cells.
- **Supportability roadmaps.** Policy language links known DMSMS issues to product and supportability roadmaps so technology insertion and obsolescence work stay synchronized.

## Mental Models

- **Policy as constraint, not chapter text.** Issuances define duty. The guidebook and this pack teach practice under that duty.
- **Same five steps, different depth.** A rapid pathway still Prepare-Identify-Assess-Analyze-Implement; it may collapse calendar and paperwork, not the logic.
- **Review forums are sensors.** SETR, ILA, and SR events are scheduled places to force DMSMS evidence into the open.

## Key Takeaways

- Build the DMP and fund it because policy expects proactive, risk-based management.
- Wire DMSMS into SEP, SETR, ILA, and SR routines rather than running a parallel private process.
- Tailor by pathway and phase; keep the five-step spine.
- Use Appendices B and C as question banks inside reviews; never paste their tables into work products from this pack.
- Name DoD issuances and FAR or DFARS hooks when relevant; do not rebuild their text here.
