# Chapter: Prepare DMSMS program infrastructure

## Core Idea

Prepare is the infrastructure step. Before monitoring tools run and resolutions are priced, the program office sets foundations, charters a DMT, writes and approves a DMP, funds operations, puts contracts in place, trains the workforce, and opens FMS communication paths when partner equipment is in play. SD-22, 06-JAN-2026 treats this as Section 3, with method detail in Appendices D, E, F, and G.

Skipping Prepare leaves Identify and later steps underpowered: no owner, no plan, no money, no contract language, and no partner channel.

## Frameworks Introduced

### DMT (DMSMS Management Team)

The PM or PSM charters the team and gives it authority. Membership spans program management, systems engineering, logistics, contracting, supply, sustainment activities, and, when needed, an FMS representative. Roles cover issue intake, forecasting support, BCA packaging, budget forecasts, and presentation of options. Training matches those roles.

### DMP (DMSMS Management Plan)

The DMP states purpose, scope, approach, team structure, operations, record keeping, and how the plan ties to program capability levels. This edition tightens that link to Appendix D levels and points to Appendix E for a checklist and example. Describe the checklist; do not copy its cells or the worked example.

### Capability levels (Appendix D)

Four levels mark progressively more proactive and strategic DMSMS management. Programs use them to self-locate, set improvement targets, and align DMP content. FMS-related capability processes appear in this edition's Appendix D. Level matrices stay out of the pack.

### Contracting and workforce (Appendices F and G)

- **Contracting.** DMSMS work may be organic, contractor-supported, or mixed. Contract language, CDRL patterns, and data rights affect BOM access and resolution speed. Name the practice; do not paste clause text.
- **Workforce competencies.** Appendix G covers how to build DMSMS skills across the DMT. Method prose only.

## Key Concepts

- **Foundations.** Leadership commitment, clear ownership, and a funded operations line come before tool shopping.
- **Operations funding.** Programming and budgeting for day-to-day DMSMS work is separate from resolution funding (see ch07 and Appendices H and N).
- **FMS communication (3.5).** LOA drafters, foreign partners, and other DoD program offices need defined channels so partner configurations are not surprises during Identify and later steps.
- **Review-question appendices.** Appendices B and C support SETR, ILA, and SR forums that Prepare must be ready to feed.

## Mental Models

- **Charter before cadence.** A team without a charter becomes a meeting without decisions.
- **Plan as funding artifact.** The approved DMP is often the document finance and leadership recognize when defending operations dollars.
- **Partner conduit.** The FMS seat is a two-way pipe: DoD issues that hit partner gear, and partner demand that changes life-of-need math.

## Key Takeaways

- Charter the DMT with named roles, including FMS when sales or partner support apply.
- Approve a DMP that matches capability-level ambition and operations reality.
- Fund Prepare operations; do not wait for the first crisis invoice.
- Use Appendix E as a checklist reference, not a cell dump.
- Put contract and data-access hooks in place before Identify depends on them.
