# SD-22 DMSMS Patterns (06-JAN-2026)

Reusable practice patterns for SD-22, 06-JAN-2026 (cover dated December 2025). Each entry: When / How / Trade-offs. Synthesized notes. No worksheet or matrix cells.

## Pattern 1: Run the five steps as cadence

**When.** Any long-lived system whose commercial suppliers turn over faster than the mission life.

**How.** Cycle Prepare, Identify, Assess, Analyze, Implement. Feed outcomes back into the DMP and roadmaps. Do not add a Strategize overlay; this edition does not name that step.

**Trade-offs.** Cadence costs operations funding. One-pass cleanup looks cheaper until the next PDN wave.

## Pattern 2: Charter DMT before buying tools

**When.** Standing up or resetting a DMSMS program.

**How.** PM or PSM issues a charter with roles (engineering, logistics, contracting, supply, FMS seat when needed). Approve the DMP. Then select monitoring tools.

**Trade-offs.** Tool-first programs collect noise with no decision owner. Charter-first programs move slower in week one and faster thereafter.

## Pattern 3: Risk-based watch, not universal surveillance

**When.** BOM is large and monitoring dollars are scarce.

**How.** Prioritize by criticality, supply vulnerability, and time-to-resolve. Keep a reactive path for the long tail. Clean item data before scoring.

**Trade-offs.** Aggressive triage saves money and can miss a quiet critical material. Broad watch finds more issues and burns staff.

## Pattern 4: Assess three questions separately

**When.** A PDN, forecast crossing, or design-risk flag arrives.

**How.** Decide whether a resolution is warranted, when funding must start, and at what assembly level. Use health-assessment method (Appendix L) for timing; skip template cells.

**Trade-offs.** Collapsing to "fix it now at piece part" wastes redesign opportunities. Endless study past the impact date wastes stock.

## Pattern 5: Screen security inside Analyze

**When.** Substitutes, foreign sources, or software loads raise assurance concerns.

**How.** Keep system security engineering as a viability screen inside Analyze (section 6.3.3). Do not move the topic to a different capability cluster or a separate chapter.

**Trade-offs.** Early security vetoes can kill a cheap option. Late security discovery kills schedule after money is spent.

## Pattern 6: Split operations money from resolution money

**When.** Building the annual budget or defending a mark.

**How.** Fund DMT/DMP operations on one line (Appendix H method). Fund chosen resolutions on another, timed to Assess impact dates and modification windows (Appendix N method). Keep record-keeping good enough to defend both (Appendix I).

**Trade-offs.** Combined pots hide underfunding until a spike. Split pots need discipline so operations is not raided to pay one fix.

## Pattern 7: Align fixes with roadmaps and mods

**When.** A preferred resolution needs hardware or software change.

**How.** Use product and technology roadmaps (Appendix K) and planned modifications as the insert window. Avoid opening the system twice.

**Trade-offs.** Waiting for the next mod can miss a readiness cliff. Opening early can desynchronize configuration baselines.

## Pattern 8: Treat FMS as a first-class channel

**When.** DoD sells or supports partner configurations of the same system family.

**How.** Put FMS communication in Prepare. Verify partner hit during Identify. Check shared versus split options in Analyze. Close partner cases in Implement.

**Trade-offs.** Shared buys improve life-of-need math and add coordination cost. Ignoring partners creates surprise demand and political friction.

## Pattern 9: State is not event

**When.** A parts list shows many obsolete lines.

**How.** Apply Appendix A logic: obsolete-with-stock may be watch-only; current-with-failed-source is still DMSMS. Route both through the same five steps.

**Trade-offs.** Treating every obsolete line as a project floods Analyze. Treating none as events under-reacts to real shortages.
