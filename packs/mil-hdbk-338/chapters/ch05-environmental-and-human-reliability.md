# Chapter 5: Environmental Design and Human Performance Reliability

## Core Idea
Sections 7.6–7.7 address environment-driven stresses and human-in-the-loop reliability. Electronic reliability collapses when thermal, vibration, humidity, and other environments are afterthoughts; operator/maintainer error is a first-class contributor the handbook expects designers to confront.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Environmental design**: Designing for life-cycle environments that actually stress the product.
- **Human performance reliability**: Treating human tasks, interfaces, and error modes as part of system reliability.

## Key Concepts
- Life-cycle environments drive thermal, vibration, humidity, and related stresses.
- Physics-of-failure thinking starts from real envelope stress drivers.
- Human tasks and interfaces belong in R and M analysis when operators matter.
- Lab profiles that ignore field environments produce false confidence.
- Environmental design pairs with ESS and growth testing later.

## Mental Models
- Environment is a reliability specification input, not only a qualification hurdle.
- Physics-of-failure thinking starts from real envelope stress drivers.
- Human error rates respond to procedures, displays, and time pressure design.

## Anti-patterns
- **Lab climate only**: Qualifying to benign profiles unlike the field.
- **Blaming operators**: Ignoring interface design after incidents.
- **Thermal last**: Packaging electronics then discovering cooling shortfalls.

## Key Takeaways
1. Capture life-cycle environments early and design to them.
2. Connect environmental stresses to derating and prediction assumptions.
3. Include human tasks in R&M analysis where operators matter.
4. Pair environmental design with ESS/growth thinking (ch08).
5. Document residual environmental risks the design cannot remove.
