# Glossary: jsp-936

Dependable AI terms the directive defines or characterises, alphabetical; each entry names the chapters that carry detail. Definitions are reconstructed reference notes, not verbatim source text.

- **2PUS (Second Permanent Under Secretary)**: senior departmental official who receives the annual Statements of AI Ethical Assurance from TLB Executive Boards and the referral of Critical-rated AI ethical risk. → ch03
- **AI (Artificial Intelligence)**: characterised broadly as machines that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, especially where the machines learn from data how to do those tasks; no single technical definition is fixed. → ch01, ch02
- **AS (Autonomous System)**: system that acts on high-level human goal-setting, chooses its own course of action without depending on human oversight and control, and may behave unpredictably in individual actions even when its overall activity is predictable; contrasted with an automated system, which only performs pre-programmed actions. → ch02
- **ASR (Ambitious, Safe, Responsible)**: the MOD policy statement behind the Defence AI Strategy that this directive implements; source of the five AI Ethical Principles. → ch01, ch03, ch07
- **C2 (Command and Control)**: application area named among in-scope examples, including reinforcement learning used in C2 and autonomous systems. → ch01, ch02
- **DAIC (Defence AI Centre)**: body that coheres training requirements for AI-specific roles and provides technical guidance and direction. → ch01, ch03
- **DAU (Defence AI and Autonomy Unit)**: body that manages this JSP, sets AI ethics policy, receives referrals of special-attention and high-ethical-risk AI, and is the consultation point for Dependable AI policy questions. → ch01, ch03
- **Def Stan (Defence Standard)**: MOD standard; the directive names Def Stan 00-055 (software safety of Programmable Elements) and Def Stan 00-056 (system safety management) among others, named and not quoted here. → ch01, ch06
- **Dependability**: how much the user can rely on the safe, secure and correct operation of a system within a given context of mission, human involvement, environment and system constraints; confidence must be commensurate with reliance. → ch01
- **DevOps (Development and Operations)**: software philosophy of continual, highly automated release; the common backdrop for AI lifecycles and source of technical-debt risk. → ch05
- **DLOD (Defence Line of Development)**: development line whose owners are named among the stakeholders the directive especially addresses. → ch01
- **End User**: all-encompassing term for every user of a capability regardless of service, rank or role, including operators, maintainers, trainers and support personnel. → ch01, ch04
- **GALE (Globally At Least Equivalent)**: performance-demonstration approach where AI replacing an existing approach shows performance at least equivalent to what it replaced. → ch03
- **HCD (Human Centred Design)**: design approach that identifies user needs, involves users in design and testing, and applies human factors best practice across the system lifecycle; must be applied to human/AI teaming. → ch04
- **HFI (Human Factors Integration)**: process mandated by JSP 912 for military capability development and acquisition; its current text does not specifically address AI. → ch04
- **HLR (High-Level Requirements)**: software requirements stating what the software must do to achieve the apportioned system requirement; where AI implements an HLR without decomposable structure, the training recipe must be demonstrated to meet the HLR's intent. → ch05
- **IHL (International Humanitarian Law)**: the law of armed conflict, part of the national and international legal frame governing MOD's AI development and use. → ch03
- **LLM (Large Language Model)**: generative AI technique named in scope examples (HR applications) and in the digital-systems warning about credibly incorrect outputs. → ch01, ch02
- **LLR (Low-Level Requirements)**: software requirements stating how the behaviour is achieved; with ML, bidirectional traceability is often lost at this stage. → ch05
- **Meaningful human control**: the grounding concept of MOD AI policy, exercised through context-appropriate human involvement across the AI lifecycle. → ch02, ch03, ch04
- **ML (Machine Learning)**: subset of AI so prevalent that AI is often called AI/ML; the primary software safety standard, Def Stan 00-055, currently addresses ML only. → ch01, ch05
- **MLOps (Machine Learning Operations)**: ML variant of DevOps in which data trains algorithms into models, models are verified, packaged and deployed, then monitored with performance feeding the next cycle. → ch05
- **MOD (Ministry of Defence)**: publisher of the directive and the organisation whose staff and capability development it binds. → ch01, ch03
- **ODD (Operational Design Domain)**: the declared context of use, conditions and host systems an AI is designed for; anchor for scope examples, data collection, testing, monitoring and assurance. → ch02, ch03, ch05, ch06, ch07, ch08
- **PE (Programmable Elements)**: category defined by Def Stan 00-056 and used to draw the JSP's software scope. → ch01, ch06
- **RAS (Robotic and Autonomous Systems)**: one of the two host-system classes, the physical platforms that typically move to achieve a desired outcome. → ch02
- **RAISO (Responsible AI Senior Officer)**: AI ethics role each TLB must appoint; ensures assurance across accountable AI projects, sets culture, manages ethics-effectiveness trade-offs, and clears internationally shared or acquired AI against ASR policy. → ch01, ch03
- **Resilience**: quality of an AI to manage and recover from failure; named alongside maintainability as related to but beyond the ASR reliability principle. → ch03
- **Robustness**: ability of AI to handle inputs outside its intended design and respond appropriately. → ch03
- **SRO (Senior Responsible Owner)**: accountable owner of a project or programme; SROs of medium- and high-risk AI projects must inform their RAISO. → ch01, ch03
- **Statement of AI Ethical Assurance**: annual statement from each TLB Executive Board to the 2PUS, underpinned by auditable evidence, also required when non-UK-developed AI or data is brought into UK use. → ch03
- **Technical debt**: gradual reduction in design quality caused by speed-driven development choices; grows more expensive to fix the longer it accrues. → ch05
- **Test Data**: data used by the development team to judge when a model is trained sufficiently to achieve intended performance; must be separate from and independently maintained against training data. → ch05
- **Training Data**: data applied to ML algorithms to produce a model; one of three separately produced and maintained data classes. → ch05
- **Verification Data**: data used by a separate team to independently verify model performance; should be collected separately and cover the breadth of the ODD including edge cases. → ch05
