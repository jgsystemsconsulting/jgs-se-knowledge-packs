# Chapter 5: FMVSS Brakes, Stability, and Lighting Selection

> Source: 49 CFR Part 571 sections 571.102, 571.103, 571.105, 571.108, 571.126, 571.127, and 571.135 at eCFR versioner date 2025-01-01 (XML section files).

## Core Idea

This selection covers transmission shift interlocks and low-speed transmission braking effect, windshield defrost/defog, hydraulic and electric brake performance for heavier light-duty classes, lighting and reflectors, electronic stability control for light vehicles, automatic emergency braking for light vehicles, and light-vehicle brake systems. Again these are **vehicle-level minimum performance standards**, not process standards.

**Heavy-vehicle brake and stability coverage is out of this selection.** In particular, 571.121 (air brake systems) and 571.136 (electronic stability control systems for heavy vehicles) are **not** included. Do not infer heavy-vehicle compliance content from the light-vehicle standards below.

Several standards in this chapter still read as if a human driver is present to shift, defrost, or apply service brakes. Federal Register modernization dockets have been reopening driver-present assumptions for automation-era vehicles. Treat **571.102, 571.103, and 571.135** as **current-as-of the 2025-01-01 pin**, not as settled forever against ADS designs that remove or redefine the driver.

Incorporated ASTM and SAE test methods named in these sections are cited by name only in this pack.

## 571.102: Standard No. 102; Transmission shift position sequence, starter interlock, and transmission braking effect

**Official short title:** Transmission shift position sequence, starter interlock, and transmission braking effect.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses.

**Performance area:** Controls shift-position sequence (including neutral placement relative to drive and reverse on passenger-car layouts), a starter interlock that blocks starter engagement in drive positions, and a low-speed transmission braking effect for automatic transmissions. The safety aim is fewer shift errors, fewer unintended starter engagements, and supplemental braking below the section's speed threshold.

**Driver-present note:** The standard's controls and interlocks are written around a driver operating a shift control and starter. Modernization work may revisit those assumptions for vehicles without conventional controls. Current-as-of 2025-01-01 only.

## 571.103: Standard No. 103; Windshield defrosting and defogging systems

**Official short title:** Windshield defrosting and defogging systems.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses.

**Performance area:** Windshield defrost and defog performance so the driver can maintain outward visibility in cold and moisture conditions under the section's test approach. Incorporated historical SAE defrost practices are named in the IBR inventory for this section.

**Driver-present note:** Visibility performance is framed for a human driver's windshield view. ADS-only glazing or sensor-cleaning regimes are not what this standard was written to govern. Current-as-of 2025-01-01 only.

## 571.105: Standard No. 105; Hydraulic and electric brake systems

**Official short title:** Hydraulic and electric brake systems.

**Applies to:** Multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses **above** the GVWR threshold stated in the section that use hydraulic or electric brake systems (not the lightest passenger-car population covered by 571.135).

**Performance area:** Service brake and parking brake performance under normal and emergency conditions for those heavier multipurpose/truck/bus classes, including antilock and brake-system integrity concepts defined in the section. It is the hydraulic/electric brake companion for vehicles above the light-brake cutover, not a heavy air-brake standard (see out-of-scope note on 571.121).

## 571.108: Standard No. 108; Lamps, reflective devices, and associated equipment

**Official short title:** Lamps, reflective devices, and associated equipment.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, buses, trailers (with stated trailer exclusions), and motorcycles; also certain replacement lamps, retroreflective sheeting, and reflex reflectors manufactured to the section's provisions.

**Performance area:** Original and replacement lighting and reflex equipment: roadway illumination, signal meaning, and conspicuity in daylight and darkness. Photometric, color, durability, and mounting performance dominate this large standard. Many ASTM and SAE lighting practices appear in its IBR list by name only.

## 571.126: Standard No. 126; Electronic stability control systems for light vehicles

**Official short title:** Electronic stability control systems for light vehicles.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses at or below the light-vehicle GVWR limit stated in the section, subject to the section's phase-in history.

**Performance area:** Equipment and performance requirements for electronic stability control (ESC) that reduce loss-of-control crashes, including those that lead to rollover. It is explicitly a **light vehicle** ESC standard. Heavy-vehicle ESC (571.136) is outside this pack.

## 571.127: Standard No. 127; Automatic emergency braking systems for light vehicles

**Official short title:** Automatic emergency braking systems for light vehicles.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars; multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses at or below the light-vehicle GVWR limit stated in the section.

**Performance area:** **Automatic emergency braking (AEB)** for light vehicles. The standard sets performance expectations so the vehicle can brake when the driver does not apply the brakes or does not apply enough braking to avoid or mitigate a crash. Lead-vehicle and pedestrian AEB scenarios and related warning concepts sit in this standard's structure. Name it clearly in program traces: **571.127 is the FMVSS AEB standard for light vehicles** in this selection. (Amendments published after the pin may exist in later eCFR issues; this pack stays on 2025-01-01 text.)

## 571.135: Standard No. 135; Light vehicle brake systems

**Official short title:** Light vehicle brake systems.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars manufactured on or after the section's start-of-applicability date, and multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses at or below the light GVWR threshold manufactured on or after their start-of-applicability date. In addition, at the manufacturer's option, passenger cars and light multipurpose/truck/bus vehicles built before those dates may meet this standard (135) instead of Standard No. 105.

**Performance area:** Service and parking brake performance for light vehicles under normal and emergency driving conditions, including adhesion utilization, fade, partial-system failure, and related brake-system checks defined in the section. This is the primary light-vehicle brake performance standard complementary to 105 for heavier hydraulic/electric classes and to 126/127 for stability and AEB.

**Driver-present note:** Test procedures and failure modes assume conventional service-brake application by a driver. Automation programs should track modernization dockets rather than treat 2025-01-01 text as ADS-final. Current-as-of 2025-01-01 only.

## Out of Selection (explicit)

| Not in this pack | Why it matters |

If a program needs heavy chassis brake or ESC compliance, look outside this pack's chapter set.

## Key Takeaways

1. Seven standards cover **shift/interlock (102), defrost (103), heavier hydraulic/electric brakes (105), lighting (108), light ESC (126), light AEB (127), and light brakes (135)**.
2. **571.127 is the automatic emergency braking standard** for light vehicles in this selection.
3. **571.102, 571.103, and 571.135** carry **driver-present assumptions**; treat them as current-as-of **2025-01-01**, with modernization dockets in motion.
4. **No heavy-vehicle claim:** 571.121 and 571.136 are out of selection.
5. These remain **minimum performance** standards, not process standards.
