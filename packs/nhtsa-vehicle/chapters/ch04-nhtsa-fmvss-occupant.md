# Chapter 4: FMVSS Occupant Protection Selection

> Source: 49 CFR Part 571 sections 571.201, 571.208, 571.209, 571.210, 571.214, and 571.226 at eCFR versioner date 2025-01-01 (XML section files).

## Core Idea

These six standards are **vehicle-level minimum performance standards** for protecting occupants in interior impacts, crashes, belt restraint, side impacts, and ejection events. They are not a systems-engineering process standard, not a software assurance standard, and not a substitute for hazard analysis methods. They state what the vehicle (or, for 571.209, the belt assembly as equipment) must achieve under defined applications and tests.

This chapter is a **selection**, not the entire 201–226 occupant band of Part 571. Neighboring standards outside the six ids below are out of scope even when a program might need them.

Incorporated industry test methods and drawings named inside these sections (for example SAE J-series practices, ASTM methods, MIL dummy specifications, or NHTSA drawing packages) are identified by name only in this pack and are never quoted.

## 571.201: Standard No. 201; Occupant protection in interior impact

**Official short title:** Occupant protection in interior impact.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars; multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks designed to carry at least one person, and buses at or below the light-vehicle GVWR threshold stated in the section. A subset of interior upper-interior requirements is further limited for certain heavier buses.

**Performance area:** Limits injury risk when occupants strike interior structures (instrument panels, seat backs, interior compartment doors, sun visors, armrests, and upper interior components such as pillars, roof side rails, and headers). The standard is about interior impact protection geometry and energy management, not about belt or air-bag crash tests (those live primarily in 571.208).

## 571.208: Standard No. 208; Occupant crash protection

**Official short title:** Occupant crash protection.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars; multipurpose passenger vehicles; trucks designed to carry at least one person; and buses. Separate pressure-vessel and explosive-device rules reach components used in restraint systems for those vehicle types.

**Performance area:** The center of US federal crashworthiness restraint performance. It pairs anthropomorphic dummy injury measures in specified crash test modes with equipment expectations for active and passive restraints (seat belts, air bags, and related suppression or readiness indicators as the section structures them). Child-restraint interaction and air-bag-related appendices sit with this standard. Read it as the crash-protection performance umbrella for occupants, complementary to belt hardware (209), belt anchorages (210), side impact (214), and ejection mitigation (226).

## 571.209: Standard No. 209; Seat belt assemblies

**Official short title:** Seat belt assemblies.

**Applies to:** Seat belt assemblies for use in passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses (equipment standard oriented to the assembly, not only to a finished vehicle line).

**Performance area:** Hardware and webbing performance for belt assemblies: strength, abrasion and environmental resistance, buckle and adjustment hardware behavior, retractor types, and related assembly-level tests. It answers whether the belt assembly itself is fit for restraint duty before and alongside vehicle-level anchorage and crash standards.

## 571.210: Standard No. 210; Seat belt assembly anchorages

**Official short title:** Seat belt assembly anchorages.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, buses, and school buses.

**Performance area:** Location and strength of the vehicle structure points that attach seat belt assemblies. Proper anchorage location supports effective occupant restraint geometry; strength requirements reduce anchorage failure under load. This is the vehicle-structure companion to the 209 assembly standard and the 208 crash-protection regime.

## 571.214: Standard No. 214; Side impact protection

**Official short title:** Side impact protection.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars; multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks designed to carry at least one person, and buses at or below the stated light-vehicle GVWR limit, with stated exclusions (including walk-in vans and other called-out cases).

**Performance area:** Occupant protection in side impacts. The section combines side-door strength expectations with dummy-based crash performance limits (forces, deflections, accelerations) and related means aimed at serious and fatal side-impact injury reduction. It is the side-crash counterpart to the frontal-oriented core of 208.

## 571.226: Standard No. 226; Ejection Mitigation

**Official short title:** Ejection Mitigation.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars; multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks designed to carry at least one person, and buses at or below the stated GVWR limit. Exclusions include walk-in vans, modified-roof vehicles, convertibles, vehicles designed to operate without doors, and certain multi-stage law-enforcement, correctional, taxi, and limousine vehicles with fixed security partitions produced by more than one manufacturer, as the section defines those carve-outs.

**Performance area:** Systems that reduce complete and partial ejection of occupants through side windows in rollovers and side-impact events. Performance is demonstrated with a prescribed headform impact approach against side-window openings. It complements 214 (side impact) and 208 (occupant crash protection) by targeting ejection paths rather than only in-cabin injury metrics.

## How to Use This Selection

- Treat each standard as a **compliance performance gate** for the vehicle or equipment class it names, under the pinned 2025-01-01 text.
- Do not read the six together as a complete occupant-protection engineering manual; analysis methods, design processes, and excluded neighboring FMVSS remain elsewhere.
- When ADS or novel seating concepts change occupancy assumptions, ADS 2.0's crashworthiness element (Chapter 3) still points back to this kind of performance thinking rather than waiving it.

## Key Takeaways

1. Six selected standards cover **interior impact (201), crash protection (208), belt assemblies (209), belt anchorages (210), side impact (214), and ejection mitigation (226)**.
2. All six are **minimum performance** standards (plus an equipment standard for belt assemblies), not process or software standards.
3. Application is mostly **light passenger vehicles and specified truck/bus classes**; each section carries its own GVWR limits and exclusions.
4. The selection is **not** the full 201–226 band of Part 571.
5. Incorporated SAE/ASTM/MIL/NHTSA test references are **named only**, never quoted, in this pack.
