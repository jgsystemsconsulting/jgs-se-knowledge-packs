# Chapter 6: FMVSS Fuel System, Electric Vehicle, and Minimum Sound Selection

> Source: 49 CFR Part 571 sections 571.301, 571.305, and 571.141 at eCFR versioner date 2025-01-01 (XML section files). Task 1 confirmed 571.141 present in the pinned snapshot.

## Core Idea

This chapter closes the FMVSS selection with post-crash and normal-operation hazards tied to stored energy: liquid fuel system integrity, electric propulsion electrolyte and shock protection, and minimum sound so hybrid and electric vehicles remain detectable to pedestrians. As with Chapters 4 and 5, these are **vehicle-level minimum performance standards**, not process standards and not the whole of Part 571's energy or miscellaneous bands.

Section **571.141 is included**. Minimum sound requirements for hybrid and electric vehicles are in this selection because the section is present in the eCFR snapshot pinned at 2025-01-01.

Incorporated references (for example ASTM friction methods in 301, or ISO 10844 and SAE J2889-1 in 141) are named only, never quoted.

## 571.301: Standard No. 301; Fuel system integrity

**Official short title:** Fuel system integrity.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars; multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses at or below the stated GVWR limit that use fuel with a boiling point above the section's threshold; and school buses above that GVWR limit that use such fuel.

**Performance area:** Integrity of the motor vehicle fuel system so that fuel spillage during and after crashes is limited, reducing fire deaths and injuries, and so that siphoning-related ingestion risks are addressed as the section frames them. Crash modes and spillage measurement definitions live in the standard; this note records only the governing performance theme: keep flammable liquid fuel where it belongs when the vehicle is struck or rolled, and limit post-impact leakage windows the section defines.

## 571.305: Standard No. 305; Electric-powered vehicles: electrolyte spillage and electrical shock protection

**Official short title:** Electric-powered vehicles: electrolyte spillage and electrical shock protection.

**Applies to:** Passenger cars and multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses at or below the stated GVWR limit that use electrical propulsion components above the section's DC/AC working-voltage thresholds and that can exceed the section's speed capability over a fixed distance on level pavement.

**Performance area:** For electric-powered vehicles, limit electrolyte spillage and keep electric energy storage/conversion devices retained during and after crash events; protect people from harmful electric shock during and after crashes and during normal operation. The safety story is the EV counterpart to liquid-fuel fire risk in 301: manage high-voltage and electrolyte hazards so crash survivors and responders are not exposed to shock or chemical spill harms, and so normal operation does not create shock or driver-error shock paths the standard targets. (Later eCFR issues may show amendments after the pin; this pack stays on 2025-01-01 text.)

## 571.141: Standard No. 141; Minimum Sound Requirements for Hybrid and Electric Vehicles

**Official short title:** Minimum Sound Requirements for Hybrid and Electric Vehicles.

**Applies to:** Electric and hybrid vehicles that are passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, or buses at or below the stated GVWR limit; and electric and hybrid low-speed vehicles, as the section lists those classes.

**Performance area:** Pedestrian alert sound performance for hybrid and electric vehicles. Quiet propulsion can remove cues pedestrians use to detect approaching vehicles; this standard sets minimum sound level and character so those vehicles remain detectable and recognizable. It is a detectability standard for vulnerable road users, not a propulsion or crash-structure standard. Industry acoustic references appear in its IBR list by name only (including ISO 10844 and SAE J2889-1).

## How the Three Fit Together

| Section | Energy / cue problem | Typical question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 571.301 | Liquid fuel leaves the vehicle in a crash or via siphoning | Does the fuel system keep spillage inside allowed bounds? |
| 571.305 | High-voltage and electrolyte hazards in EV/hybrid propulsion | Are shock and electrolyte spill risks controlled in crash and normal use? |
| 571.141 | Low exterior acoustic signature of EV/hybrid vehicles | Can pedestrians detect the vehicle by sound as required? |

Programs that field both liquid-fuel and electric variants need the matching standard for each propulsion type; dual-energy designs may touch more than one row. ADS or low-speed automated shuttles that are hybrid/electric still sit under 141 when they meet the application classes; silence is not a compliance strategy under this selection.

## Key Takeaways

1. **571.301** governs **fuel system integrity** (spillage/fire and related fuel hazards) for specified vehicle classes using qualifying liquid fuel.
2. **571.305** governs **electrolyte spillage, energy-device retention, and electric shock protection** for in-scope electric-powered vehicles.
3. **571.141 is present** at the 2025-01-01 pin and is **in this selection**: minimum sound for hybrid and electric vehicles so pedestrians can detect them.
4. All three are **minimum performance** standards, not process standards.
5. Third-party acoustic and test methods incorporated by reference are **named only** in this pack.
