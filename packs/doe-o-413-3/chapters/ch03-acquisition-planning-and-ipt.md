# Chapter 3: Acquisition Planning, Strategy, and Integrated Project Teams

## Core Idea
Before and between Critical Decisions, projects must plan how the capital asset will be acquired and managed: acquisition strategy, project execution planning, integrated project team (IPT) formation, staffing, front-end planning quality, and alignment of contract strategy with project controls. Weak front-end planning is a leading indicator of baseline failure.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Acquisition strategy**: Approach for contracting and delivery consistent with project risk and CD timing.
- **Project Execution Plan (PEP)**: Living management plan for how the project will be executed and controlled.
- **Integrated Project Team (IPT)**: Cross-functional federal/contractor team accountable for project success.
- **Front-end planning / definition quality**: Practices (including PDRI-style thinking in companion guides) that mature scope before baseline.
- **Staffing for project management**: Adequate federal project director and support capacity.
- **Change-aware planning**: Anticipating how scope/contract changes will be governed later.

## Key Concepts
- **Federal Project Director (FPD)**: Key federal leadership role for covered projects.
- **Contract–project alignment**: EVMS, change control, and deliverables must match contract structure.
- **Early risk identification**: Planning is where technical, acquisition, and organizational risks surface.
- **NEPA and other enabling processes**: Environmental and regulatory paths are planning-critical, not afterthoughts.
- **Tailored documentation**: Documents may be combined when appropriate but must still satisfy intent.
- **Companion guides**: DOE G 413.3-x series elaborates IPT, acquisition strategy, PEPs, etc., without replacing Order shalls.

## Mental Models
- Strategy chooses *how* value is bought; the PEP says *how* work is run day to day.
- IPT is the human architecture of the project system.
- Front-end definition quality is cheaper than baseline thrash.
- Planning products are CD ammunition.

## Anti-patterns
- **Acquisition strategy as boilerplate**: Not risk-based, not alternative-aware.
- **IPT in name only**: No decision rights, no cadence, no shared risk picture.
- **Understaffed FPD office on a high-TPC project**: Governance without capacity.
- **Planning only to “pass CD”**: Documents that die after approval.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build a risk-informed acquisition strategy tied to CD sequence.
2. Maintain a PEP that is used, not shelfware.
3. Stand up a real IPT with clear roles and interfaces.
4. Invest in front-end planning quality before CD-2.
5. Align contract structure with project controls expectations.
6. Use companion guides for depth; the Order for mandatory direction.
