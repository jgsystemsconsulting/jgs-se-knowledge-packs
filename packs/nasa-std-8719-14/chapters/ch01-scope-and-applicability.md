# Chapter 1: Scope, Applicability, and the NPR 8715.6 Frame

## Core Idea
NASA-STD-8719.14C is the technical companion to NPR 8715.6. The NPR requires a formal orbital-debris assessment for NASA and NASA-sponsored objects; this standard supplies the shalls, assessment methods, and report formats (ODAR / EOMP) that make that assessment auditable. The primary goal is to limit *new* orbital debris and the risk it poses to other missions, people in orbit, and people on the ground.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **NPR + standard pair**: NPR 8715.6 sets *when* assessments and reports are required; 8719.14C sets *how* compliance is shown.
- **Four purpose threads**: (1) limit debris generation, (2) assess collision risk with existing debris, (3) assess Earth-surface impact potential, (4) assess and limit End of Mission (EOM) risk.
- **Applicability filter**: objects that exceed about 130 km altitude *and* reach Earth orbital velocity. NASA-sponsored means developed or operated by NASA, under NASA contract, or under agreement with NASA.
- **Verb discipline**: shall = mandatory; should = good practice; may = permission; can = capability; will = expected outcome.

## Key Concepts
- **Earth-orbit primary**: the document is written for debris in Earth orbit. Sections 4.3–4.8 each state how (or whether) the same ideas apply beyond Earth orbit (Moon, Mars, Lagrange points).
- **Handbook is reference, not this pack**: NASA-HDBK-8719.14 explains environment, measurements, modeling, shielding, mitigation, and reentry. It is encouraged alongside the NPR and this standard; it is not ingested here.
- **Grandfathering of design, not of operations**: missions under contract or past PDR before this edition may keep that edition’s *design* requirements. In-flight decisions that change planned passivation or disposal (mission extension, profile change) are judged against the *current* edition.
- **Relief path**: NPR 8715.3 is the process for requesting relief from this standard’s requirements.
- **Who it binds**: NASA HQ and Centers directly. JPL, other contractors, grantees, and agreement parties only to the extent the contract or agreement says so. NASA is not treated as sponsor of DoD- or foreign-furnished launch vehicles; FAA-licensed launches sit under DOT orbital-debris oversight.
- **Consistency, not licensed inserts**: National Space Policy, SPD-3, 2019 ODMSP, IADC guidelines, and UN COPUOS guidelines are cited so implementing this standard and NPR 8715.6 should also satisfy those objectives.

## Mental Models
- The standard is an *assessment process*, not a catalog of every debris object.
- If it will orbit (130 km + orbital velocity) and NASA owns or sponsors it, start here.
- Design freeze does not freeze EOM judgment — operations still owe the current edition.
- Handbook depth is how practitioners understand *why*; this standard is how programs *show* compliance.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating the handbook as the shalls**: HDBK-8719.14 does not replace 8719.14C requirements.
- **Skipping assessment because “we’re a payload on someone else’s stage”**: ISS/exploration exemptions are narrow (encapsulated or returned cargo). Released or jettisoned items are in.
- **Using an old edition for an extended mission’s disposal decision**: grandfathering is for the design that was already under contract/PDR, not for later EOM choices.
- **Copying IADC/UN text as if this pack licenses those documents**: they are consistency citations only.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pair NPR 8715.6 (cadence and policy) with this standard (technical shalls and methods).
2. Apply to NASA/NASA-sponsored objects above ~130 km that reach orbital velocity.
3. Four threads: generation, collision, Earth impact, EOM.
4. Beyond-Earth destinations get a tailored applicability statement at the start of each §4.x.
5. Request relief via NPR 8715.3; do not silently tailor shalls.
6. Do not ingest the handbook or third-party guideline texts as pack content.
