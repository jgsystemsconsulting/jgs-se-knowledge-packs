# Chapter 1: CPG Purpose, Audience, and 2.0 Changes

## Core Idea
CISA’s Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) are a prioritized, outcome-driven baseline of IT and OT cybersecurity practices for critical infrastructure. Version 2.0 refreshes the set to align with NIST CSF 2.0 (including GOVERN), consolidate overlapping goals, and incorporate operational feedback since the 2022/2023 releases.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **CPG baseline model**: Streamlined protections answering “what reduces the most common, impactful risk first?”
- **CSF Function grouping**: Goals organized under GOVERN, IDENTIFY, PROTECT, DETECT, RESPOND, RECOVER.
- **Outcome + recommended action format**: Each goal states a desired outcome and practical actions, usable by technical and non-technical leaders.

## Key Concepts
- **Problem CPGs solve**: Organizations struggle to pick highest-yield practices, prioritize scarce resources, and explain cyber investment to executives/boards.
- **Scope**: Foundational practices for IT and OT across critical infrastructure sectors — not a complete security program for every threat.
- **Design traits**: Threat-informed, outcome-oriented language, investment/benchmarking baseline, approachable to executives as well as practitioners.
- **Governance addition (2.0)**: New GOVERN function elevates leadership accountability, oversight, and risk management as everyday cybersecurity, mirroring CSF 2.0.
- **Structural renumbering**: Prior goals regrouped/renumbered into six Functions; older OT-only goals folded into universal goals covering blended IT/IoT/OT environments.
- **Consolidations**: Related objectives merged for brevity (example pattern: former pairs combined into single goals such as 1.D and 3.Q).
- **Adoption gap**: Larger utilities/agencies adopted earlier; smaller orgs still struggle to turn high-level goals into action — 2.0 aims at that gap.
- **NSM-5 lineage**: Baseline CPGs originated under National Security Memorandum-5 direction for consistent cross-sector control-system cybersecurity goals.
- **Not exhaustive**: CPGs are a prioritized subset of CSF-aligned practice, not every control needed for national security or every organization.

## Mental Models
- CPGs are the “first mile” of CSF — prioritize here before boiling the ocean of full frameworks.
- One goal set for IT+OT reflects modern converged environments; do not re-silo unless risk analysis demands it.
- Use goals to brief boards: outcome language maps cleanly to risk reduction narratives.
- Track versioning: 2.0 renumbering breaks naive 1.x checklist mappings — remap deliberately.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating CPGs as a full CSF or 800-53 substitute**: They are intentionally incomplete.
- **IT-only implementation in OT-rich environments**: Undermines the cross-technology intent of 2.0 consolidations.
- **Ignoring GOVERN**: Technical protect/detect work without leadership accountability is unstable.
- **Copying 1.x goal IDs into 2.0 trackers**: Renumbering and merges will create false compliance.

## Key Takeaways
1. CPGs prioritize foundational, high-impact IT/OT practices for critical infrastructure.
2. Version 2.0 adds GOVERN and realigns to CSF 2.0.
3. Goals use outcome + recommended action structure for implementers and executives.
4. OT-specific and duplicate goals were consolidated into a universal set.
5. CPGs support investment justification and gap benchmarking, not exhaustive assurance.
6. Remap any legacy 1.x program artifacts before claiming 2.0 coverage.
