# Chapter 2: GOVERN and IDENTIFY Goals

## Core Idea
GOVERN goals establish leadership accountability, IR planning, supply-chain/incident disclosure expectations, and managed-service-provider risk oversight. IDENTIFY goals build the asset and assurance picture — inventories, independent validation, and network topology documentation — that every later control depends on.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **GOVERN goal cluster (1.x)**: Responsibilities, IR plans, supplier incident/vuln disclosure, MSP risk management.
- **IDENTIFY goal cluster (2.x)**: Asset management, control validation, network topology.
- **Outcome-driven goal cards**: Each goal pairs a measurable outcome with recommended actions.

## Key Concepts
### GOVERN (selected 2.0 goals)
- **1.A Establish cybersecurity responsibilities**: Define roles, responsibilities, and authorities so ownership of cyber outcomes is unambiguous.
- **1.B (paired outcomes on roles/authorities)**: Related governance outcomes reinforcing assignment and accountability structures called out alongside 1.A in the report layout.
- **1.C Maintain incident response plans**: Keep IR plans current so the organization can identify improvements and execute when incidents occur.
- **1.D Supply chain incident reporting and vulnerability disclosure**: Ensure suppliers/partners report incidents and vulnerabilities affecting the organization on usable timelines.
- **1.E Manage risks from managed service providers**: Treat MSP access and services as first-class risk — contracts, oversight, and monitoring — not out-of-sight IT.

### IDENTIFY
- **2.A / 2.B Manage organizational assets**: Maintain asset inventories that improve cybersecurity decision-making (what you do not know, you cannot protect).
- **2.C / 2.D Obtain independent validation of cybersecurity controls**: Validate that implemented controls operate as intended via independent assessment paths.
- **2.E Document network topology**: Maintain topology documentation so responders and architects understand connectivity, trust boundaries, and blast radius.

## Mental Models
- GOVERN is who decides and who is accountable; IDENTIFY is what exists and how it connects.
- MSP and supply-chain goals exist because many critical-infrastructure compromises enter through trusted third parties.
- Independent validation prevents “checkbox implemented” theater on foundational controls.
- Topology docs are an IDENTIFY artifact with DETECT/RESPOND payoff during incidents.

## Anti-patterns
- **Cyber “owned by IT” with no executive authority map**: Fails 1.A intent.
- **IR plan shelfware**: 1.C expects maintained, usable plans.
- **Blind MSP admin paths**: 1.E is specifically about managed-service risk.
- **Asset inventory as a CMDB science project never used for risk decisions**: Inventory must improve security decisions, not only populate tools.

## Key Takeaways
1. GOVERN goals institutionalize leadership accountability and third-party cyber expectations.
2. IR plan maintenance is a governance outcome, not only an operations document.
3. Supply-chain incident/vuln disclosure and MSP risk management extend the trust boundary.
4. Asset inventory and topology documentation are non-negotiable IDENTIFY foundations.
5. Independent control validation increases confidence that protections actually work.
6. Weak GOVERN/IDENTIFY makes PROTECT/DETECT spend inefficient.
