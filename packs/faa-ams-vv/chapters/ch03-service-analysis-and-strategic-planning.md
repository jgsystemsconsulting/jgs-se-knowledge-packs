# Chapter 3: V&V Approach in the AMS Lifecycle: Service Analysis & Strategic Planning

> The lifecycle chapters begin here. This chapter covers the section 3 introduction (the phase map, the five primary management documents, and the checklist and model machinery) and Section 3.1, V&V in Service Analysis & Strategic Planning (SASP) with Table 1.

## Core Idea

The AMS lifecycle has five phases: Service Analysis & Strategic Planning (SASP), Concept and Requirements Definition (CRD), Investment Analysis (IA), Solution Implementation (SI), and In-Service Management (ISM). Alongside them, Research for Service Analysis (RSA) is a separate activity that precedes or runs during the early phases. RSA matures and validates new concepts and technologies for potential NAS investment and deployment, and may feed SASP, CRD, IA, and early SI. The independent stakeholders and reviewers responsible for V&V of RSA activities are the same as those for SASP, CRD, IA, and early SI.

The V&V process uses common tools and techniques across the lifecycle, and each phase's outputs are the basis for the next phase. What changes by phase is which items get V&V'd and which independent stakeholders and reviewers do it. All critical work products, components, and products, including those used as V&V criteria themselves, are subject to systematic V&V by the various program disciplines in every phase.

A caution the guideline prints with Figure 4: that figure shows only the primary emphasis of V&V between major decision points. The complete minimum required set of V&V activities lives in Tables 1 through 6. Every table carries the same footnote in substance: its content is based on AMS policy as of the time this document was published, in this edition January 2023, and the detailed phase guidelines and templates that exist should be reviewed for current guidance. Treat every table row here as a January 2023 policy snapshot, illustrative, not live authority.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The five primary management documents

Five documents support the critical early decision points, are essential to planning, management, and control of the whole acquisition, and are themselves subject to formal, structured V&V. Their purposes, paraphrased:

1. **Acquisition Program Baseline (APB):** establishes the performance, cost, and schedule baselines for an investment program segment.
2. **Program Requirements Document (PRD):** defines the operational framework and performance requirements the investment program must achieve.
3. **Business Case (BC):** summarizes the business case analysis and supplies the analytical and quantitative basis for investment decisions.
4. **Implementation Strategy and Planning Document (ISPD):** defines the overall implementation strategy and planning for the investment program.
5. **Program Management Plan (PMP):** defines how the service team will manage the investment program to execute the ISPD's strategy.

### The Appendix B checklists

Appendix B provides four checklists covering the PRD, APB, ISPD, and PMP. Each checklist names the key elements of its document that must be verified or validated, and for each key element the methods used and the artifacts produced must be identified. The checklists give authors a structure that helps ensure the documents carry the information approving authorities need to make critical lifecycle decisions.

### The Business Case's separate track

The Investment Planning & Analysis office verifies and validates the Business Case under the AFI's own evaluation and assessment guideline for business cases; that office's Independent Evaluation Review (IER) closes out the documentation and reporting the Business Case's V&V requires. The Business Case is deliberately not one of the four checklist documents; it runs on its own governance track.

### The AMS Rational Model for V&V (Figure 5)

Figure 5 renders the ARM, a static visualization that distills the (often undocumented) relationships between AMS work products. The guideline is explicit that it is not a process flow but a contextual relationship diagram, focused on the products where V&V strategies and practices apply during the lifecycle. It lets a user trace an acquisition document back to its parent (driving) documents or forward to dependent work products and into a thoroughly tested solution, across the whole acquisition lifecycle from initial concept to In-Service. The interactive version is available on FAST.

## V&V in Service Analysis & Strategic Planning (Section 3.1)

SASP determines what capabilities must be in place now and in the future to meet agency goals and customer service needs. Results are captured as the as-is and to-be states of the Enterprise Architecture plus the roadmaps between them. The responsible Line of Business folds the results into its business plans, and service organizations describe them in operating plans. Together these activities produce the information used to decide which service shortfalls or improvement ideas are approved into agency strategic planning documents.

The primary focus of V&V during SASP is to verify and validate three work products: the Preliminary Shortfall Analysis, the Concept and Requirements Definition Plan, and the ISS Risk Factors Assessment.

### Table 1: what the SASP rows fix (January 2023 snapshot)

Table 1 anchors on the Concept and Requirements Definition Readiness Decision and, in prose, its decision rule is:

- **Preliminary Shortfall Analysis** is validated against the Enterprise Architecture, the NextGen Implementation Plan, the NAS Segment Implementation Plan, the FAA Strategic Initiatives, the National Air Research Plan (NARP, if applicable), and the NAS ConOps. It is verified against the guidelines for Service Analysis Planning and CRD, the service shortfall analysis guidance and templates, and AMS policy. The stakeholders are the Lines of Business (Mission Support) / Service Team (NAS), the Office of Investment Planning & Analysis, and the Advanced Concepts and Technology Development Office.
- **Enterprise Architecture Change Notices, Products, and Amendments** are validated against shortfall analysis, service needs, the EA itself, the Solution Concept of Operations, the NSIP, the NAS Requirements Documents, and the FAA Strategic Initiatives. The responsible stakeholders are the Enterprise Architecture Control Board (NAS and Mission Support) and ANG Engineering Services, with the FAA Enterprise Architecture Board.
- **Concept and Requirements Definition Plan** is verified against the templates for EA change notices, products, and amendments and against the NAS Integrated Systems Engineering Framework; it is validated against the EA, the NextGen Implementation Plan, and the NSIP, and against the SASP/CRD guidelines. The stakeholders are the Lines of Business (Mission Support) / Service Team (NAS).
- **ISS Risk Factors Assessment** is validated against the FIPS-199 Security Categorization Table and verified against the Information Security Guidance for System Acquisition (ISGSA), with AMS Policy in the verification column. The stakeholders are the Lines of Business (Mission Support) / Service Team (NAS).

Two patterns worth noticing: validation criteria are substantive upstream content (architecture, strategy, plans) while verification criteria are templates, guidance, and policy; and security enters at the very first gate, as a FIPS-199-validated, ISGSA-verified assessment.

## Key Takeaways

- Five AMS phases plus the non-phase RSA activity, with RSA sharing its reviewers with the early phases.
- Figure 4 shows emphasis; Tables 1 through 6 define the minimum required V&V set, and all are January 2023 snapshots.
- The APB, PRD, Business Case, ISPD, and PMP are the five primary management documents, all V&V'd.
- Appendix B checklists cover four of them (PRD, APB, ISPD, PMP); the Business Case runs on the Investment Planning & Analysis track, closed by the IER.
- The ARM traces any acquisition document to its parents and dependents; it is a relationship diagram, not a process flow.
- SASP's V&V focus: Preliminary Shortfall Analysis, CRD Plan, ISS Risk Factors Assessment, gated at the CRDRD.
