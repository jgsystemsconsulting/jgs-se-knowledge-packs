# Chapter 4: V&V After SASP: CRD, Investment Analysis, Solution Implementation, and In-Service Management

> The remaining four lifecycle slices in one chapter. The SASP machinery repeats: phase focus statement, decision-point table, and named stakeholders. Tables 2 through 6 below are described in prose; all are January 2023 policy snapshots, not live authority.

## Core Idea

CRD turns needs into preliminary requirements and a solution concept. Investment Analysis picks an alternative and builds a baseline. Solution Implementation builds, buys, tests, and fields it. In-Service Management keeps it fit and governs every change to it. At each step the guideline names the minimum work products that must be verified and validated, the criteria checked against, and the stakeholders responsible.

## Concept and Requirements Definition (Section 3.2)

CRD translates priority operational needs in the Enterprise Architecture into preliminary requirements and a solution concept of operations. It quantifies the service shortfall in enough detail to support realistic preliminary requirements and to estimate potential costs and benefits. It also identifies the most promising alternative solutions to the service need, and one of the alternatives must be one already in the EA.

The primary V&V focus during CRD is to validate preliminary program requirements, the concept of use, EA products and amendments, the shortfall analysis, the initial Investment Analysis plan, and the Preliminary ISS Assessment, confirming that an existing or planned product properly addresses mission needs.

Table 2 (January 2023 snapshot) anchors on the Investment Analysis Readiness Decision. In prose: the Solution Concept of Operations is validated with concept development and validation focused on the EA, NextGen Implementation Plan, NSIP, and NAS Requirements Documents, and verified against SASP/CRD guidelines, ConOps guidance and template, and the Preliminary ISS Assessment; stakeholders are Lines of Business (Mission Support) / Service Team (NAS) and ANG Engineering Services. The preliminary PRD and EA products and amendments follow the same validation spine, with EA rows verified against the PRD template, System Engineering Manual, and AMS policy, and the EA Control Board (NAS and Mission Support) and ANG Engineering Services responsible for EA products. The shortfall analysis is additionally validated against operational analysis reports and preliminary shortfall analysis V&V results where applicable. The initial IA plan and Preliminary ISS Assessment close the table: validated against the solution ConOps, preliminary PRD, and EA, verified against IA plan guidance and template and AMS policy, with the Office of Investment Planning and Analysis (AFI) joining the stakeholder list; the ISS assessment is validated against FIPS-199 and verified against ISGSA.

## Investment Analysis (Section 3.3)

IA is a flexible process tailored to the specific analysis being performed. Tailoring gets approved by the Acquisition Executive or Investment Decision Authority (IDA) and is noted in the governing plan and in the record of decision, whether the analysis is Initial or Final.

### Initial Investment Analysis (3.3.1)

Initial IA evaluates alternative solutions to mission needs and hands the IDA realistic options that satisfy FAA strategic and performance goals with the best overall value. The main outputs: initial PRD, initial ISPD, initial Business Case, preliminary TEMP, updated EA products and amendments, and the plan for Final Investment Analysis. The V&V focus is validating work products so the best alternative gets selected.

Table 3 (January 2023 snapshot) anchors on the Initial Investment Decision. The Investment Analysis Team, defined in AMS Policy Section 2.5.1 as including all specialists, is a recurring stakeholder, joined per row by ANG Engineering Services, the Office of Investment Planning and Analysis, and the ANG-E Test Standards Board. The initial PRD is validated against the EA, ConOps, analyses conducted during Initial IA, NAS Requirements Documents, preliminary PRD V&V results, and the Initial ISS assessment; it is verified against the PRD template, System Engineering Manual, AMS policy, and ISGSA. The initial Business Case, initial ISPD, and preliminary TEMP are validated against the same upstream spine (each row referencing prior versions' V&V results), and verified against their respective templates, the Business Case template, the Test and Evaluation Handbook, and AMS policy. Updated EA products and amendments are validated by the Enterprise Architecture Control Board and ANG Engineering Services, verified against the NAS Integrated Systems Engineering Framework. The final IA plan is verified against the Investment Analysis Plan template and AMS policy.

### Final Investment Analysis (3.3.2)

Final IA develops detailed plans and final requirements for the selected investment. Principal outputs: program baseline work products, final PRD, final Business Case, final ISPD, APB, PMP, initial TEMP, updated EA products and amendments, a tailored In-Service Review (ISR) checklist, and the Screening Information Request (SIR). The V&V focus is validating work products that mitigate risk and support implementation of the best alternative.

Table 4 (January 2023 snapshot) anchors on the Final Investment Decision. Rows run for the final PRD, final Business Case, APB, PMP, initial TEMP, updated EA products, final ISPD, tailored ISR checklist, and SIR. Stakeholders: Investment Analysis Team (all specialists per AMS Policy Section 2.5.1), plus the Office of Investment Planning and Analysis, the Office of Budget and Programs (on APB and PMP rows), the ANG-E Test Standards Board (TEMP row), and, for the SIR, the Contracting Officer. Validation criteria keep chaining: the final PRD carries the initial PRD's V&V results; the final Business Case carries the initial Business Case's V&V results; the APB is validated against the final PRD and Business Case; the PMP against the final ISPD, final PRD, and final Business Case; the initial TEMP is verified against the TEMP Handbook and the TEMP template per AMS Policy (the ANG-E Test Standards Board owns that row); the ISR checklist against the template for a tailored ISR checklist; and, for the SIR, the Statement of Work template, templates DD Form 1423-1 and DD Form 1664, and the System Specification template.

## Solution Implementation (Section 3.4)

SI's stated aim is meeting the user needs captured in requirements and delivering the benefits the Business Case projected. Plans and baselines produced in Final IA are rechecked and revised as needed around contract award, typically through the FAA Integrated Baseline Review (IBR), so they remain a realistic management construct for implementation. SI runs seven major activities: finalize program planning; obtain the solution; verify operational readiness; prepare the In-Service Decision; update planning for ISM; the In-Service Decision; and deploy the solution to all sites.

The primary V&V focus is on products and product components and on the work products that artifact those activities. Developmental and operational testing are the primary methods, and the plans, procedures, and reports generated to support the tests must themselves be verified and validated, so the test results handed to the In-Service Decision are accurate and complete. Technical milestones join the program: Preliminary Design Review (PDR) and Critical Design Review (CDR) are key opportunities for structured V&V of the allocated and product baselines respectively.

Table 5 (January 2023 snapshot) runs from Contract Award through the In-Service Decision. The contract (updated Statement of Work, updated Contract Data Requirements List / Data Item Descriptions, updated System Specification) is validated against the final PRD, final ISPD, and the results of negotiations with the contractor, and verified against SOW, DD Form 1423-1/1664, and System Specification templates; stakeholders are the Service Team, including functional organizations for systems engineering and test and evaluation, plus the Contracting Officer. The final TEMP row is validated against the final ISPD, final PRD, final Business Case, APB, updated SOW and CDRLs, and the initial TEMP's V&V results, with the ANG-E Test Standards Board verifying against the TEMP Handbook and AMS policy. At product demonstration (if required), contractor specifications are decomposed from the FAA System Specification and design documents and checked against the contract's own data item descriptions. At Product Acceptance / Contractor Acceptance and Inspection, Production Decision (if required), and In-Service Decision, the note names the major V&V activities: DT, OT, and IOA. The system or service is validated against the final PRD including Critical Operational Issues and Critical Performance Requirements and against the solution ConOps. Safety gets its own rows: the Safety Risk Management document or System Safety Assessment Report is verified against system and lower-level specifications, the SRM document, and the contract, validated against prior safety assessments, the solution ConOps, and the final PRD, and verified against the FAA Safety Management System (SMS); stakeholders add the ATO Office of Safety and Technical Training. System Security Authorization is validated via the Plan of Actions and Milestones (POA&M) and verified against the Security Authorization Handbook and AMS policy, with the Information Systems Security Manager joining. The completed ISR checklist is assessed, with issues, action plans, and remarks tracked against each checklist question.

## In-Service Management (Section 3.5)

ISM's primary goal is sustaining execution of the FAA mission: providing air traffic control and other services in real time, with systems, products, services, and facilities operated, maintained, secured, and sustained at the level of service users require. Fielded products get periodic monitoring and evaluation, and performance data feeds ongoing Mission Analysis and Investment Analysis, to revalidate whether to keep sustaining deployed assets or take other action to improve service delivery. The span is wide: configuration management, preventive and corrective maintenance, training, infrastructure and logistics support, emergency sustainment, removal and disposal of obsolete assets, and sustainment of flight inspection, aircraft certification, and regulatory requirements.

The primary V&V focus during ISM is to ensure that modifications to the product meet new or modified requirements, that any new or modified requirements or operational concepts are themselves valid, and that the product continues to fulfill its intended purpose throughout its service life.

Table 6 (January 2023 snapshot) anchors on the Decision to Implement an Improvement or Change and the Decision to Deploy an Improvement or Change. Updates to EA products and amendments are validated against the EA, NSIP, NAS Requirements Documents, and operational analysis reports, with the Service Team (including functional organizations for systems engineering), the Enterprise Architecture Control Board, and ANG Engineering Services responsible. NAS Change Proposals and changed requirements are verified against the NAS Integrated Systems Engineering Framework, FAA Form 1800-2 (the Case File / NCP form), and FAA Order 1800.66 configuration management policy, and validated against post-implementation reviews, operational analyses, and the final PRD. Implemented changes to the fielded baseline (supported and documented in plans, procedures, and reports) are validated against the final PRD including Critical Operational Issues and Critical Performance Requirements, the NCP, and the Functional Description Document; the table's note names ISM DT and OT activities, and IOA if applicable, as the major V&V activities. System Support Directives are verified against FAA Order 1320.58A, the instruction set for writing notices, maintenance technical handbooks, and system support directives. Updates to the ISPD are validated against the EA, solution ConOps, functional analyses products, the updated PRD, the detailed strategy for procuring, implementing, testing, and supporting the solution, and the final ISPD's own V&V results, verified against the ISPD template and AMS policy.

## Key Takeaways

- Each phase has a stated V&V focus, a decision-point table, and named stakeholders; the table row is the minimum, and phase guidance is the current authority.
- CRD must carry the EA's own alternative forward among the candidates.
- IA tailoring is real but governed: approved by the Acquisition Executive or IDA, recorded in the plan and the record of decision.
- V&V results chain forward: each new version of an artifact is validated partly against the previous version's V&V results.
- SI adds test discipline: DT verifies, OT and IOA validate, and test artifacts themselves must be V&V'd first.
- Safety (SMS) and security (POA&M) are gated obligations with named owners at the ISD.
- In service, every change is verified against requirements and policy and re-validated against purpose; NCPs run under FAA Form 1800-2 and Order 1800.66.
