# What a clinical evaluation assessment report must contain, per MDCG 2020-13

S3 is the template a notified body uses to document its assessment of a manufacturer's clinical evaluation report (CER) and the evaluation behind it.
For a manufacturer the template is a map of the questions the assessment will ask, in order.
The template states it is the minimum content, and each section ends in a compliance verdict: compliant, or compliant with named minor non-compliances plus follow-up actions and timelines.
The tick for a positive assessment may only be set once all non-compliances are closed; rare open minor non-compliances must be described with the actions and timelines that will close them.
The template also fixes who reads the output.
Expert panels run the consultation procedure from the CEAR alone, without access to the full conformity assessment, so the CEAR must carry enough detail on the clinical evidence, the benefit-risk determination, the consistency of the evidence with the intended purpose, and the PMCF plan.
Designating authorities read it with the notified body's complete audit trail when they assess whether the assessment itself was sound.
The CEAR is signed off per the notified body's quality management system, and personal data in it is handled under data protection rules.

## The general sections

Section A holds the administrative frame: device name, model, Basic UDI-DI, risk class, manufacturer and authorised representative with their single registration numbers, notified body details, the type of assessment (initial, change, re-certification, or sampling-based), and a check that the CER is dated and signed with current CVs for its authors showing the needed expertise.

Section B records the reviewers: who holds the relevant clinical expertise, whether additional internal or external reviewers joined, their competence areas, and what each was assigned to assess.

Section C is the largest.
It covers the device description: intended patient population, key functional elements including software, mode of action, variants, accessories, previous generations, and similar devices on the market.
It lists the applicable classification rules and summarises the manufacturer's clinical evaluation plan against the MDR's required plan elements: GSPRs needing clinical data, intended purpose, target groups, benefits with outcome parameters, safety methods, benefit-risk parameters, and a development plan with milestones.
It then records clinical performance and safety data, the application of common specifications and harmonised standards (or justified deviations), and the equivalence demonstration.
Each equivalence claim is tied to a single device meeting all three criteria: clinical, technical, and biological.
The section also records the manufacturer's access to data supporting the equivalence claim, the state-of-the-art description with benchmarks and alternative treatments, and how any novel feature is addressed.

Section D covers the literature review.
The search criteria must address all device sizes, variants, models, and accessories, and the same clinical condition; searches for the device, for equivalents, and for the state of the art are described separately.
The template expects multiple databases, acceptable inclusion and exclusion criteria, both favourable and unfavourable data, a documented protocol and report, full-text retention, and a data appraisal that weights studies by scientific quality and relevance.
It names the systematic methods it expects to see where appropriate: PICO framing, Cochrane handbook methods, PRISMA reporting, and MOOSE for observational meta-analyses.

Section E covers clinical investigations: whether any were run, why not if none, registration and publication status, authority correspondence, and whether the investigation plan, design, population, endpoints, and follow-up support the conclusions drawn.

Section F covers post-market surveillance and PMCF: which plans and reports were reviewed, whether PMCF is planned, the justification if not, the link between equivalence claims and post-market verification, and the schedule for clinical evaluation updates during surveillance.

Section G checks the information supplied with the device (instructions for use, labelling, promotional material, the summary of safety and clinical performance) against the clinical evidence: intended purpose, population, users, limitations, contraindications, warnings, residual risk description, and consistency with the risk management file and PMS outputs.

Section H and the overall conclusions pull it together.
They carry summaries of safety and performance data, verdicts on whether the evidence demonstrates conformity with the relevant GSPRs, and whether it supports the claims and the information supplied with the device.
The benefit-risk discussion follows, with alignment between risk management and clinical evaluation checked explicitly.
The report ends in a recommendation to the notified body's decision maker covering PMS and PMCF adequacy, milestones for reviewing clinical evidence updates, and certification conditions.

## The special sections

Three annexed sections apply when triggered.
The consultation-procedure section covers certain class III and class IIb devices that go to an expert panel, including the legacy-device checks: modifications limited to compliance needs, a valid prior certificate, and no outstanding non-compliance.
The non-clinical-route section applies where the manufacturer claims clinical data is not the appropriate way to demonstrate conformity: a clinical evaluation is still required, available clinical data must still be searched and appraised, and the justification rests on risk management results, the device-body interaction, the intended clinical performance, and claims the data actually supports.
The third records the voluntary expert panel consultation on the clinical development strategy and how the manufacturer answered the panel's views.
