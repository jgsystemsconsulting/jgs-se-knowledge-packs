# Which EU and UK document answers which question about device software

This pack orients a reader in the EU and UK regulatory treatment of software: whether software qualifies as a medical device or an in vitro diagnostic device, how qualified software is classified, and what clinical evidence obligations run through the device lifecycle.
It covers the European Union under the MDR and IVDR and Great Britain under the UKCA regime.
It does not cover the United States.
FDA questions, including 510(k) and De Novo pathways and FDA risk categorization of software as a medical device, belong to the fda-med-device pack and get no answer here.

The binding layer in the EU is the legislation itself.
Regulation (EU) 2017/745, the MDR, covers medical devices.
Regulation (EU) 2017/746, the IVDR, covers in vitro diagnostics.
Software meets these regulations as a device in its own right, as an accessory, as an integral component of a hardware device, or as software driving or influencing a device's use.
This pack names the two regulations and teaches the guidance around them; it never reproduces regulation text.
In Great Britain the binding layer is the Medical Devices Regulations 2002 as amended, usually called UK MDR 2002, with the UKCA mark for goods placed on the GB market.
Northern Ireland runs different rules, which the MHRA guidance flags on its first page.

The five MDCG documents sit under the EU regulations as non-binding guidance.
Each carries the same page-one disclaimer, and S1 states it on its cover: "Any views expressed in this document are not legally binding and only the Court of Justice of the European Union can give binding interpretations of Union law."
The MDCG is the committee of Member State representatives that the MDR established, chaired by a Commission representative, and its documents are endorsed best practice, not law.
Non-binding does not mean ignorable: notified bodies and competent authorities work to these documents in practice, and a manufacturer who departs from them carries the burden of justifying the departure.
The MHRA document is the UK counterpart: guidance from the national regulator for the GB market, positioned as the UK's interpretation of the earlier European guidance MEDDEV 2.1/6.

Question-to-document map, with the chapter that teaches each:

- Does software qualify under the EU regulations, and in which class? MDCG 2019-11 rev.1 (S1), taught in ch02.
- Does an app or stand-alone package qualify in the UK, and where does the UK reading differ from the EU one? MHRA guidance (S6), taught in ch03.
- What clinical evidence suffices for a device already CE marked under the old directives? MDCG 2020-6 (S2), taught in ch04.
- How does a notified body document its assessment of a manufacturer's clinical evaluation? MDCG 2020-13 (S3), taught in ch05.
- What does a post-market clinical follow-up plan commit to? MDCG 2020-7 (S4), taught in ch06.
- How is follow-up evidence reported, and what closes the plan? MDCG 2020-8 (S5), taught in ch07.
- What does the UK regime demand of software once it is a qualified device? MHRA guidance (S6), taught in ch08.

Two reading notes close this orientation.
First, the EU and UK documents share an ancestor in MEDDEV 2.1/6, so the core medical-purpose test is similar on both sides; the differences live in the details, and ch03 names them where they matter.
Second, these documents revise.
S1 is revision 1 of an October 2019 document, revised in June 2025, and its revision page lists what changed.
Check for the current revision of any document in this pack before relying on it in an engagement.
