# What a PMCF plan commits to, per MDCG 2020-7

S4 is the template for the post-market clinical follow-up plan.
Its premise: PMCF is a continuous process that updates the clinical evaluation, and the plan sits inside the manufacturer's post-market surveillance plan.
The notified body assesses the plan's adequacy, and a decision not to run PMCF at all needs its own justification.

## The five commitments

Every activity in the plan serves at least one of five aims the template lists:

- Confirming safety and performance, including clinical benefit where applicable, across the device's expected lifetime.
- Identifying previously unknown side-effects and monitoring the known side-effects and contraindications.
- Identifying and analysing emergent risks on the basis of factual evidence.
- Ensuring the continued acceptability of the benefit-risk ratio.
- Identifying possible systematic misuse or off-label use, to verify the intended purpose is correct.

## What the template demands

Sections A and B fix the frame: manufacturer contacts, and a device specification (intended purpose, users, patient population, medical conditions, indications, contraindications, warnings, variants, accessories, class and classification rule, expected lifetime, and any novel feature or novel clinical procedure).
The plan itself carries a number, a date, a version, and a revision history, since the notified body assesses the plan as a controlled document.
Expected lifetime is a design-input decision, set against the current state of the art for the intended use and indication.

Section C is the working core.
For each planned activity, in its own subsection, the manufacturer states where the need comes from (a notified body request, the clinical evaluation report, PMS output, risk management, a previous PMCF report), describes the activity, names its aim from the list above, and justifies the method.
The procedure menu includes screening scientific literature, post-market studies, registry data collection, surveys of clinicians or patients, and review of case reports for misuse.
Registry use needs pre-specification: what quality and quantity of data will be collected, judged against the device's risk, and which public registries will be evaluated, with search protocols named.
Real-world evidence analyses need real-world data of sufficient quality from reliable sources, with design, sample size, endpoints, and analysis population stated.
The justification must cover sample size, timescales, endpoints, comparator choice against the state of the art, study design and bias controls, and a statistical justification for the expected quality of outcomes.
The template explicitly rejects retrospective surveys waved through with a bare claim that the evidence will be good enough.
Timelines are committed per activity at quarterly or at least yearly resolution, and a summary table gathers activity, aim, rationale, known limitations, and timeline in one view.

The remaining sections bind the plan to the rest of the documentation.
Section D references the parts of the clinical evaluation report and the risk management file that the plan must follow up, or states explicitly that nothing is relevant.
Section E lists the equivalent or similar devices whose clinical data will be evaluated, cross-referenced to the CER, with the note that data demonstrating continuing safety and performance must come from the device itself, and data from equivalents mainly updates the state of the art.
Section F lists the common specifications, harmonised standards, and PMCF guidance the plan works to.
Section G commits to an estimated date for the PMCF evaluation report, on the same quarterly or yearly discipline.

One hard content requirement sits on top of the template structure: for implantable and class III devices where pre-market clinical investigations were not performed, the plan must include post-market studies to confirm the safety and performance of the device.
