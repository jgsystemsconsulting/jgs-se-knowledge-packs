# Chapter 3: Planning Stage

## Core Idea
Planning is the first SEM stage for every project size. Teams identify business needs, define objectives and scope, estimate high-level requirements, prove feasibility, and produce approved Project, Quality Assurance, and Configuration Management plans that govern the rest of the lifecycle.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Planning stage activity set**: Project management (including subcontractors, risk, performance measures, EA), user-environment analysis, objectives, scope, high-level requirements, functional-area communication, feasibility, and plan baselining.
- **Planning questionnaire / total systems approach**: Structured questions driving whole-system planning rather than narrow software tasks.
- **Risk vs stake lifecycle profile**: Planning explicitly considers how risk and amount-at-stake evolve across stages.

## Key Concepts
- **Stage outcomes**: Preliminary requirements; staff/schedule/cost estimates; technical and managerial approaches; risk assessment; approved Project Plan, QA Plan, and CM Plan.
- **System owner and users**: Owner funds the project; users are DOE employees/contractors who will use the product — both are interviewed for needs, expectations, and mission fit.
- **Mission alignment**: Determine how the project supports DOE and organizational missions and long-range IRM plans.
- **Feasibility**: Business, cost, and technical sense-making before detailed requirements; investigate software/hardware alternatives and platform options; benefits/cost analysis as appropriate; feasibility review.
- **Project management subprocesses**: Subcontractor management when used; risk management; performance measures; Enterprise Architecture considerations within the DOE EA framework.
- **Cross-functional engagement**: Work with all functional areas that will supply resources, information, or support services.
- **Deciding in advance**: Planning answers what, how, when, where, and who — then becomes the baseline for managing change.

## Mental Models
- If planning is weak, later Stage Exits become political fights instead of technical decisions.
- QA and CM plans written here are how CMM-style process areas get project-specific teeth.
- Feasibility is permission to spend the next stage’s money — treat it as a real kill-point.
- Performance measures chosen in planning should still matter at acceptance and maintenance.

## Anti-patterns
- **Jumping to requirements workshops without feasibility**: Builds the wrong thing efficiently.
- **Project plan without CM/QA plans**: Configuration and quality become afterthoughts.
- **Ignoring EA constraints**: Creates integration and funding friction later.
- **Owner absentee planning**: Users alone cannot authorize scope or resources.

## Key Takeaways
1. Planning applies to all project sizes and produces the governing triad of Project/QA/CM plans.
2. Owner and user engagement establishes mission-aligned objectives and scope.
3. Feasibility and alternatives analysis gate detailed engineering spend.
4. Risk management and performance measures are planning products, not later add-ons.
5. EA and cross-functional resource commitments are established early.
6. Planning baselines enable controlled adaptation in later stages.
