# Chapter 1: SEM Purpose, Scope, and Context

## Core Idea
DOE Systems Engineering Methodology (SEM) Version 3 is the Department’s Systems Development Lifecycle (SDLC) guidance for information technology investments. It renames and expands earlier “Software Engineering Methodology” practice to cover whole IT systems — not code alone — and aligns stage practices with SEI CMM-SW Level 2/3 process areas.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **DOE SEM / SDLC**: Staged lifecycle for planning through maintenance of DOE IT investments.
- **CMM-SW alignment**: Mapping SEM activities to Software Capability Maturity Model Level 2 and 3 key process areas (companion mapping published with the SEM program).
- **Directive identity**: Version 3 associated with DOE G 200.1-1A (evolving from DOE G 200.1-1).

## Key Concepts
- **Purpose**: Improve quality, performance, and productivity of Departmental information systems through a shared engineering lifecycle used by federal and contractor personnel.
- **Audience**: DOE system owners, project managers, developers, QA, configuration management, and supporting functional areas involved in IT investments.
- **Version 3 highlights**: Rename software→systems focus; Construction stage (formerly Programming); updated planning questionnaire for a total systems approach; stronger risk management and performance measures; updated Enterprise Architecture linkage; several former appendixes spun out as stand-alone process guides (Structured Walkthroughs, In-Stage Assessments, Stage Exits).
- **Scope of “system”**: Information system product, managed data, technical documentation, and user training/support — maintained under configuration management after delivery.
- **Relationship to other DOE programs**: SEM sits among CIO/corporate IRM and architecture guidance; projects should show mission and long-range IRM alignment.
- **Age caveat**: Source is September 2002. Principles remain useful; tooling, cloud, and modern DevSecOps practices require interpretation when applying SEM today.

## Mental Models
- SEM is a *management and quality* lifecycle as much as a technical one: plans, reviews, and exits are first-class.
- Think “IT investment lifecycle,” not “coding standard.”
- CMM alignment means process discipline (requirements management, CM, QA, peer reviews) is intentional, not optional garnish.
- Companion process guides hold procedural depth that the main SEM points to rather than fully embeds.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating SEM as software-only**: Misses v3 systems/IT investment scope.
- **Skipping DOE EA / mission alignment in planning**: Produces orphan systems.
- **Applying 2002 tool prescriptions literally in cloud-native shops**: Keep the stage intents; modernize mechanisms.
- **Ignoring contractor/federal dual workforce**: SEM assumes both participate in evolution and execution.

## Key Takeaways
1. SEM v3 is DOE’s SDLC for IT investments under CIO stewardship.
2. It expands from software-centric SEM to full systems engineering for IT projects.
3. CMM-SW Level 2/3 alignment structures process expectations.
4. Risk management, performance measures, and EA receive greater emphasis in v3.
5. Detailed walkthrough/assessment/exit procedures live in companion guides.
6. Use stage intents with contemporary engineering practices when modernizing application.
