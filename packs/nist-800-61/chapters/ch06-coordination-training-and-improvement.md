# Chapter 6: Coordination, Training, Communications, and Improvement

## Core Idea
SP 800-61r3 stresses that IR succeeds through coordinated people and continuous learning: train the right roles, communicate with discipline, share cyber threat and incident information appropriately, exercise procedures, and push lessons into ID.IM so every CSF Function improves.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Coordination fabric**: Internal RACI plus external partners (MSSP, CSP, ISACs/ISAOs, regulators, law enforcement) under pre-negotiated rules.
- **Training and exercise loop**: Role-based readiness tied to procedures/playbooks and measured through tests.
- **Lessons-to-change pipeline**: Detect/response/recovery observations become prioritized improvements across Govern/Identify/Protect as well as IR execution.

## Key Concepts
- **Internal coordination**: Leadership decision rights, handler federation, asset-owner prioritization, legal/HR/facilities/public affairs integration (see ch03).
- **External coordination**: Contracted detection/response providers, cloud shared-responsibility IR actions, sector sharing communities, and mandatory reporting authorities — contacts and data-handling rules must exist before midnight.
- **Communications discipline**: Stakeholder notification matrices; dual tracks for operational IR status vs public/media statements; alignment with legal definitions of reportable events.
- **Cyber threat information sharing**: Enrich monitoring and analysis; sharing is bidirectional where trusted channels exist; protect sensitive details under policy.
- **Training**: Not only handlers — technology staff, executives making high-impact decisions, communications leads, and third parties who execute pieces of the plan.
- **Exercises and tests**: Validate procedures/playbooks for common incident types and emergency processes (e.g., identity platform rebuild); capture gaps into improvement backlog.
- **Performance measures**: Policy-level metrics for IR effectiveness (time-to-detect, time-to-contain, restore integrity rates, exercise findings closure).
- **Continuous improvement (ID.IM)**: Analyze lessons from all Functions promptly; prioritize; update IR plans, safeguards, monitoring rules, and governance artifacts.

## Mental Models
- Coordination is a control: an unused emergency contact list equals no external capacity.
- Train for decisions under uncertainty (declare? pay? disclose? shut down?) not only for tool clicks.
- Every major incident should leave the organization with changed detections, hardened configs, and clearer authorities — or the learning system is broken.
- Communications failures create parallel incidents (regulatory, reputational) alongside the technical one.

## Anti-patterns
- **First-time introductions during a breach**: Vendors, counsel, and public affairs meeting in the war room with no prior drills.
- **Handler-only training**: Executives freeze on authority calls they never practiced.
- **Sharing nothing / sharing everything**: No trusted channels, or oversharing that violates legal/privacy constraints.
- **Exercise findings without owners**: Red-team reports that never touch ID.IM or remediation trackers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Coordination spans internal roles and external partners under prearranged governance.
2. Communications plans must cover operational, legal, partner, and public audiences.
3. Threat information sharing improves Detect/Analyze quality when channels and protections exist.
4. Training and exercises validate playbooks and decision rights, not just tooling.
5. Performance measures keep IR capability visible to leadership.
6. Continuous improvement must change the system — plans, protections, and detections — not only produce reports.
