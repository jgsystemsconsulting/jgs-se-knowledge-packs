# Chapter: Introduction

## Core Idea

The September 2026 Systems Engineering Guidebook is guidance and recommended practice for Department of War (DoW) acquisition programs. It is not a mandatory checklist. Programs tailor depth and breadth of systems engineering (SE) to pathway, risk, and need for speed while keeping technical integrity.

The Office of the Under Secretary of War for Research and Engineering (OUSW(R&E)), Office of Systems Engineering and Architecture (SE&A), prepared the book with Military Service, Defense Agency, industry, and academic subject-matter experts. Primary readers are program managers (PMs), systems engineers, software engineers, and related development disciplines.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Adaptive Acquisition Framework (AAF)** under DoDI 5000.02 as the pathway set that SE work is tailored to.
- **DoDI 5000.88** (Engineering of Defense Systems) as the binding SE policy the guidebook supports.
- **DoDD 5000.01** as the top defense acquisition system directive.
- **Life-cycle process framing** that places SE activities across Materiel Solution Analysis through Operations and Support, with pathway-specific entry and emphasis.
- **Enabling system elements** that develop, produce, test, field, sustain, or retire the operational system.

## Key Concepts

- **Purpose of SE.** Methodical specification, design, development, realization, technical management, operations, and retirement of a system. SE balances cost, schedule, performance, and risk while improving effectiveness, suitability, survivability, and sustainability.
- **What counts as the system.** An aggregation of system elements plus enabling elements that together deliver a capability. Interfaces and dependencies with other systems belong in the problem frame from the start.
- **Audience and use.** The book is a flexible reference. It supports planning and executing SE activities with other acquisition resources. It does not replace cited policy issuances.
- **Policy and guidance stack.** Named issuances include DoDD 5000.01, DoDI 5000.02, DoDI 5000.88, and related pathway and engineering instructions. The guidebook implements guidance under that stack; it is not itself policy.
- **Development planning.** Early technical work before or at the start of product development that positions the program for later success. Planning choices set the SE approach, review strategy, and process scale.
- **SE processes overview.** Eight technical management processes and eight technical processes form the process model. They map to ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 process intent and are applied in an integrated, iterative way along a V-style life cycle.
- **Scaling rule.** Organizations scale process application to maturity, complexity, size, pathway, and phase. Rigor stays where risk and safety demand it; non-value activity is cut.
- **SE approach.** Regardless of pathway, the PM should develop an SE approach that integrates technical management and technical work into the overall plan and schedule, including success criteria for reviews and audits.

## Mental Models

- **Guidance under policy.** Open the binding issuance first. Use the guidebook to operationalize practice, not to invent policy.
- **Balance four constraints.** Cost, schedule, performance, and risk move together. SE work makes the trade explicit.
- **System plus enabling system.** Capability fails if enabling elements (test, production, sustainment, training) are ignored.
- **Pathway-tailored rigor.** Same process names; different depth by AAF pathway and risk profile.

## Key Takeaways

- Treat the guidebook as tailored guidance under DoDI 5000.88 and the AAF, not as a rigid checklist.
- Name policy issuances when they drive a requirement; do not quote them as pack text.
- Frame every program with system elements, enabling elements, interfaces, and life-cycle phase.
- Scale the 16 SE processes to pathway and risk; keep technical integrity where it matters.
- Capture the SE approach in planning artifacts so reviews and process work stay aligned.
