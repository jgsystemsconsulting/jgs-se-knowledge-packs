# Chapter: Systems Engineering Modernization

## Core Idea

SE modernization is a new top-level section in the September 2026 edition. It shifts traditional, document-heavy engineering toward digital, agile, and model-based practice. The goal is to use authoritative data across the life cycle to increase speed, reduce cost, and improve adaptability to new threats.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Digital Engineering (DE)** as codified under DoDI 5000.97 and guided by related handbooks and bodies of knowledge.
- **Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE)** as a modeling practice inside the broader DE shift.
- **Mission Engineering** as mission-thread and architecture trade practice in an SoS context.
- **Software Engineering** with commercial Agile/DevSecOps patterns, CI/CD, and technical-debt capacity.
- **Modular Open Systems Approach (MOSA)** as modularity and open-interface practice.
- **Systems Engineering in Agile development** including continuous review and learning loops.

## Key Concepts

- **Tools and techniques overview.** SE data, tools, and techniques, including modeling and simulation, support performance of SE activities. They help the PM and technical team improve productivity and system cost, schedule, capability, and adaptability.
- **Models and simulations.** Models should be matured through the life of the effort, documented with metadata and assumptions, managed as part of the authoritative source of truth, and included in the technical data package for later phases or reuse. Model and data artifacts should show up in technical reviews and baselined technical data.
- **Digital Engineering.** DE uses digital computing to move programs away from document-centric handoffs toward integrated digital representations across development and sustainment. It supports better risk insight, knowledge capture, and change impact analysis.
- **MBSE.** Model-based practice captures requirements, architecture, and design relationships in models rather than disconnected documents. It is a means under DE, not a separate religion.
- **Mission Engineering.** Mission threads and architectures support trades in an SoS context so programs optimize for mission success, not only single-system optimization.
- **Software Engineering.** Adopt commercial SWE skills, Agile/DevSecOps automation, pipelines, tools, metrics, and CI/CD where they fit defense constraints. Early software prototyping explores alternatives and reduces risk. Programs should reserve sprint capacity for technical debt and unplanned work.
- **MOSA.** Modular open systems practice structures major system components and interfaces so modules can be competed, upgraded, and reused with less lock-in.
- **SE in Agile.** Agile subsystem iterations sit inside the larger SE life cycle. Continuous agile review and learning keep technical reviews aligned with iterative delivery rather than only waterfall gates.

## Mental Models

- **Authoritative source of truth.** One managed digital baseline beats many stale documents.
- **Modernization is means, not goal.** Speed and adaptability matter only if technical integrity and safety hold.
- **Agile inside SE, not instead of SE.** Iterations still answer requirements, architecture, verification, and validation questions.
- **Mission over platform.** Mission engineering pulls trades up to the operational thread.

## Key Takeaways

- Treat Section 3 as a first-class job: DE, MBSE, mission engineering, software engineering, MOSA, and Agile SE.
- Manage models and data as controlled life-cycle assets, not side products.
- Use DE and MBSE to improve risk insight and knowledge capture across phases.
- Adopt Agile/DevSecOps practices with explicit capacity for debt and unplanned work.
- Keep modular open interfaces so upgrades do not force full-system redesign.
