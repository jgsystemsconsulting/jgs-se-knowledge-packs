# Chapter: Technical Management Processes

## Core Idea

DoW SE uses eight technical management processes as foundational controls across the life cycle. They plan, assess, and control the technical effort so the eight technical processes stay coherent. In the September 2026 edition they sit under Section 6.1 inside the single Systems Engineering Processes section, alongside the technical processes in 6.2. The Systems Engineering Plan (SEP) is a primary output of technical planning and a living control document.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Eight technical management processes:** Technical Planning, Decision Analysis, Technical Assessment, Requirements Management, Risk Management, Configuration Management, Technical Data Management, Interface Management.
- **SEP** as the program's technical planning and control narrative.
- **IMP/IMS hierarchy** linking events, accomplishments, and tasks.
- **Technical Performance Measures (TPMs)** and leading indicators inside technical assessment.
- **Risk, issue, and opportunity** framing inside risk management.

## Key Concepts

- **Technical Planning.** Defines the technical effort, organization, resources, and schedule integration. Outputs feed the SEP, Integrated Master Plan (IMP), Integrated Master Schedule (IMS), and work breakdown structure. Planning scales process depth to risk and pathway.
- **Decision Analysis.** Structures trades with explicit criteria, alternatives, and recorded rationale so major choices are repeatable and reviewable.
- **Technical Assessment.** Measures technical progress and health. TPMs track performance parameters against planned profiles. Leading indicators warn before lagging metrics fail. Contingency and margin concepts protect against late discovery.
- **Requirements Management.** Controls requirement baselines, changes, traceability, and allocation so design stays coupled to need.
- **Risk Management.** Identifies, analyzes, mitigates, and monitors technical risks, and handles issues (realized risks) and opportunities. New subsections in this edition deepen the practice; programs still integrate with the broader DoD RIO discipline.
- **Configuration Management.** Establishes and maintains functional, allocated, and product baselines; runs change control; supports audits.
- **Technical Data Management.** Plans, acquires, marks, stores, and transitions technical data and data rights so sustainment and competition remain possible. Handles distribution and proprietary constraints as control problems, not afterthoughts.
- **Interface Management.** Defines, controls, and monitors internal and external interfaces. An interface control working group (ICWG) is the usual forum across interfacing activities.
- **SEP role.** The SEP records the SE approach, organization, processes, review strategy, design considerations table intent, and digital/data approach. It is updated as the program learns.

## Mental Models

- **Management processes enable technical processes.** Without planning, CM, and risk control, design work drifts.
- **SEP is a contract with yourself.** If it is shelfware, technical control is theater.
- **Measure leading, not only lagging.** TPMs and leading indicators buy time to correct.
- **Data rights are technical.** Lost data is lost future change capability.

## Key Takeaways

- Run all eight technical management processes as an integrated control system.
- Keep the SEP current as the authoritative technical planning artifact.
- Use TPMs, leading indicators, and risk management to see trouble early.
- Control baselines, interfaces, and technical data with the same seriousness as requirements.
- Tailor depth; do not drop a process silently when risk still needs it.
